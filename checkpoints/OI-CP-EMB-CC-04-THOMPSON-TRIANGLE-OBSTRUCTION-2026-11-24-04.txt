============================================================
1. PURPOSE AND SCOPE
============================================================

This checkpoint tests whether a finite family of finite source objects can have finite common witnesses for every proper subdiagram while the complete finite diagram has no finite common witness.

The result is a classical finite group amalgam due to Kenneth S. Brown, based on a triangle of finite groups whose direct limit is Thompson's infinite simple group.

This is a result in the category of groups with injective homomorphisms. It is NOT identified with the OMEGA-ISOP realization class or its unspecified Adm predicate.

Primary source:
Kenneth S. Brown, "The Geometry of Finitely Presented Infinite Simple Groups", 1992, Section 6, Theorem 3 and Corollary, pp. 133-134.
PDF:
https://pi.math.cornell.edu/~kbrown/scan/1992.0000.0121.pdf

Relevant statements:
- Section 1 defines a triangle of groups and its direct limit.
- A triangle is realizable when the vertex groups embed and the edge groups are exactly the intersections of the corresponding vertex groups.
- Theorem 3 constructs a realizable triangle of finite groups whose amalgamated free product is Thompson's infinite simple group.
- The corollary proves that this finite amalgam is not embeddable in any finite group.

3. THE FINITE DIAGRAM
============================================================

Vertex groups:
A = S_5
B = S_6
C = S_7

Edge groups:
U = S_4, embedded in A and B;
V = S_5, embedded in B and C;
W = S_3 x S_2, embedded in A and C.

All six edge-to-vertex maps are injective.

Map conventions as specified by Brown:

(1) U = S_4 -> A = S_5 and U -> B = S_6:
standard inclusions, with S_4 permuting {1,2,3,4}.

(2) V = S_5 -> B = S_6 and V -> C = S_7:
the embeddings described by the corresponding five-letter supports in Brown's construction.

(3) W = S_3 x S_2 -> A = S_5:
standard embedding; S_3 acts on {1,2,3} and S_2 acts on {4,5}.

(4) W = S_3 x S_2 -> C = S_7:
the first factor acts on {1,2,3}; the nonidentity element of the S_2 factor maps to (46)(57), not to a single transposition.

The three edge groups encode the pairwise identifications. The diagram is commutative by construction.

Brown's Theorem 3 states that this triangle is realizable and that its amalgamated free product/direct limit is Thompson's infinite simple group G.

============================================================
4. PROOF THAT NO FINITE COMMON TARGET EXISTS
============================================================

Let G be the direct limit (colimit) of the triangle diagram.

Brown's Theorem 3 identifies G with Thompson's infinite simple group. Therefore:
- G is infinite;
- G is simple;
- G is nontrivial.

Assume, for contradiction, that there is a finite group Q and a compatible cocone of injective homomorphisms:
f_A: A -> Q
f_B: B -> Q
f_C: C -> Q

Compatibility means that the two maps from each edge group into Q agree after composition with the corresponding edge-to-vertex maps.

By the universal property of the direct limit, this compatible cocone induces a homomorphism:
phi: G -> Q.

Since G is simple, ker(phi) is either:
ker(phi) = {e}
or
ker(phi) = G.

Case 1:
ker(phi) = {e}.
Then phi is injective. This is impossible because G is infinite and Q is finite.

Case 2:
ker(phi) = G.
Then phi is the trivial homomorphism. Its restriction to each vertex group is trivial, contradicting injectivity of f_A, f_B, and f_C because A, B, and C are nontrivial.

Both cases are impossible.

Therefore:
NO FINITE GROUP Q admits a compatible cocone of injective maps for the complete triangle.

This excludes every finite common target in K, not merely one chosen pushout or generated subgroup.

============================================================
5. INFINITE WITNESS AND STRONGNESS
============================================================

An infinite common target does exist: G itself.

Brown's construction gives a realizable triangle. In Brown's definition, realizability means:
- each vertex group embeds in G;
- each edge group maps onto the intersection of the corresponding vertex-group images.

Thus G is an infinite common witness with the exact pairwise intersections prescribed by U, V, and W.

This is stronger than merely having compatible embeddings. It preserves the specified pairwise intersections.

For this three-vertex diagram, the exact pairwise-intersection property is the relevant strongness condition.

Consequently:
WEAK COMMON WITNESS IN SOME GROUP: YES.
STRONG / EXACT-INTERSECTION COMMON WITNESS IN SOME GROUP: YES.
WEAK COMMON WITNESS IN A FINITE GROUP: NO.
STRONG COMMON WITNESS IN A FINITE GROUP: NO.

The finite impossibility is stronger than a failure of strongness: even weak compatible embeddings into a finite group are impossible.

============================================================
6. PROPER SUBDIAGRAM CHECK
============================================================

Every proper two-vertex edge amalgam consists of two finite groups with a common finite subgroup and injective maps.

The classical two-factor theorem states that an amalgam of two finite groups can be embedded in a finite group. This is also summarized in the Encyclopedia of Mathematics entry "Amalgam of groups":
https://encyclopediaofmath.org/wiki/Amalgam_of_groups

Therefore each individual edge amalgam has a finite common completion.

The obstruction appears at the complete three-vertex diagram, where all three pairwise identifications must hold simultaneously.

This establishes the required finite-family pattern:
- each vertex object is finite;
- each edge/base object is finite;
- each two-vertex edge amalgam has a finite completion;
- the full three-vertex diagram has no finite common completion;
- the full diagram does have an infinite strong realization.

The statement concerns the three pairwise edge amalgams as separate proper subdiagrams. It does not claim that every conceivable subdiagram or every alternative choice of embeddings has the same property.

============================================================

C. Failure of all amalgams:
NO. An infinite realization exists.

D. Failure of finite amalgams:
YES. No finite common target exists.

E. Strongness:
An infinite exact-intersection realization exists; no finite weak realization exists, hence no finite strong realization exists.

F. Two-vertex versus three-vertex distinction:
Two finite groups with a common finite subgroup admit a finite embedding; the three-vertex triangle can nevertheless fail finite embeddability.
