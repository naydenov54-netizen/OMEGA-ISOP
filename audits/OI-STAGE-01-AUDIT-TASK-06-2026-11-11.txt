2. FORMAL SETTING

Let X be a set and let D:X×X→{0,1}. Define x~y iff D(x,y)=0.

A1: D(x,x)=0 for every x∈X.
A2: D(x,y)=D(y,x) for every x,y∈X.
A3: D(x,z)=1 implies D(x,y)=1 or D(y,z)=1 for every x,y,z∈X.

For the signature formulation, let (Y_i) be a family of sets and f_i:X→Y_i. Define:
D_f(x,y)=1 iff there exists i such that f_i(x)≠f_i(y).

3. FINDINGS

F06-01 — AXIOMATIC COMPLETENESS

For a primitive binary predicate D:X×X→{0,1}, A1, A2, and A3 are equivalent to the assertion that ~, defined by x~y iff D(x,y)=0, is an equivalence relation.

A1 gives reflexivity of ~. A2 gives symmetry. A3 gives transitivity: if D(x,y)=0 and D(y,z)=0, then A3 rules out D(x,z)=1, hence D(x,z)=0 because D is {0,1}-valued. Conversely, reflexivity, symmetry, and transitivity of ~ imply A1, A2, and A3.

STATUS: PROVED.

F06-02 — IRREDUNDANCY AND SCOPE

The countermodels recorded in Audit Task 04 show that none of A1, A2, A3 follows from the other two over the class of all set-theoretic models. Thus the axiom set is irredundant in that model class.

This does not establish absolute syntactic minimality among every possible formal presentation or every alternative axiom language.

STATUS: PROVED WITH SCOPE LIMITATION.

F06-03 — SIGNATURE/PREDICATE COMPATIBILITY

In classical set theory, every signature-induced predicate D_f satisfies A1–A3. Conversely, for every primitive D satisfying A1–A3, define Q=X/~ and q:X→Q by q(x)=[x]. Then:
D(x,y)=1 iff q(x)≠q(y).

Thus every admissible primitive predicate has a quotient-signature representation. The representation is not unique: different codomains and injective recodings can induce the same equality kernel.

STATUS: PROVED IN CLASSICAL SET-THEORETIC SETTING.

F06-04 — INFORMATION CONTENT OF D

For an admissible D, let P be its partition into ~-equivalence classes. Then:
D(x,y)=0 iff x and y belong to the same block of P.
D(x,y)=1 iff x and y belong to different blocks of P.

The partition determines D uniquely, and D determines the partition uniquely. Therefore, within the declared binary-predicate framework and under A1–A3, D contains no additional mathematical information beyond the induced partition.

This conclusion concerns the abstract structure (X,D), not any physical or empirical interpretation assigned to its elements or blocks.

STATUS: PROVED.

F06-05 — DIFFERENCE/RELATION BOUNDARY

A binary predicate D:X×X→{0,1} is, in standard mathematical terminology, itself a binary relation (its 1-valued extension is a subset of X×X). It would therefore be inaccurate to claim that Stage 1 contains no relation in the general mathematical sense and that Stage 2 introduces binary relations for the first time.

The stage distinction must instead be stated structurally:
- Stage 1 — Difference: a symmetric binary distinguishability predicate whose zero-kernel is an equivalence relation and whose induced structure is a partition/quotient.
- Stage 2 — Relation: additional relational structure R on X, or between derived states/classes, not uniquely determined by D alone.

D alone does not select direction, causal order, temporal order, transition composition, influence, or dynamics. Any such structure requires additional definitions or assumptions.

This is a clarification of terminology and stage boundaries, not an architectural modification.

STATUS: CONCEPTUAL BOUNDARY CLARIFIED.

F06-06 — LOGICAL SCOPE

Two constructions must not be conflated.

(A) Primitive binary predicate D:X×X→{0,1}. Its codomain is a two-element decidable set. The proof that the zero-kernel is transitive from A3 can use the binary case distinction.

(B) Signature predicate defined by an existential inequality condition, D_f(x,y)=1 iff ∃i(f_i(x)≠f_i(y)). Deriving cotransitivity directly for arbitrary codomains Y_i can require classical logic, decidable equality, or an appropriate constructive apartness/witness structure, depending on the foundational setting.

In classical set theory the formulations are compatible as established in F06-03. In constructive settings, no unrestricted equivalence between the two proof routes is asserted without the relevant logical and equality assumptions.

STATUS: LOGICAL SCOPE CLARIFICATION REQUIRED IN ANY FOUNDATIONAL PRESENTATION.
