1. PRIMARY SOURCE STATEMENTS

1.1 Volume II, section III.4 — Local Configurations

The source defines a local configuration as:

C = (S_C,#_C)

where:

S_C subseteq U0
#_C = # restricted to S_C x S_C.

A minimal nontrivial configuration satisfies:

|S_C| >= 2.

The exact admissibility conditions remain realization-dependent.

The set of admissible configurations may be represented as a category C_METACORE whose objects are admissible configurations and whose morphisms preserve the selected structural properties.

1.2 Volume II, section III.5 — Configuration Embeddings

For:

C1 = (S1,#1)
C2 = (S2,#2),

an embedding f:C1->C2 is injective and satisfies:

x #1 y  iff  f(x) #2 f(y).

The source explicitly states that the biconditional is essential and that preservation alone does not establish structural reflection.

It also states that an embedding represents structural correspondence within the chosen mathematical realization, not ontological identity.

1.3 Volume II, sections IV.1-IV.3 — Compatibility and Colimit Boundary

Compatibility is defined by:

CC(C1,C2)=1 iff an admissible amalgamation of C1 and C2 exists;

otherwise:

CC(C1,C2)=0.

D_CC denotes the diagram generated by selected configurations and their admissible compatibility relations.

Where the required categorical construction exists:

R := colim(D_CC).

The source makes existence conditional on:
- a valid compatibility diagram;
- the relevant categorical structure;
- existence of the required colimit or an equivalent constraint-preserving construction.

No universal existence theorem for R is asserted.

1.4 Volume III, sections 5-6 — Nontriviality

For a finite carrier with apartness, the nontriviality test is:

|X| >= 2
and
there exist x,y in X such that x # y.

At configuration level, the test applies to S_C itself:

|S_C| >= 2
and
there exist x,y in S_C such that x # y.

Unused elements elsewhere in the carrier do not satisfy the configuration-level test.

1.5 Volume III, sections 8-9 — Categorical Completeness

A relational specification may be represented as:

R = colim(D_CC),

provided the colimit exists.

The source states that D_CC alone is not a complete categorical construction. It requires:
- an indexing category J;
- a functor D:J->C_METACORE;
- relevant objects and morphisms;
- compatibility conditions;
- the required universal property.

Until these are supplied, the formalization remains OPEN.

B. LOGICALLY DERIVABLE FROM THE EXPLICIT EMBEDDING DEFINITION

Assuming the same selected apartness structure is used on the configurations:

1. Every embedding is injective by definition.

2. Every embedding preserves apartness.

3. Every embedding reflects apartness.

4. The identity map on a configuration is an embedding.

5. The composition of two embeddings is an embedding:
if f:C1->C2 and g:C2->C3 are embeddings, then g o f is injective and

x #1 y
iff f(x) #2 f(y)
iff g(f(x)) #3 g(f(y)).

6. The inverse of a bijective embedding is an embedding.

These facts show that the class of all such embeddings is mathematically closed under identities and composition, for a fixed object class. They do not prove that C_METACORE selects all such embeddings as its morphisms.

3. ANSWERS TO THE SIX AUDIT QUESTIONS

Q1. What exactly is a configuration embedding?

SOURCE ANSWER:
An injective map f:C1->C2 satisfying the biconditional:

x #1 y iff f(x) #2 f(y).

The biconditional, not one-way preservation, is required.

Q2. Is injectivity alone sufficient?

NO.
Injectivity is necessary but not sufficient. Preservation and reflection of the selected apartness structure are also required.

Q3. Is preservation and reflection required?

YES.
It is explicit in Volume II, III.5.

Q4. Are arbitrary local embeddings between configurations on subsets of U0 permitted?

The source defines an embedding between configurations C1=(S1,#1) and C2=(S2,#2) as an injective map with the stated biconditional. It does not add a requirement that the map be a literal inclusion. Therefore a non-inclusion map can satisfy the source's definition of an embedding.

However, the source does not explicitly state that every such embedding is admitted as a morphism of C_METACORE. This distinction is decisive.

Q5. Must an embedding extend to a global automorphism?

No such extension requirement appears in the inspected Volume II passages III.4-III.5 or IV.1-IV.3, or Volume III sections 5-9.

This is a statement about the inspected passages only. It is not a claim that no other project document could add such a requirement.

Q6. Are configuration embeddings explicitly identified with C_METACORE morphisms?

Not completely.
Volume II III.4 says C_METACORE morphisms preserve selected structural properties. Volume II III.5 separately defines configuration embeddings by injectivity and apartness preservation/reflection. The inspected text does not explicitly state the equality:

Mor(C_METACORE) = all configuration embeddings.

Nor does it state a different complete morphism class.

Therefore the relationship is strongly suggested as a candidate interpretation but is not fully specified by these passages.

4. CONSEQUENCES FOR THE THREE TEST CATEGORIES

C_U0-INC:
Literal inclusions are a possible restricted morphism class. They satisfy the embedding condition whenever the configurations carry induced apartness. The source does not state that only inclusions are permitted.

C_U0-ISO:
Isomorphisms are bijective embeddings. They form a valid restricted class of embeddings. The source does not state that only bijective embeddings are permitted.

C_U0-EMB:
All local injective maps preserving and reflecting apartness form a mathematically coherent candidate morphism class for a fixed admissible object class. The source's embedding definition supports this candidate at the level of individual maps. But the source does not explicitly identify all of them with the morphisms of C_METACORE.

Therefore none of the three test categories is yet established as the unique source-mandated C_METACORE.

5. CONSEQUENCES FOR AMALGAMATION AND COLIMIT

The source defines CC through existence of an admissible amalgamation, but the exact meaning of admissibility depends on the relevant category and its morphisms.

The source also defines R through a colimit only where the required categorical construction exists.

Consequently:
- A positive amalgamation in C_U0-INC is category-relative.
- A positive pushout in C_U0-ISO is category-relative.
- A failure in a fixed-carrier all-embeddings category is also category-relative.
- None of these results alone settles the project-level CC or proves existence/nonexistence of the source-level R.

The finite constructions S2-04C-05 and S2-04C-06 remain valid as test-category results. They must not be promoted to project-level categorical conclusions.
