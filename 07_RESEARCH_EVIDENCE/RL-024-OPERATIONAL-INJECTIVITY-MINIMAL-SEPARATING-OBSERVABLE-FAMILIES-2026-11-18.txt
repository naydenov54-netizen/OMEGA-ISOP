1. ROLE
Parent RL for the operational-observability line.
RL-024 establishes the formal relation between operational observables, injectivity, separation, exact operational reconstruction, and minimal separating observable families.
It does NOT establish semantic OMEGA identification, physical ontology, universal observer families, or a universal minimum number of observables.

2. FORMAL SETTING
Let:
P_O : R_real -> O_R
be the operational observation map induced by observable family O.
For r1,r2 ∈ R_real define:
D_O(r1,r2) = Δ(P_O(r1), P_O(r2))
where Δ is the specified separation metric.
Define the operational equivalence relation:
r1 ~_O r2 iff P_O(r1) = P_O(r2)
For S ⊆ O:
P_S = π_S ∘ P_O
and correspondingly:
D_S(r1,r2) = Δ(P_S(r1), P_S(r2))

3. ASSUMPTIONS
A1. R_C is an explicitly specified admissible realization class.
A2. P_O is well-defined on R_C.
A3. Δ is nonnegative.
A4. Δ(x,y)=0 iff x=y.
A5. Operational identification is distinct from semantic OMEGA identification.
A6. Exact reconstruction is always relative to the specified realization class R_C.
A7. No universal minimum observable count is assumed.
A8. No uniqueness of a minimal observable subset is assumed.
A9. A concrete model/domain result is not elevated to a universal identification law.

4. DEFINITION: SEPARATING OBSERVABLE FAMILY
O separates R_C iff:
∀r1,r2 ∈ R_C,r1 != r2 => P_O(r1) != P_O(r2)
Under A3-A4 this is equivalent to:
∀r1,r2 ∈ R_C,r1 != r2 => D_O(r1,r2) > 0

5. MAIN DERIVATION
P_O is injective on R_C iff O separates R_C.
Therefore:
P_O injective on R_C
iff
O separates R_C
iff
all distinct realization pairs have D_O > 0.
Consequently, exact operational reconstruction is possible whenever P_O is injective on R_C.
This reconstruction is explicitly relative to R_C.
No claim is made that operational reconstruction constitutes semantic identification of an OMEGA component.

6. MONOTONICITY
If:
S ⊆ S' ⊆ O
and S separates R_C, then S' also separates R_C.
Thus adding observables cannot destroy an already established separating property.

7. MINIMAL SEPARATING FAMILIES
If O is finite and separates R_C, at least one inclusion-minimal separating subset exists.
S is inclusion-minimal separating iff:
S separates R_C
and for every q ∈ S:
S  {q}
does not separate R_C.
Hence each member of an inclusion-minimal separating family has a witness degeneracy pair that becomes indistinguishable when that member is removed.
Define minimum cardinality:
m(R_C,O) = min{|S| : S ⊆ O and P_S is injective on R_C}
when at least one separating subset exists.
A minimum-cardinality separating family is inclusion-minimal.
The converse is not generally true.
Different inclusion-minimal separating families may:
differ in membership;
differ in cardinality;
both satisfy the same separation requirement.
Therefore no universal unique minimal observable family follows from the theorem.

8. SCIENTIFIC BOUNDARY
The result establishes a formal operational theorem:
injectivity <-> separation <-> exact operational reconstruction
within the specified realization class.
It does NOT establish:
operational separation = semantic OMEGA identification;
operational identity = semantic identity;
observable injectivity = physical ontology;
a universal observer family;
a universal minimum observable count;
uniqueness of minimal separating families;
a universal identification law.

SCIENTIFIC REGISTRATION NOTE
The supplied text is archived without changing its stated classifications. Registration is not an independent proof audit. Two formal scope points remain for any later mathematical audit:
1. The notation Δ is applied to both P_O and P_S outputs; its domain/codomain or a compatible family of separation metrics must be specified so each expression is well-defined.
2. Injectivity gives a set-theoretic inverse on the image of P_O. Continuity, stability, measurability, or computability of a reconstruction procedure require additional assumptions and are not implied by injectivity alone.
The finite-family existence claim in Section 7 is valid under the stated finite-O and separation assumptions. Inclusion-minimality and minimum cardinality are correctly distinguished.
