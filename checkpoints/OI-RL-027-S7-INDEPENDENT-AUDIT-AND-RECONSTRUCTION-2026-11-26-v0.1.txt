3. RECONSTRUCTED MATHEMATICAL RESULTS
==================================================

Problem:
F(p,eta) = {q >= 0 : sum_i q_i = 1, D(p,q) <= eta}
purity(q) = sum_i q_i^2

MIN:
- If D(p,u) <= eta, the unique minimizer is u.
- If eta = 0, the minimizer is p.
- Otherwise the minimizer is q_i = clip(p_i, beta, alpha), where alpha and beta satisfy:
  sum_i (p_i-alpha)+ = eta
  sum_i (beta-p_i)+ = eta
- The supplied proof uses strict convexity and a variational inequality. It is mathematically coherent under the stated finite-dimensional probability-simplex assumptions.

MAX:
- Let p be sorted descending and delta = min(eta, 1-p_1).
- Add delta to a largest component and remove mass delta from the smallest components, bottom-up.
- The supplied proof uses partial-sum bounds and majorization. It is mathematically coherent under the stated assumptions.
- The maximizing vector is unique only up to permutations in degenerate cases.

Spectral reduction:
The stated trace-distance spectral inequality gives the necessary spectral TV bound. Commuting states attain equality, so the spectral optimization captures the attainable purity extrema for a fixed reference spectrum and trace-distance ball, under the stated finite-dimensional setting.
