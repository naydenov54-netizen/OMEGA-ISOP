1. QUESTION
Can an independently calibrated observer clock make the de Sitter expansion scale H identifiable from an observable without supplying a standard ruler or an external volume measure?

2. MODEL AND OBSERVABLE
Assume an ideal spatially flat de Sitter/FLRW model with constant H > 0, comoving source and observer, and a well-defined cosmological redshift z > 0. In the standard FLRW redshift-drift relation:
dz/dtau_0 = (1 + z) H_0 - H(z),
where tau_0 is the observer's proper time and H(z) is the expansion rate at the source redshift in the specified FLRW model.

For exact de Sitter:
H(z) = H_0 = H.
Therefore:
dz/dtau_0 = z H.
If z is nonzero and the derivative is measured per independently calibrated observer proper-time unit:
H = (1/z) dz/dtau_0.

This is a conditional identifiability formula for the ideal de Sitter model, not a claim that redshift drift has already been measured with sufficient precision in a real survey.

3. INJECTIVITY TEST
Define the ideal observable:
O(H) = (1/z) dz/dtau_0.
Under the stated model and fixed calibration of tau_0:
O(H) = H.
For H_1 != H_2:
O(H_1) != O(H_2).
Thus the map H -> O(H) is injective on H > 0 when z > 0 and the observer-time unit is fixed independently.

PROOF STATUS: PROVED UNDER THE MODEL AND CALIBRATION ASSUMPTIONS IN SECTION 2.

4. WHERE THE SCALE ENTERS
The result is not a derivation of an absolute time scale from causal order alone. The observable dz/dtau_0 is a rate per observer proper time, and its numerical value requires an operational time unit. That unit is supplied by an independently calibrated clock or equivalent physical process.

If the clock's calibration or all matter parameters co-transform with the metric rescaling, the numerical value of H in the corresponding rescaled units also changes, and the apparent scale determination does not establish an absolute scale independent of that calibration.

For g' = k^2 g in the de Sitter family:
H' = H/k.
Proper-time intervals computed from the rescaled metric satisfy:
d tau' = k d tau.
The product H' d tau' = H d tau is invariant. Hence dimensionless redshift histories remain compatible with the same rescaled geometry. The inverse-time numerical value of H becomes identifiable only relative to a time unit whose physical calibration is held fixed in the stated model.
