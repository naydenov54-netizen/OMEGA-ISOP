3. STAGE A: PRIMITIVE OPERATIONAL ENTITIES
==================================================

For a concrete realization, the following entities must be distinguished.

A1. REALIZED MODEL

M contains at least:
- a state space X;
- a measurable structure Sigma_X when probabilistic or measurable operations are used;
- a selected dynamics D_M, deterministic or stochastic;
- the declared admissible control/intervention structure, if any;
- the initialization convention, either included in M or supplied by each operational specification.

This is realization-level data. It is not a new Frozen Core primitive.

A2. INITIALIZATION / EXPERIMENTAL CONTEXT

An execution requires an initial state x0 or initial probability measure mu0, and may require:
- a time or iteration horizon;
- boundary or environmental conditions;
- a permitted control set;
- a sampling convention;
- an outcome/readout convention.

The primary interface does not say whether this context is part of M or part of a in L_op. A realization must choose one convention explicitly and use it consistently.

A3. OPERATIONAL SPECIFICATION

An element a in L_op must denote a well-typed operational specification, not merely an untyped label.

At minimum it must determine:
- its operational role or type;
- the model/context on which it is defined;
- its admissibility conditions;
- its outcome or state-update semantics;
- its response/readout convention;
- its protocol position or composition rule, when sequential.

A4. OUTCOME / RESPONSE OBJECT

The raw result of executing a may be a value, a random variable, a probability distribution, a trajectory, or a structured record. These are distinct possible outcome types and must not be silently identified.

A5. RESPONSE SPACE

Y is the normed response space in which the final reported responses are compared. Its norm and the mapping from raw outcomes to Y must be declared.

A6. ADMISSIBILITY

Adm(M,a) states whether operational specification a is defined and permitted for model M under the declared context.

A7. COMPARISON CLASS

C is the selected class of models being compared. It must be fixed before defining a shared operational domain or D_L over C.

4. STAGE B: TYPE SEPARATION
==================================================

The phrase "probes or interventions" does not establish that all operational objects have one semantic type. At least the following types must be kept distinct.

TYPE P: OBSERVATIONAL PROBE

A pure observational probe obtains an outcome without including a system-state update as part of its specified action.

A general stochastic outcome model may be written:
Q_p : X -> Prob(O_p)

where O_p is the outcome space and Q_p(x,.) is the outcome distribution at state x.

A deterministic readout is a special case.

Important limitation:
A physical measurement may disturb the system. Such a measurement cannot be modeled as a non-disturbing probe unless that property is justified. If the measurement includes a post-measurement state update, the operational object must include that update and must be typed as an instrument or as a combined probe/intervention.

TYPE I-D: DETERMINISTIC INTERVENTION

A deterministic intervention is represented, at state level, by:
A_i : X -> X

It may be partial, in which case its domain and admissibility conditions must be specified.

A readout may follow the intervention, but the readout is not identical to the intervention.

TYPE I-S: STOCHASTIC INTERVENTION

A stochastic intervention is represented by a kernel:
pi_i : X x Sigma_X -> [0,1]

with the probability-kernel conditions on the declared measurable state space.

The intervention kernel is not identical to the system's uncontrolled Markov kernel unless that equality is independently specified.

TYPE A: ADAPTIVE PROTOCOL

An adaptive protocol is a history-dependent sequence of operational choices.

A history may have the form:
h_t = (o_0, a_0, o_1, a_1, ..., o_t)

A policy may be represented by:
alpha_t : H_t -> A_t

where H_t is the admissible history space and A_t is the action set at step t.

The protocol selects later actions using earlier outcomes. It is therefore a higher-level composition of probes and/or interventions, not automatically one primitive observable.

TYPE RELATIONS

These types are not assumed mutually exclusive at the level of a complete experiment:
- an adaptive protocol may contain probes and interventions;
- a measurement instrument may combine an outcome kernel with a post-measurement state-update kernel;
- a protocol may alternate observation, control, and readout.

The required distinction is semantic and typed: the outcome map, state-update map, and history-dependent policy must remain separately identifiable.

5. STAGE C: DOMAINS, ADMISSIBILITY AND COMPOSITION
==================================================

C1. DOMAINS

For each operational specification a, define its execution domain:
Dom(a) subset of the declared model/context space.

For each model M, define:
L_adm(M) = {a : Adm(M,a) holds}.

A specification is admissible only when:
- its input type matches the state/outcome/context type;
- its required measurable or probabilistic structure exists;
- its control or intervention is permitted in M;
- its required readout is defined;
- its protocol order is executable;
- its response is defined in the declared response space.

These are realization-level requirements, not assumptions that every formal operation is physically available.

C2. DETERMINISTIC COMPOSITION

For state maps A1 and A2, sequential execution "A1 then A2" is:
A2 o A1

provided the image of A1 lies in the domain of A2.

Order matters:
A2 o A1 is not generally equal to A1 o A2.

In particular, for system evolution T and intervention A:
T o A and A o T are generally different protocols.

No protocol order may be inferred from the words "probe" or "intervention" alone.

C3. STOCHASTIC COMPOSITION

If pi1 is executed first and pi2 second, their composite kernel is:
(pi1 pi2)(x,E) = integral_X pi2(y,E) pi1(x,dy)

This composition is defined only when the measurable spaces and kernels are compatible.

The convention above explicitly means first pi1, then pi2. Other operator conventions must be stated rather than silently substituted.

C4. ADAPTIVE COMPOSITION

An adaptive protocol is executable only if:
- each alpha_t is defined on the histories that can actually occur;
- its selected action is admissible after that history;
- the next system transition and outcome rule are defined;
- the protocol specifies termination or a horizon.

A policy defined on all imaginable histories is not required; it must be defined on the reachable admissible history domain relevant to the experiment.

C5. PARTIAL COMPOSITION

Operational composition is generally partial. Two actions cannot be composed merely because both belong to a set of labels. Their input/output types, domains, and order must match.

6. STAGE D: RESPONSE SEMANTICS AND RESPONSE SPACE Y
==================================================

D1. EXECUTION-TO-RESPONSE PIPELINE

A response semantics must specify a map from an executed operational specification to a reported element of Y.

A generic realization-level decomposition is:

RawOutcome(M,a) = outcome produced by executing a on M
rho_a : RawOutcome(M,a) -> Y
Resp_M(a) = rho_a(RawOutcome(M,a))

This is a semantic decomposition for research. It does not assert that the primary source already defines RawOutcome or rho_a.

D2. POSSIBLE RESPONSE TYPES

Depending on the task, the raw outcome may be:
- a deterministic value;
- a random variable;
- an empirical sample;
- a probability distribution;
- a finite or infinite trajectory;
- a structured multi-channel record.

A response in Y may be a scalar, vector, function, or another element of a declared normed space. The scalar case Y=R is explicitly allowed by Volume III, but it is not universal.

D3. EXPECTATION RESPONSE IS OPTIONAL

An expectation response such as:
Resp_mu(a) = integral f_a(x) dmu(x)

is one possible semantics for a passive observable under a specified measure and integrability conditions.

It is not the only possible response semantics and is not supplied as the universal definition of Resp_M in the primary sources.

D4. COMPARABILITY REQUIREMENT

For the operational distance:
D_L(M1,M2) = sup over a in L_op of ||Resp_M1(a) - Resp_M2(a)||_Y

the compared responses must:
- be defined for both models for every a in L_op;
- belong to the same normed response space Y;
- use the same norm and compatible response interpretation.

If the models return different raw outcome types, an explicit common reporting map into Y is required before subtraction/norm comparison is meaningful.

D5. CONTEXT PLACEMENT

The initial state/distribution, horizon, environment, and sampling convention must be included either in M or in the full operational specification a. Leaving them implicit can make Resp_M(a) ambiguous.

The sources do not choose between these placements. A concrete realization must declare one.

7. STAGE E: COMMON OPERATIONAL DOMAIN
==================================================

Let C be a fixed comparison class of models.

For each M in C, let L_adm(M) be the set of operational specifications admissible for M.

Define the common operational domain:
L_common(C) = intersection over M in C of L_adm(M).

This definition is a candidate set-theoretic construction, not a claim that the intersection is nonempty.

A common operational language suitable for D_L requires:

E1. NONEMPTY:
L_common(C) is not empty.

E2. SHARED SPECIFICATION:
Each a in L_common(C) denotes the same operational task/protocol across the compared models, not merely similarly named model-specific actions.

E3. RESPONSE TOTALITY:
For every M in C and every a in L_common(C), Resp_M(a) is defined.

E4. COMMON CODOMAIN:
All such responses lie in one fixed normed space Y with one declared norm.

E5. FIXED COMPARISON CLASS:
C and the common protocol semantics are fixed for the comparison.

Then one may set:
L_op := L_common(C)

for that specified comparison problem, and define D_L on C under the stated conditions.

If L_common(C) is empty, or if it contains only labels whose meanings differ across models, the common operational distance is not established by the primary formula. Model-specific distances may still be defined, but they are not automatically comparable through the same L_op.

The primary sources do not prove that L_common(C) is nonempty for an arbitrary comparison class. Existence is a realization-specific obligation.

8. L1-L8 DEPENDENCY / LOOP CHECK FOR THE COMMON-DOMAIN REQUIREMENT
==================================================

TARGET_C:
For a comparison class C, a common operational domain suitable for the stated D_L formula must consist of operational specifications on which every compared response map is defined and returns values in a common normed space Y.

PREMISES_AND_DEFINITIONS:
- Volume III defines Resp_M on L_op and D_L by a supremum over L_op.
- Volume III requires Y to be normed and L_op nonempty for the stated extended-pseudometric property.
- A function must be defined on its declared domain.
- A norm difference requires two elements of the same normed space.

DEPENDENCY_CHAIN:
The supremum formula compares Resp_M1(a) and Resp_M2(a) for each a in L_op.
Therefore both response values must exist for each such a.
The subtraction and norm require a shared codomain and norm.
The nonempty-domain condition is explicitly stated in the Volume III operational-distance section.

POSSIBLE_EQUIVALENT_OR_EMBEDDED_TARGET:
The common-domain requirement is not inserted as an additional physical axiom. It follows from the well-formedness of the response comparison already written in D_L.
The existence of a nonempty common domain is not definitionally guaranteed by defining the intersection.

INDEPENDENT_JUSTIFICATION:
This is a type/domain check on the existing response and distance definitions. It does not rely on H3, tau, Koopman invariance, or the desired non-expansion conclusion.

COUNTERMODEL_ATTEMPT_AND_RESULT:
Take two models M1 and M2 with disjoint admissible protocol sets:
L_adm(M1) = {a}
L_adm(M2) = {b}
where a and b are not the same shared protocol specification.
Then:
L_common({M1,M2}) = empty.
The primary source does not exclude this possibility.
Thus a nonempty common domain does not follow from the abstract existence of individual operational languages.

PREMISE_REMOVAL_TEST:
- Remove response totality: the supremum expression may contain undefined terms.
- Remove the common codomain/norm: the difference or norm may be undefined.
- Remove nonemptiness: the stated Volume III extended-pseudometric condition is not met; an empty-supremum convention must not be used to imply operational indistinguishability.
- Remove shared protocol meaning: the comparison may conflate different tasks under identical labels.

CLASSIFICATION:
CONDITIONAL FORMAL REQUIREMENT.
The common-domain conditions are necessary for the stated cross-model D_L comparison to be well-formed. Nonemptiness and semantic compatibility remain unproved for any particular OMEGA-ISOP realization.

NON-IMPLICATIONS:
This does not prove that a common domain exists in every realization.
It does not identify the intended operational carrier.
It does not define j.
It does not establish a common tau.
It does not establish H3 or Theorem 91 beyond its existing conditional status.
It does not establish physical measurement semantics or empirical accessibility.

9. RESULTS AND CURRENT STAGE STATUS
==================================================

RESULT A:
The primary sources provide an abstract operational interface, not a complete typed operational semantics.

RESULT B:
At minimum, observation/readout, state update/intervention, and history-dependent protocol selection must be semantically distinguished.

RESULT C:
A probe may be non-disturbing, but this cannot be assumed universally. If a measurement changes the state, its state-update semantics must be included explicitly.

RESULT D:
Operational composition is typed and generally order-sensitive. Deterministic maps and stochastic kernels have distinct composition rules; adaptive protocols require a history domain and policy.

RESULT E:
The response map requires a declared execution context and a map from raw outcomes to a common normed response space Y. Expectation response is one possible realization, not a universal definition.

RESULT F:
For cross-model D_L, the shared operational domain must be nonempty, semantically common, response-total, and valued in one common normed space. The primary sources do not establish that such a domain exists for a given comparison class.
