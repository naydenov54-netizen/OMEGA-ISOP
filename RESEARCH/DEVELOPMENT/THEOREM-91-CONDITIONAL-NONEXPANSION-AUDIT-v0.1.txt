==================================================
1. SOURCE AND SCOPE CONTROL
==================================================

This audit concerns the statement labelled THEOREM 91: CONDITIONAL NON-EXPANSION in Section 9 of Handoff v1.8.

It does not claim to have recovered or inspected the original primary theorem source.

It is not an audit of any separately numbered THEOREM 91.3 or THEOREM 91.4. Those primary records were not located in the inspected GitHub repository or Library results during this work stage.

The reviewed Handoff statement is:

D_L(M1,M2) = sup over a in L_op of ||Resp_M1(a)-Resp_M2(a)||_Y

with:
H1: R exists, T is admissible, and R is preserved.
H2: Y is normed.
H3: There exists a map tau:L_op->L_op such that Resp_(T(M))(a)=Resp_M(tau(a)).
H4: The operational criterion and comparison class remain fixed.

Conclusion:
D_L(T(M1),T(M2)) <= D_L(M1,M2).

==================================================
2. REQUIRED FORMAL DOMAIN
==================================================

For the inequality to be stated precisely, require:

1. A nonempty operational probe set L_op.
2. A normed response space Y.
3. A comparison class C of admissible models.
4. For every M in C, response maps Resp_M:L_op->Y.
5. T(M) is defined for every M in C.
6. The post-transformation response maps are defined on the same L_op and take values in the same Y.
7. The same norm and operational comparison criterion are used before and after T.

Under these conditions D_L is an extended pseudometric on C. It may take the value infinity. It is not necessarily a metric because distinct models may have identical response maps on L_op.

==================================================
3. LOOP AUDIT
==================================================

TARGET_C:

For all M1,M2 in C:

D_L(T(M1),T(M2)) <= D_L(M1,M2).

PREMISES_AND_DEFINITIONS:

- Definition of D_L as a supremum over the same probe set.
- Norm structure on Y.
- T maps each compared model into a model with a defined response map.
- A common probe transformation tau maps L_op into L_op.
- The response identity holds for every model in C and every probe a in L_op.

DEPENDENCY_CHAIN:

For a fixed pair M1,M2 and each a in L_op:

||Resp_(T(M1))(a)-Resp_(T(M2))(a)||_Y

= ||Resp_M1(tau(a))-Resp_M2(tau(a))||_Y

<= sup over b in L_op of ||Resp_M1(b)-Resp_M2(b)||_Y

= D_L(M1,M2),

because tau(a) belongs to L_op.

Taking the supremum over a in L_op gives:

D_L(T(M1),T(M2)) <= D_L(M1,M2).

POSSIBLE_EQUIVALENT_OR_EMBEDDED_TARGET:

The response identity in H3 is not identical to the non-expansion conclusion. However, it is the essential compatibility premise that directly yields the inequality.

The theorem therefore proves a conditional consequence of H3. It does not derive H3 from H1, from the Frozen Core, or from the general OMEGA-ISOP architecture.

INDEPENDENT_JUSTIFICATION:

The supremum inequality follows from set inclusion:

tau(L_op) subseteq L_op.

No assumption that tau is injective or surjective is needed for non-expansion.

COUNTERMODEL_ATTEMPT_AND_RESULT:

The quantification of tau is essential.

If tau is allowed to depend on the model, the non-expansion conclusion need not hold.

Take:

L_op={a,b}
Y=R

Define two models by:

Resp_M1(a)=0
Resp_M1(b)=100

Resp_M2(a)=1
Resp_M2(b)=101

Then:

D_L(M1,M2)=1.

Now allow separate maps:

tau_M1(a)=a
tau_M1(b)=a

tau_M2(a)=b
tau_M2(b)=b

Define the transformed responses through the model-dependent identities:

Resp_(T(M))(a)=Resp_M(tau_M(a)).

Then:

Resp_(T(M1))(a)=0
Resp_(T(M1))(b)=0

Resp_(T(M2))(a)=101
Resp_(T(M2))(b)=101

Therefore:

D_L(T(M1),T(M2))=101>1=D_L(M1,M2).

Thus a model-dependent probe map does not suffice.

CLASSIFICATION OF THIS COUNTERMODEL:

It refutes the weaker interpretation in which H3 permits a different tau for each M. It does not refute the theorem when H3 supplies one common tau for every model in the comparison class.

PREMISE-REMOVAL_TEST:

- Remove the common-tau requirement: the countermodel above shows the conclusion can fail.
- Remove tau(L_op) subseteq L_op: the supremum comparison is no longer justified.
- Remove the common response norm or common operational comparison class: the displayed comparison may not be defined on the same basis.
- H1 is not used in the algebraic derivation of the inequality. It can serve as a structural admissibility condition for the intended OMEGA-ISOP interpretation, but its terms require independent definitions and verification.
- H2 is necessary for the norm expression as written.
- H4 must ensure that the same L_op, response codomain, norm, and comparison class are retained.

CLASSIFICATION:

CONDITIONAL RESULT, VALID WHEN H3 IS READ AS ONE COMMON MAP tau FOR ALL M IN THE COMPARISON CLASS AND THE SHARED-DOMAIN CONDITIONS ARE EXPLICIT.

==================================================
4. PRECISE FORMULATION RECOMMENDED
==================================================

Let C be a class of admissible models and let L_op be a nonempty set.

Let (Y,||.||_Y) be a normed space.

For every M in C, let:

Resp_M:L_op->Y.

Let T:C->C be defined on the comparison class.

Assume there exists ONE map:

tau:L_op->L_op

independent of M, such that:

for every M in C and every a in L_op,

Resp_(T(M))(a)=Resp_M(tau(a)).

Define:

D_L(M1,M2)=sup(a in L_op) ||Resp_M1(a)-Resp_M2(a)||_Y.

Then, for every M1,M2 in C:

D_L(T(M1),T(M2)) <= D_L(M1,M2).

PROOF:

For every a in L_op:

||Resp_(T(M1))(a)-Resp_(T(M2))(a)||_Y

=||Resp_M1(tau(a))-Resp_M2(tau(a))||_Y

<=D_L(M1,M2),

because tau(a) is an element of L_op.

Taking the supremum over all a in L_op yields:

D_L(T(M1),T(M2))<=D_L(M1,M2).

Q.E.D.

No surjectivity of tau is required.

If tau is surjective, then tau(L_op)=L_op and the same argument gives equality:

D_L(T(M1),T(M2))=D_L(M1,M2).

If tau is not surjective, strict decrease may occur because the transformed comparison samples only the subset tau(L_op) of the original operational probes.

==================================================
5. ROLE OF H1 AND H4
==================================================

H1:

R exists, T is admissible, and R is preserved.

H1 is not used in the metric inequality once T and the response maps are already defined. It may be required to ensure that T is structurally admissible within the intended framework. The exact meanings of R, admissibility, and preservation must be supplied by the authoritative source definitions.

H4:

The operational criterion and comparison class remain fixed.

H4 is important for interpretation, but it should be made mathematically explicit by requiring the same probe set L_op, response space Y, norm, and model comparison class before and after T.

Neither H1 nor H4, in its abbreviated current wording, independently proves the response identity in H3.

==================================================
6. INTERPRETATION AND NON-IMPLICATIONS
==================================================

The theorem establishes non-expansion of the specified operational response distance under a common probe-reparameterization identity.

It does not establish:

1. Universal information conservation.
2. Shannon information conservation.
3. Injectivity of T.
4. Reversibility of T.
5. Equality of operational response distances unless tau is surjective.
6. Physical realization of T.
7. That every admissible transformation satisfies H3.
8. That H3 follows from the Frozen Core.
9. A universal contraction law.
10. A causal or dynamical law without additional definitions and evidence.

If tau is not surjective, non-expansion may reflect that the transformed comparison probes only a subset of the original operational probes. A decrease in D_L therefore must not automatically be interpreted as physical information loss or conservation.

==================================================
7. FINAL FINDINGS
==================================================

FINDING 1:
The non-expansion inequality follows rigorously from one common probe map tau:L_op->L_op and the response identity holding for all models in the comparison class.

FINDING 2:
The wording of H3 should explicitly quantify the same tau uniformly over every compared model. If tau is model-dependent, the conclusion is false in general.

FINDING 3:
H1 is a structural admissibility condition, not an algebraic premise needed for the supremum inequality itself.

FINDING 4:
H4 should explicitly preserve the probe set, response norm, and comparison class.

FINDING 5:
The result is conditional. It does not derive H3 or establish universal non-expansion for arbitrary transformations.

RECOMMENDATION:

Do not modify any canonical theorem source on the basis of this audit.
Retain the result as a conditional theorem.
Before any canonical revision, inspect the authoritative primary source and determine whether the common-tau quantifier is already explicit there.
If a revision is later authorized, state H3 with a single tau independent of M and retain the exact domain and non-implications.
