F1 — OBSERVATIONAL EQUIVALENCE
STATUS: CONDITIONAL PASS
CLOSURE: NOT CLOSED
============================================================

Finding:
The distinction between single-experiment equivalence and protocol equivalence is necessary and correctly represented.

Protocol equivalence:
h ~_Pi h' iff Law_h^pi = Law_h'^pi for every admissible protocol pi in Pi.

Single-experiment equivalence:
h ~_e h' iff P_h^e = P_h'^e.

Experiment-set equivalence:
h ~_E h' iff P_h^e = P_h'^e for every e in E.

If Pi contains all single non-adaptive runs of every e in E, then protocol equivalence implies experiment-set equivalence. The converse does not generally hold when adaptive or sequential protocols can reveal dependence structures not represented by one-step marginals.

The counterexample based on equal Bernoulli(1/2) one-step marginals but different two-observation dependence structures is valid: a measurable statistic of the pair can distinguish the resulting history laws.

Qualification:
Equality of conditional kernels is required only almost surely under the relevant common history law. Equality on null or unreachable histories is not required. The equivalence relation is relative to the declared protocol class and is not a claim of metaphysical or universal equivalence.

Notation cleanup:
Use P_{h'}^e and Law_{h'}^pi consistently.

Reason not closed:
This is an internal targeted verification of proposal v3.3, not an independent external review or canonical acceptance.

F2 — ADAPTIVE PROTOCOLS AND CONDITIONAL KERNELS
STATUS: CONDITIONAL PASS
CLOSURE: NOT CLOSED
============================================================

Finding:
The adaptive-protocol treatment is mathematically coherent under the stated measurability and kernel assumptions.

For a fixed finite-horizon protocol, equality of the initial history laws together with equality of the successive conditional kernels almost surely under the common history law yields equality of the full history laws by induction.

Reachability is protocol-relative:
- For one fixed protocol, kernel equality is needed on histories reachable under that protocol, up to null sets.
- For equivalence across every protocol in Pi, the condition must cover the union of histories reachable under the admissible protocols.

Null histories:
Conditional kernels need not agree on histories having probability zero under the common history law. Claims of pointwise equality on every formally describable history would be too strong.

Infinite horizon:
An infinite trajectory law requires suitable measurable spaces and consistent stochastic kernels. The Ionescu-Tulcea theorem provides a standard construction under its assumptions; invoking the theorem does not remove the need to verify those assumptions.

Protocol constraints:
Experiment selection, stopping, and internal randomization must be measurable and must not depend on the hidden hypothesis except through observable history, as specified by the protocol model.

Reason not closed:
The verification is conditional on the formal model assumptions and is not an independent external review or canonical acceptance.

F3 — POSTERIOR ODDS UNDER OBSERVATIONAL EQUIVALENCE
STATUS: CONDITIONAL PASS
CLOSURE: NOT CLOSED
============================================================

Finding:
The claim is valid under the stated protocol-equivalence and posterior regularity assumptions. If h ~_Pi h', the complete observable-history laws are equal for each admissible protocol. Every measurable test based on that history and the same h-independent randomization therefore has the same distribution under h and h'.

Likelihood-ratio qualification:
In a dominated formulation with a common dominating measure lambda, equality of the two history laws implies equality of their Radon-Nikodym densities lambda-almost everywhere. The likelihood ratio is therefore 1 where the ratio is defined on the common support. It must not be asserted where the denominator vanishes or where the required domination/densities do not exist.

Posterior-odds qualification:
For two hypotheses with positive prior masses, and on histories where the posterior odds are defined, the posterior odds equal the prior odds almost surely under the relevant prior-predictive law. Zero prior mass makes the corresponding odds undefined. Point-hypothesis odds must not be transferred without qualification to continuous priors, where individual points may have zero mass.

Finding:
The v3.3 wording includes the necessary domination and definedness caveat. Equality of measures is the fundamental result; density and likelihood-ratio language is a conditional representation of it.

Reason not closed:
Internal verification only. No independent review or canonical acceptance of v3.3.

F4 — PRIOR-MASS REDISTRIBUTION UNDER LOSS EQUIVALENCE
STATUS: CONDITIONAL PASS
CLOSURE: NOT CLOSED
============================================================

Finding:
Proposition 1 is correct in its declared discrete setting. If h ~_L h', moving prior mass t from h to h' leaves the expected loss of every fixed action unchanged because the change is
-t L(a,h) + t L(a,h') = 0.

Consequently, the pre-data ranking of actions and the pre-data Bayes risk R(mu) are unchanged, provided the expectations are well-defined and finite and the action set and loss function remain fixed.

Scope limitation:
This result does not establish invariance of posterior decisions, EVSI, or stopping values. The discrete mass-transfer proof cannot be silently generalized to arbitrary measurable/continuous state spaces where individual points may have zero mass.

Related policy-value condition:
Proposition 1b adds protocol equivalence, common policy feasibility, h-independent policy rules, equal expected remaining costs, state-based loss, and the same expected-objective convention. Under those conditions, each policy has equal expected objective under h and h', so the infimum over the same policy class is invariant under the stated discrete prior-mass transfer. The text correctly limits application to EVSI/R_e to cases where the measurable-policy and posterior-risk formulations are properly connected.

Reason not closed:
The result is conditional on all listed model and policy assumptions and has not received independent review as part of v3.3.

F5 — LOSS EQUIVALENCE DOES NOT PRESERVE EVSI
STATUS: PASS FOR THE STATED COUNTEREXAMPLE
CLOSURE: NOT CLOSED
============================================================

Finding:
The finite counterexample is arithmetically and logically correct.

State space H={h1,h2,h3}; actions A={a1,a2}; losses:
L(a1)=(0,0,1)
L(a2)=(1,1,0)

Thus h1 ~_L h2. The experiment has P_h1(d=1)=1 and P_h2(d=1)=P_h3(d=1)=0.

For mu_A=(1/2,0,1/2):
R=1/2, R_e=0, EVSI=1/2.

For mu_B=(0,1/2,1/2):
R=1/2, R_e=1/2, EVSI=0.

The prior mass assigned to the loss-equivalence class {h1,h2} is the same in both cases, and pre-data risk is unchanged, while EVSI differs because h1 and h2 have different observation laws.

Conclusion:
The counterexample correctly blocks any inference from loss equivalence alone to EVSI invariance. It does not contradict Proposition 1b, whose additional observational and policy conditions are stronger.

Reason not closed:
Internal verification of the example only; no independent review or canonical acceptance.

F6 — NONNEGATIVITY OF EVSI
STATUS: CONDITIONAL PASS
CLOSURE: NOT CLOSED
============================================================

Finding:
The proof of R_e(mu) <= R(mu), hence EVSI(e) >= 0, is valid under assumptions A1-A6 and the stated measurable-rule/selection and finite/extended-real conventions.

Core argument:
For any fixed pre-experiment action a0, the posterior infimum is no greater than the posterior risk of a0 almost everywhere. Integrating and applying posterior disintegration gives
R_e(mu) <= integral L(a0,h) mu(dh).
Taking the infimum over a0 yields R_e(mu) <= R(mu).

Required scope:
- The same action set A remains available after observing the experiment.
- Posterior risks and prior risks are measurable and well-defined.
- The bounded-below/integrability and finiteness conditions prevent undefined differences.
- The posterior-risk infimum is measurable for integration.
- The measurable-policy interpretation requires the selection/epsilon-optimal-rule condition stated in A4, or another valid justification of the equality between the relevant formulations.

Limitations:
The result is not asserted for observation-dependent loss L(a,d,h), a reduced post-experiment action set, or undefined/infinite risks that make subtraction indeterminate. If R_e=-infinity is allowed under the stated extended-real convention, EVSI is defined as +infinity rather than as an undefined difference.

Reason not closed:
Conditional proof verification only; not an independent certification of v3.3.
