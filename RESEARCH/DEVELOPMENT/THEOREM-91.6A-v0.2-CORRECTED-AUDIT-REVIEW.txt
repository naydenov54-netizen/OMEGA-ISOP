==================================================
1. PURPOSE AND SCOPE
==================================================

This version incorporates the convergent findings of the Claude and Perplexity audits. It preserves the original mathematical scope: finite-dimensional linear observations with Euclidean norms.

The theorem establishes:
1. Exact identifiability through a common-kernel and rank criterion.
2. Equivalence between injectivity and positive minimum observation gain in finite dimensions.
3. A Lipschitz bound for recovery on the observation image.
4. Correctly separated statements for bounded-noise separation and recovery uncertainty.

This remains a new research construction. No claim is made that it reproduces a lost historical THEOREM 91.6A.

==================================================
2. FRAMEWORK
==================================================

Let X = R^m with its Euclidean norm.
Let r >= 1 be finite.
For each i=1,...,r, let A_i:R^m->R^(p_i) be linear.

Define the stacked observation map:

A(x) = (A_1*x,...,A_r*x)

The observation space is the product Euclidean space
Y = R^(p_1+...+p_r).

Let A_stacked be the matrix formed by vertically stacking the matrices A_i.

Define the common kernel:

K = intersection(i=1 to r) ker(A_i).

Define:

gamma_A = inf(||z||=1) ||A_stacked*z||

M_A = ||A_stacked||.

==================================================
3. EXACT IDENTIFIABILITY
==================================================

THEOREM 91.6A-1:

The following are equivalent:

(1) A is injective.
(2) K = {0}.
(3) rank(A_stacked) = m.

PROOF:

If A is injective and z belongs to K, then A(z)=A(0), so z=0.

If K={0} and A(x1)=A(x2), linearity gives
A_i*(x1-x2)=0 for every i. Thus x1-x2 belongs to K, so x1=x2.

The kernel of A_stacked is exactly K. By rank-nullity,
rank(A_stacked)+dim(K)=m. Therefore K={0} iff rank(A_stacked)=m.

Q.E.D.

==================================================
4. POSITIVE MINIMUM GAIN
==================================================

THEOREM 91.6A-2:

For the stated finite-dimensional Euclidean setting:

A is injective iff gamma_A>0.

PROOF:

If gamma_A>0, then
||A_stacked*z|| >= gamma_A*||z||
for every z. Equality of observations therefore forces the state difference to be zero.

Conversely, if A is injective, its kernel is {0}. The continuous function
z -> ||A_stacked*z||
is strictly positive on the Euclidean unit sphere. The sphere is compact, so the function attains a strictly positive minimum.

Q.E.D.

The finite-dimensional compactness step is essential to this proof as written. The infinite-dimensional counterexample in Section 8 shows why injectivity alone does not generally imply a positive lower gain.

==================================================
5. LIPSCHITZ RECOVERY
==================================================

For all x1,x2 in R^m:

gamma_A*||x1-x2||
<= ||A(x1)-A(x2)||
<= M_A*||x1-x2||.

This follows directly from the definition of gamma_A and the operator norm.

If A is injective, the inverse on the image,

A^(-1):A(R^m)->R^m,

is well-defined and satisfies:

Lip(A^(-1)) <= 1/gamma_A.

Thus, in this finite-dimensional linear setting, exact identifiability and global Lipschitz-stable recovery are equivalent. This equivalence is not asserted for arbitrary nonlinear maps or arbitrary infinite-dimensional spaces.

==================================================
6. BOUNDED NOISE: TWO DISTINCT CLAIMS
==================================================

Consider y=A(x)+eta with ||eta||<=epsilon.

For a fixed candidate x, the compatible observation set is the closed epsilon-ball centered at A(x).

SEPARATION CLAIM:

For two fixed states x1,x2, their closed noise balls are disjoint iff:

||A(x1)-A(x2)|| > 2*epsilon.

If the center distance is <=2*epsilon, the balls intersect or touch. This means the two candidates cannot be guaranteed to be distinguished from every observation allowed by this bounded-noise model.

SUFFICIENT STATE-SEPARATION CONDITION:

Since
||A(x1)-A(x2)|| >= gamma_A*||x1-x2||,

the condition

||x1-x2|| > 2*epsilon/gamma_A

is sufficient for disjoint noise balls. It is not necessary for every pair.

RECOVERY-UNCERTAINTY BOUND:

If one observed y is compatible with both x1 and x2, then

||A(x1)-A(x2)|| <= 2*epsilon.

Consequently,

||x1-x2|| <= 2*epsilon/gamma_A.

This is a diameter bound on the set of states compatible with the same observation. It is not a guarantee of unique recovery. Unique recovery under nonzero bounded noise does not follow merely from injectivity of A.

==================================================
7. EXAMPLE
==================================================

Let X=R² and define:

A_1(u,v)=(u,0)
A_2(u,v)=(0,v).

Neither component map is injective on R². The stacked map is:

A(u,v)=(u,0,0,v).

Its common kernel is {0}, and its two nonzero singular values are 1. Hence:

gamma_A=1
M_A=1.

The combined map preserves Euclidean distances exactly.

==================================================
8. INFINITE-DIMENSIONAL COUNTEREXAMPLE
==================================================

On l² define:

T(x1,x2,x3,...)=(x1,x2/2,x3/3,...).

T is bounded and injective. For the standard unit vector e_n:

||e_n||=1
||T(e_n)||=1/n -> 0.

Therefore no gamma>0 satisfies
||T(x)||>=gamma*||x|| for all x.

The inverse on the image is not bounded. Thus the finite-dimensional equivalence between injectivity and positive minimum gain does not extend automatically to infinite dimensions.

==================================================
10. NON-IMPLICATIONS AND LIMITATIONS
==================================================

This theorem does not establish:
- results for arbitrary nonlinear observations;
- positive minimum gain in arbitrary infinite-dimensional spaces;
- unique recovery under nonzero noise;
- statistical identifiability under a probability model;
- Shannon information preservation or loss;
- physical realizability, empirical validity, or a physical interpretation;
- universal applicability to OMEGA-ISOP models.

The proven content concerns mathematical distinguishability under the stated observation map and norms. The word "information" must not be used as a synonym for Shannon information in this theorem.
