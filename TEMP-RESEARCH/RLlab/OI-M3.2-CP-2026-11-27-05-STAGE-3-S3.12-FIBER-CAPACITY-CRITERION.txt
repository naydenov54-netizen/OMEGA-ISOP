============================================================
1. OBJECTIVE
============================================================

S3.9 established that equality between the free union relation and quotient pullback is equivalent to fiber saturation.

S3.10 showed that quotient pullback can create a forbidden induced structure.
S3.11 showed that failure of fiber saturation does not necessarily destroy membership in a particular hereditary class.

S3.12 seeks an exact criterion for the appearance of a finite forbidden induced structure after pullback.

The target is a necessary-and-sufficient condition expressed through:
- the forbidden structure F;
- the quotient relational structure Q;
- the fibers of pi_P;
- the number of distinct representatives available in each fiber.

The result is restricted to purely relational signatures. No functions, constants, or dynamics are included.

============================================================
2. FORMAL SETUP
============================================================

Let Sigma be a relational signature. Each relation symbol R has a fixed finite arity n_R.

Let:
pi:P->Q

be a surjective map of underlying sets.

Let Q be a Sigma-structure. Define the pullback Sigma-structure P^pi on P by:

R_P(x1,...,xn_R)
IFF
R_Q(pi(x1),...,pi(xn_R)).

Let F be a finite Sigma-structure with finite carrier V_F.

An induced embedding e:F->P^pi means:
1. e is injective;
2. for every relation symbol R and every tuple (v1,...,vn_R) in V_F^n_R:

R_F(v1,...,vn_R)
IFF
R_P(e(v1),...,e(vn_R)).

The equivalence is required for both positive and negative relation instances, including tuples with repeated coordinates.

Define the fiber:
P_q = pi^(-1)({q}).

============================================================
3. DEFINITION — INDUCED RELATIONAL MAP NOT REQUIRED TO BE INJECTIVE
============================================================

Call a map:
h:V_F->Q

an induced relational map if, for every relation symbol R of arity n_R and every tuple (v1,...,vn_R) in V_F^n_R:

R_F(v1,...,vn_R)
IFF
R_Q(h(v1),...,h(vn_R)).

Unlike an induced embedding, h is not required to be injective.

This distinction is central:
- An induced embedding of F into Q must be injective.
- A map h as defined above may identify distinct vertices of F.
- Such identifications can be lifted to distinct elements of P when the corresponding fibers contain enough representatives.

No terminology beyond this explicit definition is needed for the theorem.

============================================================
4. MAIN THEOREM — EXACT FIBER-CAPACITY CRITERION
============================================================

THEOREM S3.12.

Let F be finite and let P^pi be the pullback of Q along the surjection pi:P->Q.

Then F embeds as an induced substructure of P^pi if and only if there exists an induced relational map:

h:V_F->Q

such that, for every q in h(V_F):

|h^(-1)({q})| <= |pi^(-1)({q})|.

The inequality is cardinal comparison. Since F is finite, each left-hand side is finite.

Equivalently:
Every quotient value used by h must have enough distinct representatives in its pi-fiber to lift all vertices of F mapped to that value.

============================================================
5. PROOF — NECESSITY
============================================================

Assume there is an induced embedding:
e:F->P^pi.

Define:
h = pi o e.

Thus:
h(v)=pi(e(v)).

For every relation symbol R and every tuple (v1,...,vn_R):

R_F(v1,...,vn_R)
IFF
R_P(e(v1),...,e(vn_R))

because e is an induced embedding.

By the definition of pullback:

R_P(e(v1),...,e(vn_R))
IFF
R_Q(pi(e(v1)),...,pi(e(vn_R))).

Substituting h:

R_F(v1,...,vn_R)
IFF
R_Q(h(v1),...,h(vn_R)).

Therefore h is an induced relational map.

Now fix q in h(V_F).

Every vertex v in h^(-1)({q}) is sent by e to an element of pi^(-1)({q}).

Because e is injective, distinct vertices in h^(-1)({q}) are sent to distinct elements of that fiber.

Hence there is an injection:
h^(-1)({q}) -> pi^(-1)({q}).

Therefore:
|h^(-1)({q})| <= |pi^(-1)({q})|.

Necessity is proved.

============================================================
6. PROOF — SUFFICIENCY
============================================================

Assume there exists an induced relational map:
h:V_F->Q

satisfying, for every q in h(V_F):

|h^(-1)({q})| <= |pi^(-1)({q})|.

For each such q, choose an injection:
s_q:h^(-1)({q})->pi^(-1)({q}).

Because distinct fibers of pi are disjoint, these injections combine into a single map:
e:V_F->P

defined by:
e(v)=s_(h(v))(v).

The map e is injective:
- vertices mapped by h to different q-values land in disjoint pi-fibers;
- vertices mapped by h to the same q-value are separated by the chosen injection s_q.

Also:
pi(e(v))=h(v).

For every relation symbol R and every tuple (v1,...,vn_R):

R_P(e(v1),...,e(vn_R))
IFF
R_Q(pi(e(v1)),...,pi(e(vn_R)))
IFF
R_Q(h(v1),...,h(vn_R))
IFF
R_F(v1,...,vn_R).

Thus e is an induced embedding F->P^pi.

Sufficiency is proved.

Therefore the theorem is established.

============================================================
7. COROLLARY — EXACT PRESERVATION TEST FOR A FORBIDDEN FAMILY
============================================================

Let K=Forb(ForbSet), where ForbSet is any family of finite Sigma-structures.

Then:

P^pi belongs to K

IFF

for every F in ForbSet, there is no induced relational map h:F->Q satisfying:

|h^(-1)({q})| <= |pi^(-1)({q})|

for every q in h(F).

This is a necessary-and-sufficient criterion for quotient-pullback preservation of the forbidden family.

If Q itself belongs to K, then no such h can be injective, because an injective induced relational map would be an induced embedding F->Q.

Consequently, under Q in K, pullback failure occurs exactly when some forbidden F has a NON-INJECTIVE induced relational map into Q whose quotient fibers have enough representatives to lift it injectively into P.

This identifies the precise gap between:
- absence of induced forbidden copies in Q;
- appearance of induced forbidden copies in P^pi.

============================================================
8. TEST AGAINST S3.10
============================================================

S3.10 used:
F with three vertices and universal binary relation R_F=V_F x V_F.

Q is a singleton {q}, with R_Q={(q,q)}.

Define h:F->Q by mapping all three vertices to q.

This h is an induced relational map:
every tuple is R_F-true, and every image tuple is R_Q-true.

The fiber has:
|pi^(-1)({q})|=3.

The h-preimage has:
|h^(-1)({q})|=3.

Thus:
3<=3.

The theorem predicts an induced copy of F in P^pi.

Indeed P^pi has three elements and universal R, so it is exactly F.

S3.10 is recovered by the criterion.

============================================================
9. TEST AGAINST S3.11
============================================================

S3.11 used:
F={u,v},
R_F={(u,v)} only.

Q is a singleton {q}, with:
R_Q={(q,q)}.

Any map h:F->{q} must be constant.

For the tuple (u,u):
R_F(u,u)=FALSE,
but R_Q(h(u),h(u))=R_Q(q,q)=TRUE.

Therefore h is not an induced relational map.

There is no alternative map because Q has only one element.

The criterion predicts that no induced copy of F exists in the pullback, regardless of the fiber's cardinality.

Indeed the pullback relation on P is universal. Every two-element induced substructure has all four ordered pairs, so it is not isomorphic to F.

S3.11 is recovered by the criterion.

IMPORTANT:
The difference between S3.10 and S3.11 is not merely the size of F or the failure of fiber saturation. It is whether the complete positive-and-negative relation pattern of F can be represented after identifying vertices in Q.

============================================================
10. CONSEQUENCE — QUOTIENT COLLAPSE PATTERNS
============================================================

For a fixed forbidden structure F, every map h:F->Q determines a partition of V_F into blocks:

v ~_h w IFF h(v)=h(w).

The map is an induced relational map precisely when this partition is compatible with every relation table of F and the relation tables of Q.

A block may contain several vertices only if identifying those vertices does not create a positive/negative relation conflict in Q.

The fiber-capacity condition then asks whether each block can be represented by distinct points of the corresponding pi-fiber.

Thus a forbidden induced structure can appear after pullback through two simultaneous mechanisms:

(A) RELATIONAL COMPATIBILITY:
The quotient Q admits a non-injective induced relational map from F.

(B) REPRESENTATIVE CAPACITY:
Each identified block has enough distinct representatives in its pi-fiber.

Both conditions are necessary. Together they are sufficient.

This is an exact characterization, not merely a sufficient obstruction.

============================================================
11. CONSEQUENCE — WHEN QUOTIENT MEMBERSHIP DOES GUARANTEE PULLBACK MEMBERSHIP
============================================================

Assume Q belongs to K=Forb(ForbSet).

A sufficient condition for P^pi to belong to K is:

For every F in ForbSet, every induced relational map h:F->Q is injective.

Proof:
If P^pi contained an induced copy of some F, Theorem S3.12 would give an induced relational map h:F->Q.
By the stated condition h would be injective.
An injective induced relational map is an induced embedding, contradicting Q in Forb(ForbSet).

This condition is sufficient and may be stronger than necessary for a particular map pi, because some non-injective h may fail the fiber-capacity inequalities.

The exact necessary-and-sufficient criterion remains the combined relational-map plus fiber-capacity test in Sections 4-7.

============================================================
12. CONSEQUENCE — FIBER SIZE BOUND
============================================================

Suppose every fiber of pi has cardinality at most m.

Then a forbidden F can appear in P^pi only through an induced relational map h:F->Q whose every fiber block has size at most m.

In particular, if every induced relational map h:F->Q necessarily has some block larger than m, then F cannot appear in P^pi.

For finite P and Q, the criterion is algorithmically decidable by:
1. Enumerating maps h:V_F->Q.
2. Checking the full induced relation equivalence for every relation symbol and every tuple.
3. Counting each h-fiber.
4. Comparing its size with the corresponding pi-fiber.

This is a finite decision procedure for each finite F, P, and Q.

============================================================
13. LOGICAL DISTINCTIONS PRESERVED
============================================================

S3.12 does not conflate the following:

(A) STRUCTURAL EQUALITY:
R_P^free=R_P^pull.
This is characterized by fiber saturation in S3.9.

(B) CLASS MEMBERSHIP:
P^pi in K.
This is characterized for finite forbidden families by S3.12's induced-map and fiber-capacity criterion.

(C) EXISTENCE OF SOME AMALGAM IN K:
This is a separate existence problem. The fact that P^pi is or is not in K does not by itself settle whether another amalgam in K exists.

(D) STRONG AMALGAMATION:
This concerns whether the two side images intersect exactly in the common image. It is a carrier/map property and must not be inferred from class membership alone.

============================================================

PROVED:

PROVED:
Recovery of S3.10 and S3.11 as two different outcomes of the criterion.

PROVED:
A sufficient quotient-side condition for K-preservation.

NOT CLAIMED:
1. A corresponding theorem for signatures with functions or constants.
2. A theorem for arbitrary infinitary relation arities.
3. A classification of all hereditary classes beyond those represented as forbidden induced families.
4. A result about the existence of some other amalgam in K.
5. A result about categorical pushouts.
6. A physical interpretation.

The theorem itself does not require F to be irreducible. Irreducibility is relevant to free-amalgamation results such as the S3.4 criterion, but not to the pullback characterization proved here.
