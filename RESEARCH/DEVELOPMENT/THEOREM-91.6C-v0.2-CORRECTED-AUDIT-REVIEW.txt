FINITE WITNESS AND STABLE IDENTIFIABILITY OF OBSERVATION HISTORIES

==================================================
1. PURPOSE
==================================================

This version clarifies which conclusions require uniform boundedness, which require finite dimensionality, and why the finite-witness result is algebraic rather than dependent on compactness.

The theorem concerns a countable family of linear observations and the specific supremum history metric defined below.

==================================================
2. FRAMEWORK
==================================================

Let X=R^m with Euclidean norm and Y=R^p.
Let A_n:X->Y be linear for n>=1.

Assume:

M=sup(n>=1)||A_n||<infinity.

Define:

H(x)=(A_1*x,A_2*x,A_3*x,...)

and:

d_H(H(x1),H(x2))=sup(n>=1)||A_n*(x1-x2)||.

Uniform boundedness makes this quantity finite and makes the history response function below Lipschitz continuous.

Define the common kernel:

K=intersection(n>=1)ker(A_n).

Define:

phi(z)=sup(n>=1)||A_n*z||

gamma_H=inf(||z||=1)phi(z).

==================================================
3. HISTORY INJECTIVITY
==================================================

PROPOSITION 91.6C-1:

H is injective iff K={0}.

Proof:
H(x1)=H(x2) iff A_n*(x1-x2)=0 for every n, which is equivalent to x1-x2 in K. Thus K={0} is equivalent to equality of histories forcing x1=x2.

Q.E.D.

This common-kernel equivalence itself does not require uniform boundedness. Uniform boundedness is imposed for the finite-valued supremum metric and the stability theorem.

==================================================
4. POSITIVE HISTORY GAIN
==================================================

Under uniform boundedness:

|phi(z)-phi(w)| <= M*||z-w||.

Therefore phi is continuous.

THEOREM 91.6C-2:

For X=R^m and the stated assumptions:

H is injective iff gamma_H>0.

Proof:
If gamma_H>0, then phi(z)>=gamma_H||z||, so equal histories force equal states.

If H is injective, Proposition 91.6C-1 gives K={0}. Thus phi(z)>0 for every unit vector z. Since phi is continuous and the unit sphere in finite-dimensional R^m is compact, phi attains a strictly positive minimum. Hence gamma_H>0.

Q.E.D.

The compactness step is the finite-dimensional ingredient. No automatic extension to arbitrary infinite-dimensional spaces is claimed.

==================================================
5. BI-LIPSCHITZ STABILITY
==================================================

For all x1,x2:

gamma_H*||x1-x2||
<= d_H(H(x1),H(x2))
<= M*||x1-x2||.

The lower bound follows from gamma_H. The upper bound follows from ||A_n||<=M and taking the supremum.

If gamma_H>0, H is injective and its inverse on H(X) is Lipschitz with:

Lip(H^(-1))<=1/gamma_H.

The result is tied to the chosen supremum metric. Other history metrics may have different stability properties.

==================================================
6. FINITE IDENTIFYING SUBFAMILY
==================================================

THEOREM 91.6C-3:

If H is injective on finite-dimensional X=R^m, then there exists a finite set J of indices such that the finite stacked map H_J is injective.

PROOF BY DIMENSION DESCENT:

Start with V_0=X.

If V_k={0}, stop.

If V_k is nonzero, then the intersection of all observation kernels is {0}. Therefore there exists an index n_(k+1) such that V_k is not contained in ker(A_n_(k+1)).

Set:

V_(k+1)=V_k intersection ker(A_n_(k+1)).

This is a proper linear subspace of V_k, so:

dim(V_(k+1))<dim(V_k).

The dimension is a nonnegative integer initially equal to m. Therefore after at most m steps the resulting intersection is {0}.

The selected finite family has trivial common kernel, so its stacked map is injective.

Q.E.D.

This proof does not use compactness or countability beyond the existence of the indexed family. It also shows that no more than m selected observations are needed, where each A_n may itself be vector-valued.

The theorem does NOT say that the first m observations suffice.

==================================================
7. EXAMPLE AND METRIC CONSTANTS
==================================================

Let X=R² and:

A_1(u,v)=(u,0)
A_2(u,v)=(0,v).

For this two-observation history with supremum metric:

d_H=max(|delta_u|,|delta_v|).

Therefore:

(1/sqrt(2))*||delta_x|| <= d_H <= ||delta_x||.

Thus:

gamma_H=1/sqrt(2)
M=1.

Neither individual observation is injective, but the two-observation family is injective and bi-Lipschitz.

==================================================
8. FULL HISTORY VERSUS A LATE TAIL
==================================================

Consider the sequence:

A_(2k-1)(u,v)=(u/k,0)
A_(2k)(u,v)=(0,v/k), for k>=1.

The full history is injective and its supremum metric is:

d_H=max(|delta_u|,|delta_v|).

Its history gain is 1/sqrt(2).

Now restrict to the tail beginning at k>=K, where K>=1. The tail metric is:

d_(H,K)=sup(k>=K) max(|delta_u|/k,|delta_v|/k)
       =max(|delta_u|,|delta_v|)/K.

Consequently its gain is:

gamma_(H,K)=1/(sqrt(2)*K).

As K tends to infinity, this tail gain tends to zero.

Interpretation:
The full history remains stably identifying because it contains early observations. The progressively later tail becomes arbitrarily poorly conditioned. Do not conflate the full history with its late tail.

==================================================
9. ASSUMPTION MAP
==================================================

COMMON-KERNEL EQUIVALENCE:
Requires only the linear family and the definition of the history.

FINITE WITNESS:
Requires finite-dimensional X and trivial common kernel. The dimension-descent proof does not require uniform boundedness, compactness, or countability of the index set.

FINITE-VALUED SUPREMUM HISTORY METRIC:
Uniform boundedness ensures d_H is finite for every pair.

CONTINUITY OF phi:
Uniform boundedness gives the Lipschitz estimate for phi.

INJECTIVITY IFF POSITIVE HISTORY GAIN:
Uses finite-dimensionality, continuity of phi, and compactness of the unit sphere.

BI-LIPSCHITZ UPPER BOUND:
Uses uniform boundedness.

BI-LIPSCHITZ LOWER BOUND:
Uses positive gamma_H.

==================================================
10. LIMITATIONS AND NON-IMPLICATIONS
==================================================

This theorem does not establish:
- the same result for arbitrary nonlinear observation operators;
- automatic positive history gain in infinite dimensions;
- stability under every history metric;
- robust recovery under arbitrary noise models;
- Shannon information preservation or loss;
- physical realizability or empirical validity;
- universal applicability to all OMEGA-ISOP models.

The mathematical result concerns injectivity and metric stability of a specified observation history. It is not a theorem about Shannon information.
