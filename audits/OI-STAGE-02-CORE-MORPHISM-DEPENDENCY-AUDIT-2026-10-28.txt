PURPOSE
Determine whether the project source fixes the morphism class strongly enough to identify the union-colimit test category P_F with the intended category C_METACORE. Also audit the finite example against the stated configuration nontriviality condition.

1. SOURCE FACTS RECOVERED FROM THE PROTECTED LIBRARY
Volume II states:
(a) A configuration has form C=(S_C,#_C), with S_C a subset of U0 and #_C the selected apartness restricted to the configuration.
(b) A minimal nontrivial configuration satisfies |S_C| >= 2.
(c) C_METACORE may represent the admissible configurations and has morphisms that preserve selected structural properties.
(d) An embedding f:C1 -> C2 is injective and preserves and reflects apartness: x #1 y iff f(x) #2 f(y).
(e) CC(C1,C2)=1 iff an admissible amalgamation exists.
(f) R=colim(D_CC) is conditional on the categorical construction existing.
Volume III further states that the indexing category J, functor D:J -> C_METACORE, relevant objects and morphisms, compatibility conditions, and universal property must be specified. Until then, categorical formalization remains open.

2. IDENTIFICATION RESULT
The source identifies a relevant embedding condition, but the statements recovered here do not explicitly say that every embedding is a morphism of C_METACORE. Nor do they say that morphisms are restricted to literal subset inclusions. The phrase “morphisms preserve the selected structural properties” is not a complete definition of the morphism class.
Therefore neither of the following is established from the source:
(A) C_METACORE has all embeddings as morphisms.
(B) C_METACORE has only literal inclusions among subsets of one fixed carrier as morphisms.
The category P_F from the union-colimit model is a legitimate restricted test category, but it cannot yet be identified with the intended C_METACORE. The all-embeddings category used in the amalgamation obstruction is also a legitimate test category, but cannot yet be identified with the intended C_METACORE.
This is an underspecification finding, not a contradiction in the Core.

3. WHAT THE TWO PRIOR THEOREMS ACTUALLY SHOW
In P_F, where objects are admissible subsets of a fixed carrier and arrows are only literal inclusions, a functorial diagram has a colimit given by the union whenever that union is an admissible object. The proof follows from the subset order and the universal property.
In the category of finite discrete-apartness structures with all embeddings as morphisms, the tested span A <- S -> B has an admissible amalgam but no pushout.
These results do not conflict. They use different morphism classes and object conventions. Together they show that existence of a weak amalgam does not settle colimit existence independently of the category.

4. NONTRIVIALITY AND THE SINGLETON BASE
The source's stated minimal nontriviality condition is |S_C| >= 2. Consequently, the span using A={s,a}, B={s,b}, and S={s} is not automatically a diagram in a category whose objects are only minimal nontrivial configurations: its shared base S is a singleton.
The all-embeddings obstruction proof is valid in the explicitly declared test category that admits the singleton object S. It must not be presented as a direct instantiated Core obstruction unless the singleton base is admissible under the selected realization or the example is adapted to the actual object class.
Similarly, the finite example in the union-colimit model properly flags this condition, but its shared-base-span illustration is conditional on admitting the singleton configuration.

5. A NONTRIVIAL UNION-COLIMIT TEST THAT RESPECTS |S| >= 2
Let U0={0,1,2}, with x # y iff x != y. Let F={{0,1},{1,2},{0,1,2}} and use the category whose objects are C_S for S in F and whose arrows are literal inclusions whenever the source subset is contained in the target subset.
Let J be the discrete category with two objects j1,j2 and only identity arrows. Define D(j1)=C_{ {0,1} } and D(j2)=C_{ {1,2} }. Both objects satisfy |S| >= 2. Their union R-set {0,1,2} belongs to F.
Then C_{ {0,1,2} } is a colimit of D in this inclusion-only category: its two inclusions form a cocone. For any cocone into C_T, both {0,1} and {1,2} must be subsets of T, so {0,1,2} is a subset of T; hence there is a unique inclusion from the union object to C_T, and it factors the cocone. QED.
This example proves the restricted union-colimit theorem without relying on a singleton configuration. It does not encode a shared-base arrow or establish that the project compatibility diagram is this discrete diagram.

6. EXACT UNRESOLVED DEPENDENCIES FOR THE PROJECT-LEVEL R
A project-level construction requires explicit answers to:
(a) What exactly are the objects of C_METACORE, including any nontriviality or admissibility restrictions?
(b) Is every injective apartness-preserving-and-reflecting embedding a morphism, or only a designated subclass?
(c) Are configurations all realized as subsets of one fixed U0 with inherited #, or may their carriers and realizations vary?
(d) What is the indexing category J and which arrows encode overlap or compatibility?
(e) Is D_CC a functor into the declared C_METACORE?
(f) What is the candidate cocone and what precise universal property defines R?
(g) What closure or stronger amalgamation assumptions are available, and are they stated in the Core or introduced only for a test realization?
