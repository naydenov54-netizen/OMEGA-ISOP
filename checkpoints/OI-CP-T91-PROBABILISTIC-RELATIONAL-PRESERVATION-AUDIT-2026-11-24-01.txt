============================================================
1. PURPOSE AND RESULT BOUNDARY
============================================================

This checkpoint examines how a Markov kernel might preserve an n-ary relation rho on a state space X.

Three approaches are distinguished:
A. independent product-kernel / almost-sure support preservation;
B. coupling-based probabilistic relational lifting;
C. an enriched relational realization carrying coherent joint transition kernels.

The checkpoint does not select a canonical probabilistic semantics for R. It does not establish that Markov kernels belong to the existing Evol(X), that they are in A(R;Phi), or that Phi admits the proposed enriched realization.

============================================================
2. SETUP
============================================================

Let (X,Sigma_X) be a measurable space and let:
kappa : X x Sigma_X -> [0,1]
be a Markov kernel.

Let rho subseteq X^n be an n-ary relation. For measure-theoretic statements involving rho, require rho to be measurable in the relevant product sigma-algebra.

For each tuple xbar=(x1,...,xn), the coordinatewise independent product kernel is:
kappa^(n)(xbar, dybar)
= kappa(x1,dy1) x ... x kappa(xn,dyn).

============================================================
3. CANDIDATE A: INDEPENDENT PRODUCT-KERNEL PRESERVATION
============================================================

A strong sufficient condition for preservation of rho under independent coordinate transitions is:
For every xbar in rho:
kappa^(n)(xbar, rho) = 1.

Interpretation:
Whenever a tuple satisfies rho initially, independently sampled successor coordinates satisfy rho with probability one.

This is a precise almost-sure preservation condition. It is stronger than merely requiring each coordinate to have a valid marginal transition.

COUNTEREXAMPLE / RESTRICTION:
Let rho be the equality relation Delta={(x,x):x in X}. On a standard Borel space, requiring:
(kappa(x,.) x kappa(x,.))(Delta)=1
forces kappa(x,.) to be a Dirac probability measure. Two independent random variables with the same law coincide almost surely only when that law is concentrated at one point.

Therefore independent product-kernel preservation can eliminate genuine stochasticity for relations such as equality. It is mathematically clean but potentially too restrictive.

============================================================
4. CANDIDATE B: COUPLING-BASED RELATIONAL LIFT
============================================================

For probability measures nu1,...,nun on X, define:
(nu1,...,nun) in rho_hat
if and only if there exists a probability measure pi on X^n such that:

1. Marginal_i(pi)=nu_i for every i;
2. pi(rho)=1.

The measure pi is a coupling of the prescribed marginals concentrated on rho.

Define probabilistic preservation of rho by:
For every (x1,...,xn) in rho:
(kappa(x1,.),...,kappa(xn,.)) in rho_hat.

This condition allows correlated joint transitions while preserving the individual marginal kernels.

EXAMPLE: EQUALITY RELATION
For rho=Delta and equal marginals nu, the diagonal coupling (Y,Y), with Y distributed according to nu, is concentrated on Delta. Thus stochastic marginals can preserve equality under a correlated coupling even when the independent product condition would force a Dirac measure.

EXAMPLE: ORDER RELATION
For rho={(x,y):x<=y} and additive-noise marginals Law(x+Z), Law(y+Z), when x<=y a shared-noise coupling (x+Z,y+Z) is concentrated on rho. Independent noises need not preserve the order.

LIMITATION:
The existence of a coupling for each tuple separately does not automatically define a measurable joint transition kernel.

============================================================
5. MEASURABLE SELECTION AND COHERENCE
============================================================

To obtain an actual joint Markov kernel on relational tuples, one needs a measurable assignment:
Lambda_rho : rho -> Prob(X^n)

such that for each xbar=(x1,...,xn) in rho:

Marginal_i(Lambda_rho(xbar)) = kappa(xi,.)
Lambda_rho(xbar)(rho) = 1.

Existence of a coupling separately for each xbar is not enough. Measurability of the assignment must be proved under explicit regularity hypotheses.

Potentially useful regularity assumptions include standard Borel or Polish structure on X, measurable (and in some selection arguments closed) relations rho, and suitable measurability/closedness properties of the coupling correspondence. These assumptions are not asserted here to be sufficient in every case; the applicable measurable-selection result and its hypotheses must be checked for the chosen setting.

COHERENCE REQUIREMENT:
If tuples belong to multiple relations, or relations overlap through common coordinates, the selected joint kernels must be mutually compatible. Separate existence of Lambda_rho for each relation does not automatically produce one coherent stochastic evolution of the full relational structure.

A stronger construction may require projective consistency under coordinate restriction and permutation, together with compatibility across the family of relations.

============================================================
6. CANDIDATE C: ENRICHED RELATIONAL REALIZATION
============================================================

A possible realization-level enrichment is:
M_kappa = (X, Sigma_X, R, mu, Lambda_R)

where Lambda_R records coherent joint transition kernels for the relevant relations.

In this construction:
- kappa supplies individual transition marginals;
- Lambda_R supplies relationally compatible joint transitions;
- R remains the structural constraint;
- preservation is expressed through the joint transition semantics.

This is a candidate enriched realization, not a new Frozen Core definition.

It is not known whether the existing canonical realization map Phi:
Phi : Real(R) -> M
admits or selects such Lambda_R data.

============================================================
7. COMPATIBILITY WITH THE EXISTING ARCHITECTURE
============================================================

The existing architecture includes:
A(R;Phi) subseteq Evol(X).

The following questions remain open and must not be assumed:

1. Does the existing definition of Evol(X) include stochastic kernels, or only another class of evolution maps?
2. If kernels are included, under what exact conditions is a given kappa an element of A(R;Phi)?
3. Does Phi produce measurable state spaces and measurable relational interpretations suitable for the coupling construction?
4. Does Phi admit coherent Lambda_R data without changing the canonical realization?
5. Is the operational language L_op represented by functions on X and closed under the Markov operator P_kappa?
6. Can one common kappa and one common tau=P_kappa serve every model in the comparison class?

The coupling-based relational lift addresses a possible meaning of probabilistic relation preservation. It does not answer these architectural questions.

============================================================
8. RELATION TO THEOREM 91
============================================================

For the fixed-kernel measurable probability realization, the response identity remains:
Resp_(T_hat_kappa(M_mu))(a) = Resp_(M_mu)(P_kappa(a)).

With:
P_kappa(a)(x)=integral_X a(y) kappa(x,dy),

H3 is derived when:
- the same kernel is used across the compared models;
- P_kappa(L_op) subseteq L_op;
- measurability and integrability conditions hold.

The operational non-expansion inequality then follows.

The present checkpoint addresses only a candidate interpretation of structural preservation. It does not establish H1 in the canonical OMEGA-ISOP sense and therefore does not complete a canonical proof of Theorem 91 for Markov kernels.
