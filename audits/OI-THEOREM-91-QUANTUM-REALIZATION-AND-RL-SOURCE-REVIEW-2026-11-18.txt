3. RL-021 — MATHEMATICAL CHECK

Domain:
G_adm = {G=(M,R): M finite, M nonempty, R subset M x M}.
For a fixed nonempty proper subset B of M, let X=M\B.

Observable:
I_B^0(G) = R intersect (B x B).

Target:
P_B(G)=1 iff R intersect (B x X) is nonempty.

Construction:
Choose S subset B x B, b in B, and x in X.
R1=S.
R2=S union {(b,x)}.

Then:
I_B^0(G1)=I_B^0(G2)=S,
P_B(G1)=0,
P_B(G2)=1.

Therefore no function F on the internal observable alone can recover P_B over the stated unrestricted domain.

CHECK:
The witness is valid under the declared finite relational domain and unrestricted external-relation assumptions.
The non-identifiability conclusion follows directly from equal observations and unequal target values.

SCOPE:
This is a result about the specified relational class and target predicate. It does not by itself establish physical causation, a physical boundary law, or a universal statement about all relational formalisms.

4. RL-021.1 — MATHEMATICAL CHECK

Target:
Q_B(G)=1 iff there exist b1,b2 in B and x in X such that (b1,x) and (x,b2) belong to R.

For each boundary node b:
d_out(b)=number of x in X with (b,x) in R.
d_in(b)=number of x in X with (x,b) in R.
D_B is the complete boundary degree profile.

A. Unrestricted domain, |X| >= 2

Choose distinct x1,x2 in X and b1,b2 in B.
R1=S union {(b1,x1),(x2,b2)}.
R2=S union {(b1,x1),(x1,b2)}.

The boundary degree profile is identical in both structures.
In R1 the incoming and outgoing incidences occur at different external nodes, so Q_B=0.
In R2 both incidences meet at x1, so Q_B=1.

This remains valid when b1=b2: the same boundary node has one outgoing and one incoming incidence in both structures, while only R2 has a common external witness.

Conclusion:
For |X| >= 2, Q_B is not identifiable from (R_B_int,D_B) over the unrestricted domain.

B. Joint-incidence sufficient data

j_B(b1,b2)=number of x in X for which (b1,x) and (x,b2) both belong to R.
J_B is the matrix of these joint counts.

T_B = sum over b1,b2 of j_B(b1,b2)
    = sum over x in X of o_B(x) times i_B(x).

Therefore:
Q_B=1 iff T_B>0.
Also Q_B=1 iff some entry of J_B is positive.

The identity for T_B follows by counting each ordered pair of boundary incidences through its common external node.

C. Restricted domains

On G_forward, where every positive outgoing external incidence at x implies a positive incoming incidence at x:
Q_B=1 iff D_out>0.

On G_backward, where every positive incoming external incidence at x implies a positive outgoing incidence at x:
Q_B=1 iff D_in>0.

For |X|=1:
Q_B=1 iff D_out>0 and D_in>0.

D. Necessity correction

C_forward is sufficient for identification through D_out, but it is not necessary for identification from the full D_B.
On G_backward, D_B identifies Q_B through D_in, even when C_forward fails for a graph containing an external state (o,i)=(1,0).

The source correctly leaves a general necessary-and-sufficient characterization for arbitrary allowed state sets H open. The degenerate example H={(1,0)} makes Q_B identically zero and therefore identifiable despite containing an unpaired outgoing state.

5. RL-027 — MATHEMATICAL CHECK

Finite-dimensional bipartite quantum system:
H_A tensor H_B, with density operator rho_AB.
rho_A=Tr_B(rho_AB).
rho_B=Tr_A(rho_AB).
Q_A=Tr(rho_A squared).

Operational identifiability on a domain D means that a function F exists such that:
Q_A(rho_AB)=F(rho_B)
for every rho_AB in D.

Equivalent condition:
If two states in D have the same rho_B, they must have the same Q_A.

A. Pure global states

For a pure bipartite state, Schmidt decomposition gives:
|psi>=sum_i sqrt(lambda_i) |a_i> tensor |b_i>,
where lambda_i are nonnegative and sum to one.

Both reduced states have the same nonzero eigenvalues lambda_i. Therefore:
Tr(rho_A squared)=sum_i lambda_i squared
                 =Tr(rho_B squared).

Thus on the pure global domain:
Q_A=F(rho_B), with F(X)=Tr(X squared).

This result holds for finite dimensions without requiring d_A=d_B.

B. Mixed-state counterexample

For d_A=d_B=2, use:
rho_AB_1=|00><00|.
rho_AB_2=(1/2)|00><00|+(1/2)|10><10|.

Then:
rho_B_1=rho_B_2=|0><0|,
Q_A_1=1,
Q_A_2=1/2.

Equal local observations with unequal target values disprove identifiability on the full mixed-state domain.

C. Fixed-marginal range

For fixed sigma=rho_B, product states rho_AB=tau_A tensor sigma preserve rho_B while allowing Q_A=Tr(tau_A squared).

For a d_A-dimensional density operator, purity ranges from 1/d_A to 1. Every intermediate value is attainable by a continuous family of density operators. Hence:
{Q_A : rho_AB has fixed rho_B=sigma}=[1/d_A,1]
for d_A >= 2.

REVIEW STATUS:
The pure-state identity, mixed-state witness, and fixed-marginal range follow from the supplied finite-dimensional definitions and constructions.
This checks the mathematical content of the recovered copy. It does not verify external RL registration or establish the OMEGA-ISOP-to-quantum correspondence.

6. THEOREM 91 — EXACT MATHEMATICAL CONTENT

Volume II defines:
Resp_M : L_op -> Y,
where Y is normed, and:
D_L(M1,M2)=sup over a in L_op of ||Resp_M1(a)-Resp_M2(a)||.

Theorem 91 assumes, among other contextual conditions, a map tau:L_op -> L_op satisfying:
Resp_(T(M))(a)=Resp_M(tau(a)).

For a common tau applying to both models, the proof is:

For every a in L_op:
||Resp_(T(M1))(a)-Resp_(T(M2))(a)||
=||Resp_M1(tau(a))-Resp_M2(tau(a))||
<=D_L(M1,M2).

Since tau(a) belongs to L_op, taking the supremum over a yields:
D_L(T(M1),T(M2))<=D_L(M1,M2).

The proof is valid when the response maps and comparison domains are well-defined and the same tau is used for both models.

7. THEOREM 91 — PRECISION FINDINGS

7.1 Common-map condition

The same map tau must apply to both M1 and M2. If each model is allowed a different map tau_M, the inequality does not follow in general.

7.2 Surjectivity

Surjectivity of tau is not needed for non-expansion.
If tau is surjective, the supremum is taken over the whole L_op through tau, and equality follows:
D_L(T(M1),T(M2))=D_L(M1,M2).

If tau is not surjective, strict decrease is possible but not guaranteed.

7.3 Redundant or contextual hypotheses

H1, concerning existence and preservation of R under T, is not used in the algebraic inequality once the response covariance in H3 is assumed. H1 may remain as an architectural condition identifying T as an admissible relational dynamics, but it is not a proof premise for the norm inequality itself.

H4 fixes the operational comparison context. It is needed for an unambiguous physical comparison, although it does not add a separate algebraic step to the displayed proof.

7.4 Minimal mathematical setting

The proof does not require vector-space structure on Y. It is sufficient for Y to be a metric space with distance d_Y, replacing the norm by d_Y.

For a clean statement, require L_op to be nonempty and the response maps to be defined on the full common operational domain. Partial response maps require an explicit common-domain condition.

7.5 Boundary test for model-dependent tau

Let L_op={a,b}. Let two models have identical response maps:
Resp_M1(a)=Resp_M2(a)=0,
Resp_M1(b)=Resp_M2(b)=1.
Then D_L(M1,M2)=0.

If model-dependent pullbacks are permitted, choose tau_1(a)=a and tau_2(a)=b. The transformed responses at a differ by 1. Thus non-expansion can fail.

This confirms that a common tau is a substantive hypothesis, not a notational convenience.

8. CONDITIONAL QUANTUM REALIZATION OF THEOREM 91

This section supplies an explicit application-level realization. It does not derive quantum mechanics from OMEGA-ISOP.

Physical input:
- finite-dimensional Hilbert space H;
- density operators rho and sigma;
- a completely positive trace-preserving quantum channel E;
- the Born measurement rule.

Operational language:
L_op={A: 0<=A<=I}, the set of quantum measurement effects.

Response space:
Y=R with distance |u-v|.

Response map:
Resp_rho(A)=Tr(rho A).

Dynamics:
T(rho)=E(rho).

Adjoint map:
tau(A)=E*(A), where E* is the Heisenberg adjoint of E.

Because E is trace-preserving, E*(I)=I.
Because E is completely positive, E* is positive.
Therefore E* maps every effect 0<=A<=I to another effect in the same L_op.

By the adjoint relation:
Resp_(E(rho))(A)
=Tr(E(rho)A)
=Tr(rho E*(A))
=Resp_rho(tau(A)).

Thus the common-map response covariance required by Theorem 91 is satisfied.

The operational distance becomes:
D_L(rho,sigma)=sup over 0<=A<=I of |Tr((rho-sigma)A)|
              =(1/2)||rho-sigma||_1.

Theorem 91 therefore yields:
(1/2)||E(rho)-E(sigma)||_1
<=
(1/2)||rho-sigma||_1.

This is the standard contractivity of quantum trace distance under completely positive trace-preserving maps.

9. PHYSICAL INTERPRETATION AND LIMITS

Established mathematical correspondence:
Theorem 91's response-map covariance is realized by the Heisenberg adjoint of a quantum channel acting on measurement effects.

Physical interpretation:
A quantum channel cannot increase the operational distinguishability of two states when distinguishability is measured by the optimal measurement over all effects.

10. RELATION TO RL-027

RL-027 concerns domain-dependent identifiability of subsystem purity from a reduced state.
Theorem 91 concerns non-expansion of operational distinguishability under a response-compatible transformation.

These are distinct mathematical claims.

Theorem 91 does not imply that subsystem purity is preserved under every quantum channel.
Trace-distance contractivity does not imply purity preservation.
The pure-state identity Tr(rho_A squared)=Tr(rho_B squared) does not by itself imply persistence under dynamics.

Any future combination of RL-027 and Theorem 91 must specify the channel class, state domain, observable set, and exact quantity whose persistence or identifiability is being tested.
