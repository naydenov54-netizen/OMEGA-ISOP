1. PURPOSE AND MODEL

This audit gives a concrete budgeted comparison of repeated cosmological observations and calibration measurements. It separates structural identifiability from precision and shows how correlated errors can create a precision plateau. The calculation is conditional on the stated observation model; it is not a universal experimental optimum.

Let H>0 and q>0 be unknown. For n observations at a fixed known redshift z>0, use

s_i = z H/q + epsilon_i, i=1,...,n.

Assume initially that epsilon_i are independent, zero-mean Gaussian errors with variance sigma_s^2. Add one genuine calibration observation

u = q + eta,

where eta is zero-mean Gaussian with variance sigma_q^2, independent of all epsilon_i. Treat z, sigma_s^2, and sigma_q^2 as known positive constants. The calibration convention must genuinely make u a measurement of q; otherwise the identifiability analysis changes.

2. IDENTIFIABILITY BEFORE OPTIMIZATION

Without u, the mean of every s_i is zH/q. For every a>0, the transformation

(H,q) -> (aH, aq)

preserves all means. Under the specified noise model, the full distribution of the cosmological data is also preserved. Therefore H is not statistically identifiable from the s_i alone, no matter how large n becomes.

The sample mean s_bar has variance sigma_s^2/n, so increasing n improves estimation of the identifiable combination H/q. It does not identify H separately from q.

With the genuine calibration observation u=q+eta, the joint mean map is

(H,q) -> (zH/q, q).

This map is globally injective on H>0, q>0: the second coordinate determines q and the first then determines H. The noise affects precision but, under this specified model, does not undo structural identifiability.

3. LOCAL VARIANCE BOUND FOR H

Write the means of (s_bar,u) as

mu(H,q) = (zH/q, q),

with covariance matrix diag(sigma_s^2/n, sigma_q^2). The Jacobian is

J = [ z/q, -zH/q^2
      0,       1     ].

The Gaussian Fisher information is I=J^T Sigma^{-1}J. It is positive definite for z,q,sigma_s,sigma_q>0. Its entries are

I_HH = n z^2/(q^2 sigma_s^2),
I_Hq = -n z^2 H/(q^3 sigma_s^2),
I_qq = n z^2 H^2/(q^4 sigma_s^2) + 1/sigma_q^2.

The nuisance-adjusted information for H is the Schur complement

I_eff(H) = I_HH - I_Hq^2/I_qq
         = 1 / [q^2 sigma_s^2/(n z^2) + H^2 sigma_q^2/q^2].

Thus the Cramer-Rao lower bound for any regular unbiased estimator of H is

Var(Hhat) >= 1/I_eff(H)
           = q^2 sigma_s^2/(n z^2) + H^2 sigma_q^2/q^2.

The same expression is the first-order delta-method variance for Hhat = s_bar u/z. In this independent Gaussian product model, the displayed Cramer-Rao statement is a local regular bound, not a claim that every estimator attains it exactly at finite sample size.

Interpretation:
- The first term decreases as 1/n and is the cosmological measurement contribution.
- The second term is the calibration contribution and does not decrease by repeating only s_i.
- For fixed nonzero calibration uncertainty sigma_q^2, the bound approaches H^2 sigma_q^2/q^2 as n grows without bound.
- If sigma_q^2 is reduced, the calibration floor falls. If calibration uncertainty is not represented by a true independent measurement of q, this formula is not justified.

4. CORRELATED COSMOLOGICAL ERRORS

Now keep the same marginal variance sigma_s^2 but assume equicorrelated cosmological errors:

Cov(epsilon_i, epsilon_j) = sigma_s^2 rho for i not equal to j,
Var(epsilon_i) = sigma_s^2.

For a valid positive-definite equicorrelation matrix, -1/(n-1) < rho < 1 (with the usual interpretation for n=1). The sample mean variance is exactly

Var(s_bar) = sigma_s^2 [rho + (1-rho)/n].

If the calibration error remains independent of the cosmological errors, the preceding information calculation applies with sigma_s^2/n replaced by this sample-mean variance. Therefore

Var(Hhat) >= q^2 sigma_s^2 [rho + (1-rho)/n]/z^2
             + H^2 sigma_q^2/q^2

as the corresponding regular local Cramer-Rao bound for this two-summary Gaussian model.

For rho=0 this reduces to the independent-error expression. For fixed rho>0, increasing n leaves a cosmological noise floor sigma_s^2 rho in the sample mean. Repetition then has diminishing returns and cannot remove the separate calibration floor.

This result depends on the equicorrelation model. Arbitrary covariance structures must be analyzed using the full covariance matrix; correlation does not universally increase or decrease the variance of every estimator.

5. EXPLICIT BUDGET ALLOCATION

Let total budget be B. Each cosmological observation costs c_s>0 and the calibration observation costs c_q>0. If one calibration is required and B>=c_q+c_s, the feasible number of cosmological observations is

n(B) = floor((B-c_q)/c_s).

Under the independent-error model, the target-specific local variance bound is

V(B;c_q) = q^2 sigma_s^2/[z^2 n(B)] + H^2 sigma_q^2/q^2.

This expression is conditional on the calibration precision sigma_q^2 being fixed for the selected calibration procedure. If calibration precision itself depends on its cost or repeated calibration effort, that cost-to-variance function must be supplied before an allocation can be optimized.

Under the correlated equicorrelation model, replace the first term by

q^2 sigma_s^2 [rho + (1-rho)/n(B)]/z^2.

These formulas permit comparison of feasible designs but do not establish a universal optimum. The floor operation, discrete measurement counts, calibration cost, and actual cost-to-precision relationship all matter.

6. WHEN SHOULD BUDGET GO TO CALIBRATION?

Suppose, as a further explicit design assumption, calibration effort C_q yields a known decreasing variance function sigma_q^2(C_q), and cosmological effort C_s yields n(C_s) observations. With C_s+C_q<=B, the independent-error variance criterion becomes

V(C_s,C_q) = q^2 sigma_s^2/[z^2 n(C_s)] + H^2 sigma_q^2(C_q)/q^2.

An interior allocation, when differentiability and continuous relaxation are appropriate, balances marginal reductions in the two terms subject to the budget constraint. For example, with C_s+C_q=B, a stationary point satisfies

q^2 sigma_s^2 z^{-2} n'(C_s)/n(C_s)^2
=
- H^2 q^{-2} d sigma_q^2(C_q)/d C_q.

This equality equates the marginal variance reduction from added cosmological effort to the marginal variance reduction from added calibration effort. It is only a necessary condition for an interior optimum; global optimality requires checking the feasible domain, boundary designs, and curvature. The equation is not usable numerically until n(C_s) and sigma_q^2(C_q) are specified.

If calibration variance is fixed regardless of cost, this continuous allocation equation is not a valid model for optimizing calibration effort. In that case, calibration cost is a fixed overhead and n should be maximized among feasible designs, subject to any other constraints.

7. DESIGN COMPARISON PROCEDURE

For each candidate design:
(a) specify the complete parameter vector and admissible domain;
(b) write the full mean map and full joint noise distribution;
(c) check whether any transformation preserves the full distribution while changing H;
(d) reject as an H-identification design any scheme with such an unresolved symmetry;
(e) calculate the full Fisher information using the complete covariance, if the regularity assumptions apply;
(f) calculate target-specific nuisance-adjusted information, not just total information;
(g) impose explicit costs and compare feasible designs under the chosen objective;
(h) report the calibration floor, correlation assumptions, and sensitivity to model misspecification.

A design with many observations but no independent calibration can estimate H/q precisely while remaining structurally unable to identify H. A design with a genuine calibration can identify H, but the uncertainty may remain dominated by calibration. Those are different failure modes and should not be conflated.
