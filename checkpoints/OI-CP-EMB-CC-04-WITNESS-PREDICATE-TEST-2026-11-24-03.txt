3. NON-CANONICAL WORKING WITNESS PREDICATE
============================================================

Fix a selected category C_METACORE and a selected realization-specific admissibility predicate Adm.

For a span of configuration embeddings:
C0 --i1--> C1
C0 --i2--> C2

define a candidate amalgamation witness as a tuple:
W = (A,j1,j2)

where:

(A) A is an object of the selected category C_METACORE;

(B) j1:C1->A and j2:C2->A are arrows in the selected embedding class;

(C) j1 o i1 = j2 o i2;

(D) Adm(C0,C1,C2; A,j1,j2) holds.

Define:
Witness_CC(C0,C1,C2;W) iff (A)-(D) all hold.

Define:
CC_work(C1,C2 | C0)=1
iff there exists W such that Witness_CC(C0,C1,C2;W).

Otherwise:
CC_work(C1,C2 | C0)=0.

This predicate deliberately leaves Adm uninterpreted pending a source-authorized realization. It is therefore a formal interface/schema, not a completed OMEGA-ISOP definition.

FINITE WITNESS:
W is finite only if the selected mathematical realization specifies a finite carrier/object set and finite relevant arrow/relation data, and those data are finite.

The word "finite" must be interpreted relative to the selected category. For a finite relational configuration, this may mean finite carrier and relation. For a finite category, it means finitely many objects and morphisms.

STRONGNESS:
Strong(W) iff:
j1(|C1|) intersect j2(|C2|) = j1(i1(|C0|)).

Strongness is a separately recordable predicate. It is not built into Witness_CC unless a future authorized realization explicitly places it inside Adm.

============================================================
4. FINITE-FAMILY VERSION
============================================================

For a finite diagram family (D_k) indexed by a finite diagram shape J, a candidate common witness consists of:

- a target object A in the selected category;
- a family of selected-class arrows j_k:D_k->A;
- commutativity for every arrow in J;
- the selected realization-specific admissibility predicate Adm_J(A,(j_k)).

Define:
Witness_J(A,(j_k)) iff all these conditions hold.

A finite common witness exists iff there is such an admissible cocone whose target A is finite in the selected realization.

This formulation does not assert that the target must be the categorical colimit, the jointly generated subobject, or a minimal object.

A colimit, if required, is an additional universal-property condition and must not be conflated with mere existence of an admissible cocone.

5. CRITICAL CORRECTION: D_INFINITY DOES NOT PROVE THE STRONGER CLAIM
============================================================

Earlier checkpoint OI-CP-EMB-CC-04-FINITE-FAMILY-TEST-2026-11-24-01 correctly showed:

Inside the fixed one-object category with endomorphism group:
D_infinity = <a,b | a^2=e, b^2=e>,

the subcategory generated by the two finite subcategories:
W1={e,a}
W2={e,b}
is infinite.

This proves:
the jointly generated subcategory inside that fixed ambient category is infinite.

It does NOT prove:
every possible common amalgamation target for W1 and W2 is infinite.

Countercheck:
Let:
V4 = C2 x C2 = {e,u,v,uv},
with u^2=v^2=e and uv=vu.

There are injective group homomorphisms:
C2 -> V4, mapping the nontrivial element of the first C2 to u;
C2 -> V4, mapping the nontrivial element of the second C2 to v.

Their images intersect only at {e}. Hence this is a finite strong amalgam of the two C2 groups over the trivial group.

Thus:
- the pushout/free product C2 * C2 is infinite;
- a finite common strong amalgam nevertheless exists in V4.

The distinction is:
PUSHOUT UNIVERSALITY != EXISTENCE OF SOME COMMON AMALGAM.

Therefore the D_infinity example is not a counterexample to finite common amalgamation when the witness target may vary and Adm permits V4.

It remains valid only for the narrower fixed-ambient/generated-subcategory convention.

The earlier statement that it refutes the unrestricted finite-family common-witness implication must be narrowed accordingly. Do not cite it as a general CC counterexample.

6. TEST RESULT UNDER THE WORKING PREDICATE
============================================================

For the pair of one-object categories C2 and C2 over the trivial one-object category:

If the selected category contains finite group objects and Adm accepts injective group homomorphism amalgams, V4 supplies a finite strong witness.

Therefore:
FINITE COMMON WITNESS: YES, UNDER THIS TEST REALIZATION.
STRONG WITNESS: YES, UNDER THIS TEST REALIZATION.
PUSHOUT: INFINITE, D_infinity.
FINITE PUSHOUT: NO.

This separates three questions:
1. Does some common admissible witness exist?
2. Does some finite common admissible witness exist?
3. Does a finite categorical colimit/pushout exist?

They are not equivalent.

The result is model-dependent and does not establish that V4 is an admissible OMEGA-ISOP realization.

============================================================
7. FINITE-CLOSURE LEMMA: CORRECT SCOPE
============================================================

The finite-closure lemma from CP-02 remains correct under its explicit generated-subcategory convention:

If the generated closure is admissible and finite, it is a finite common witness.

But finite generated closure is a sufficient construction method, not a necessary condition for existence of some finite common witness.

The V4 example demonstrates why:
the free/generated pushout is infinite, while another finite strong amalgam exists.

Thus do not elevate finite generated closure into a necessary CC axiom.
