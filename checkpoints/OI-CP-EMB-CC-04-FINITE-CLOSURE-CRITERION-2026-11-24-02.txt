3. EXPLICIT WITNESS CONVENTION
============================================================

For this checkpoint only, fix a category C.

A subcategory witness for a diagram D is a subcategory W of C that contains the image of D (on objects and arrows).

For a finite family D_1,...,D_n, a common subcategory witness W must contain the images of every D_i.

This convention is a mathematical test definition, not a recovered OMEGA-ISOP definition.

Let S be the union of the object and arrow data of a family of subcategories W_1,...,W_n. Define <S>_C as the least subcategory of C containing S, obtained by adjoining all required identity arrows and all finite composites of composable arrows in S.

If no such subcategory exists under the chosen ambient restrictions, the convention itself is not applicable; in an ordinary category C, the generated subcategory exists.

============================================================
4. FINITE-CLOSURE LEMMA
============================================================

LEMMA:
Let C be a category and let D_1,...,D_n be a finite family of diagrams. Suppose:

(1) For each i, there is a finite subcategory W_i of C containing the image of D_i.

(2) The generated subcategory
    W := < union_i W_i >_C
    is an admissible witness under the selected witness convention.

(3) The closure W has finitely many objects and finitely many arrows.

Then W is a finite common subcategory witness for the entire family.

PROOF:
By construction, W contains every W_i, hence contains the image of every D_i. By assumption (2), W is an admissible witness. By assumption (3), its object and arrow sets are finite. Therefore W is a finite common witness. QED.

IMPORTANT:
Assumption (3) is not implied by finiteness of the individual W_i.
If admissibility is not closed under generated subcategories, assumption (2) is an independent requirement.
The lemma is conditional and does not prove either condition from the OMEGA-ISOP source architecture.

============================================================
5. FINITE AMBIENT SUFFICIENT CONDITION
============================================================

COROLLARY:
If all W_i are subcategories of one finite category C_f, and C_f is an admissible common witness containing all D_i, then a finite common witness exists.

PROOF:
Take W=C_f. It contains all D_i by assumption and is finite because C_f is finite. QED.

A sharper generated witness is < union_i W_i >_(C_f), which is also finite because it is a subcategory of a finite category.

This corollary is sufficient, not necessary. A finite generated common witness may exist inside an infinite ambient category.

============================================================
6. CONTRASTING EXAMPLES
============================================================

6.1 INFINITE CLOSURE

Let:
C = one-object category with endomorphism group
D_infinity = <a,b | a^2=e, b^2=e>.

Set:
W_1 = {e,a}
W_2 = {e,b}.

Each W_i is a finite subcategory.

The generated common subcategory contains every alternating reduced word:
(ab)^n, for n >= 0.

Since ab has infinite order in C_2 * C_2, these arrows are pairwise distinct. Therefore:
|<W_1 union W_2>_C| = infinity.

Thus finite individual witnesses do not imply a finite common generated witness.

6.2 FINITE CLOSURE

Let:
C = one-object category with endomorphism group
C_2 x C_2 = <a,b | a^2=e, b^2=e, ab=ba>.

Again set:
W_1={e,a}
W_2={e,b}.

The generated common subcategory is:
{e,a,b,ab}.

It is finite, with four arrows.

The two examples have the same finite individual witness sizes and involution relations. The difference is the interaction/closure relation between generators: in the second example they commute, while in the first there is no relation forcing ab to have finite order.

============================================================
7. EXACT LOGICAL CONCLUSION
============================================================

The finite-family implication is not valid from individual finiteness alone.

Under the explicit subcategory-witness convention, the exact additional requirement is that the jointly generated closure be finite and admissible.

A finite ambient category is one sufficient way to guarantee finite closure.

The condition is not a universal OMEGA-ISOP axiom. It is a conditional criterion for this witness convention.

The D_infinity example refutes the unrestricted implication in the general category-theoretic setting. The C_2 x C_2 example shows that finite common closure can hold in another category under the same individual finite-witness pattern.
