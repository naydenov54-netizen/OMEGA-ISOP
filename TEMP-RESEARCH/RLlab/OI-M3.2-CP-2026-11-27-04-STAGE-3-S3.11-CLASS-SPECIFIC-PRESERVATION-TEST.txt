============================================================
1. OBJECTIVE
============================================================

S3.10 showed that quotient pullback can destroy membership in a hereditary class K, even when K has the free amalgamation property.

S3.11 tests the converse possibility:
Can quotient pullback preserve membership in a particular nontrivial hereditary class even when the free union relation is not fiber-saturated and therefore differs from the quotient pullback?

The purpose is to prevent an invalid converse inference from S3.10.

============================================================
2. SIGNATURE AND FORBIDDEN STRUCTURE
============================================================

Use:
Sigma={R},
where R is binary.

Let F have:
S_F={u,v},
R_F={(u,v)}.

Thus F has exactly one ordered relation tuple and no loops or reverse tuple.

F is irreducible under the S3.4 definition: its only pair of distinct elements, u and v, occurs together in the tuple (u,v).

Define:
K=Forb(F),

where membership means that no induced two-element substructure is isomorphic to F.

K is hereditary. By the single finite forbidden structure criterion S3.4-T1, irreducibility of F gives the free amalgamation property for induced embeddings in this pure relational setting.

============================================================
3. INPUT SPAN
============================================================

Let:
S_C={c},
S_A={c,a},
S_B={c,b}.

Let all apartness relations be empty:
#_A=#_B=#_C=empty.

Define:
R_A=S_A x S_A,
R_B=S_B x S_B,
R_C={(c,c)}.

The embeddings of C into A and B are induced for both apartness and R. The side relations agree on C.

Each of A and B has two elements, but its induced two-element relation is universal and therefore is not isomorphic to F, which has only the tuple (u,v).

Consequently:
A in K,
B in K,
C in K.

Each side relation is coordinatewise invariant under its side equivalence relation because it is universal.

============================================================
4. QUOTIENT AND PULLBACK
============================================================

All apartness-equivalence classes on each side are single classes.

Therefore:
Q_A={q_A},
Q_B={q_B},
Q_C={q_C},
Q={q}.

The descended quotient relation is:
Rbar_Q={(q,q)}.

The quotient Q belongs to K because it has one element and F has two.

The underlying set pushout is:
S_P={c,a,b}.

The map pi_P sends every element to q.

The quotient pullback is:
R_P^pull=S_P x S_P.

Every two-element induced substructure of P_pull has all four ordered pairs, including both loops and both directions.

No such induced two-element substructure is isomorphic to F, which has exactly one ordered tuple (u,v).

Therefore:
P_pull in K.

============================================================
5. FREE UNION AND FAILURE OF FIBER SATURATION
============================================================

The free relational amalgam on S_P is:
R_P^free=R_A union R_B.

It omits:
(a,b),
(b,a).

Every two-element induced substructure is either:
- universal on {c,a};
- universal on {c,b}; or
- empty on {a,b}.

None is isomorphic to F.

Therefore:
P_free in K.

However:
R_P^free != R_P^pull.

In particular:
pi_P(a)=pi_P(b),
R_P^free(a,a)=TRUE,
R_P^free(a,b)=FALSE.

Thus R_P^free is not fiber-saturated.

This confirms:
- Fiber saturation is necessary and sufficient for equality of the free union and quotient pullback relations.
- Fiber saturation is NOT necessary for membership of the quotient pullback in every particular hereditary class K.

============================================================
6. RESULT AND LOGICAL BOUNDARY
============================================================

In this example:
Q in K,
P_pull in K,
P_free in K,

while:
R_P^pull != R_P^free.

Therefore class membership may survive quotient pullback even when the relational structure changes.

This does not contradict S3.10. S3.10 used a different forbidden structure F for which the added mixed tuples completed a forbidden induced configuration. Here the selected forbidden structure is not created by the saturation of the free union.

The correct distinction is:

(A) STRUCTURAL EQUALITY:
R_P^pull = R_P^free.
This is equivalent to fiber saturation.

(B) CLASS PRESERVATION:
P_pull in K.
This is class-dependent and can hold even when (A) fails.

(C) EXISTENCE OF AN AMALGAM IN K:
There may exist an amalgam in K without the quotient-pullback structure being that amalgam. This is a separate question.

============================================================
7. EVIDENCE CLASS
============================================================

EXPLICIT FINITE TEST WITH DIRECT VERIFICATION.

The example is finite and all relation tables are specified. Membership in K is checked by inspecting every two-element induced substructure.

No general theorem about all hereditary classes is inferred from this example.

============================================================
8. COMPARISON WITH S3.10
============================================================

S3.10:
F was the three-element structure with universal binary R.
The quotient pullback completed F and left K.

S3.11:
F is the two-element structure with only R(u,v).
The quotient pullback is universal on S_P and does not contain F as an induced substructure.
K is preserved despite the failure of fiber saturation.

Together, the two examples establish that no uniform implication in either direction is available from the current assumptions:

- Quotient-level K preservation does not guarantee pullback K preservation.
- Failure of fiber saturation does not guarantee failure of K preservation.

The forbidden pattern and the exact saturation effect matter.
