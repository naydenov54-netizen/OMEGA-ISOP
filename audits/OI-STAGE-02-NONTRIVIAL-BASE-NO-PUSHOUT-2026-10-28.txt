PURPOSE
Remove the singleton-base dependency from the prior finite no-pushout example. This audit checks whether weak amalgamation can fail to imply pushout even when every object in the tested span satisfies the minimal size condition |S| >= 2.

1. CATEGORY
Let E be the category of finite sets equipped with discrete apartness x # y iff x != y. Its morphisms are all embeddings, meaning injective functions that preserve and reflect apartness. Since apartness is discrete, every injective function between objects is an embedding.
This is an explicit test category. It is not asserted to be the uniquely intended C_METACORE category.

2. SPAN WITH NONTRIVIAL BASE
Let S={s1,s2}, A={s1,s2,a}, and B={s1,s2,b}, with all listed elements distinct and pairwise apart within each object. Consider the span A <- S -> B formed by the evident inclusions.
All three objects satisfy the minimum-size condition: |S|=2, |A|=|B|=3.

3. WEAK AMALGAM EXISTS
Let C={s1,s2,a,b}, with discrete apartness. The evident embeddings A -> C and B -> C agree on S. Thus an admissible amalgam exists in E.

4. CLAIM
The span A <- S -> B has no pushout in E.

5. PROOF
Assume a pushout P exists, with embeddings j_A:A -> P and j_B:B -> P agreeing on S.

First use the cocone into C from Section 3, where the images of a and b are distinct. By the pushout universal property, there is a morphism h_C:P -> C whose composites with j_A and j_B are the given embeddings. Every morphism in E is injective. Since h_C(j_A(a))=a and h_C(j_B(b))=b are distinct, j_A(a) and j_B(b) must be distinct in P.

Now let Z={0,1,2}, with discrete apartness. Define embeddings f:A -> Z and g:B -> Z by f(s1)=g(s1)=0, f(s2)=g(s2)=1, and f(a)=g(b)=2. These maps are injective on A and B individually and agree on S, so they form a cocone over the span. The pushout property would produce a morphism h_Z:P -> Z satisfying h_Z∘j_A=f and h_Z∘j_B=g. But h_Z sends the distinct elements j_A(a) and j_B(b) both to 2, so h_Z is not injective. This contradicts the definition of morphism in E. Therefore the pushout does not exist. QED.

6. INTERPRETATION
PROVED: In E, the span has a weak amalgam but no pushout.
PROVED: This counterexample does not require a singleton shared base; every object in the span satisfies |S| >= 2.
NOT PROVED: That E is the intended project category C_METACORE.
NOT PROVED: That the project-level R fails to exist.
The result establishes only that weak amalgamation is not sufficient, by itself, to guarantee a pushout in this explicit all-embeddings category.

7. RELATION TO THE UNION-COLIMIT MODEL
The inclusion-only poset category P_F can admit a union colimit under closure assumptions because its morphisms are only literal inclusions. E permits every embedding, including cocones that identify the images of a and b in the target, and this larger cocone class obstructs the pushout above. The results remain compatible because the categories and their morphisms differ.

8. PROJECT-LEVEL DEPENDENCY
The Library source defines embeddings by injectivity plus preservation and reflection of apartness, but the recovered statements do not conclusively specify whether all such embeddings are morphisms of C_METACORE. The project must explicitly settle the morphism class, object admissibility, indexing category, diagram, and universal property before this test obstruction can be applied to R.
