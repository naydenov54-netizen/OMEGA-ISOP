1. DEFINITIONS

Let X be a physical state space, T_t an evolution map, and d a specified distinguishability measure. Define d_t(x,y) = d(T_t(x), T_t(y)).

Existence at t=0: d_0(x,y) > 0.
Persistence over a time domain I: d_t(x,y) > 0 for every t in I.
Threshold persistence at epsilon > 0: d_t(x,y) >= epsilon for every t in I.
Robust threshold persistence: the threshold condition continues to hold under a declared class of perturbations with a stated error bound.

These are different claims. Initial distinction alone does not imply either persistence property.

2. COUNTERMODEL: DISTINCTION CAN BE ERASED

Let rho and sigma be distinct quantum states. Consider the reset channel E(omega) = tau for every input omega, where tau is a fixed density operator. Then D(rho,sigma) > 0 may hold initially, but
D(E(rho), E(sigma)) = D(tau,tau) = 0.

Thus arbitrary initial quantum distinguishability does not imply persistence under all quantum-admissible channels. This is a counterexample to universal persistence, not a claim that every physical system implements a reset channel.

3. CONDITIONAL PERSISTENCE UNDER UNITARY EVOLUTION

If the dynamics is a common unitary U_t, then trace-distance invariance gives
D(U_t rho U_t†, U_t sigma U_t†) = D(rho,sigma).
Therefore any initially distinct pair remains equally distinguishable under this specified dynamics.

The result depends on the unitary-dynamics premise. PD-A does not select that premise or derive it in the present Core interpretation.

4. CONTRACTIVITY DOES NOT IMPLY STRICT PERSISTENCE

For a completely positive trace-preserving channel E,
D(E(rho), E(sigma)) <= D(rho,sigma).
This is contractivity. It permits equality, strict decrease, and complete erasure of distinguishability. Therefore contractivity alone does not prove D(E(rho), E(sigma)) > 0.

5. ROBUSTNESS BOUND

Suppose D(rho,rho') <= delta and D(sigma,sigma') <= delta. By the triangle inequality for trace distance,
|D(rho,sigma) - D(rho',sigma')| <= D(rho,rho') + D(sigma,sigma') <= 2 delta.

Consequently, if D(rho,sigma) >= epsilon + 2 delta, then D(rho',sigma') >= epsilon. This is a conditional robustness guarantee for the declared trace-distance error bounds. It does not establish dynamical persistence unless the error bounds hold throughout the time interval under study.

7. PHYSICAL REQUIREMENTS FOR A PERSISTENCE CLAIM

A physical persistence claim must specify:
(a) the distinguishability metric and its operational meaning;
(b) the dynamics and time interval;
(c) the allowed perturbation/noise family;
(d) the threshold and uncertainty budget;
(e) a measurement protocol and failure condition.

Without these, “persistence” remains underspecified and cannot function as a quantitative physical law.
