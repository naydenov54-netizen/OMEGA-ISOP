2. GENERAL RESULT

3. COMPARISON OF TWO DYNAMICS

Take two qubit states
rho_plus = |+><+| and rho_minus = |-><-|,
where |+> = (|0>+|1>)/sqrt(2) and |-> = (|0>-|1>)/sqrt(2).

Initially, these states are orthogonal and their trace distance is
D(rho_plus,rho_minus) = (1/2)||rho_plus-rho_minus||_1 = 1.

Model U: Apply the same unitary channel to both states. Trace distance is invariant under a common unitary:
D(U rho_plus U†, U rho_minus U†) = D(rho_plus,rho_minus) = 1.

Model D: Apply a phase-damping channel that leaves diagonal density-matrix entries unchanged and multiplies off-diagonal entries by eta(t) = exp(-gamma t), with gamma >= 0. Then
D(rho_plus(t),rho_minus(t)) = exp(-gamma t).

Thus the same initial formal/quantum distinction is preserved by Model U and contracts under Model D. Both are compatible with standard quantum theory under their respective dynamical assumptions. PD-A alone does not choose between them.

4. STANDARD QUANTUM CONSTRAINT

For any completely positive trace-preserving channel E, trace distance obeys contractivity:
D(E(rho),E(sigma)) <= D(rho,sigma).

A common unitary is a special case with equality. This is a standard consequence of quantum channel theory, not a new OMEGA-ISOP law and not derived from PD-A here.
