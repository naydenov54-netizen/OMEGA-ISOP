2. CORRECTION VERIFIED
============================================================

The v0.2.1 correction replaces the inaccurate description of the
coproduct structure maps i and j as "injections" with the more precise
term "canonical coproduct morphisms".

It explicitly distinguishes:
- the canonical maps i:B -> P and j:C -> P;
- the separate cocone maps from B and C into W1 or W2.

In the categories considered in FR-1 cases (i) and (ii), i and j are
not assumed to be injective as functions. In case (iii), injectivity
follows from the definition of that category.

The contradiction argument requires preservation of apartness by the
relevant morphisms. It does not require injectivity of i or j.

AUDIT FINDING:
The terminology and scope correction is mathematically appropriate.
It removes the implication that injectivity is available in cases
where it has not been established.

============================================================
3. FR-1 PROOF CHECK
============================================================

The proof structure reviewed is as follows.

Assume that P is a coproduct of B and C, with canonical coproduct
morphisms:

i:B -> P
j:C -> P

The selected apartness relation in B is preserved by i. Hence, for the
apart elements x,y in B used in the counterexample:

i(x) # i(y)

Axiom A3 in P then gives the relevant disjunction:

i(x) # j(z) OR j(z) # i(y)

The two cases are tested against the specified cocones.

CASE 1:
If i(x) # j(z), use the universal morphism from P to W2. Since the
cocone maps from B and C into W2 preserve the specified relations,
the resulting images would require the relation x # z in W2. That
relation is absent by construction. This is a contradiction.

CASE 2:
If j(z) # i(y), use the universal morphism from P to W1. Preservation
would require z # y in W1. That relation is absent by construction.
This is a contradiction.

Thus neither disjunct can hold, contradicting A3 in P. Under the
stated assumptions and in the stated category scope, the proposed
coproduct cannot exist.

The W1 and W2 relations described in the reviewed construction
satisfy A1-A3. W2 is obtained from W1 by swapping x and y, as stated
in the reviewed argument.

AUDIT FINDING:
No substantive mathematical defect was found in the FR-1 proof
structure after the v0.2.1 terminology correction.
