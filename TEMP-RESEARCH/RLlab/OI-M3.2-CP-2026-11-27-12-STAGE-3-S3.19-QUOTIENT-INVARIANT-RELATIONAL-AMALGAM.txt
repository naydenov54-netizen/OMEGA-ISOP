============================================================
1. OBJECTIVE AND SCOPE
============================================================

S3.18 established an exact bridge between S3.12 and the Boolean-apartness reduct of the P2.3 construction. It also showed that the P2.4.1 free relational expansion is not, in general, a pullback from the quotient.

S3.19 isolates a stricter and useful fragment:

  Signature: one Boolean apartness relation # and a family of
  finitary positive-arity relation symbols R_i.

  Structural condition: every R_i is coordinatewise invariant
  under the indistinguishability equivalence induced by #.

The task is to determine:
  (a) whether the P2.4.1 free union preserves this condition;
  (b) whether failure of that free union rules out every strong
      amalgam on the P2.3 carrier;
  (c) whether a corrected quotient-level construction works.

No operations, function symbols, dynamics, realization maps, kernels,
or operational response maps are included in this theorem.


============================================================
2. BASE BOOLEAN-APARTNESS SETUP
============================================================

Let X=(S_X,#_X) be a Boolean apartness structure satisfying:

A1. irreflexivity:
    NOT(x #_X x).

A2. symmetry:
    x #_X y IFF y #_X x.

A3. cotransitivity:
    x #_X y IMPLIES (x #_X z) OR (z #_X y),
    for every z in S_X.

Assume classical Boolean logic. Define:

    x ~_X y IFF NOT(x #_X y).

Under A1-A3, ~_X is an equivalence relation.

An embedding of Boolean-apartness structures is injective and
preserves and reflects #. Such an embedding induces an injective
map on quotient sets.

Take a span of embeddings:

    C -> A
    C -> B

Replace the embeddings by inclusions, with A and B intersecting
exactly in C after the usual disjoint-copy construction.

Write:

    Q_X = S_X / ~_X
    q_X : S_X -> Q_X

The P2.3 quotient pushout is:

    Q = Q_A union_(Q_C) Q_B

and the P2.3 carrier is the corresponding pushout set:

    S_P = S_A union_C S_B.

There is a canonical surjection:

    pi : S_P -> Q

and the P2.3 apartness relation is:

    x #_P y IFF pi(x) != pi(y).

Consequently:

    x ~_P y IFF pi(x) = pi(y).

This is the fixed carrier and apartness reduct used below.


============================================================
3. QUOTIENT-INVARIANCE CONDITION
============================================================

Let R be an n-ary relation symbol, n >= 1.

R_X is coordinatewise invariant under ~_X when:

For all x_1,...,x_n,y_1,...,y_n in S_X,

    (x_1 ~_X y_1 AND ... AND x_n ~_X y_n)
    IMPLIES
    (R_X(x_1,...,x_n) IFF R_X(y_1,...,y_n)).

This condition is equivalent to the existence of a unique relation
R_X^Q on Q_X such that:

    R_X(x_1,...,x_n)
    IFF
    R_X^Q(q_X(x_1),...,q_X(x_n)).

Proof:
  If R_X^Q exists, equality of quotient coordinates gives the
  invariance condition.

  Conversely, define R_X^Q on quotient tuples by choosing any
  representatives. Coordinatewise invariance makes the truth value
  independent of the representatives. The descended relation is
  unique because q_X is surjective.

For a finite or infinite relational signature consisting of
positive-arity relation symbols, impose this condition separately
on every relation symbol.

Call these structures quotient-invariant relational expansions
of Boolean-apartness structures.


============================================================
4. FINITE COUNTEREXAMPLE: FREE UNION DOES NOT PRESERVE
   QUOTIENT INVARIANCE
============================================================

This example is deliberately small and explicit.

4.1 Common substructure C

Let:

    S_C = {c1,c2}

and define:

    c1 #_C c2
    c2 #_C c1

with no self-apartness.

Thus C has two indistinguishability classes:

    [c1] = {c1}
    [c2] = {c2}.

4.2 Extension A

Let:

    S_A = {c1,a1,c2,a2}

with equivalence classes:

    E1_A = {c1,a1}
    E2_A = {c2,a2}.

Define #_A by apartness exactly between E1_A and E2_A.

Define the binary relation:

    R_A = E1_A x E2_A.

In other words, R_A contains every ordered pair whose first
coordinate lies in E1_A and whose second coordinate lies in E2_A.

R_A is coordinatewise invariant under ~_A.

4.3 Extension B

Let:

    S_B = {c1,b1,c2,b2}

with equivalence classes:

    E1_B = {c1,b1}
    E2_B = {c2,b2}.

Define #_B by apartness exactly between E1_B and E2_B.

Define:

    R_B = E1_B x E2_B.

R_B is coordinatewise invariant under ~_B.

The restrictions of A and B to C agree:

    R_A restricted to C = {(c1,c2)}
    R_B restricted to C = {(c1,c2)}.

Thus C -> A and C -> B are embeddings in the signature {#,R},
and both input structures satisfy quotient invariance.

4.4 P2.4.1 free relational union

Construct the P2.3 carrier S_P and give it the P2.4.1 free
relational amalgam:

    R_free = R_A union R_B.

As usual, tuples using elements from both sides that are not
already supplied by either side are false.

The tuple (a1,c2) belongs to R_free because it belongs to R_A.

The tuple (a1,b2) does not belong to R_free because it is a
mixed-side tuple not supplied by either R_A or R_B.

But in the P2.3 quotient:

    a1 ~_P c1
    c2 ~_P b2.

Therefore the two ordered pairs are coordinatewise equivalent:

    (a1,c2) ~_P^2 (a1,b2).

Their R_free truth values differ. Hence R_free is not
coordinatewise invariant under ~_P.

CONCLUSION:
The P2.4.1 free relational union need not preserve the class of
quotient-invariant relational expansions, even when both input
structures satisfy quotient invariance and agree on C.

This is a failure of this particular construction, not a proof
that no admissible amalgam exists.


============================================================
5. CORRECTED CONSTRUCTION: DESCEND, AMALGAMATE, PULL BACK
============================================================

Assume A, B, and C satisfy the setup in Sections 2-3.

For each relation symbol R:

Step 1. Descend the relations.

By quotient invariance, there are unique relations:

    R_A^Q on Q_A
    R_B^Q on Q_B
    R_C^Q on Q_C

such that:

    R_A(x_1,...,x_n)
    IFF
    R_A^Q(q_A(x_1),...,q_A(x_n))

and analogously for B and C.

Step 2. Verify agreement on the common quotient.

The quotient maps induced by C -> A and C -> B are injective.
Identify Q_C with its images in Q_A and Q_B.

Because the original embeddings are induced relational embeddings,
the quotient relations R_A^Q and R_B^Q restrict to R_C^Q on Q_C.

Thus they agree on the intersection Q_C.

Step 3. Form a relational amalgam on Q.

Define the free quotient-level relation:

    R_Q = image(R_A^Q) union image(R_B^Q)

inside Q^n.

This is well-defined because the two quotient relations agree
on Q_C. Mixed quotient tuples not supplied by either side are
left false.

Step 4. Pull the relation back to S_P.

Define:

    R_P(x_1,...,x_n)
    IFF
    R_Q(pi(x_1),...,pi(x_n)).

Do this independently for every relation symbol.

The resulting P has:
  - the P2.3 carrier S_P;
  - the P2.3 apartness relation #_P;
  - the pullback relational expansion defined above.


============================================================
6. THEOREM S3.19-A: QUOTIENT-INVARIANT STRONG AMALGAM
============================================================

THEOREM.

In classical Boolean logic, consider a span of induced embeddings
C -> A and C -> B of Boolean-apartness structures expanded by a
finitary relational signature of positive-arity relation symbols.

Assume every relation symbol in A, B, and C is coordinatewise
invariant under the corresponding indistinguishability relation.

Then the P2.3 carrier S_P admits a strong amalgam P in the same
quotient-invariant relational class, obtained by:

  (i) descending every relation to the quotient;
  (ii) taking the free relational amalgam on the quotient pushout Q;
  (iii) pulling the quotient relations back along pi:S_P -> Q.

PROOF.

1. The P2.3 construction supplies the Boolean-apartness structure
   on S_P and the canonical map pi:S_P -> Q.

2. By Section 3, every relation on A, B, and C descends uniquely
   to its quotient.

3. The induced quotient embeddings of Q_C into Q_A and Q_B are
   injective. Since the original embeddings are induced for every
   relation, the quotient relations agree on Q_C.

4. Therefore their free relational union R_Q is a well-defined
   relation on Q extending both quotient structures as induced
   substructures.

5. Define R_P as the pullback of R_Q along pi in every coordinate.

6. By construction, for every tuple x:

       R_P(x) IFF R_Q(pi(x)).

   If x_i ~_P y_i for every coordinate i, then
   pi(x_i)=pi(y_i) for every i. Hence R_P(x) IFF R_P(y).
   Thus R_P is coordinatewise invariant.

7. The canonical maps A -> P and B -> P are induced for # by
   the P2.3 construction.

8. They are also induced for every R. For a tuple from A, its
   quotient tuple lies in Q_A. The free quotient relation R_Q
   restricts to R_A^Q on Q_A, so the pullback relation on A is
   exactly R_A. The same argument applies to B.

9. The P2.3 carrier is a strong set amalgam: the two images
   intersect exactly in the image of C. Adding relations does
   not identify elements. Therefore P is a strong amalgam.

This proves the theorem.


============================================================
7. THEOREM S3.19-B: FIXED-CARRIER CHARACTERIZATION
============================================================

Fix the P2.3 carrier and projection pi:S_P -> Q.

For a relation R_P on S_P, the following are equivalent:

(A) R_P is coordinatewise invariant under ~_P.

(B) There exists a unique relation R_Q on Q such that:

    R_P(x_1,...,x_n)
    IFF
    R_Q(pi(x_1),...,pi(x_n)).

This is exactly the quotient-descent criterion applied to the
P2.3 projection.

Consequently, quotient-invariant relational expansions on the
fixed P2.3 carrier correspond exactly to relational expansions
on Q via pullback.

For a span A <- C -> B, an induced quotient-level relational
amalgam on Q exists precisely when the descended quotient
relations from A and B agree on their common quotient substructure.
Under the induced-embedding and quotient-invariance assumptions
of Theorem S3.19-A, this agreement is automatic.

Important distinction:
The quotient-level amalgam is not unique in general. The free
quotient union is one valid choice. Other choices may assign
different truth values to mixed quotient tuples while preserving
the two induced embeddings.


============================================================
8. WHAT THIS RESULT DOES AND DOES NOT ESTABLISH
============================================================

ESTABLISHED:

1. Coordinatewise invariance is necessary and sufficient for
   relation descent to the quotient.

2. The P2.4.1 free union on the original carrier can fail to
   preserve quotient invariance. Section 4 gives a finite
   counterexample where both inputs already satisfy invariance.

3. The failure of that free union does not imply failure of all
   amalgams.

4. For the explicit quotient-invariant pure-relational fragment,
   descending relations, freely amalgamating them on Q, and
   pulling them back gives a strong amalgam on the P2.3 carrier.

5. For this fragment, the quotient-level view is the correct
   construction. It preserves induced embeddings and the P2.3
   strong carrier amalgam.

NOT ESTABLISHED:

1. Preservation of arbitrary additional first-order constraints
   on the quotient relations. A subclass of quotient structures
   may fail to be closed under free relational amalgamation.

2. Preservation of constraints that couple R to operations,
   dynamics, realization maps, kernels, or operational responses.

3. Any theorem for function symbols or non-relational signatures.

4. Any categorical pushout claim in the full OMEGA-ISOP category.

5. Any transfer to the complete OMEGA-ISOP realization architecture.

6. Any change to Frozen Core, canonical theorem status, RL registry,
   or Volume V.

9. CONSTRAINT AUDIT REQUIRED FOR THE NEXT STAGE
============================================================

For any proposed extra axiom or constraint on quotient-level
relations, test separately:

C1. Is the axiom invariant under representatives, so that it is
    genuinely expressible on Q?

C2. Is the class of quotient relational structures closed under
    the chosen quotient-level amalgam?

C3. Does the quotient-level amalgam preserve the axiom on mixed
    quotient tuples?

C4. Does pullback preserve the axiom on S_P?

C5. Are the embeddings still induced, not merely relation-preserving?

C6. Is the target the preservation of this specific construction
    or existence of some admissible amalgam?

Do not infer C2-C4 from the quotient-descent condition alone.

A useful next test is to add one explicit quotient-level constraint
at a time and determine whether the free quotient amalgam preserves
it. If not, search for another quotient-level amalgam before
claiming nonexistence of all strong amalgams.


============================================================
10. RELATION TO S3.12 AND S3.18
============================================================

S3.12 applies to a specified relational pullback P^pi and gives
a fiber-capacity criterion for embedding a finite forbidden
relational pattern into that pullback.

S3.18 identified the exact P2.3 apartness pullback:

    x #_P y
    IFF
    pi(x) != pi(y).

S3.19 adds a controlled class of extra relations for which the
P2.3 carrier can be expanded by quotient pullback without losing
the input structures.

This does not turn every arbitrary P2.4.1 free expansion into
a pullback. The counterexample in Section 4 explicitly rules
out that identification.

The S3.12 fiber-capacity criterion can be applied to a specified
pullback expansion in this fragment, but it does not by itself
classify all possible quotient-level constraints or all possible
amalgams in richer classes.

S3.19 RESULT:
A positive strong-amalgamation theorem is proved for the explicit
class of quotient-invariant pure-relational expansions of Boolean
apartness structures.

KEY CORRECTION:
Do not use the free union on the original carrier when quotient
invariance is required. Use quotient descent, quotient-level
amalgamation, and pullback.

SCOPE:
The theorem is conditional on the stated pure-relational and
quotient-invariance assumptions. It is not a theorem about all
OMEGA-ISOP operational or realization structures.
