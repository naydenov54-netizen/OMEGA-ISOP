2. SOURCE FACTS
============================================================

2.1 Objects and embeddings

Volume II, Section III.4, represents a local configuration as:
C = (S_C,#_C)
where:
S_C subset U0
and:
#_C = # restricted to S_C x S_C.

It states |S_C| >= 2 for a minimal nontrivial configuration and says exact admissibility conditions remain realization-dependent.

Volume II, Section III.5, defines an embedding f:C1 -> C2 as injective and preserving and reflecting apartness:
x #1 y iff f(x) #2 f(y).

Volume II describes C_METACORE as a category of admissible configurations whose morphisms preserve selected structural properties. It does not fully specify the selected property set or state that every embedding is necessarily a C_METACORE morphism.

Volume III, Section 6, gives a nontriviality test requiring both:
|S_C| >= 2
and
there exist x,y in S_C such that x #_C y.

This is a stated nontriviality test. It must not be silently generalized into the complete admissibility predicate for every realization.

2.2 Compatibility and amalgamation

Volume II, Section IV.1, states:
CC(C1,C2)=1 iff an admissible amalgamation of C1 and C2 exists;
otherwise CC(C1,C2)=0.

The source does not, in that definition, specify:
- a shared base object C0;
- the two span morphisms f1:C0 -> C1 and f2:C0 -> C2;
- whether the amalgam is required to be over a designated common subconfiguration;
- whether embeddings into the amalgam must be injective maps in C_METACORE;
- whether the amalgam carrier may be abstract or must be a subset of the same U0;
- the complete admissibility predicate for the amalgam object.

The standard categorical amalgamation property is a property of spans. For a specified span
C0 --f1--> C1
C0 --f2--> C2,
an amalgam consists of an admissible object M and morphisms
j1:C1 -> M
j2:C2 -> M
such that:
j1 o f1 = j2 o f2.

If CC(C1,C2) is intended to quantify over a particular common base, that base and its maps must be part of the input or be canonically determined by an explicit rule. If it instead means that C1 and C2 merely admit embeddings into some common object, that is a different, weaker joint-embedding-type condition. The source text inspected does not settle this distinction.

2.3 Categorical completeness

Volume II, Sections IV.2-IV.3, makes R := colim(D_CC) conditional on a valid compatibility diagram, the relevant categorical structure, and existence of the colimit or an equivalent constraint-preserving construction.

Volume III, Sections 8-9, explicitly says D_CC alone is not a complete categorical construction. It requires:
- an indexing category J;
- a functor D:J -> C_METACORE;
- relevant objects and morphisms;
- compatibility conditions;
- the required universal property.

Therefore a Boolean value CC(C1,C2) is not, by itself, a categorical diagram or a colimit specification.

3. RECONCILIATION OF EXISTING CATEGORY TESTS
============================================================

The existing records define distinct test categories. Their results must remain scoped to those definitions.

A. Abstract category C_EQ-EMB

Objects: sets equipped with an equivalence relation.
Morphisms: injective functions preserving and reflecting equivalence.

The S2-04C-04 record proves the amalgamation property for spans in this abstract category by constructing a pushout carrier in Set and equipping it with the equivalence closure of the images of the source relations.

This does not prove that the constructed object is a subset of a previously fixed U0. It also does not prove that the same object is a pushout in the category whose morphisms are restricted to embeddings.

B. Fixed-carrier inclusion-only category P_F

Objects: selected admissible induced subconfigurations C_S of one fixed (U0,#).
Morphisms: literal inclusions only, S subseteq T.

The union-colimit model proves:
If the diagram is a functor into P_F and the union of its carriers is an admissible object, then the union is its colimit in P_F.

This is a valid conditional theorem in P_F. The source explicitly does not identify P_F as the intended C_METACORE.

C. Fixed-carrier all-embeddings category E_F / C_U0-EMB

Objects: the declared admissible induced subconfigurations of one fixed carrier.
Morphisms: all injective maps preserving and reflecting the selected apartness/equivalence relation.

S2-04C-06 gives an explicit finite realization in which:
- the inclusion-only category has the union candidate as a pushout;
- the all-embeddings category has the same candidate as an amalgam but not a pushout;
- the stronger two-case argument establishes that no pushout exists for that particular span in that particular finite E_F test category.

This is a scoped finite result. It does not identify E_F with C_METACORE and does not establish that project-level R fails to exist.

D. Fixed-carrier automorphism-restriction category C_U0-AUT

The earlier S2-04C-03 / S2-04C-04 records consider morphisms obtained by restricting global automorphisms of the ambient structure. The stated amalgamation construction uses M=U0 and depends on U0 itself being an admissible object. This category is another distinct test option, not a source-established choice for C_METACORE.

============================================================
4. MATHEMATICAL FINDINGS
============================================================

FINDING 1 — MORPHISM CLASS IS MATHEMATICALLY MATERIAL

The finite comparison in S2-04C-06 demonstrates that changing only the morphism class can change pushout existence/status while retaining the same finite carrier, ambient relation, admissible test-object family, and span.

Therefore the unresolved morphism choice is not merely terminological.

FINDING 2 — ABSTRACT AND FIXED-CARRIER AMALGAMS ARE DISTINCT

An abstract pushout carrier constructed from a disjoint union modulo identifications is not automatically a subset of U0. Fixed-carrier realizability requires a separate construction or a proof that an appropriate induced subconfiguration of U0 exists.

FINDING 3 — WEAK AMALGAMATION DOES NOT IMPLY PUSHOUT

The finite records exhibit a common amalgam while the pushout universal property fails in the all-embeddings test category. The universal property quantifies over every compatible cocone in the chosen category. A single valid counter-cocone whose forced factorization is not a morphism defeats the proposed pushout.

FINDING 4 — CC IS UNDER-SPECIFIED AS AN INPUT TO D_CC

The binary predicate CC(C1,C2) records an existence assertion. It does not provide the witness amalgam, the embeddings, the common base/span where one is intended, the morphism class, or the arrows and composition laws of J.

A construction of D_CC therefore needs more data than the Boolean compatibility predicate alone.

FINDING 5 — OBJECT ADMISSIBILITY AND CLOSURE ARE ALSO OPEN

Even after choosing a morphism class, one must specify which configurations are objects and prove that proposed amalgam or union objects remain admissible. The cardinality test |S_C| >= 2 does not alone guarantee an apartness witness. Volume III's witness condition is a nontriviality test, while Volume II leaves exact admissibility realization-dependent.
