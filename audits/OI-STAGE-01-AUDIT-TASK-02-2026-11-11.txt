1. PURPOSE
Record the results of Stage 1 Audit Task 02, continuing the candidate signature-based Difference formalization. This checkpoint records mathematical consequences of the stated candidate definition only. It does not establish that the definition is canonical for OMEGA-ISOP or entailed by the Frozen Core.

2. TYPE-CORRECT FORMALIZATION
Let X be a set and I an index set. For each i∈I, let Y_i be a set equipped with equality, and let f_i:X→Y_i.
Define the signature map:
σ:X→∏_{i∈I}Y_i
by σ(x)=(f_i(x))_{i∈I}.
Define D:X×X→{0,1} by:
D(x,y)=1 iff ∃i∈I: f_i(x)≠f_i(y).
D(x,y)=0 iff ∀i∈I: f_i(x)=f_i(y).
Equivalently:
D(x,y)=1 iff σ(x)≠σ(y).
D(x,y)=0 iff σ(x)=σ(y).

The codomains Y_i need not be identical. The product over an empty index set is the singleton consisting of the empty function.

3. EDGE CASES
3.1 Empty I:
If I=∅, the existential condition is false and the universal condition is true. Thus D(x,y)=0 for all x,y∈X. Every state has the same empty signature. This is valid and non-contradictory.

3.2 Finite I:
The definition and proofs remain valid for finite feature families, including a finite family with zero features.

3.3 Infinite I:
The definition and proofs remain valid for arbitrary set-sized I. No finiteness assumption is required; the definition uses an existential witness for D=1 and pointwise equality for D=0.

3.4 Non-injective signature:
Define x~y iff σ(x)=σ(y), equivalently D(x,y)=0. Equality of signatures makes ~ an equivalence relation. Its equivalence classes are the sets of states indistinguishable by the declared feature family.

4. SIGNATURE SEPARATION THEOREM
The following are equivalent:
(a) σ is injective.
(b) For all x,y∈X, D(x,y)=0 implies x=y.
(c) For all x,y∈X, D(x,y)=0 iff x=y.
(d) For all x,y∈X, D(x,y)=1 iff x≠y.

Proof:
(a)⇒(b): D(x,y)=0 gives σ(x)=σ(y), and injectivity gives x=y.
(b)⇒(a): σ(x)=σ(y) is equivalent to D(x,y)=0, so x=y.
The equivalences with (c) and (d) follow from the definition and classical logic.

Terminology boundary:
“Complete” or “separating” means injective relative to the identity relation on the declared carrier X. This does not mean that the signature contains every physically, semantically, or ontologically significant feature. Such completeness requires an independently specified target criterion.

5. METRIC CONSEQUENCE
For all x,y,z∈X, D satisfies:
(i) D(x,y)≥0.
(ii) D(x,y)=D(y,x).
(iii) D(x,x)=0.
(iv) D(x,z)≤D(x,y)+D(y,z).

For (iv), if D(x,z)=0 the inequality is immediate. If D(x,z)=1, choose i with f_i(x)≠f_i(z). It cannot be the case that both f_i(x)=f_i(y) and f_i(y)=f_i(z); hence at least one of D(x,y), D(y,z) is 1, and their sum is at least 1.

Therefore D is a pseudometric on X. It is a metric on X exactly when σ is injective. Equivalently, it is the pullback along σ of the discrete metric on the signature image σ(X).

This corrects the earlier tentative statement that the triangle inequality had not been established. The finite example showing non-transitivity of positive difference does not violate the triangle inequality.

6. POSITIVE DIFFERENCE IS NOT TRANSITIVE
For X={a,b,c}, with one feature f(a)=0, f(b)=1, f(c)=0:
D(a,b)=1, D(b,c)=1, D(a,c)=0.
Thus D-positive distinction is not transitive. Cotransitivity remains valid.

7. LOGICAL FRAMEWORK
The cotransitivity proof is valid in standard classical logic. In a constructive setting, the step from the negation of simultaneous equalities to a disjunction of inequalities is not automatic when inequality is defined merely as negated equality. A constructive proof may require decidable equality in the relevant Y_i, or an explicit apartness/witness structure. The present status is therefore: PROVED UNDER CLASSICAL LOGIC.

8. RELATION UNDERDETERMINATION
Let X={a,b}, with D(a,b)=D(b,a)=1. The following distinct directed relations are all compatible with the same D-positive pair data:
R1={(a,b)}
R2={(b,a)}
R3={(a,b),(b,a)}
Thus D does not uniquely determine an oriented binary relation. More generally, D fixes symmetric distinguishability information, but it does not select direction, causation, temporal order, interaction, or a unique additional relation without extra structure or a selection rule.

This establishes non-uniqueness for the candidate definition. It does not refute the possibility that a richer OMEGA-ISOP construction, with additional premises, defines a particular relation.
