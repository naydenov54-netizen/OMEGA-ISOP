PURPOSE
Develop OMEGA-ISOP into a more understandable, testable, and useful hypothetical theory by giving each of its six named stages an explicit mathematical meaning and testing every proposed transition. The sequence is:
Difference → Relation → Constraint → Dynamics → Persistence → Organization

The arrows are research questions, not assumed theorems. A result at one stage does not automatically establish the next stage.

STAGE 1 — DIFFERENCE
Question: What formal data make two states distinguishable?
Required deliverables: carrier X; feature/signature map or primitive distinction predicate; exact axioms; examples and counterexamples; test of whether distinctions are observable, structural, or stipulated.
Exit criterion: definitions are unambiguous and their claimed properties are proved.

STAGE 2 — RELATION
Question: Under what additional rule does a distinction support a relation between states?
Required deliverables: typed relation R⊆X×X or a richer relational structure; directionality, reflexivity, symmetry, transitivity, and composition conditions explicitly tested.
Exit criterion: prove which relations follow from the difference data and identify which require extra structure.

STAGE 3 — CONSTRAINT
Question: Which configurations or relations are admissible?
Required deliverables: constraint predicate C, constraint set, or admissibility operator; satisfiability test; examples of allowed and forbidden configurations; consistency and independence tests.
Exit criterion: demonstrate that the constraints are well-defined and non-vacuous.

STAGE 4 — DYNAMICS
Question: What rule, if any, maps one admissible state to another?
Required deliverables: state space, transition relation or operator, domain/codomain, existence and uniqueness conditions where relevant, and explicit assumptions about time or iteration.
Exit criterion: prove the stated dynamical properties; do not infer dynamics merely from static relations or constraints.

STAGE 5 — PERSISTENCE
Question: Under which dynamics and conditions does a state, property, relation, or invariant survive?
Required deliverables: a precise persistence predicate or invariant; a theorem linking it to the specified dynamics; counterexamples showing the limits of the theorem.
Exit criterion: persistence is proved relative to explicit update rules and assumptions, not asserted as a universal consequence.

STAGE 6 — ORGANIZATION
Question: When do persistent structures form a higher-level, non-arbitrary organization?
Required deliverables: a formal organization criterion, composition/coarse-graining rule, stability or coherence conditions, and tests distinguishing genuine organization from mere aggregation.
Exit criterion: prove the criterion's consequences and provide at least one worked model where it is satisfied and one where it is not.

CROSS-STAGE PROOF RULE
For each arrow A→B, provide:
1. precise hypotheses;
2. a construction of B from A, or a theorem showing the implication;
3. a proof;
4. a test case;
5. a counterexample or explicit boundary of applicability;
6. a statement of all external assumptions.
If B requires information not contained in A, classify the arrow as conditional and list the additional input. Do not describe it as a derivation from A alone.

EVIDENCE LABELS
PROVED: follows from stated definitions and premises by a checked argument.
TESTED: evaluated on stated examples; testing alone is not proof.
CONDITIONAL: proved only under explicitly listed additional assumptions.
HYPOTHESIS: proposed interpretation awaiting proof or decisive tests.
REJECTED: failed a stated test or contradicted by a counterexample.
INTEGRATED: accepted into the working theory only after the proof audit and explicit change-control step. A mathematically correct result is not automatically integrated into Frozen Core.

WORKING METHOD
- One stage at a time, in the sequence above.
- Prefer small explicit models before general claims.
- Separate definitions from theorems and interpretations.
- Search for counterexamples early.
- Maintain a result ledger: claim, assumptions, proof, tests, limitations, status.
- Preserve valid earlier mathematics without allowing unrelated speculative branches to dominate the program.
- Do not alter Frozen Core, register results, or claim canonical acceptance without the applicable explicit procedure.
