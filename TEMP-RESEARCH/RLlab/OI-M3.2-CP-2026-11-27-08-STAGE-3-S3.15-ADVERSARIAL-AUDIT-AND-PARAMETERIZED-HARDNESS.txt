1. PURPOSE
============================================================

This checkpoint attempts to identify the weakest point in the S3.12-S3.14 chain.

The audit separates three questions:

(A) Is there a mathematical error in the exact fiber-capacity theorem?
(B) Does the complexity analysis hide a stronger computational obstruction?
(C) Are the results being used to support conclusions that they do not establish?

The audit is adversarial. A limitation is not to be mislabeled as a counterexample to a theorem.

============================================================
2. AUDIT OF THE S3.12 PROOF
============================================================

S3.12 states:

F embeds as an induced substructure of P^pi
iff
there exists an induced relational map h:F->Q such that
|h^(-1)({q})| <= |pi^(-1)({q})|
for every q in h(F).

The proof was checked in both directions.

NECESSITY:
Given an induced embedding e:F->P^pi, define h=pi o e.
The pullback definition transfers the full positive-and-negative relation equivalence from e to h.
Injectivity of e gives an injection from each h-fiber into the corresponding pi-fiber.

SUFFICIENCY:
Given h and the pointwise capacity inequalities, choose an injection from each finite h-fiber into its corresponding pi-fiber.
The pi-fibers are pairwise disjoint, so these injections combine into an injective map e:F->P.
Since pi o e=h, every relation equivalence transfers from h to e by the pullback definition.

AUDIT RESULT:
No logical gap was found in this proof under the stated assumptions:
- F is finite;
- the signature is purely relational;
- relation arities are finite;
- all relation tuples, including repeated-coordinate tuples, are checked;
- induced maps preserve and reflect relations;
- the pullback is defined exactly by pi.

This is not a proof that the theorem applies outside those assumptions.

============================================================
3. AUDIT OF THE S3.13 THRESHOLD
============================================================

For uniform m-fold fibers, S3.12 reduces to:

F embeds into Q^(m)
iff
there exists an induced relational map h:F->Q with c(h)<=m.

Taking the minimum collision number gives the S3.13 threshold.

The two implications are direct:
- an embedding projects to an induced map with at most m preimages per quotient point;
- an induced map with collision number at most m can be lifted by assigning distinct copy indices within each fiber.

AUDIT RESULT:
No counterexample or proof defect was found under the stated purely relational, finite-pattern assumptions.

============================================================
4. S3.14 HAS A STRONGER PARAMETERIZED OBSTRUCTION
============================================================

S3.14 establishes:
- an XP-style exhaustive upper bound O(|Q|^|F| * poly(input));
- NP membership;
- NP-completeness already at m=1 for finite simple undirected graphs.

The NP-hardness reduction can be made explicit and also yields parameterized hardness.

THEOREM S3.15-A — W[1]-HARDNESS PARAMETERIZED BY |F|.

Consider FIBER-THRESHOLD on finite simple undirected graphs, parameterized by n=|F|.

Reduce from k-CLIQUE.

Given a graph G and integer k>=2, construct:
F = K_k,
Q = G,
m = 1.

Then:

kappa_(K_k)(G) <= 1
iff
there exists an induced relational map K_k->G with every fiber of size at most 1
iff
there exists an injective induced map K_k->G
iff
G contains a k-clique.

The construction is polynomial, and the parameter |F| equals k.

Since k-CLIQUE is W[1]-complete, FIBER-THRESHOLD is W[1]-hard when parameterized by |F|.

COROLLARY:
The exhaustive O(|Q|^|F| * poly(input)) algorithm is an XP upper bound in parameter |F|, not an FPT algorithm.

Unless FPT=W[1], no algorithm of the form:
f(|F|) * poly(|F|+|Q|)
exists for the unrestricted graph problem.

No W[1]-membership claim is made here. Therefore the result is W[1]-hardness, not W[1]-completeness.

============================================================
5. TREEWIDTH ALONE DOES NOT REMOVE THE HARDNESS
============================================================

For the same reduction, the pattern is F=K_k.

Its treewidth is:
tw(F)=k-1.

Thus the reduction also preserves the parameter up to a shift of one.

Consequently, the unrestricted problem is W[1]-hard when parameterized by the treewidth of F alone.

This rules out a general FPT algorithm parameterized only by tw(F), unless FPT=W[1].

IMPORTANT LIMIT:
This does NOT prove hardness when treewidth is bounded by a fixed constant.
It does NOT rule out polynomial-time algorithms for every fixed treewidth bound.
Those are separate questions and require separate analysis.

============================================================
6. PARAMETER m ALONE IS NOT A TRACTABILITY PARAMETER
============================================================

The problem is NP-hard already for the fixed value m=1.

Therefore an algorithm running in:
f(m) * poly(input size)

would, at m=1, give a polynomial-time algorithm for an NP-hard problem.

Hence parameterization by m alone is para-NP-hard, unless P=NP.

This is a stronger warning than merely saying that the direct enumeration is expensive.

============================================================
7. FIXED-PATTERN TRACTABILITY REMAINS VALID BUT NONUNIFORM
============================================================

For each fixed F, the enumeration over maps F->Q is polynomial in |Q|.

However, the polynomial degree depends on |F|.

This is a nonuniform family of polynomial-time algorithms indexed by fixed patterns. It does not imply a single polynomial-time algorithm when F varies.

For a fixed finite forbidden family, the finite minimum over its patterns is also polynomial-time computable in Q.

These positive results remain valid and are not contradicted by W[1]-hardness of the variable-pattern problem.

============================================================
8. THE MORE SERIOUS LOGICAL SCOPE GAP: PULLBACK IS NOT AMALGAM EXISTENCE
============================================================

S3.12-S3.14 characterize one specific construction:
the relational pullback P^pi.

They do not characterize all possible amalgams of the same span.

In particular:

P^pi not in K

does NOT imply:

there exists no amalgam D in K.

S3.10 already supplies the relevant warning:
- the quotient pullback contains the forbidden universal three-point structure;
- the free relational amalgam omits the mixed cross-relations and remains in K.

Thus the same span can have:
- a quotient pullback outside K;
- another amalgam inside K.

This is not a flaw in S3.12. It is a strict boundary on what S3.12 can prove.

Any later argument that infers failure of amalgamation, failure of strong amalgamation, or nonexistence of every admissible amalgam solely from failure of P^pi membership is invalid unless it supplies an additional theorem.

============================================================
9. THE ACTUAL OMEGA-ISOP TRANSFER TEST
============================================================

Before using S3.12-S3.14 as a general OMEGA-ISOP result, the following bridge must be proved.

BRIDGE REQUIREMENTS:

1. The target OMEGA-ISOP structures must be representable in the purely relational signature used by the theorem.

2. Every additional axiom defining the intended class must be included or separately shown to be preserved by the pullback.

3. The relevant morphisms must match the preservation-and-reflection conditions used in the definition of induced relational map.

4. Any functions, constants, operations, dynamics, or higher-order constraints must be handled separately. They are not covered merely because the relational reduct satisfies S3.12.

5. The specific quotient map pi and pullback used by the OMEGA-ISOP construction must be shown to be the same construction as the abstract theorem's pullback.

Until this bridge is established, S3.12 is a correct abstract theorem with conditional applicability, not a universal theorem about every OMEGA-ISOP realization.

10. CURRENT ACHILLES-HEEL ASSESSMENT
============================================================

NO PROOF REFUTATION:
No counterexample to S3.12 or S3.13 and no logical defect in their stated proofs has been found in this audit.

ALGORITHMIC WEAKNESS:
The unrestricted variable-pattern problem is W[1]-hard parameterized by |F| and by tw(F) alone. Parameter m alone is para-NP-hard.

LOGICAL WEAKNESS:
The results concern membership of one particular pullback in a forbidden class. They do not settle existence of some other amalgam.

ARCHITECTURAL WEAKNESS:
The transfer from the purely relational theorem to the full OMEGA-ISOP structure has not been established by S3.12-S3.14 themselves.

The latter two are the strongest current attack points. The algorithmic result is a genuine complexity barrier, but it is not a mathematical contradiction.
