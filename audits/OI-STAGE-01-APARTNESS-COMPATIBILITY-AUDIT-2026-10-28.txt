1. CANDIDATE DIFFERENCE PREDICATE
Let X be a set, I a feature index set, and f_i:X→V_i feature maps. Define
D(x,y)=1 iff there exists i∈I such that f_i(x)≠f_i(y).
Equivalently, let F:X→∏_{i∈I}V_i be the combined signature map. Then D(x,y)=1 iff F(x)≠F(y).

The zero relation E(x,y) iff D(x,y)=0 is equality of signatures. It is an equivalence relation: reflexive, symmetric, and transitive. D is its complement. In classical set-theoretic logic, D is irreflexive and symmetric; the witness-feature argument also proves cotransitivity. D need not be transitive. If F is not injective, D(x,y)=0 can hold for distinct x,y. If F is injective, D(x,y)=0 iff x=y.

2. COMPARISON WITH VOLUME III APARTNESS
The available Volume III reconstruction states that a carrier X may be equipped with an apartness relation #, but does not specify the axioms of # in that section. It gives the nontriviality condition |X|≥2 and existence of x,y with x#y. That condition alone does not determine the intended apartness axioms or identify # with D.

Conditional result:
- D has irreflexivity, symmetry, and cotransitivity under the stated signature-based definition.
- These properties make D compatible with some apartness-style axiom systems, but do not establish equivalence with the project's intended #.
- Whether D satisfies the project's intended apartness specification remains OPEN until the exact defining axioms and relevant source text are available and checked.
- In particular, the signature's identity-of-indiscernibles property depends on injectivity of F; it must not be silently assumed.

3. COMPARISON WITH VOLUME III COMPATIBILITY
Volume III describes compatibility as relative to a model and its specified constraints, and explicitly says it is not a universal causal relation. This is a model-relative constraint-satisfaction notion, not the same definition as D or #.

No implication between D(x,y) and compatibility is established by the available text. Two objects can be distinguishable yet compatible under a model's constraints; conversely, the word “compatible” alone does not imply that two objects have equal signatures. Any relationship would need a separately stated model, domains, and constraint predicate.
