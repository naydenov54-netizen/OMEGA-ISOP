II. FOUNDATIONAL FORMAL CONVENTION
The relational structure R is not identified directly with a unique physical or mathematical evolution.
A realization map is introduced:
Φ : Real(R) → M
where:
R = relational constraint structure;
Real(R) = admissible mathematical realizations of R;
M = selected mathematical model or state-space realization.
For a fixed, explicitly declared realization Φ:
A(R;Φ) ⊆ Evol(X)
denotes the class of evolution laws on X compatible with the realized relational structure.
When Φ is fixed and unambiguous, the shorthand
A(R) := A(R;Φ)
may be used.
Without a fixed realization, A(R;Φ) must be retained.
The relation
R → A(R;Φ)
means constraint on admissible dynamics.
It does not mean:
R → unique dynamics.
In general:
|A(R;Φ)| may be greater than 1.
It is also possible that:
A(R;Φ) = ∅.
In that case the selected realization is dynamically incompatible with the relational constraints.
A universal principle selecting one element of A(R;Φ) from R alone is not established.
Therefore:
R ↛ unique realized dynamics.

III. PRIMITIVE RELATIONAL FOUNDATION
III.1 Primitive Difference
Primitive Difference, PD, is a meta-level formal starting condition.
PD is not itself:
an entity;
a physical object;
a process;
a relation;
a causal mechanism.
PD establishes the admissibility of formal distinction.
A formal carrier is then introduced:
U0
together with an apartness structure:
A = (U0,#)
where:
# : U0 × U0 → {true,false}
and
x # y
means that x and y are formally apart under the selected realization.
III.2 Apartness Structure
A standard example of an apartness relation satisfies:
irreflexivity:
not(x # x)
symmetry:
x # y iff y # x
cotransitivity:
x # y implies (x # z) or (z # y)
for all z.
These axioms are one admissible realization of apartness.
They are not asserted as the only possible realization.
The distinction between PD and # must be preserved:
PD = meta-level admissibility of distinction.
# = formal realization of apartness on U0.
A witness of apartness is therefore a formal object within the selected mathematical realization and is not automatically a physical causal mechanism.
III.3 Indistinguishability
Define:
x ~ y
iff no admissible apartness witness separates x and y.
Under a complete apartness realization this may be represented as:
x ~ y iff not(x # y).
Under the standard apartness assumptions above, ~ can be shown to satisfy the equivalence-relation properties.
Accordingly:
x ~ x;
x ~ y implies y ~ x;
x ~ y and y ~ z imply x ~ z.
The quotient structure
DS := U0 / ~
is therefore derived when ~ is an equivalence relation.
DS is not a primitive structure.
Its construction depends on the formal properties of the selected apartness realization.
III.4 Local Configurations
A local configuration is represented as:
C = (S_C,#_C)
where:
S_C ⊆ U0
and:
#_C = # restricted to S_C × S_C.
A minimal nontrivial configuration satisfies:
|S_C| ≥ 2.
The exact admissibility conditions remain realization-dependent.
The set of admissible configurations may be represented as a category:
C_METACORE
whose objects are admissible configurations and whose morphisms preserve the selected structural properties.
III.5 Configuration Embeddings
For configurations
C1 = (S1,#1)
and
C2 = (S2,#2),
an embedding
f : C1 → C2
is injective and preserves and reflects the selected apartness structure:
x #1 y iff f(x) #2 f(y).
The "if and only if" condition is essential.
Preservation alone does not establish structural reflection.
An embedding therefore represents structural correspondence within the chosen mathematical realization, not ontological identity.

IV. COMPATIBILITY AND RELATIONAL SPECIFICATION
IV.1 Compatibility
For configurations C1 and C2 define:
CC(C1,C2) = 1
iff an admissible amalgamation of C1 and C2 exists.
Otherwise:
CC(C1,C2) = 0.
The binary form is a minimal representation of compatibility.
It is not claimed to be the unique possible representation.
Compatibility is therefore a structural admissibility relation, not a causal relation.
IV.2 Compatibility Diagram
Let:
D_CC
denote the diagram generated by the selected configurations and their admissible compatibility relations.
Where the required categorical construction exists, define:
R := colim(D_CC).
The resulting R is a relational constraint structure generated from the compatibility structure.
The colimit, when it exists, is unique up to the relevant categorical equivalence.
This does not mean that there is a unique canonical structural representation in every formalism.
Structural order is not necessarily canonical structural order.
IV.3 Existence Boundary
The construction of R requires:
a valid compatibility diagram;
the relevant categorical structure;
existence of the required colimit or an equivalent constraint-preserving construction.
If these conditions fail, R is not automatically defined by the construction above.
Therefore:
R existence is conditional.
No universal theorem of existence of R across all mathematical domains is asserted.

V. REALIZATION AND DYNAMICS
V.1 Realization Space
A relational structure R may admit multiple realizations.
For example:
R → graph structures
R → state spaces
R → algebraic structures
R → computational models.
The mapping
Φ : Real(R) → M
therefore represents a realization choice rather than a unique interpretation.
Different realizations may produce different state spaces, observables, and dynamics while preserving selected relational constraints.
V.2 Realized State Space
Given a realization with state space X, define:
X_R = {x ∈ X | x satisfies the constraints induced by R}.
The realization is dynamically admissible only if there exists at least one compatible evolution:
A(R;Φ) ≠ ∅.
If:
A(R;Φ) = ∅,
the selected realization is dynamically incompatible with R.
This is a failure of the selected realization, not a contradiction of R itself.
V.3 Deterministic Dynamics
A deterministic evolution is:
T : X → X
with:
x_(t+1) = T(x_t).
When:
T ∈ A(R;Φ),
the evolution is compatible with the relational constraints in the selected realization.
A reversible realization may be represented by:
T ∈ Aut(X,R).
An irreversible compatibility-preserving evolution may be represented by an appropriate endomorphism class:
T ∈ End(X,R).
V.4 Stochastic Dynamics
A stochastic evolution is represented by a Markov kernel:
κ : X × Σ_X → [0,1]
such that for each x ∈ X:
κ(x,·)
is a probability measure, and for each measurable E:
x ↦ κ(x,E)
is measurable.
The transition law therefore satisfies the standard kernel requirements.
A deterministic map T can be represented by the Dirac kernel:
κ_T(x,E) = δ_{T(x)}(E).
Equivalently, the transition probability is concentrated at T(x).
V.5 Dynamic Selection Boundary
The relational structure constrains admissible dynamics.
It does not select a unique dynamic law unless an independent selection principle is supplied.
Therefore:
R → A(R;Φ)
does not imply:
|A(R;Φ)| = 1.
Dynamic selection remains an open problem.
Selection is not unique selection.
A realization may be compatible with multiple dynamics.
A compatible dynamic is not thereby the uniquely realized dynamic.

VI. OPERATORS AND INVARIANT STRUCTURES
VI.1 Observable Space
Let B be a Banach space of observables:
f : X → Y
with the required norm and closure properties.
For deterministic T, define the Koopman operator:
K_T f = f ∘ T.
K_T is well-defined on B when:
f ∘ T ∈ B
for every f ∈ B.
Its linearity follows from:
K_T(af + bg) = aK_T f + bK_T g.
For composition:
K_(T2∘T1) = K_T1 K_T2.
The order reversal is a consequence of operator composition.
VI.2 Markov Operator
For a Markov kernel κ, define the corresponding operator:
K_κ f(x) = ∫ f(y) κ(x,dy).
The deterministic case is recovered through the Dirac kernel.
VI.3 Invariant Measures
A probability measure ρ* is invariant under κ when:
ρ* κ = ρ*.
For a deterministic map T this reduces to:
ρ*(T^(-1)(E)) = ρ*(E)
for measurable E.
Existence of an invariant measure is not automatic.
An invariant structure must therefore be established under explicit assumptions.
Invariance is not equivalent to persistence.
Invariance is not equivalent to robustness.
Invariance does not establish ontology.
VI.4 Perturbation and Response
For a perturbed evolution:
T_ε = T + εΔT + O(ε²).
For an observable f and invariant measure μ*_ε define:
R_f(ε) = μ*_ε(f) - μ*_0(f).
When differentiability exists, a response coefficient may be represented as:
Λ_f = dR_f(ε)/dε |_(ε=0).
The existence and regularity of such derivatives require independent assumptions.
Resolvent methods, spectral analysis, Koopman analysis, Markov operators, and pseudospectral analysis are mathematical tools for analysis of realized dynamics.
They are not primitive elements of the OMEGA-ISOP core.

VII. PERSISTENCE, OPERATIONAL RESPONSE, AND ORGANIZATION
VII.1 Persistence
Persistence concerns the continuation of identity-relevant properties over an operational horizon.
A property is operationally persistent when the relevant response class remains identifiable under the declared transitions and admissible perturbations.
Persistence is therefore criterion-relative.
Persistence does not require exact state invariance.
Persistence does not require robustness in every metric.
Persistence does not require an invariant measure.
A persistent organization may therefore exist in a dynamically changing state.
VII.2 Invariance, Persistence, and Robustness
These concepts must remain distinct.
Invariance:
a mathematical object or property is preserved under a specified transformation or evolution.
Persistence:
identity-relevant operational properties remain within the selected response class over the operational horizon.
Robustness:
the response changes within a specified tolerance under perturbation.
For example:
||R(ε) - R(0)|| ≤ Cε
is a robustness statement under specified assumptions.
It is not by itself a persistence theorem.
It is not by itself an organization theorem.
VII.3 Operational Response Classes
Let:
L_op
be an operational language.
Let:
Resp_M : L_op → Y
be the response map of model M.
The relevant response class consists of models or states that remain equivalent under the selected operational criterion.
This provides the bridge from realized dynamics to operational identity.
VII.4 Operational Organization
Operational organization is defined relative to an explicitly selected organizational criterion.
A relational dynamical structure qualifies as operationally organized when it maintains an operationally identifiable response class under specified admissible perturbations and operational conditions.
A candidate organization may involve:
distinguishable internal states or configurations;

compatibility;
admissible dynamics;
persistence of identity-relevant response classes;
operational distinguishability;
response stability;

These are not all universal necessary conditions in every possible organizational criterion.
In particular, functional closure is not a universal requirement of operational organization.
Organization is not inferred from dynamical activity alone.
Organization is defined by maintained relational coherence under explicit operational conditions.
VII.5 Candidate Formal Representation
A candidate operational organization may be represented by a tuple such as:
O_org = (X_org,R_org,A_org,P_org,Resp_org,Id_org)

VII.6 Functional Closure

VIII. OPERATIONAL IDENTITY AND RESPONSE GEOMETRY
VIII.1 Operational Language
Let:
L_op
be an operational language.
For a model M:
Resp_M : L_op → Y.
Let Y be normed.
Let:
G
be an optional transformation class.
For tolerance ε ≥ 0 define:
M1 ~_(L_op,ε,G) M2
iff:
||Resp_(g(M1))(a) - Resp_(g(M2))(a)|| ≤ ε
for all:
g ∈ G
and:
a ∈ L_op
whenever the responses are defined.
VIII.2 Exact and Approximate Identity
For:
ε = 0,
the relation represents exact operational equivalence under the selected language and transformation class.
For:
ε > 0,
the relation is generally not automatically transitive with the same ε.
A two-step comparison can yield a bound of:
2ε.
Therefore approximate operational equivalence must not be silently treated as an exact equivalence relation.
Operational identity is criterion-relative.
It is not ontological identity.
VIII.3 Operational Distance
Define:
D_L(M1,M2) = sup_(a∈L_op) ||Resp_M1(a) - Resp_M2(a)||_Y.
When well-defined, D_L is an extended pseudometric.
If:
D_L(M1,M2) = 0,
the models are operationally indistinguishable under L_op.
This does not establish ontological identity.
For tolerance ε:
D_L(M1,M2) ≤ ε
represents approximate operational indistinguishability.
D_L itself does not depend on ε or G unless those are explicitly incorporated into the definition of the operational language or distance.
VIII.4 Response Stability
For a perturbed system, response stability may be studied using:

and its derivative or appropriate finite-difference bounds.
Spectral and resolvent quantities may characterize transient amplification or sensitivity.
Non-normality can generate transient amplification.

VIII.5 Operational Response Non-Expansion
THEOREM 91
OPERATIONAL RESPONSE DISTANCE NON-EXPANSION UNDER RELATIONAL DYNAMICS
Let:
H1: R exists and T is admissible, with R preserved under T.
H2: Y is a normed space.
H3: there exists a map
τ : L_op → L_op
such that:
Resp_(T(M))(a) = Resp_M(τ(a)).
H4: the operational criterion and comparison class are fixed.
Then:
D_L(T(M1),T(M2)) ≤ D_L(M1,M2).
Proof:
For every a ∈ L_op:
||Resp_(T(M1))(a) - Resp_(T(M2))(a)||
= ||Resp_M1(τ(a)) - Resp_M2(τ(a))||
≤ D_L(M1,M2).
Taking the supremum over a gives:
D_L(T(M1),T(M2)) ≤ D_L(M1,M2).
No surjectivity of τ is required.
The theorem does not establish universal information conservation.
It establishes a conditional non-expansion property under the stated response-map compatibility.
STATUS: CONDITIONAL THEOREM.
