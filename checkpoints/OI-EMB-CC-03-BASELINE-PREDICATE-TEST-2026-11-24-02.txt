============================================================
2. BASELINE PREDICATE
============================================================

Fix an ambient category C.

For a functor Delta:J->C and a subcategory W<=C, define:

Suff_0(Delta,W) iff Delta factors through the inclusion W->C.

Equivalently, W contains every object and every morphism in the image of Delta.

No additional admissibility condition is imposed in this baseline model.

Define the generated witness:

Gen(Delta) = the smallest subcategory of C through which Delta factors.

Then Gen(Delta) is a subcategory witness and is contained in every subcategory witness for Delta.

Therefore Gen(Delta) is the unique least witness under Suff_0, and hence is also the unique minimal witness.

Important: Gen(Delta) need not be finite merely because J is finite. A finite functor source can identify distinct source objects in C, making arrows composable in C even when their chosen preimages were not composable in J.

============================================================
3. SINGLE FINITE-DIAGRAM COUNTERMODEL FOR LOCAL FINITENESS
============================================================

Let C be the one-object category with object X and
End_C(X)=D_infinity=<a,b | a^2=e, b^2=e>.

Let J be the finite category with four objects A,B,C0,D and exactly two nonidentity arrows:
u:A->B
v:C0->D
There are no nonidentity composable pairs in J.
The category J is finite.

Define a functor Delta:J->C by mapping all four objects to X and:
Delta(u)=a
Delta(v)=b.

This is a valid functor. Since J has no nonidentity composable pairs, there are no further nontrivial composition equations to check.

The image data of Delta is finite: one object X and two morphisms a,b, together with the identity.

However, in the target category C, both a and b are endomorphisms of X and are composable.
Every subcategory containing the image of Delta must therefore contain all finite composites of a and b.
In particular it contains (ab)^n for every n>=0.

Since ab has infinite order in D_infinity, these composites are pairwise distinct.
Thus Gen(Delta) is infinite.

Under Suff_0:
- a witness exists;
- the least witness exists;
- the least witness is minimal;
- the unique minimal witness is infinite.

CONCLUSION:
For arbitrary finite categories J and arbitrary functors Delta:J->C, finite J does not guarantee a finite generated subcategory witness.
The assertion that minimality implies finiteness requires additional assumptions.

This does not contradict a finite-minimality theorem whose hypotheses include local finiteness, object-injectivity, closure restrictions, or another finite-generation condition.

============================================================
4. FINITE-FAMILY D_INFINITY TEST
============================================================

In the same ambient category C, let J_a and J_b each be the finite one-arrow category with two objects and one nonidentity arrow.

Define Delta_a by mapping both objects to X and its arrow to a.
Define Delta_b by mapping both objects to X and its arrow to b.

The generated individual witnesses are:
W_a={X; e,a}
W_b={X; e,b}

Both are finite subcategories because a^2=e and b^2=e.

Any common subcategory witness containing both diagrams contains a and b and therefore all (ab)^n.
Hence every common subcategory witness is infinite.

CONCLUSION:
Finite individual witnesses for a finite family do not imply a finite common witness under the baseline containment-only predicate.

The obstruction is failure of local finiteness of the joint closure operation.

============================================================
5. FINITE-SUPPORT AND LOCAL-FINITENESS AUDIT
============================================================

FINITE SUPPORT:
For a fixed finite diagram Delta, its literal image data is finite.
Under the baseline predicate, the generated closure of that finite support is always an admissible witness and is contained in every witness.
Thus finite-support reduction holds in the weak sense that the finite image data generates the least witness.

LOCAL FINITENESS:
Local finiteness fails.
The finite support {X,a,b} in D_infinity generates an infinite subcategory.
The single finite-diagram construction in Section 3 already demonstrates this failure.

Therefore the implication:
minimal witness + finite-support reduction + local finiteness => finite witness
remains valid, but its local-finiteness premise is not a theorem of arbitrary category theory.

For the baseline predicate, finite-support reduction is available, while local finiteness is false in general.

============================================================
6. EXACT THEOREM STATUS
============================================================

THEOREM A:
If every finite valid support S has finite generated closure Gen(S), then every finite diagram Delta has a finite least witness under Suff_0.

PROOF:
The image data of Delta is finite. By the assumed local-finiteness property, its generated closure is finite. By definition it is a witness and is contained in every witness. Therefore it is the finite least witness.

THEOREM B:
Under Suff_0, the least witness for Delta is Gen(Delta), whether or not it is finite.

PROOF:
Gen(Delta) is the smallest subcategory containing the image of Delta. It is itself a witness. Every witness must contain the image and be closed under identities and composition, hence must contain Gen(Delta). Therefore Gen(Delta) is least.

COUNTEREXAMPLE:
Section 3 shows Theorem B's least witness can be infinite when local finiteness is absent.

FAMILY THEOREM:
A finite family has a finite common least witness under Suff_0 if the subcategory generated by the union of all diagram image data is finite.
Individual finiteness of Gen(Delta_i) does not imply this joint finiteness.

============================================================
7. STRUCTURAL CONDITIONS THAT CAN RESTORE FINITENESS
============================================================

The following are possible sufficient conditions, not established OMEGA-ISOP axioms:

OPTION 1 — LOCAL FINITENESS OF C:
Every subcategory generated by a finite set of objects and morphisms is finite.
Then all finite diagram image data has finite generated closure.

OPTION 2 — FINITE JOINT CLOSURE:
For the particular finite family under study, the closure of the union of image data is finite.
This is a local, family-specific condition.

OPTION 3 — RESTRICTED COMPOSABILITY / REALIZATION:
The realization may restrict which morphisms are jointly composable or may use a witness object not equal to an arbitrary ambient subcategory.
Such a restriction must be formally defined and checked for closure under identities and composition.

OPTION 4 — FINITE-RANK CLOSURE:
A well-founded rank or finite closure bound may be imposed on generated witnesses.
The rank must be independently defined and must not encode the desired finiteness conclusion circularly.

No option is adopted as canonical in this checkpoint.

8. LOOP AUDIT
============================================================

Target: minimal witness is finite.

The baseline proof cannot establish this without a finite-closure hypothesis.
The D_infinity model is an explicit counterexample to the unrestricted claim.
The conditional proof in the predecessor reconstruction is non-circular only when finite-support reduction and local finiteness are independently established.

Target: finite individual witnesses imply finite common witness.

The D_infinity family refutes this unrestricted inference.
A finite common witness follows only with an independently verified finite joint closure and joint admissibility.

No conclusion has been smuggled into the definitions.
