1. GROUP ACTIONS AND COVARIANCE

Let G be a group acting on spaces X and Y. Write:
alpha_g : X -> X
beta_g : Y -> Y

The action laws are:
alpha_e = id_X
alpha_(gh) = alpha_g composed with alpha_h
beta_e = id_Y
beta_(gh) = beta_g composed with beta_h

For F:X->Y, covariance means:
For every g in G and x in X:
F(alpha_g(x)) = beta_g(F(x)).

This is an intertwining relation between the two actions.

Invariance is the special case in which the action on Y is trivial:
For every g and x:
F(alpha_g(x)) = F(x).

Thus:
Covariance does not generally mean invariance.
Invariance is covariance with a trivial output action.

Example:
Let G=SO(3) act on X=Y=R^3 by rotations.
F(x)=x is covariant, because F(gx)=gF(x).
The scalar function f(x)=||x|| is invariant, because ||gx||=||x||.

These are different transformation laws.

RESULT: PROVEN.

2. A1 UNDER DIFFERENT GROUP TYPES

2.1 Compact, nonabelian group: SO(3)

Let G=SO(3), acting on X=Y=R^3 by the standard rotation action.
For every positive integer i, define:
F_i(x)=i*x.

Then:
F_i(gx)=i*(gx)=g*(i*x)=gF_i(x).

Therefore every F_i is equivariant under the compact nonabelian group SO(3).

However, the family is not equicontinuous. For epsilon=1 and any proposed delta>0, choose x=(delta/2)e_1 and x'=0, then choose i>2/delta. We have:
||x-x'||=delta/2<delta,
but:
||F_i(x)-F_i(x')||=i*delta/2>1.

RESULT:
COUNTERMODEL: even covariance under a compact nonabelian group does not imply family equicontinuity.

2.2 Noncompact, abelian group: positive real scalings

Let G=(R_{>0}, multiplication), a noncompact abelian group, act on X=Y=R by:
alpha_g(x)=g*x
beta_g(y)=g*y.

Define:
F_i(x)=i*x.

Then:
F_i(alpha_g(x))=i*g*x=g*i*x=beta_g(F_i(x)).

So each F_i is covariant under this noncompact abelian action.

The family is not equicontinuous, by the same scaling argument as in section 2.1.

RESULT:
COUNTERMODEL: covariance under a noncompact abelian group also does not imply family equicontinuity.

2.3 Noncompact, nonabelian group: SL(2,R)

Let G=SL(2,R) act on X=Y=R^2 by matrix multiplication.
For each positive integer i, define:
F_i(x)=i*x.

For every g in SL(2,R):
F_i(gx)=i*(gx)=g*(i*x)=gF_i(x).

Thus the family is covariant under a noncompact nonabelian group. It remains non-equicontinuous in the Euclidean norm because the scaling factors i are unbounded.

This example shows that neither commutativity nor compactness is required for the algebraic covariance identity.

RESULT: PROVEN.

2.4 Infinite discrete group: integer shifts on sequence space

Let G=Z act on ell2 by the bilateral shift S:
(Sx)_k = x_(k-1), for k in Z.

The shift is a linear isometry, and each integer n acts by S^n.

Define:
F_i(x)=i*x.

Then:
F_i(S^n x)=i*S^n x=S^n*(i*x)=S^n F_i(x).

The family is covariant under the infinite discrete group Z, but it is not equicontinuous because the operator norms of F_i equal i.

RESULT: PROVEN.

3. A2 — SYMMETRY DOES NOT FORCE UNIFORM ATTRACTION

Let G=SO(3) act on X=R^3 by rotations.
Let:
A={0}
B={e_1}
I={1,2,3,...}
q_i=1-1/(i+1)
T_i(x)=q_i*x.

Every T_i commutes with the rotation action:
T_i(gx)=q_i*gx=g*q_i*x=g*T_i(x).

For each fixed i:
T_i^n(e_1)=q_i^n*e_1 -> 0.

But there is no common N for all i such that all T_i^n(B) lie inside the ball of radius 1/2 around zero for every n>=N.

For any proposed N, choose i large enough that q_i^N>=1/2. Then:
||T_i^N(e_1)||>=1/2.

RESULT:
COUNTERMODEL: even when every operator commutes with a compact nonabelian symmetry group and every parameter separately attracts B to A, uniform attraction over the parameter family can fail.

The group symmetry does not repair the missing uniformity in the parameter index.

4. A3 — GROUP EQUIVARIANCE DOES NOT FORCE EQUICONTINUITY

The family F_i(x)=i*x in sections 2.1–2.4 supplies explicit examples.

For compact SO(3), noncompact R_{>0}, noncompact SL(2,R), and the discrete group Z, the family is equivariant under the stated actions but not equicontinuous in the relevant norm.

Therefore:
A1 covariance does not imply A3 equicontinuity.
The group being compact does not by itself imply A3.
The group being abelian or nonabelian does not by itself imply A3.
The group being discrete or continuous does not by itself imply A3.

These conclusions concern the stated families and structures. They do not rule out additional theorems under stronger hypotheses, such as a common operator-norm bound or an invariant compact domain.

5. WHEN GROUP STRUCTURE CAN HELP

5.1 Isometric actions

If each alpha_g is an isometry on X and each beta_g is an isometry on Y, then the group action preserves the relevant distances.

This can simplify covariance estimates. But isometry of the group action alone says nothing about a family F_i unless the operators have additional common bounds or structural constraints.

5.2 Common Lipschitz control

If there exists L<infinity such that:
||F_i(x)-F_i(x')|| <= L*||x-x'||
for every i,x,x',
then A3 follows, independently of whether G is compact, noncompact, abelian, or nonabelian.

If the maps are equivariant and the actions are isometric, the same Lipschitz bound is compatible with the symmetry. However, equivariance does not generate the bound.

5.3 Averaging over compact groups

For compact groups, invariant averaging can be available when the action and integration setting meet the required hypotheses. In finite-dimensional representations, one can often average an inner product using normalized Haar measure to obtain an invariant inner product.

This does not imply that an arbitrary operator family is uniformly bounded, equicontinuous, or attracting. Averaging is a construction under additional representation and measurability/continuity assumptions, not an automatic consequence for every nonlinear family.

RESULT: CONDITIONAL.

6. INVARIANCE VERSUS COVARIANCE — FAILURE MODES

If beta_g is the identity for all g, covariance reduces to invariance:
F(alpha_g(x))=F(x).

If beta is nontrivial, covariance allows F(x) to transform in the output space. Replacing covariance by invariance can therefore discard valid equivariant maps.

Conversely, an invariant scalar observable such as ||x|| under an orthogonal action does not establish that the underlying vector-valued map is equivariant.

A proof of A1 must state both actions and verify the transformation identity. It is insufficient to state only that a quantity is "symmetric" or "invariant" without specifying the representation on the output.

RESULT: PROVEN.

7. NECESSITY VERSUS SUFFICIENCY

A1:
The intertwining identity is the exact algebraic criterion under the reconstructed covariance definition.
Group compactness, commutativity, connectedness, or discreteness is neither necessary nor sufficient by itself for covariance. Covariance depends on the selected actions and maps.

A2:
A group symmetry commuting with each T_i is not sufficient for uniform attraction.
A common contraction estimate and suitable target/invariance assumptions can imply uniform attraction, but symmetry alone cannot replace these estimates.

A3:
Equivariance is not sufficient for family equicontinuity.
A common Lipschitz bound is sufficient.
Compactness of G alone is not sufficient for A3, as shown by F_i=i*I under SO(3).

8. ROBUSTNESS SUMMARY

PROVEN:
1. Covariance and invariance are distinct; invariance is the trivial-output-action case.
2. A1 is an intertwining identity and does not require linear maps.
3. Covariant families exist for compact nonabelian, noncompact abelian, noncompact nonabelian, and infinite discrete groups.
4. Equivariance under any of these group structures does not by itself imply family equicontinuity.
5. Commutation with a compact symmetry group does not by itself imply uniform attraction.
6. Isometric group actions preserve distances but do not automatically bound an unrelated family of operators.
7. A common Lipschitz bound implies A3 independently of group type.

COUNTERMODEL:
1. F_i(x)=i*x is equivariant but not equicontinuous for the displayed group actions.
2. T_i(x)=q_i*x commutes with SO(3), each T_i attracts individually, but uniform attraction fails.

CONDITIONAL:
1. Compact-group averaging requires appropriate representation, continuity, and integration assumptions.
2. Symmetry can simplify estimates when combined with common bounds or invariant structures.
3. Additional invariant-domain or compactness hypotheses may support stronger results, but they are not proved by group compactness alone.

RECONSTRUCTED:
1. The A1 covariance identity is applied to the tested group actions.
2. The A2 and A3 tests use the reconstructed definitions from earlier GRT audits.

UNVERIFIED:
1. Whether original A1 used a single common group action or parameter-dependent actions.
2. Whether the original symmetry group was compact, noncompact, connected, discrete, or a gauge groupoid.
3. Whether A4's unresolved anchor is a fixed point, orbit, invariant set, or relational object.
4. Whether the original A1–A3 independence constructions remain valid under the exact unrecovered Stage 3 domains and quantifiers.
