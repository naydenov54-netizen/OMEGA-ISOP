4. TEST T1: CC INFORMATION
============================================================

For a span D=(S1 <- S0 -> S2), define Cocone_M(D,Q) as the set of pairs of admitted morphisms:
f1:S1 -> Q
f2:S2 -> Q
such that f1 o i1 = f2 o i2.

Define:
CC_M(S1,S2 | S0)=1 iff there exists an object Q with Cocone_M(D,Q) nonempty.

For a change or comparison functor F:C -> C', the minimum truth-value preservation test is:

CC_C(D)=1 iff CC_C'(F o D)=1.

This equivalence is necessary if the two categories are claimed to encode the same CC predicate.

A stronger information test compares cocones:
- every relevant source cocone must transport to a target cocone;
- every target cocone relevant to the selected realization must be represented or reflected by source data;
- distinctions between strong and non-strong amalgams must not be silently erased when those distinctions are CC-relevant.

For an equivalence-of-realizations claim, require a bijection on the relevant isomorphism classes of cocones over the fixed diagram, or state and prove a weaker exact invariant that is sufficient for the architecture.

Important:
Preserving the Boolean value CC=1 alone does not determine a unique amalgam and does not establish preservation of R.

============================================================
5. TEST T2: R INFORMATION
============================================================

For the selected apartness relation #, a map f:X -> Y is:

PRESERVING if:
x #X y => f(x) #Y f(y).

REFLECTING if:
f(x) #Y f(y) => x #X y.

FULLY APARTNESS-FAITHFUL if:
x #X y iff f(x) #Y f(y).

The full test must also state what happens to non-apartness:
not(x #X y).

A preserving-only map does not in general reflect non-apartness. It may map a non-apart source pair to an apart target pair. Therefore it can add relational distinctions not forced by the source.

For a cocone leg j_i:S_i -> R, exact induced-substructure retention requires:
x #S_i y iff j_i(x) #R j_i(y)
for all x,y in S_i.

This is stronger than mere preservation.

Strongness is a separate condition:
j1(S1) intersect j2(S2) = j1(i1(S0)) = j2(i2(S0)),
up to the specified identifications.

Do not conflate:
(a) exact induced-substructure retention;
(b) strongness of the amalgam;
(c) categorical universality.

Each is a separate test.

The existence of a Markov kernel or a relational endomorphism alone does not prove membership in A(R;Phi).

11. PRELIMINARY MATHEMATICAL CONCLUSION
============================================================

The finite test does not justify selecting E or H as the canonical category.

E retains the full apartness truth profile along its morphisms, but the tested span has no pushout in that category.

H has a pushout, but its universal property deliberately forgets the non-apartness constraint between c and d. It is therefore not automatically an information-equivalent replacement for E or for canonical C_METACORE.

M is the only remaining design space in which one can attempt to separate:
- relational information that must be exact;
- relational information that may be generated monotonically;
- support/carrier information that must remain injective;
- information needed by Phi and A(R;Phi).

However, M is not yet a solution. Its protected signature must be derived from the canonical definitions, not selected to force the desired pushout.

Critical non-implication:
A category that preserves CC=1 and admits a pushout need not preserve the full R structure or A(R;Phi).

Critical non-implication:
A category that preserves R exactly along morphisms need not admit the required colimit.

Therefore the category-selection problem is a joint information-preservation and existence problem, not a colimit-existence problem alone.
