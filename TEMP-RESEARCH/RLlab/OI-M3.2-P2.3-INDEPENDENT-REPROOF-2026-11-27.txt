============================================================
2. EXACT THEOREM
============================================================

Let A=(S_A,#_A), B=(S_B,#_B), and C=(S_C,#_C) be Boolean apartness structures.

Assume for each structure:

(A1) Irreflexivity:
For every x, NOT(x # x).

(A2) Symmetry:
For every x,y, x # y IFF y # x.

(A3) Cotransitivity:
For every x,y,z, if x # y, then (x # z) OR (z # y).

The argument uses Boolean-valued relations and classical negation.

Let:
f:C->A,
g:C->B

be injective apartness embeddings, meaning:

x #_C y IFF f(x) #_A f(y),
x #_C y IFF g(x) #_B g(y).

Define:
x ~_A y IFF NOT(x #_A y),
and analogously ~_B and ~_C.

Let S_P be the pushout of the underlying sets S_A and S_B over S_C. Write its canonical maps as:
i_A:S_A->S_P,
i_B:S_B->S_P.

Then there exists a Boolean apartness relation #_P on S_P such that:

1. (S_P,#_P) satisfies A1-A3.
2. i_A and i_B are injective apartness embeddings.
3. i_A(S_A) intersect i_B(S_B) is exactly the common image of S_C.

Thus every span of injective apartness embeddings in this class has a strong amalgam.

This is the strong amalgamation property for the category with these structures as objects and injective apartness embeddings as morphisms.

============================================================
3. LEMMA 1 — INDISTINGUISHABILITY IS AN EQUIVALENCE RELATION
============================================================

For any Boolean apartness structure (S,#), define:

x ~ y IFF NOT(x # y).

Proof:

REFLEXIVITY:
A1 gives NOT(x # x). Hence x~x.

SYMMETRY:
If x~y, then NOT(x#y). By A2, x#y IFF y#x. Therefore NOT(y#x), so y~x.

TRANSITIVITY:
Assume x~y and y~z.
Thus:
NOT(x#y),
NOT(y#z).

Suppose x#z. By A3, with intermediary y:
(x#y) OR (y#z).

Both alternatives contradict the assumptions. Therefore NOT(x#z), so x~z.

Hence ~ is reflexive, symmetric, and transitive.

RESULT:
~ is an equivalence relation.

The proof of transitivity is independent of any amalgamation construction. It follows directly from A3 and the two assumed non-apartness statements.

============================================================
4. LEMMA 2 — APARTNESS IS QUOTIENT INEQUALITY
============================================================

Let [x] denote the equivalence class of x under ~.

By the definition of quotient equality:
[x]=[y] IFF x~y
IFF NOT(x#y).

Because # is Boolean-valued:
x#y IFF NOT(NOT(x#y))
IFF [x] != [y].

Therefore:

x # y IFF [x] != [y].

This representation is derived from A1-A3 and classical Boolean logic. It is not an additional axiom.

============================================================
5. LEMMA 3 — EMBEDDINGS INDUCE INJECTIVE QUOTIENT MAPS
============================================================

Let f:C->A be an injective apartness embedding.

Define:
f_bar:Q_C->Q_A
by:
f_bar([c]_C)=[f(c)]_A.

WELL-DEFINEDNESS:
If [c1]_C=[c2]_C, then NOT(c1#_C c2).
Since f preserves and reflects apartness:
NOT(f(c1)#_A f(c2)).
Thus [f(c1)]_A=[f(c2)]_A.

INJECTIVITY:
Suppose:
f_bar([c1]_C)=f_bar([c2]_C).
Then:
[f(c1)]_A=[f(c2)]_A.
Hence NOT(f(c1)#_A f(c2)).
Reflection of apartness gives NOT(c1#_C c2).
Therefore [c1]_C=[c2]_C.

So f_bar is injective. The proof for g_bar is identical.

RESULT:
Both induced quotient maps are well-defined and injective.

============================================================
6. INDEPENDENT CONSTRUCTION I — TWO SET PUSHOUTS
============================================================

Form:
Q_A=S_A/~_A,
Q_B=S_B/~_B,
Q_C=S_C/~_C.

Use Lemma 3 to obtain injective maps:
f_bar:Q_C->Q_A,
g_bar:Q_C->Q_B.

Let:
Q=Q_A coproduct_(Q_C) Q_B
be the pushout in Set, with canonical maps:
j_A:Q_A->Q,
j_B:Q_B->Q.

Because f_bar and g_bar are injective, j_A and j_B are injective. Their images intersect exactly in the common image of Q_C.

Independently form:
S_P=S_A coproduct_(S_C) S_B
with canonical maps i_A and i_B.

Because f and g are injective, i_A and i_B are injective and:
i_A(S_A) intersect i_B(S_B)
=
i_A(f(S_C))
=
i_B(g(S_C)).

Define:
pi_P:S_P->Q

by:
pi_P(i_A(a))=j_A([a]_A),
pi_P(i_B(b))=j_B([b]_B).

WELL-DEFINEDNESS:
The only identifications imposed in S_P identify i_A(f(c)) with i_B(g(c)).
For such an element:
j_A([f(c)]_A)
=
j_A(f_bar([c]_C))
=
j_B(g_bar([c]_C))
=
j_B([g(c)]_B).

Therefore pi_P is well-defined.

The map pi_P is surjective because every element of the set pushout Q is represented by an element from Q_A or Q_B.

Define:
x #_P y IFF pi_P(x) != pi_P(y).

============================================================
7. PROOF THAT P SATISFIES A1-A3
============================================================

A1 — IRREFLEXIVITY:
For every x in S_P:
pi_P(x)=pi_P(x).
Therefore NOT(pi_P(x)!=pi_P(x)).
Hence NOT(x#_P x).

A2 — SYMMETRY:
For all x,y:
pi_P(x)!=pi_P(y)
IFF
pi_P(y)!=pi_P(x).
Therefore:
x#_P y IFF y#_P x.

A3 — COTRANSITIVITY:
Assume x#_P y.
Then:
pi_P(x)!=pi_P(y).

Let z be any element of S_P.

Suppose both alternatives fail:
NOT(x#_P z) and NOT(z#_P y).

By definition:
pi_P(x)=pi_P(z),
pi_P(z)=pi_P(y).

By transitivity of equality:
pi_P(x)=pi_P(y),
contradicting x#_P y.

Therefore at least one alternative holds:
x#_P z OR z#_P y.

Thus A3 holds.

RESULT:
(S_P,#_P) is a Boolean apartness structure.

============================================================
8. PROOF THAT THE CANONICAL MAPS ARE APARTNESS EMBEDDINGS
============================================================

Take a,a' in S_A.

By definition:
i_A(a)#_P i_A(a')
IFF
pi_P(i_A(a)) != pi_P(i_A(a')).

Substitute the definition of pi_P:
IFF
j_A([a]_A) != j_A([a']_A).

Since j_A is injective:
IFF
[a]_A != [a']_A.

By Lemma 2:
IFF
a#_A a'.

Therefore:
a#_A a' IFF i_A(a)#_P i_A(a').

The map i_A is injective as a set-pushout map. Hence it is an injective apartness embedding.

The same proof applies to i_B.

============================================================
9. PROOF OF STRONGNESS
============================================================

The underlying set S_P is the pushout of S_A and S_B over S_C along injective maps.

For such a set pushout, the canonical maps are injective and their images intersect exactly in the common image.

Thus:
i_A(S_A) intersect i_B(S_B)
=
i_A(f(S_C))
=
i_B(g(S_C)).

No additional cross-identifications are introduced into the carrier S_P.

Together with Sections 7 and 8, this proves that P is a strong amalgam.

10. INDEPENDENT CONSTRUCTION II — GENERATED EQUIVALENCE RELATION
============================================================

The two-pushout construction admits a more direct reformulation.

Work on the already formed carrier S_P.

Transport the side equivalence relations to S_P:
E_A = {(i_A(a),i_A(a')) : a~_A a'},
E_B = {(i_B(b),i_B(b')) : b~_B b'}.

Let E be the smallest equivalence relation on S_P containing E_A and E_B.

Equivalently, E is the reflexive, symmetric, transitive closure of E_A union E_B.

CLAIM:
For all x,y in S_P:
x E y IFF pi_P(x)=pi_P(y).

PROOF, FIRST DIRECTION:
If x E_A y, then x and y are represented on side A by equivalent elements. Their A-quotient classes agree, so their images under pi_P agree.
The same holds for E_B.
Equality of pi_P-images is itself an equivalence relation. Therefore it contains the equivalence relation generated by E_A and E_B.
Hence:
x E y IMPLIES pi_P(x)=pi_P(y).

PROOF, REVERSE DIRECTION:
The quotient S_P/E has the following universal property.

A function h:S_P->X factors through S_P/E if and only if it is constant on E-classes.

By the definition of E, this is equivalent to:
- h restricted to i_A(S_A) is constant on ~_A-classes;
- h restricted to i_B(S_B) is constant on ~_B-classes.

Since S_P is the set pushout over C, the two restrictions also agree on the identified common elements.

These data are equivalent to a pair of functions:
h_A:Q_A->X,
h_B:Q_B->X,
which agree on Q_C.

That is precisely the universal property defining the set pushout:
Q=Q_A coproduct_(Q_C) Q_B.

Therefore S_P/E is canonically bijective to Q, and the quotient map S_P->S_P/E has exactly the same fibers as pi_P.

Consequently:
pi_P(x)=pi_P(y) IFF x E y.

RESULT:
The quotient classes used in P2.3 are exactly the equivalence classes generated by the two side indistinguishability relations after the common substructure has been identified.

This is an independent one-carrier characterization of the P2.3 construction.

============================================================
11. STRUCTURAL CONSEQUENCE — P2.3 CHOOSES THE LEAST IDENTIFICATION
============================================================

The relation E from Section 10 is the smallest equivalence relation on S_P that contains the two side equivalence relations.

P2.3 defines:
x#_P y IFF NOT(x E y).

Consider any other Boolean apartness relation #_D on the same carrier S_P for which the canonical side maps remain apartness embeddings.

Let:
x E_D y IFF NOT(x#_D y).

By Lemma 1, E_D is an equivalence relation.

Because the side maps preserve and reflect apartness, E_D contains E_A and E_B. Since E is the smallest equivalence relation containing those side relations:
E is a subset of E_D.

Taking Boolean complements:
#_D is a subset of #_P.

Thus, among all Boolean apartness relations on this fixed set-pushout carrier that make both canonical maps apartness embeddings, P2.3 gives the greatest apartness relation under set inclusion.

Equivalently:
- P2.3 makes no cross-side identifications except those forced by the side equivalences and the common span.
- Any other admissible equivalence relation on this same carrier may identify additional cross-side classes.
- Such additional identifications can only remove apartness pairs; they cannot add new apartness pairs.

This is a precise extremal characterization. It does not assert uniqueness of all strong amalgams.

12. CATEGORICAL PUSHOUT IS A STRICTLY STRONGER CLAIM
============================================================

P2.3 proves existence of a strong amalgam. It does not prove a categorical pushout in the category whose morphisms are injective apartness embeddings.

A counterexample shows why.

Let:
C be the empty structure,
A have one element a,
B have one element b.

There are no nontrivial apartness pairs in either singleton.

The P2.3 carrier has two distinct elements:
S_P={a,b}.

Their quotient classes are distinct because the quotient pushout is taken over the empty set. Hence:
a#_P b.

Now let D be a one-element structure:
S_D={d}.

There is an injective apartness embedding:
u:A->D,
and an injective apartness embedding:
v:B->D.

They agree on C because C is empty.

If P2.3 were a categorical pushout in the category of injective apartness embeddings, there would have to be a morphism:
h:P->D
extending u and v.

But extension requires:
h(a)=d,
h(b)=d.

Since a and b are distinct, h is not injective. Therefore h is not a morphism in this category.

No such categorical pushout mediator exists.

CONCLUSION:
The P2.3 object is a strong amalgam, but not a categorical pushout in the embedding category in general.

This confirms the exact boundary stated for P2.3. It is not a defect in the strong-amalgamation proof.
