TITLE
Operational test of organization in a minimal coupled-oscillator model

1. QUESTION

Can a physical realization supply a measurable criterion that distinguishes mere relational connectivity from a dynamically organized state?

This audit constructs a minimal two-oscillator example. It is a model-level demonstration of operationalization, not a derivation of oscillator physics from Primitive Difference (PD) or the Frozen Core.

2. MODEL AND EXPLICIT ASSUMPTIONS

Consider two phase oscillators with measured phases theta_1(t), theta_2(t), equal natural angular frequency omega, and symmetric attractive coupling K>0:
d theta_1/dt = omega + K sin(theta_2 - theta_1)
d theta_2/dt = omega + K sin(theta_1 - theta_2).

Assumptions supplied by the physical model:
- the phase description is adequate over the measurement interval;
- both oscillators have the same natural frequency;
- coupling is symmetric and attractive, with constant K>0;
- phases can be measured with stated uncertainty;
- the equations above approximate the observed dynamics.

None of these assumptions is derived from PD.

3. RELATIONAL CONNECTIVITY VERSUS ORGANIZATION

For K>0, the two oscillators are linked by a nonzero interaction. That is a relational fact within the model.

Define phase difference delta = theta_1 - theta_2. Subtracting the equations gives:
d delta/dt = -2 K sin(delta).

Define the measurable phase-coherence order parameter:
R(t) = |(exp(i theta_1(t)) + exp(i theta_2(t)))/2|
     = |cos(delta(t)/2)|.

R=1 means equal phases modulo 2 pi. R=0 means anti-phase for this two-oscillator system. Intermediate values quantify partial phase coherence.

Connectivity alone does not imply R=1 at the initial time. For example, K>0 with delta(0)=pi/2 gives R(0)=sqrt(2)/2, not 1.

4. CONDITIONAL DYNAMICAL PREDICTION

On a trajectory with delta(0) in (-pi,pi), away from the unstable endpoint delta=pi and using a continuous phase-difference branch, integration gives:
tan(delta(t)/2) = tan(delta(0)/2) exp(-2 K t).

Therefore:
R(t) = |cos(delta(t)/2)|,
with delta(t) predicted by the expression above.

For initial delta(0) in (-pi,pi), the model predicts convergence toward delta=0 and R(t) tending to 1 as t increases. The exactly anti-phase state delta=pi is an equilibrium and is unstable under perturbations; it is an important exception to an unconditional convergence claim.

This is a quantitative prediction conditional on the stated oscillator model, not a universal prediction of OMEGA-ISOP or PD.

5. OPERATIONAL TEST AND FALSIFIABILITY

Measurement protocol:
A. Record theta_1(t) and theta_2(t) over a declared time window.
B. Estimate K independently or fit it on a predeclared training segment.
C. Compute delta(t) and R(t) from the measured phases.
D. Compare the held-out trajectory with the model prediction, propagating phase-measurement uncertainty and parameter uncertainty.
E. Predeclare a tolerance band based on instrument noise and model discrepancy.

A possible operational organization criterion for this model is:
R(t) >= R_min throughout a declared interval I,
where R_min and I are fixed before testing.

This criterion is a convention tied to the measurement task, not a universal definition of organization. A separate dynamical criterion is relaxation toward higher R as predicted by the equations.

Failure conditions:
- observed phase differences systematically depart from the predicted trajectory beyond the predeclared tolerance;
- the independently measured coupling is not attractive or the equal-frequency assumption fails;
- coherence does not evolve as predicted under conditions where the model assumptions are supported.

Such failures reject this model or its assumptions in the tested regime; they do not by themselves refute PD or the entire OMEGA-ISOP program.

6. WHAT THIS MODEL DOES AND DOES NOT SHOW

Shows:
- a concrete physical model can distinguish interaction connectivity from measured collective coherence;
- an operational order parameter can be defined;
- a conditional, quantitative, falsifiable time-course prediction can be written down.

Does not show:
- that PD entails coupled oscillators, the phase equations, or the value of K;
- that phase coherence is the unique or universal measure of organization;
- that every connected system synchronizes;
- a novel empirical effect attributable to OMEGA-ISOP beyond the adopted oscillator model.

7. DERIVATION LEDGER

Derived mathematically from the model equations:
- d delta/dt = -2K sin(delta);
- the order parameter R = |cos(delta/2)|;
- for the stated phase branch, tan(delta(t)/2)=tan(delta(0)/2) exp(-2Kt).

Assumed or measured externally:
- oscillator ontology and phase description;
- symmetric attractive coupling and equal natural frequencies;
- validity of the model over the tested regime;
- parameter K and measurement uncertainty;
- the operational threshold R_min and interval I.

Not derived:
- the physical model from PD;
- a universal organization principle;
- any new empirical prediction independent of the chosen physical realization.

8. RELATION TO THE FROZEN CORE

The model is a candidate physical interface consistent with the general methodological separation between core theory, realization, and validation. It does not alter PD, U0, #, R, CC, or any Frozen Core statement.

No CE-V promotion, canonical acceptance, or RL registration is asserted.

9. RESULT

A defensible physical organization claim requires a chosen realization, a measurable order parameter, a specified dynamical model, uncertainty-aware comparison, and explicit failure conditions. The coupled-oscillator example provides these conditionally. The next scientific test would be comparison with real phase data and competing models; until then, this remains a mathematical and methodological model, not validated new physics.
