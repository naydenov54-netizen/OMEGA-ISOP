2. MATHEMATICAL DEFINITIONS AND SOURCE ALIGNMENT
============================================================

2.1 Primitive Difference and apartness

Primitive Difference (PD) is a meta-level condition for the admissibility of formal distinction. It is not itself an object, relation, or causal mechanism.

One formal realization is:
A = (U0,#)
# : U0 x U0 -> {true,false}

x # y means that x and y are formally separated under the selected realization.

Do not identify PD with the binary relation #. The latter is a realization of the former.

A standard apartness realization may satisfy:
(AP1) not(x # x)
(AP2) x # y iff y # x
(AP3) x # y implies (x # z) or (z # y), for every z.

These are conditions of the standard realization, not automatically axioms of every possible PD realization.

2.2 Indistinguishability

Define:
x ~ y iff no admissible apartness witness separates x and y.

Under a complete apartness realization:
x ~ y iff not(x # y).

Under AP1-AP3, this relation is an equivalence relation. This conclusion is conditional on those apartness axioms and must not be generalized to every possible realization of PD without proof.

2.3 Configurations

A configuration is represented by:
C = (S_C,#_C)
where:
S_C subset U0
#_C = # restricted to S_C x S_C.

Cardinality condition:
|S_C| >= 2.

For nontriviality in the stronger Volume III sense, there must also exist x,y in S_C such that x #_C y.

The cardinality condition alone does not imply an actual apartness witness.

2.4 Embeddings

An embedding f:C1 -> C2 is injective and preserves and reflects apartness:
x #1 y iff f(x) #2 f(y).

Identity maps are embeddings.
Compositions of embeddings are embeddings:
x #1 y iff f(x) #2 f(y) iff g(f(x)) #3 g(f(y)).

This establishes the relevant category only after the object class is specified and shown to be closed under the stated morphisms.

2.5 Compatibility and amalgamation

The compatibility predicate is:
CC(C1,C2)=1 iff an admissible amalgamation of C1 and C2 exists.

CC is a binary existence predicate. By itself it does not define:
- an amalgam object;
- the embeddings into that object;
- the morphisms of a diagram;
- an indexing category J;
- a functor D:J -> C_METACORE;
- a colimit or its universal property.

Therefore CC alone is not a category and does not complete the definition of D_CC.

2.6 Colimit requirements

The expression:
R := colim(D_CC)

is valid only if the diagram is fully specified and the relevant colimit exists in the selected category.

At minimum, the following must be fixed:
- an indexing category J;
- a functor D:J -> C_METACORE;
- the objects and morphisms of J;
- the compatibility maps and their equations;
- the target category and its morphisms;
- the candidate cocone;
- the universal property.

A colimit, when it exists, is unique up to unique isomorphism. This does not establish that it exists in every proposed formalism or that it is a universal canonical representation.

S2-04B — GENERAL AMALGAMATION IN C_EQ-EMB
Candidate category:
C_EQ-EMB.

Objects:
Sets equipped with an equivalence relation.

Morphisms:
Injective functions preserving and reflecting equivalence.

For a span:
C0 --f1--> C1
C0 --f2--> C2

construct the pushout of carriers in Set:
S = S1 ⊔_(S0) S2
with canonical injections j1 and j2.

Define:
E = EquivClosure(j1(~1) union j2(~2)).
Set:
M=(S,E).

Lemma:
For x,y in S1:
j1(x) E j1(y) iff x ~1 y.
The corresponding statement holds for S2.

Reason:
The embeddings reflect equivalence. Any chain of generated equivalences that crosses between the two sides through the common base can be replaced by an equivalence inside the base. Therefore the closure introduces no new equivalences within either side.

Consequences:
j1 and j2 are embeddings, and the span commutes. Thus the amalgamation property is proved in C_EQ-EMB.

Important categorical distinction:
- Amalgamation property in C_EQ-EMB: PROVED.
- The constructed object has the pushout universal property in C_EQ-HOM, where morphisms are all equivalence-preserving functions.
- It is not in general a pushout in C_EQ-EMB. A cocone may identify points from opposite sides while each leg remains injective; the induced map from M then need not be injective.
- None of this automatically proves amalgamation or pushout in C_METACORE.

Status:
GENERAL AMALGAMATION PROVED IN C_EQ-EMB.

S2-04C — SOURCE / CATEGORY ALIGNMENT AUDIT
Volume II specifies configurations as subsets of U0 with restricted apartness and embeddings preserving and reflecting apartness. It describes C_METACORE as representing admissible configurations and morphisms, but does not fully specify the exact object class, all morphisms, all additional admissibility conditions, or closure properties.

Volume III explicitly notes that D_CC is not yet a complete categorical construction and requires an indexing category, a functor, specified objects and morphisms, compatibility conditions, and a universal property.

Alignment result:
- Under the standard realization # = complement of ~, the injectivity and preservation/reflection clauses match those in C_EQ-EMB.
- C_EQ-EMB permits abstract carriers.
- Volume II configurations are subsets of a fixed U0.
- C_EQ-EMB fixes an equivalence-relation realization.
- Volume II leaves admissibility realization-dependent.
- It has not been proved that all C_METACORE morphisms are exactly C_EQ-EMB morphisms, or conversely.

Fixed-carrier issue:
The abstract carrier pushout S1 ⊔_(S0) S2 may contain elements not in U0. Thus S subset U0 does not follow automatically.

Two cases must be kept separate:
A. Abstract extension category: new carrier elements may be allowed.
B. Fixed-carrier category: an amalgam exists only if a suitable subconfiguration of the same U0 realizes it.

The general abstract amalgamation proof does not settle case B.

CC limitation:
CC(C1,C2)=1 records existence of an admissible amalgam but does not specify the amalgam, embeddings, diagram morphisms, indexing category, or functor.

5. NEXT TASK: S2-04C-01 — FIXED-CARRIER AMALGAMATION TEST
============================================================

Objective:
Determine whether amalgamation follows when all configurations must be realized as induced substructures of one fixed carrier U0.

Formal setup:
Fix a finite U0 and an equivalence relation ~ on U0.
Define:
x # y iff not(x ~ y).

Configurations:
C_i=(S_i,# restricted to S_i x S_i), where S_i subset U0.

Embeddings:
Injective maps preserving and reflecting apartness, equivalently preserving and reflecting ~ under this standard realization.

Required checks:
1. Fix an explicit finite U0 and its equivalence classes.
2. Select C0, C1, and C2 as induced subconfigurations of U0.
3. Specify the embeddings in the span.
4. Test whether a common amalgam M can be represented by a subset S_M of the same U0.
5. Verify injectivity, preservation, reflection, and commutativity.
6. Find and prove a positive example.
7. Search for a counterexample under the exact fixed-carrier definition.
8. If a counterexample exists, record that fixed-carrier amalgamation does not follow automatically.
9. If a sufficient condition is identified, formulate it as a conditional theorem with proof.
10. Do not assume that the abstract pushout carrier is a subset of U0.

Important caution:
If the span maps are literal inclusions and the base is the actual common substructure, the union S1 union S2 inside U0 is a natural amalgam candidate.
This does not settle arbitrary embeddings between subconfigurations of U0. Arbitrary embeddings may require compatible class sizes and enough available elements in the fixed carrier.

Do not invent a counterexample. If the property holds under the precise definitions, prove it. If it fails, provide an explicit finite counterexample and verify every map.
