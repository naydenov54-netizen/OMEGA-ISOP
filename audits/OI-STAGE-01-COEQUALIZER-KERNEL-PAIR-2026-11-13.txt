1. DECLARED FRAMEWORK AND DEFINITIONS

Let X be a nonempty set. Let I be an index set. For every i in I, let V_i be a set and let f_i:X→V_i be a total function.

Define the signature map:
F:X→∏_{i∈I}V_i
F(x)=(f_i(x))_{i∈I}.

Define D:X×X→{0,1} by:
D(x,y)=1 iff there exists i∈I such that f_i(x)≠f_i(y).
D(x,y)=0 otherwise.

Define:
x~y iff D(x,y)=0.

By coordinatewise equality in the product:
x~y iff F(x)=F(y).

Equality in the codomain of F makes ~ an equivalence relation. Let:
Q=X/~

and let the canonical quotient projection be:
q:X→Q
q(x)=[x].

Define the kernel-pair relation:
R={(x,y)∈X×X : q(x)=q(y)}.

Since q(x)=q(y) iff x~y, we have:
R={(x,y)∈X×X : x~y}.

Let p1,p2:R→X be the coordinate projections:
p1(x,y)=x
p2(x,y)=y.

2. THEOREM CP-01 — COEQUALIZER UNIVERSAL PROPERTY

THEOREM:
The quotient projection q:X→Q is a coequalizer in Set of the pair:
p1,p2:R→X.

More explicitly:

(A) q∘p1=q∘p2.

(B) For every set Z and every function g:X→Z satisfying:
g∘p1=g∘p2,
there exists a unique function:
g_bar:Q→Z
such that:
g=g_bar∘q.

PROOF:

Part A — Equalization.

Take any r∈R. By definition, r=(x,y) with q(x)=q(y).

Therefore:
(q∘p1)(r)=q(x)=q(y)=(q∘p2)(r).

Since r was arbitrary:
q∘p1=q∘p2.

Part B — Existence of the factor map.

Let Z be a set and let g:X→Z satisfy:
g∘p1=g∘p2.

Unpacking this equality, for every (x,y)∈R:
g(x)=g(y).

Define:
g_bar:Q→Z
g_bar([x])=g(x).

We must prove that g_bar is well-defined.

Suppose:
[x]=[y].

For an equivalence relation, equality of classes is equivalent to x~y. Hence:
(x,y)∈R.

The assumed equalization condition gives:
g(x)=g(y).

Therefore the value assigned by g_bar is independent of the representative. Thus g_bar is well-defined.

For every x∈X:
(g_bar∘q)(x)=g_bar([x])=g(x).

Consequently:
g=g_bar∘q.

Part C — Uniqueness.

Suppose h:Q→Z also satisfies:
g=h∘q.

For every class C∈Q, by the definition of Q there exists x∈X with C=[x]=q(x).

Then:
h(C)=h(q(x))=g(x)=g_bar(q(x))=g_bar(C).

Therefore h and g_bar agree on every element of Q:
h=g_bar.

The factor map exists and is unique. Hence q is the coequalizer of p1 and p2 in Set. QED.

3. THEOREM CP-02 — THE RELATION IS THE KERNEL PAIR

For all x,y∈X:
(x,y)∈R iff q(x)=q(y) iff [x]=[y] iff x~y iff D(x,y)=0.

Therefore R is exactly the kernel pair relation of q, represented as a subset of X×X.

The quotient projection q is thus the coequalizer of its own kernel pair in Set.

This is the effective-equivalence-relation property for this particular quotient construction in Set.

PROOF:
Each equivalence follows directly from the definitions of R, q, Q and ~. QED.

4. COROLLARIES

C1 — FACTORIZATION CRITERION

A function g:X→Z factors through q if and only if it is constant on every ~-equivalence class.

Equivalently:
g(x)=g(y) whenever D(x,y)=0.

C2 — UNIQUE FACTORIZATION

Whenever the factorization exists, its factor map g_bar is unique.

C3 — NO REPRESENTATIVE SELECTION

The construction g_bar([x])=g(x) does not require choosing one representative from each equivalence class. Well-definedness follows from constancy on the entire class. No Axiom of Choice is used.

C4 — EMPTY FEATURE INDEX

If I=∅, the indexed product is a singleton. Since X is nonempty, F is constant, D≡0, Q has exactly one element, and R=X×X.

The equalization condition g∘p1=g∘p2 then requires g(x)=g(y) for every x,y∈X. Such a g factors uniquely through the singleton Q. Thus CP-01 also holds in the degenerate I=∅ case.

5. EXACT SCOPE AND NON-IMPLICATIONS

The theorem is a result in the category Set, under the declared set-theoretic definitions.

It establishes:
- the quotient projection equalizes the two kernel-pair projections;
- every function constant on equivalence classes factors through the quotient;
- the factor map is unique;
- the relation used to form the quotient is exactly the kernel pair of q.

It does NOT establish:
- a corresponding coequalizer theorem in every category;
- effectiveness of arbitrary equivalence relations in arbitrary categories;
- a unique physical realization of the quotient;
- causal, dynamical, temporal or ontological conclusions;
- universality of OMEGA-ISOP as a physical theory;
- any modification or extension of the Frozen Core.

The result is not a CAPD theorem and does not provide interval validation or computational certification.
