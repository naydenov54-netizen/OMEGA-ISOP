ISSUE:
N5 (A §6: missing explicit gamma_A>0 premise and missing proof of sharpness).

1. DEFECT IN THE PREDECESSOR

A v0.2 stated:

||x1-x2||<=2*epsilon/gamma_A

without explicitly introducing gamma_A>0 as the premise of the Section 6 recovery-uncertainty argument. It also did not establish that the universal constant is optimal.

The inequality is valid when gamma_A>0. The defect was an incomplete statement and proof, not a false bound under the intended injective assumptions.

2. CORRECTION

The v0.3 text explicitly assumes A is injective. By THEOREM 91.6A-2, this is equivalent to gamma_A>0 in the finite-dimensional Euclidean setting.

If one observation y is compatible with x1 and x2, then:

y=A(x1)+eta1
y=A(x2)+eta2

||eta1||<=epsilon
||eta2||<=epsilon.

Subtracting gives:

A(x1-x2)=eta2-eta1.

Therefore:

||A(x1-x2)||<=2*epsilon.

The positive minimum gain gives:

gamma_A*||x1-x2||<=||A(x1-x2)||.

Thus:

||x1-x2||<=2*epsilon/gamma_A.

For C(y)={x:||y-A(x)||<=epsilon}, this yields:

diam(C(y))<=2*epsilon/gamma_A.

3. SHARPNESS PROOF

The continuous function z->||A(z)|| attains its minimum on the compact Euclidean unit sphere. Hence there is v with:

||v||=1
||A(v)||=gamma_A.

For epsilon>0 choose:

x1=0
x2=(2*epsilon/gamma_A)*v
y=A(x2)/2.

Then:

||y-A(x1)||=epsilon
||y-A(x2)||=epsilon

and:

||x2-x1||=2*epsilon/gamma_A.

Thus both states belong to C(y) and attain the upper bound exactly. No smaller universal diameter constant is valid under the stated assumptions.

4. BOUNDARY CASES

If epsilon=0 and gamma_A>0, exact injectivity makes C(y) a singleton for y in the image; the diameter is zero.

If gamma_A=0, THEOREM 91.6A-2 gives non-injectivity in the finite-dimensional setting. A nonzero kernel direction produces arbitrarily distant states with exactly the same observation. Thus no finite uniform compatible-state diameter bound follows, even at zero noise.
