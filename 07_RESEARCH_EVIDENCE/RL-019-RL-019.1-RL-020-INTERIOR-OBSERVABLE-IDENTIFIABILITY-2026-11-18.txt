II. RL-019 — INTERIOR-DOMAIN EXACT SYMMETRY TEST

Identifier: RL-019
Section: §17
Focus:
Test whether a single observable can exactly identify the parameters of a specified interior-domain model, and whether observed symmetries generate structural non-identifiability.

Model:

H(a,b) = aA + bB

A = ZZZ

Initial state:
rho* = |psi*><psi*|

where:
|psi*> = (1,2,...,8) / sqrt(204)

Heisenberg evolution and exact symbolic formulas are used for the observables in the tested set.

A. AUDIT RESULT REPORTED IN THE SOURCE

All eight closed formulas from §17.4 were independently re-derived using exact symbolic/rational arithmetic, according to the supplied research record.

Reported outcome:

YYZ correction:

Previous expression:
25ab/(51r^2)[cos(2rt)-1]

IV. RL-020 — MULTI-OBSERVABLE EXACT IDENTIFICATION TEST

Identifier: RL-020
Section: §17
Relation to RL-019:
Direct continuation of the identification question, with a distinct result.

Focus:
Test whether a combination of more than one observable removes degeneracies found in RL-019.

Model:

H(a,b) = aA + bB

with the same fixed initial state:
rho* = |psi*><psi*|

Selected observables:
O1 = YYY
O2 = XXY

A. EXACT SIGNAL FORMULAS

For:
r = sqrt(a^2+b^2)

the reported formulas are:

f_YYY(t) = -(10a/(17r)) sin(2rt)

f_XXY(t) = -(8a-9b)/(51r) sin(2rt)

Define:

alpha = a/r
beta = b/r
q = (8a-9b)/r

Then:

q = 8alpha - 9beta

B. IDENTIFICATION ARGUMENT

Assume equality of both complete time signals:

f_YYY(t) = f'_YYY(t)
f_XXY(t) = f'_XXY(t)

Under the stated conditions and Assumption A8, the supplied derivation reports:

1. The common oscillation frequency determines r = r'.
2. The YYY amplitude determines alpha = alpha'.
3. The XXY amplitude determines q = q'.
4. Since q = 8alpha - 9beta, equality of q and alpha determines beta = beta'.
5. From a = r alpha and b = r beta, it follows that:
   a = a'
   b = b'.

Therefore, for the stated model and assumptions:

(a,b) = (a',b').

C. ASSUMPTION A8

The complete time signal must be available over an unbounded or sufficiently long interval so that the frequency:

2r

can be uniquely extracted.

Without A8, exact global identification does not follow automatically.

A finite or noisy observation window defines a different identification problem and requires a separate analysis.

D. REPORTED CHECKS

The supplied record reports checks for:
- sign-of-b ambiguity;
- same-r degeneracy;
- scaling degeneracy;
- global-sign ambiguity;
- special case a = 0;
- special case q = 0.

Additional numerical checks reported:
- 200 random inversion tests;
- a 79 × 79 parameter grid;
- 401 time points;

These numerical checks are supporting evidence only. They are not, by themselves, a proof of universality.

E. RL-020 MAIN RESULT

For the specific model:

H(a,b) = aA + bB

with the fixed initial state rho*, and for:

r > 0,

the pair of observables:

YYY + XXY

is reported to identify the parameters (a,b) exactly from the complete exact time signals, provided Assumption A8 holds.

Thus, the two-observable combination removes the specific degeneracies established in the single-observable analysis under the stated conditions.

F. CRITICAL LIMITATIONS

The result does not establish:
- that two observables are the minimum possible number;
- that every pair of observables works;
- validity for other initial states;
- validity for other Hamiltonians;
- robustness under noise;
- exact identification from a finite observation window;
- a universal OMEGA identification theorem;
- universal observable completeness;
- a universal implication from observable identification to semantic legitimacy.
