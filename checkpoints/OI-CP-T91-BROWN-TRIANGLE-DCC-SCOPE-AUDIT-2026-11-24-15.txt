2. BROWN SOURCE: EXACT DIAGRAM SHAPE
============================================================

Primary source:
Kenneth S. Brown,
"The Geometry of Finitely Presented Infinite Simple Groups",
1992, Sections 1 and 6, especially pp. 123-124 and 133-134.

Source:
https://pi.math.cornell.edu/~kbrown/scan/1992.0000.0121.pdf

Brown's triangle-of-groups diagram has:
- three vertex groups A, B, C;
- three edge groups U, V, W;
- monomorphisms from each edge group into its two incident vertex groups.

It is a six-object diagram with six edge-to-vertex arrows.

Brown separately defines a stronger condition called fillability, which adds a central group K and further monomorphisms/intersection-square conditions. Fillability is not the same thing as the six-object triangle diagram and is not required merely to state the direct-limit diagram.

For the example:
A, B, C are isomorphic to S5, S6, S7;
U, V, W are isomorphic to S4, S5, S3 x S2.

The maps are injective. Brown proves the triangle is realizable, meaning:
- each vertex group embeds in the direct limit;
- each edge group maps onto the intersection of the corresponding two vertex-group images.

The direct limit is Thompson's infinite simple group G.

Brown's corollary proves that the finite amalgam H formed by the union of the vertex groups, with the induced partial multiplication, is not embeddable in any finite group.

The proof is universal:
If H embedded in a finite group Q, the universal property of the direct limit would induce a homomorphism G -> Q whose restriction to each vertex group is injective.
Since G is infinite and simple, every homomorphism from G to a finite group is trivial.
That contradicts injectivity on the nontrivial vertex groups.

This rules out every finite common target for the complete six-object triangle, not merely a particular pushout candidate.

3. PROPER TWO-VERTEX SUBDIAGRAMS
============================================================

Each individual edge subdiagram is a span:
A <- U -> B,
B <- V -> C,
or
C <- W -> A.

Each consists of two finite groups and a common finite subgroup, with injective maps.

For a span of finite groups with injective maps, the ordinary amalgamated free product is virtually free and residually finite. A finite quotient can therefore be chosen that preserves injectivity on both finite vertex groups.

Thus each individual two-vertex edge amalgam has a finite common completion.

The Brown obstruction appears only when all three edge identifications are imposed simultaneously around the full triangle.

Classification:
- each individual edge span: finite common completion exists;
- full three-vertex triangle: no finite common completion;
- full triangle: an infinite exact-intersection realization exists.

The no-finite-target result is a weak-amalgamation obstruction. It therefore also excludes finite strong witnesses.

6. CONSEQUENCE FOR R
============================================================

Three statements must remain separate:

A. FINITE COMMON WITNESS:
For Brown's full triangle, no finite group target exists.

B. COMMON WITNESS:
An infinite common target exists, namely Thompson's infinite simple group.

C. COLIMIT:
In the category of groups with homomorphisms, the direct limit exists and is the infinite group G.

Thus Brown does not demonstrate failure of all amalgamation or failure of the colimit construction.

It demonstrates failure of finite target realization for this specific finite diagram in the category of groups with injective edge maps.

OMEGA-ISOP does not currently state that R must be finite. Consequently, Brown does not refute the general existence of R.

If a future OMEGA-ISOP realization imposes a finite-target requirement for all finite diagrams and admits the Brown triangle, that requirement would be contradicted.

If R may be infinite, Brown is compatible with the mere existence of R in the group test model.

If R is an alternative constraint-preserving representation rather than a categorical colimit, its adequacy must be checked against the source-prescribed information-preservation condition. Brown does not settle that question.

7. CONSEQUENCE FOR THEOREM 91
============================================================

The M3.2 v1.5 handoff states Theorem 91 as a conditional non-expansion result.

Its assumptions include:
H1: R exists, T is admissible, and R is preserved.
H2: Y is normed.

Resp_(T(M))(a) = Resp_M(tau(a)).

Brown's finite-target obstruction does not directly contradict these assumptions.

Reason:
- Brown's example concerns existence of finite common targets for a specific group diagram.
- H1 requires existence and preservation of R, not finiteness of R.
- Brown's infinite direct limit supplies a possible R in the group-category test model.
- Brown says nothing about a normed space Y, operational map tau, response identity, or the admissibility of an evolution T in OMEGA-ISOP.

Therefore:
THEOREM 91: NOT REFUTED.
THEOREM 91: NOT PROVED BY BROWN.
THEOREM 91: REMAINS CONDITIONAL ON H1-H4.
