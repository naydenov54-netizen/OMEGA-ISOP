============================================================
1. AUDIT TARGET AND RESULT
============================================================

Audit target:
S3.23 Theorem 6.1, its side-locality lemma, and the
fixed spanning-tree side-coherence hypothesis.

Main result:

The proof mechanism in S3.23 is valid under its stated
free relational amalgam assumptions.

However, S3.23 requires every atom of the relational body
to be connected through one fixed spanning tree whose
every edge has an overlap variable assigned outside C
under every body-satisfying assignment.

That requirement is stronger than the proof actually needs.

The proof only needs all relational body atoms whose tuples
contain an element outside C to be connected, for each
body-satisfying assignment. Atoms whose entire tuple lies
inside C are neutral interface atoms. They need not be
connected to the exclusive atoms.

Moreover, positive equality premises can connect variable
positions that are syntactically distinct. Their equality
closure can be used to identify an outside-C bridge.

The refined result is called ACTIVE-EXCLUSIVE
SIDE-COHERENCE.

This is still a sufficient condition, not a necessary and
sufficient characterization of free-amalgam preservation.


============================================================
2. RECONSTRUCTION OF THE S3.23 PROOF
============================================================

Let A and B be structures with common induced substructure C
and A intersect B = C.

Let D be their free relational amalgam:
- domain is A union B;
- each relation in D is the union of its interpretations
  in A and B.

For any relational tuple in D containing an element outside
C, that tuple cannot belong to both side structures.
It has a unique side of support.

Reason:
If a tuple belonged to both A and B, all its coordinates
would belong to A intersect B = C.

Thus an atom with a tuple containing an element outside C
has a unique side label, A or B.

S3.23 uses a fixed tree of syntactic atom overlaps to show
that adjacent atoms have the same side label. Connectedness
then propagates one label through all atoms.

This argument is correct.

The audit finds no circular inference in that propagation.
The side label is obtained from the free-union semantics
and the outside-C coordinate, not from the Horn head.

The audit does identify two avoidable restrictions:
1. atoms whose tuples are wholly in C are treated as if they
   must participate in the connecting tree;
2. the connecting tree is fixed before the assignment,
   although the proof only needs connectivity for the
   particular assignment currently under consideration.

3. REFINED DEFINITIONS
============================================================

Let a clause have relational body atoms:

  A1, A2, ..., Ak

and positive equality premises.

Assume every clause variable occurs in at least one
relational body atom.

For a body-satisfying assignment s into D, call atom Ai
C-EXCLUSIVE under s if its relational tuple contains at
least one element outside C.

Equivalently, there is a variable v occurring in Ai such
that s(v) is outside C.

Let E_s be the set of all C-exclusive relational body atoms.

Define the ACTIVE-EXCLUSIVE OVERLAP GRAPH G_s:

- vertices are exactly the atoms in E_s;
- two distinct vertices Ai and Aj are adjacent if there
  exist variable positions in Ai and Aj that are identified
  by the equality premises and whose common assigned value
  lies outside C.

For the version without equality premises, identification
means simply that the same variable occurs in both atoms.

For positive equality premises, let ≡ be the equivalence
relation on clause variables generated by all equality
premises in the body.

Because the assignment satisfies the equality premises:

  u ≡ v  implies  s(u) = s(v).

An edge in G_s exists when some variable u in Ai and some
variable v in Aj satisfy:

  u ≡ v
  and
  s(u) is outside C.

DEFINITION 3.1 - ACTIVE-EXCLUSIVE SIDE-COHERENCE

A clause is active-exclusive side-coherent on the span
A <- C -> B if, for every assignment s into D satisfying
all relational and equality premises of the body:

Either:

  E_s is empty,

or:

  G_s is connected.

A graph with one vertex is connected by convention.

The condition is assignment-relative. It does not require
one fixed spanning tree to work for every assignment.

It does not inspect the Horn head.


============================================================
4. REFINED SIDE-LOCALITY LEMMA
============================================================

LEMMA 4.1

Under the assumptions above, if the clause is
active-exclusive side-coherent, then every body-satisfying
assignment s has its entire range contained in A or in B.

PROOF

Case 1: E_s is empty.

Every relational body tuple has all coordinates in C.

Every clause variable occurs in a relational body atom.
Therefore every variable is assigned a value in C.

Hence:

  range(s) is a subset of C.

Since C is a substructure of both A and B, the assignment
is contained in both sides.

Case 2: E_s is nonempty.

Every atom in E_s has a tuple containing an element
outside C. Its tuple therefore has a unique side of support.

Take an edge Ai-Aj in G_s.

By definition, there are variables u in Ai and v in Aj
such that u ≡ v and s(u) is outside C.

The equality premises are satisfied, so:

  s(u) = s(v).

Thus both relational tuples contain the same element
outside C.

Both tuples must have the same unique side of support.

Therefore adjacent vertices of G_s have the same side label.

Since G_s is connected, every atom in E_s has the same
side label, either A or B.

Now take any variable x of the clause.

By variable coverage, x occurs in some relational body atom.

If s(x) is outside C, that atom is C-exclusive and hence
belongs to E_s. Its value must lie in the common side
identified above.

If s(x) is in C, it belongs to both A and B.

Thus every assigned variable value belongs to the same
side.

Consequently:

  range(s) is a subset of A

or:

  range(s) is a subset of B.

QED.


============================================================
5. REFINED PRESERVATION THEOREM
============================================================

THEOREM 5.1

Let A and B be models of a positive universal Horn theory T.

Assume:
1. A and B have common induced substructure C;
2. A intersect B = C;
3. D is their free relational amalgam;
4. every clause variable occurs in a relational body atom;
5. every clause is active-exclusive side-coherent.

Then D satisfies T.

The clause head may be:
- a positive relational atom;
- a positive equality;
- BOTTOM.

PROOF

Take an arbitrary clause and assignment s into D.

If the complete body is false, the implication is true.

Suppose the complete body is true.

By Lemma 4.1, the assignment range is contained in A
or contained in B.

Assume it is contained in A.

Every relational premise true in D under s is true in A,
because the relations of A are included in D and the
assignment is wholly in A.

Every positive equality premise true in D remains true
in A, since equality is actual identity.

Thus the complete body is true in A.

Since A satisfies the Horn clause, its head is true in A,
or the body is impossible when the head is BOTTOM.

A relational head true in A remains true in D.
An equality head remains true because identity is unchanged.
A BOTTOM head cannot have a true body in A.

The argument is identical if the assignment is contained
in B.

Therefore every clause is satisfied in D.

QED.

6. STRICT WEAKENING: A COUNTEREXAMPLE TO THE NECESSITY
OF THE S3.23 FIXED-TREE CONDITION
============================================================

Consider three relational body atoms:

  A1 = R(x,y)
  A2 = S(y,z)
  A3 = T(x,w)

and a relational head:

  H = U(x).

There are no equality premises.

Choose C = {c,d}.

Let:

  A domain = {a,c,d}
  R_A = {(a,c)}
  S_A = {(c,d)}
  T_A = {(a,d)}
  U_A = {a}

  B domain = {c,d,b}
  R_B = empty
  S_B = {(c,d)}
  T_B = empty
  U_B = empty

The common induced substructure is C.
The free amalgam is the union structure.

Both A and B satisfy the clause:
- in A, the active body assignment has x=a and U(a);
- in B, R is empty, so the body is never fully active.

In the amalgam, the only active assignment has:

  x=a, y=c, z=d, w=d.

The exclusive atoms are A1 and A3:
- R(a,c) contains a outside C;
- T(a,d) contains a outside C.

They are connected by their shared variable x, assigned
outside C.

A2 is not exclusive:
- S(c,d) is wholly inside C.

Thus E_s = {A1,A3} and G_s is connected.

The refined condition holds.

But the full S3.23 body-overlap graph has edges:
- A1-A2 through y, assigned c in C;
- A1-A3 through x, assigned a outside C;
- A2-A3 through z/w only if variables are syntactically
  shared; here they are not.

The full graph is connected through A1, but any spanning
tree containing A1-A2 fails the S3.23 outside-C edge
condition. The only possible tree must include A1-A2,
whose overlap value is in C.

Therefore S3.23's fixed-tree condition fails while the
refined condition holds.

This demonstrates a genuine strict weakening.

The example does not prove that the refined condition is
necessary. It proves only that S3.23 excludes some safe
instances for reasons not required by the side-locality
argument.

7. EQUALITY BRIDGES: STRICT EXTENSION BEYOND SYNTACTIC
VARIABLE OVERLAP
============================================================

Consider the body:

  R(x) AND S(y) AND x=y

and head:

  U(x).

The relational atoms R(x) and S(y) share no syntactic
variable.

The S3.23 overlap graph therefore has two disconnected
vertices.

However, every body-satisfying assignment satisfies:

  s(x) = s(y).

If this common value lies outside C, both tuples have the
same unique side of support.

The equality-closure graph connects the two exclusive
atoms, and active-exclusive side-coherence can apply.

If the common value lies in C, both tuples are entirely
inside C for these unary atoms, so E_s is empty.

This is a valid bridge because equality is a positive
premise and is required to hold in the assignment.
No disequality or negative premise is used.

The equality-closure extension is therefore sound under
the stated positive-equality assumptions.

It does not license:
- disequality premises;
- negated relational premises;
- function terms;
- arbitrary quotient identifications not entailed by
  positive equality premises.

8. THE MIXED-TRIGGER COUNTERMODEL STILL DEFEATS
UNRESTRICTED CONNECTIVITY
============================================================

Take:

  R(x,y) AND S(y,z) IMPLIES T(x,z).

Let:

  C = {c}
  A = {a,c}, with R_A = {(a,c)}
  B = {c,b}, with S_B = {(c,b)}

and let T be empty on both sides.

Both A and B satisfy the clause.

In the free amalgam:

  R(a,c)
  S(c,b)
  not T(a,b)

The active-exclusive atoms are:
- R(x,y), exclusive to A;
- S(y,z), exclusive to B.

Their only shared variable y is assigned c in C.
Therefore there is no edge in G_s.

G_s has two disconnected vertices.

The refined condition correctly fails.

Thus merely requiring the ordinary syntactic body-overlap
graph to be connected is not enough. The connectivity must
be witnessed by a shared value outside C, or by a positive
equality class carrying such a value.

9. MULTIWAY FREE AMALGAM AUDIT
============================================================

Let {A_i : i in I} be a finite family of structures with
a common induced substructure C and pairwise intersections:

  A_i intersect A_j = C  whenever i != j.

Let D be the free relational amalgam whose domain is the
union of all A_i and whose relations are the unions of
the side relations.

For any tuple containing an element outside C, its side
of support is unique.

Define E_s as before.

Define G_s with an edge between exclusive atoms when
some common variable, or positive-equality-equivalent
variable positions, is assigned an element outside C.

If E_s is nonempty and G_s is connected, all exclusive
atoms have the same side label A_j.

Every variable assigned outside C occurs in an exclusive
atom and therefore belongs to A_j.

Every value in C belongs to every A_i.

Hence the entire assignment range is contained in A_j.

If E_s is empty, all variable values lie in C.

The proof of Theorem 5.1 then applies using A_j.

RESULT:

The refined side-locality lemma and preservation theorem
extend to finite multiway free amalgams over a common C,
provided all pairwise intersections are exactly C.

Boundary:
If the diagram has intersections outside C, a tuple
containing an element outside C may have more than one
possible side of support. The unique-side argument fails.
No multiway theorem is claimed for such diagrams.

10. CYCLES AND TREE CHOICE
============================================================

The refined condition uses connectivity of G_s, not a
preselected spanning tree.

For each individual assignment s, if G_s is connected,
a spanning tree can be selected for that assignment.

Different assignments may require different spanning trees.

This does not create circularity:
the proof fixes one assignment, constructs its graph,
and uses any spanning tree of that graph to propagate
the side labels.

Cycles add no additional requirement for this proof.
A connected graph remains connected after choosing any
spanning tree.

Therefore requiring all overlap edges, or requiring every
cycle edge to carry an outside-C witness, is unnecessarily
strong.

The proof needs connectivity, not cycle-completeness.


============================================================
11. LIMITS AND UNRESOLVED QUESTIONS
============================================================

The active-exclusive condition is still semantic and
assignment-relative.

It is not automatically a finite syntactic test for an
arbitrary class of structures.

For a fixed finite span with finite domains, it can be
checked by enumerating body-satisfying assignments.

For an infinite class of spans, a separate structural
argument is needed to establish the condition uniformly.

The condition remains sufficient, not necessary:
a disconnected exclusive graph may still yield a model
of the Horn clause if the head is already true in D.

The condition also does not handle:
- negative relational premises;
- disequality premises;
- function symbols;
- non-free amalgam relation interpretations;
- diagrams with pairwise intersections larger than C;
- arbitrary quotient pullback of equality-generating heads.

Pullback remains a separate proof obligation.
For equality heads u=v, the clause-specific fiber condition
from S3.23 remains necessary for the stated inverse-image
pullback argument.

12. ADVERSARIAL LOOP AUDIT
============================================================

TARGET:
Prove preservation under active-exclusive side-coherence.

DEPENDENCY CHAIN:
1. A body-satisfying assignment is fixed.
2. Each exclusive relational tuple has a unique side.
3. An active graph edge identifies an outside-C value.
4. Equality premises, when used, force equal values.
5. Adjacent exclusive atoms therefore have the same side.
6. Connectivity forces all exclusive atoms to one side.
7. Every outside-C variable occurs in an exclusive atom.
8. Every other variable value lies in C.
9. The entire assignment lies in one side.
10. The Horn clause is satisfied in that side.
11. Its head remains true in the free amalgam.

CIRCULARITY CHECK:
The graph is defined from body atoms, C, equality premises,
and the fixed assignment. It does not use the Horn head
or the desired conclusion.

CONCLUSION-SMUGGLING CHECK:
No assumption that the head is true in D is made.
The head is obtained only by applying the fact that the
chosen side is a model of the clause.

INDEPENDENCE CHECK:
The mixed R/S countermodel has disconnected exclusive
graph and falsifies the head in D.

STRICTNESS CHECK:
The neutral-interface example satisfies the refined
condition but fails the S3.23 fixed-tree condition.

EQUALITY CHECK:
Positive equality premises create legitimate bridges
because their truth forces actual equality of assigned
values. No such inference is made for disequality.

MULTIWAY CHECK:
Unique side support follows from pairwise intersections
exactly C. The proof does not silently assume two sides.

CLASSIFICATION:

NON-CIRCULAR CONDITIONAL DERIVATION
under the stated free-amalgam, induced-substructure,
variable-coverage, and positive-equality assumptions.
