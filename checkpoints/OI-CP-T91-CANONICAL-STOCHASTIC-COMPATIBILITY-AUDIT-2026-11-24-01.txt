2. CANONICAL ARCHITECTURE: WHAT IS ACTUALLY DEFINED
============================================================

Volume II defines:
Phi : Real(R) -> M

For a selected realization with state space X:
X_R = {x in X | x satisfies the constraints induced by R}.

Admissible dynamics are:
A(R;Phi) subseteq Evol(X).

The selected realization is dynamically admissible only if:
A(R;Phi) is nonempty.

For deterministic dynamics, Volume II identifies compatibility-preserving maps through:
T in End(X,R)
for irreversible evolution, and:
T in Aut(X,R)
for reversible evolution.

For stochastic evolution, Volume II defines a Markov kernel:
kappa : X x Sigma_X -> [0,1]
with the standard probability and measurability conditions.

Volume II also defines the Markov operator:
K_kappa f(x) = integral_X f(y) kappa(x,dy).

Volume III independently lists stochastic dynamics as a possible class of realized dynamics and repeats the Markov-kernel definition. It does not state that every Markov kernel is automatically admissible for every R or every Phi.

Volume V fixes the notation for the realization map, admissible dynamics, stochastic structure, observable space and operational distance.

SOURCE-LEVEL CONCLUSION:
A Markov kernel is an available mathematical representation of stochastic dynamics in a selected realization. The sources do not establish:
- every Markov kernel belongs to A(R;Phi);
- every Markov kernel preserves every relation in R;
- every candidate enriched probabilistic relational model is selected by Phi;
- every canonical operational probe belongs to a measurable function space on X.

Therefore:
MARKOV-KERNEL FORMALISM EXISTS
does not imply
UNRESTRICTED MARKOV-KERNEL ADMISSIBILITY.

3. H1: STOCHASTIC STRUCTURAL PRESERVATION
============================================================

For deterministic T, the relational endomorphism condition is pointwise:
rho_i(x1,...,xn) => rho_i(T(x1),...,T(xn)).

A Markov kernel does not select one successor state. It supplies a probability distribution. Consequently, the deterministic condition cannot be transferred to kappa merely by replacing T(x) with kappa(x,.).

A probabilistic preservation statement needs an explicit relational lifting.

The previously audited candidates remain distinct:

A. Independent product-kernel preservation:
For every xbar in rho:
kappa^(n)(xbar,rho) = 1.

This is precise but may be too restrictive. For the equality relation, independent successor samples with identical marginals coincide almost surely only when the common marginal is Dirac.

B. Coupling-based preservation:
For each tuple xbar=(x1,...,xn) in rho, require a coupling pi_xbar with marginals:
Marginal_i(pi_xbar) = kappa(xi,.)
and:
pi_xbar(rho) = 1.

This permits correlated successor coordinates and can preserve stochastic marginals. However, separate coupling existence for each relation or tuple does not imply one globally coherent joint transition kernel.

C. Measurable coherent lift:
A full stochastic relational realization would require a measurable assignment of joint kernels, with correct marginals, support on the required relations, and compatibility across overlapping relations and coordinate restrictions.

The inspected canonical sources do not define this enriched object or establish that Phi admits it.

============================================================
4. FINITE COHERENCE OBSTRUCTION
============================================================

This finite example shows that pairwise coupling feasibility does not guarantee global joint feasibility.

Let:
X = {0,1}.

Let each of three coordinates have the same marginal:
mu_1 = mu_2 = mu_3 = Bernoulli(1/2).

Define three pairwise relations:
rho_12 = {(0,0),(1,1)}          (equality)
rho_23 = {(0,0),(1,1)}          (equality)
rho_13 = {(0,1),(1,0)}          (inequality)

Each pair separately admits a coupling with the prescribed Bernoulli(1/2) marginals:
- pi_12 assigns probability 1/2 to (0,0) and 1/2 to (1,1);
- pi_23 assigns probability 1/2 to (0,0) and 1/2 to (1,1);
- pi_13 assigns probability 1/2 to (0,1) and 1/2 to (1,0).

Thus each pairwise coupling is supported with probability one on its own relation.

But no joint probability measure pi_123 on X^3 can satisfy all three constraints with probability one:
X1 = X2,
X2 = X3,
X1 != X3.

The first two equalities imply X1 = X3, contradicting the third relation.

Therefore:
PAIRWISE COUPLING EXISTENCE
does not imply
GLOBAL COHERENT JOINT COUPLING EXISTENCE.

This is a finite obstruction to inferring global relational coherence from separate pairwise couplings. It does not refute every possible coherent-lift construction; it identifies a necessary compatibility condition that must be checked.

5. H3: OPERATIONAL CLOSURE REMAINS SEPARATE
============================================================

The canonical operational architecture defines:
Resp_M : L_op -> Y.

The existence of the Markov operator:
K_kappa f(x) = integral_X f(y) kappa(x,dy)
does not by itself establish a common map:
tau : L_op -> L_op.

For the specific measurable probability realization audited in the preceding checkpoint, H3 follows conditionally by setting:
tau(a) = K_kappa(a)
provided:
1. the same kappa is used for every model in the comparison class;
2. all required measurability and integrability conditions hold;
3. the response maps are represented by integration against the model measures;
4. K_kappa(L_op) subseteq L_op;
5. the transformed models remain in the selected comparison class.

The canonical sources do not identify every L_op with bounded measurable functions on X, nor do they assert the required closure for every realization.

Therefore:
H3 IS DERIVED IN THE SPECIFIED MEASURABLE MARKOV REALIZATION.
H3 IS NOT ESTABLISHED FOR EVERY CANONICAL REALIZATION.
