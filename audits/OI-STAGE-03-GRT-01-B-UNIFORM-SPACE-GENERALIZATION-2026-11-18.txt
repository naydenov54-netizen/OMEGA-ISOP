1. UNIFORM SPACES

A uniform space is a pair (X,U), where U is a uniformity on X. Members E of U are called entourages. They encode uniform closeness of pairs of points without requiring a numerical distance.

The uniformity axioms used here are:
(U1) Every E in U contains the diagonal Δ_X = {(x,x): x in X}.
(U2) If E is in U and E is a subset of F subset X×X, then F is in U.
(U3) If E1,E2 are in U, then E1 intersect E2 is in U.
(U4) For every E in U, there exists V in U such that V composed with V is a subset of E.
One may replace V by V intersect V inverse to obtain a symmetric entourage refinement.

For E subset X×X and A subset X, define:
E[A] = {x in X : there exists a in A such that (x,a) is in E}.

When E is symmetric, E[A] is the uniform E-neighborhood of A. The diagonal axiom implies A subset E[A].

A uniform space need not be separated. It is separated (Hausdorff as a uniform space) when the intersection of all entourages is exactly Δ_X. Separation is not required for the algebraic statements below, but may matter for uniqueness of limits.

2. GENERALIZED A1 — COVARIANCE

Let I be a nonempty parameter set, G a group, X and Y sets, and suppose G acts on X and Y through maps alpha_g:X->X and beta_g:Y->Y.

Let F_i:X->Y for each i in I.

Reconstructed A1:
For all i in I, g in G, and x in X:
F_i(alpha_g(x)) = beta_g(F_i(x)).

This is an algebraic covariance identity. It does not require a metric, topology, or uniformity.

If X and Y are uniform spaces, additional regularity conditions may be imposed:
(a) each alpha_g is uniformly continuous;
(b) each beta_g is uniformly continuous;
or, more strongly, the actions are jointly uniformly continuous under an explicitly specified product uniformity.

These regularity conditions do not follow from the covariance identity. Covariance alone is not a continuity theorem.

A1 RESULT:
PROVEN: the covariance identity is meaningful and unchanged in uniform spaces.
PROVEN: metric structure is unnecessary for the algebraic identity.
CONDITIONAL: preservation of uniform continuity under the actions requires separate uniform-continuity assumptions.
UNVERIFIED: whether the original OMEGA-ISOP A1 intended any topological or uniform regularity requirement. The original specification is not recovered.

3. GENERALIZED A2 — UNIFORM ATTRACTION

Let (X,U_X) be a uniform space, I a nonempty parameter set, A a nonempty subset of X, B a nonempty subset of X, and T_i:X->X for every i in I.

For n in the nonnegative integers, T_i^n denotes n-fold iteration, with T_i^0 the identity.

Reconstructed uniform A2:
For every entourage E in U_X, there exists N in the nonnegative integers such that for all i in I and all n>=N:
T_i^n(B) subset E[A].

This is uniform attraction of B toward A in the uniform-neighborhood sense.

The quantifier order is essential:
For every E, there exists one N, such that for every i and every n>=N the inclusion holds.

This is stronger than pointwise-in-parameter attraction:
For every i and every E, there exists N(i,E), such that for every n>=N(i,E):
T_i^n(B) subset E[A].

The latter allows the convergence threshold to depend on i.

3.1 Finite-parameter theorem

Assume I is finite. Suppose that for every i in I, every entourage E, there exists N_i(E) such that:
for all n>=N_i(E), T_i^n(B) subset E[A].

Then uniform A2 holds.

Proof:
Fix an arbitrary entourage E. For each i in the finite set I, choose N_i(E). Define N=max{N_i(E):i in I}. Since I is finite, this maximum exists. For every i in I and n>=N, we have n>=N_i(E), hence T_i^n(B) subset E[A]. This is exactly uniform A2.

RESULT: PROVEN.

3.2 Infinite-parameter countermodel

Let X=R with its usual metric uniformity.
Let A={0}, B={1}, and I={1,2,3,...}.
For each i define:
T_i(x) = (1 - 1/(i+1))x.

For every fixed i, the coefficient q_i=1-1/(i+1) satisfies 0<q_i<1. Therefore:
T_i^n(1)=q_i^n -> 0 as n->infinity.
Thus each parameter value attracts B to A individually.

However, uniform A2 fails. Choose the entourage:
E = {(x,y) in R×R : |x-y|<1/2}.
Then E[A]=(-1/2,1/2).

For any proposed common N, choose i sufficiently large that:
(1 - 1/(i+1))^N >= 1/2.
Then T_i^N(1) is not in E[A]. Thus no single N works for all i.

RESULT:
COUNTERMODEL: individual attraction for every fixed parameter does not imply uniform attraction over an infinite parameter family, even on R with its standard metric uniformity.
This confirms the same logical gap found in the earlier metric reconstruction; it is not a new claim about the original specification.

3.3 Boundary cases

If B is empty, A2 holds vacuously. Therefore B nonempty is imposed to avoid a vacuous result.
If A is empty and B is nonempty, E[A] is empty for every entourage E, so A2 cannot hold. Therefore A nonempty is imposed.
If A=X, then E[A]=X for every entourage E because every entourage contains the diagonal. A2 is then automatic and carries no attraction information.
If I is empty, the universal quantifier over i is vacuous. Therefore I is assumed nonempty.
For finite I, individual attraction implies uniform attraction by the theorem above.
For infinite I, the countermodel shows that this implication fails in general.

3.4 Relation to metric attraction

For a metric space with its metric uniformity, entourages include sets of the form:
E_epsilon = {(x,y): d(x,y)<epsilon}, epsilon>0.

The uniform-neighborhood condition becomes:
For every epsilon>0, there exists N such that for all i and n>=N:
T_i^n(B) subset {x : there exists a in A with d(x,a)<epsilon}.

This is a neighborhood-of-A formulation. It should not be silently identified with every possible convention for distance from a set, especially where infimum distances, closures, or attainment of the infimum differ. Equivalence to the exact original metric A2 remains UNVERIFIED until the original definition and its conventions are recovered.

4. GENERALIZED A3 — UNIFORM EQUICONTINUITY

Let (X,U_X) and (Y,U_Y) be uniform spaces, and let {F_i:X->Y : i in I} be a family of maps.

Reconstructed A3:
For every entourage V in U_Y, there exists an entourage U in U_X such that for every i in I and every x,x' in X:
if (x,x') is in U, then (F_i(x),F_i(x')) is in V.

The same input entourage U must work for every member of the family. This is equicontinuity of the family with respect to the specified uniform structures.

This definition is independent of a numerical modulus and therefore applies beyond metric spaces.

For a single map, the corresponding condition is uniform continuity. Family equicontinuity is stronger than requiring each F_i to be uniformly continuous separately when I is infinite.

4.1 Finite-family theorem

If I is finite and every F_i is uniformly continuous, then the family is equicontinuous.

Proof:
Fix V in U_Y. For each i, uniform continuity gives an entourage U_i in U_X such that (x,x') in U_i implies (F_i(x),F_i(x')) in V. Since I is finite, U = intersection over i in I of U_i is an entourage. Membership in U implies membership in every U_i, so the same U works for every i. Therefore the family is equicontinuous.

RESULT: PROVEN.

4.2 Countermodel: individual continuity is insufficient

Let X=Y=R with the usual metric uniformity and I={1,2,3,...}.
Define:
F_i(x)=i*x.

Each F_i is continuous and uniformly continuous. Also F_i(0)=0 for every i.

The family is not equicontinuous at 0. Choose the target entourage:
V = {(y,y') : |y-y'|<1}.

Suppose a common input entourage U existed. Since U is an entourage of the usual metric uniformity, it contains some metric entourage E_delta for a delta>0. Choose x=0 and x'=delta/2. Then (x,x') is in U. Choose i>2/delta. We obtain:
|F_i(x)-F_i(x')| = i*delta/2 > 1.
This contradicts the required inclusion in V.

RESULT:
COUNTERMODEL: individual continuity, and even individual uniform continuity, does not imply equicontinuity of an infinite family.

4.3 Boundary cases

If X has the discrete uniformity, the diagonal Δ_X is an entourage. Taking U=Δ_X makes every family of maps from X into any uniform space equicontinuous. This is a structural consequence of the discrete domain uniformity, not a general property of discrete-looking notation.
If I is finite and all maps are uniformly continuous, equicontinuity follows.
If I is infinite, individual uniform continuity is insufficient, as shown above.
A3 depends on the chosen uniformities, not merely on the underlying sets.

4.4 Same topology, different uniformities

A uniform structure contains information not determined by topology alone.

On R, consider:
d(x,y)=|x-y|
rho(x,y)=|arctan(x)-arctan(y)|.

Both metrics induce the usual topology on R. However, their induced uniformities differ.

The identity map id:(R,d)->(R,rho) is uniformly continuous because:
rho(x,y) <= |x-y|.

The reverse identity id:(R,rho)->(R,d) is not uniformly continuous. For example, take x_n=n and y_n=n+1. Then:
rho(x_n,y_n) -> 0,
while d(x_n,y_n)=1 for every n.

Thus the same topology can support distinct uniform structures. Consequently, a general A3 statement based only on topology cannot replace the uniform-space definition without extra structure.

RESULT: PROVEN.

5. NECESSITY VERSUS SUFFICIENCY

A1:
The covariance equality is the exact algebraic criterion under the reconstructed definition. Equivariant factorization remains a sufficient construction, not a necessary one. Uniform continuity of actions is an additional regularity property, not implied by covariance.

A2:
The uniform-neighborhood convergence statement is the exact criterion under reconstructed uniform A2. A common contraction estimate can be sufficient when a common invariant target, a common contraction factor q<1, and suitable boundedness or initial-set control are available. Such contraction assumptions are not necessary for uniform attraction in general.
No general necessity theorem for contraction is established here.

A3:
The common-entourage condition is the exact criterion under reconstructed uniform A3. A common Lipschitz bound is sufficient in normed metric settings, but is not necessary. In a general uniform space, a Lipschitz constant may not even be defined.
For finite families, individual uniform continuity is sufficient. For infinite families, it is not.

6. ROBUSTNESS FINDINGS

PROVEN:
1. A1 covariance is algebraic and does not require metric or uniform structure.
2. A2 has a coherent uniform-neighborhood formulation.
3. Finite parameter families convert individual attraction into uniform attraction when each parameter has the stated attraction property.
4. Infinite parameter families can fail uniform attraction despite individual attraction.
5. A3 has a precise uniform-space formulation through entourages.
6. Finite families of uniformly continuous maps are equicontinuous.
7. Infinite families of individually uniformly continuous maps need not be equicontinuous.
8. A topology alone does not uniquely determine a uniform structure in general; the two metrics d and rho above induce the same topology but different uniformities.

CONDITIONAL:
1. Uniform continuity of symmetry actions requires explicit assumptions.
2. Common contraction estimates imply uniform attraction only under the associated invariance and boundedness hypotheses.
3. Common Lipschitz estimates imply equicontinuity only in settings where the relevant metrics or norms and common bound are defined.

COUNTERMODEL:
1. The family T_i(x)=(1-1/(i+1))x disproves individual attraction => uniform attraction for infinite I.
2. The family F_i(x)=i*x disproves individual uniform continuity => family equicontinuity for infinite I.

RECONSTRUCTED:
1. Uniform A2 is defined using E[A].
2. Uniform A3 is defined by a common input entourage for each target entourage.
3. These are working generalizations, not recovered original axioms.

UNVERIFIED:
1. Equivalence of uniform A2 to the original Stage 3 A2.
2. Whether the original A3 used equicontinuity, uniform continuity, a common modulus, or another family-control condition.
3. Whether the original A1 imposed regularity on group actions.
4. Whether the A4 anchor condition admits a meaningful uniform-space formulation.
5. Stability of the earlier A1–A3 independence constructions under all uniform-space generalizations.
