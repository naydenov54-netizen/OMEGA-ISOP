==================================================
1. SCOPE
==================================================

Let X be a set, I a set of indices, and V_i a set for each i in I.
For every i in I, let f_i : X -> V_i be a total function.

Define the combined signature:

F : X -> product_{i in I} V_i
F(x) = (f_i(x))_{i in I}

Define:

D : X x X -> {0,1}

D(x,y)=1 iff F(x) != F(y).
D(x,y)=0 iff F(x) = F(y).

The property under audit is cotransitivity:

D(x,y)=1 => D(x,z)=1 OR D(z,y)=1.

==================================================
2. PROOF
==================================================

Assume D(x,y)=1.

By definition, F(x) != F(y). Since equality in a Cartesian product is coordinatewise, there exists i in I such that:

f_i(x) != f_i(y).

Fix such an index i.

By the law of excluded middle in classical logic, either:

f_i(x) != f_i(z),

or:

f_i(x) = f_i(z).

Case 1:
If f_i(x) != f_i(z), then F(x) != F(z), hence D(x,z)=1.

Case 2:
If f_i(x) = f_i(z), then, using f_i(x) != f_i(y) and substitution of equals, f_i(z) != f_i(y). Hence F(z) != F(y), so D(z,y)=1.

Thus in either case:

D(x,z)=1 OR D(z,y)=1.

Therefore:

D(x,y)=1 => D(x,z)=1 OR D(z,y)=1.

The proof is valid in classical set theory.

==================================================
3. ASSUMPTIONS AND DEPENDENCIES
==================================================

The proof uses:

1. Each f_i is a total function into the same codomain V_i for its index.
2. Equality on the Cartesian product is coordinatewise.
3. Inequality of two product elements yields at least one coordinate witnessing inequality.
4. Equality is substitutive and transitive.
5. The law of excluded middle is available to split the coordinate comparison into equality or inequality.
6. I is a set, so the indexed product is within the stated set-theoretic framework.

No finiteness of I is required.
No injectivity of F is required.
No non-emptiness of I is required.

Boundary case:
If I is empty, the indexed product is a singleton (the empty function), so F(x)=F(y) for all x,y and D is identically zero. Cotransitivity then holds vacuously because its antecedent is never true.

==================================================
4. CLASSICAL VERSUS CONSTRUCTIVE SCOPE
==================================================

The proof is classical. In general constructive logic, one cannot assume:

a=b OR a!=b.

Also, in a constructive setting, the passage from inequality of product elements to an explicitly available coordinate witness requires an appropriate constructive interpretation of inequality and product equality.

Consequently, the classical proof does not by itself establish a general constructive cotransitivity theorem.

A constructive result would require additional conditions sufficient to extract a differing coordinate and to resolve the relevant coordinate comparison. Decidable equality of individual coordinates alone should not be treated as an automatic solution for an arbitrary infinite index set without checking the witness-extraction step.

Three distinct issues must remain separate:

- classical validity;
- constructive provability;
- algorithmic decidability of equality.

The stated cotransitivity implication follows from the candidate signature construction in classical set theory.

FORMAL RESULT:
For all x,y,z in X:

D(x,y)=1 => D(x,z)=1 OR D(z,y)=1.
