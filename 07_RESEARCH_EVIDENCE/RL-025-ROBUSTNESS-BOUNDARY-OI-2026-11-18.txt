INPUTS
General Relativity as the physical domain.
Realized physical configuration G.
Realized dynamics D.
Persistence criterion P.
Observable set Q.
Operational tolerance epsilon.
Identity rule IR.

Operational Identity represented as:
OI = F(G, D, P, Q, epsilon, IR)

ASSUMPTIONS / CONSTRAINTS
A1. The GR domain remains the exclusive research domain.
A2. Block-universe interpretations are excluded.
A3. Q is an operational specification and is not assumed to be uniquely selected by GR.
A4. IR is not assumed to be uniquely derived from GR dynamics.
A5. No identification criterion may be silently encoded into Q.
A6. epsilon may be set to zero to test exact equivalence.
A7. The test concerns robustness of Operational Identity, not ontological status.
A8. IR is Q-relative. For configurations X and Y it decides:
X ~OI Y iff |q(X) - q(Y)| <= epsilon
for every q in Q.

For epsilon = 0:
q(X) = q(Y)
for every q in Q.

For epsilon > 0, each observable q in Q is assumed to have a specified comparison metric, norm, or normalized scale under which |q(X) - q(Y)| <= epsilon is well-defined. The same epsilon is used only after this comparison convention has been fixed.

Within the construction, Q-values are the only operational channel through which IR distinguishes configurations.

A9. G, D, P, IR and epsilon are identical in both operational specifications. Only Q is varied.
A10. A and B are admissible observables of the GR domain and are well-defined on both O1 and O2. Their admissibility is not derived here and must be established independently for any concrete GR model.

FORMAL CONSTRUCTION
Let two operational specifications be:

S1 = (G, D, P, Q1, epsilon, IR)
S2 = (G, D, P, Q2, epsilon, IR)

where Q1 and Q2 are sets of observable channels, with:
Q1 = {A}
Q2 = {A, B}

and:
Q1 is a proper subset of Q2.

Formally:
Q1 ⊊ Q2

Consider two physical configurations O1 and O2 satisfying:
A(O1) = A(O2)
but:
B(O1) != B(O2)

For epsilon = 0:

Under S1:
O1 ~OI O2

Under S2:
O1 not~OI O2

For epsilon > 0, the same construction requires:
|A(O1) - A(O2)| <= epsilon
and:
|B(O1) - B(O2)| > epsilon

The primary test is epsilon = 0.

DERIVATION
Under Q1, only A contributes to the operational partition. Since A(O1) = A(O2), the condition of A8 holds for every q in Q1. Therefore O1 and O2 belong to the same operational equivalence class.

Under Q2, B is additionally observable. Since B(O1) != B(O2), the condition of A8 fails for q = B. Therefore O1 and O2 are separated.

The operational partition of the pair {O1, O2} is:

Under S1:
{{O1, O2}}

Under S2:
{{O1}, {O2}}

Thus, the partition under S2 strictly refines the partition under S1 on this pair.

Therefore:
F(G, D, P, Q1, epsilon, IR) != F(G, D, P, Q2, epsilon, IR)

That is:
OI(Q1) != OI(Q2)

with G, D, P, IR and epsilon identical.

The difference persists for epsilon = 0. Therefore the effect cannot be attributed merely to measurement noise or finite tolerance.

Because Q1 is a subset of Q2, equivalence under S2 implies equivalence under S1. The converse fails by the construction above.

Formally:
X ~S2 Y implies X ~S1 Y

but:
X ~S1 Y does not necessarily imply X ~S2 Y.

This implication holds because the comparison conditions imposed by Q1 are a subset of those imposed by Q2.

Consequently, identical G, D, P, IR and epsilon do not uniquely determine OI unless Q is independently fixed.

VERIFICATION
The counterconstruction preserves:
- the same physical domain;
- the same realized dynamics;
- the same persistence structure;
- the same identity rule;
- the same operational tolerance;
- exact operational comparison with epsilon = 0.

Only the observable specification Q is changed.

FAILURE TEST
Candidate rescue principle R1:
"Q must contain all physically relevant observables."

This does not independently solve the problem unless "physically relevant" is itself defined by a non-circular selection rule. If the rule selecting Q is constructed specifically to guarantee a desired operational partition, that rule functions as an additional IdentityRule or selection criterion rather than as a derivation from GR dynamics.

Candidate rescue principle R2:
"Nonzero epsilon absorbs the difference in B."

This does not rescue the universal claim because the primary construction holds at epsilon = 0.

For epsilon > 0, the counterexample remains valid whenever:
|A(O1) - A(O2)| <= epsilon
and:
|B(O1) - B(O2)| > epsilon.

Candidate rescue principle R3:
"Fix Q by an independently justified physical rule."

This removes the Q-dependence only by introducing an additional independent input. It is therefore compatible with, rather than contradictory to, the established boundary:

G + D + P + IR
does not uniquely determine OI unless Q is independently fixed.

RESULT
Operational Identity is not universally robust under admissible changes of the observable specification.

Established conditional boundary:
G + D + P + IR
does not uniquely determine OI
unless Q is independently fixed.

The result remains valid in the exact limit:
epsilon = 0

Therefore the demonstrated Q-dependence is structural rather than merely a measurement-error effect.

FAILURE BOUNDARIES
- Does not prove ontological independence of OI.
- Does not prove that every admissible Q produces different OI.
- Does not establish that Q is arbitrary in physical practice.
- Does not establish a unique physically preferred Q.
- Does not reduce OI to dynamics, persistence, or realization.
- Does not introduce a new universal IdentityRule.
- Does not cover identity rules that use information outside the Q-values specified by A8.
- Does not establish the physical existence of a particular pair of observables A and B for every GR solution.
- Does not establish that every enlargement Q1 -> Q2 changes the operational partition.

LIMITATIONS
The countermodel is conditional on:
- the existence of at least two admissible observable specifications Q1 and Q2 satisfying the stated separation condition;
- the existence of configurations O1 and O2 in the GR domain such that:
  A(O1) = A(O2)
  and:
  B(O1) != B(O2);
- A and B being physically admissible and well-defined observables for the concrete GR model under consideration;
- the Q-relative form of IR stated in A8;
- the assumption that G, D, P, IR and epsilon remain identical between S1 and S2.

The physical admissibility and relevance of particular observables must therefore be established independently for any concrete GR model.
