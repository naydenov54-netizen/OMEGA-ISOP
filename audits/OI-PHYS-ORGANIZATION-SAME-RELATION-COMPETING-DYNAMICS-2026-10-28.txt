TITLE
Same relational connectivity, different dynamics: a controlled comparison of organization measures

1. QUESTION

Which properties of organization follow from a relational graph itself, and which depend on the chosen dynamics?

Compare two phase oscillators connected by the same interaction edge, while changing the evolution law. This isolates graph connectivity from dynamical assumptions.

2. COMMON CARRIER AND RELATION

Let X={1,2} and let the interaction graph contain the single undirected edge {1,2}. This graph is identical in both models. Let theta_1 and theta_2 be phases and delta=theta_1-theta_2.

For comparison, define the phase-coherence order parameter
Q(delta)=|(exp(i theta_1)+exp(i theta_2))/2|=|cos(delta/2)|.

Q is a chosen operational measure of phase coherence for this two-oscillator model, not a universal measure of organization.

3. MODEL A: ATTRACTIVE COUPLING

Dynamics:
d theta_1/dt = omega + K sin(theta_2-theta_1)
d theta_2/dt = omega + K sin(theta_1-theta_2), K>0.

Then:
d delta/dt = -2K sin(delta).

For delta(0) in (-pi,pi), the solution obeys
tan(delta(t)/2)=tan(delta(0)/2) exp(-2Kt).
Hence delta(t) tends to zero and Q(t) tends to one, except the special equilibrium delta=pi (modulo 2pi), which is unstable to perturbations.

Under this model, phase coherence typically increases toward synchrony.

4. MODEL B: REPULSIVE COUPLING

Keep the same two nodes and same interaction edge, but reverse the coupling sign:
d theta_1/dt = omega - K sin(theta_2-theta_1)
d theta_2/dt = omega - K sin(theta_1-theta_2), K>0.

Then:
d delta/dt = +2K sin(delta).

The in-phase equilibrium delta=0 is unstable to small perturbations, while the anti-phase equilibrium delta=pi is stable to small perturbations. For initial delta in (0,pi), delta(t) moves toward pi and Q(t) tends to zero. For initial delta in (-pi,0), it moves toward -pi modulo 2pi, also giving Q(t) tending to zero.

Thus the same graph connectivity and same Q definition can accompany opposite collective outcomes under different dynamical laws.

5. COMPARATIVE RESULT

The common relational graph alone does not select:
- attractive versus repulsive interaction;
- synchrony versus anti-synchrony;
- the time scale set by K;
- the trajectory Q(t).

The outcome is determined by the additional dynamical law and parameters, not by graph connectivity alone.

This is a controlled model comparison. It establishes non-uniqueness of dynamics relative to the chosen relational structure. It does not establish that either law is physically correct for any specific real system.

6. FALSIFIABLE DISCRIMINATION

For a real two-oscillator system:
A. Measure phase trajectories and estimate delta(t) and Q(t).
B. Independently characterize the sign and magnitude of coupling where possible.
C. Fit attractive and repulsive models on a predeclared training segment.
D. Compare held-out trajectories and uncertainty-aware residuals.
E. Reject a model in the tested regime if residuals systematically exceed a predeclared tolerance while its assumptions are independently supported.

The models make distinguishable predictions for initial conditions away from equilibria. Near exact equilibria, finite noise and perturbations must be modeled; no discrimination claim should rely on idealized exact initial states alone.

7. DERIVATION LEDGER

Derived from Model A equations:
- delta_dot=-2K sin(delta);
- attraction toward delta=0 for initial phase difference in (-pi,pi), excluding the anti-phase equilibrium;
- Q tends to 1 on those trajectories.

Derived from Model B equations:
- delta_dot=+2K sin(delta);
- attraction toward anti-phase for initial delta in (0,pi) or (-pi,0), modulo 2pi;
- Q tends to 0 on those trajectories.

Shared assumptions:
- two phase oscillators with equal natural frequency;
- a single undirected interaction edge;
- constant coupling magnitude K;
- adequacy of phase-only dynamics.

Not derived from the graph:
- the sign of coupling;
- either evolution law;
- a unique organizational outcome;
- a physical law from PD or the Frozen Core.

8. RELATION TO THE FROZEN CORE

The comparison is consistent with the established separation between relational constraints and dynamics: a relational structure may admit multiple dynamics. The models are illustrative realizations, not a claim that all dynamics in A(R;Phi) have been enumerated.

No Frozen Core text is modified. No CE-V promotion, canonical acceptance, or RL registration is asserted.

9. RESULT

The same relational connectivity supports distinct organization trajectories under attractive and repulsive coupling. Therefore, connectivity alone does not entail synchrony or a unique order-parameter trajectory. To make a physical organization claim, the coupling law and parameters must be independently justified and the resulting predictions tested against observations and alternatives.
