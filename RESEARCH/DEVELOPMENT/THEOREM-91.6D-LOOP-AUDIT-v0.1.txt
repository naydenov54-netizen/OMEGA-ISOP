1. AUDIT SCOPE
==================================================

Reviewed claims:

D-1. Exact identifiability of q from F is equivalent to existence of a unique map g:F(Theta)->Q satisfying q=g o F.

D-2. Lipschitz-stable identifiability of q from F is equivalent to Lipschitz continuity of the unique induced map g on F(Theta), equipped with the subspace metric inherited from d_Y.

Additional checks:

- The special case q=id_Theta.
- The non-implication from exact identifiability to global Lipschitz stability.
- The non-implication from continuity of g to global Lipschitz continuity.
- The stated limitations and non-implications.
- Whether any conclusion is embedded in a definition or imported as an unproved compatibility condition.

This audit does not modify THEOREM 91.6D, the Frozen Core, canonical documents, or any registry.

2. FRAMEWORK CHECK
==================================================

Given:

Theta: nonempty set.
(Y,d_Y): metric observation space.
F:Theta->Y.
(Q,d_Q): metric target space.
q:Theta->Q.

The image F(Theta) is equipped with the subspace metric inherited from d_Y.

Observation:

The metric on Q is not needed for the set-theoretic factorization result D-1. It is required for the metric inequality and Lipschitz statement D-2. Keeping Q metric in the common framework is harmless and makes the two results compatible.

No topology on Theta is required for D-1 or D-2, except in the explicitly identified special case q=id_Theta, where Theta must also be the target metric space.

No completeness, compactness, linearity, finite dimensionality, or vector-space structure is required for D-1 or D-2.

3. LOOP AUDIT: THEOREM 91.6D-1
==================================================

TARGET_C:

There exists a unique function g:F(Theta)->Q such that q=g o F if and only if:

F(theta1)=F(theta2) implies q(theta1)=q(theta2)

for all theta1,theta2 in Theta.

PREMISES_AND_DEFINITIONS:

1. F is a function from Theta to Y.
2. F(Theta) is the image of Theta under F.
3. q is a function from Theta to Q.
4. Equality of observations and equality of target values have their ordinary set-theoretic meanings.
5. Function composition is defined on the stated domains.

DEPENDENCY_CHAIN:

The forward direction uses only q=g o F and equality substitution.

The reverse direction defines g(y)=q(theta) for any theta satisfying F(theta)=y. The fiber-constancy condition guarantees that this value is independent of the selected preimage.

Uniqueness follows because every y in F(Theta) has at least one preimage.

POSSIBLE_EQUIVALENT_OR_EMBEDDED_TARGET:

The factorization q=g o F is not assumed in the reverse direction. It is constructed after well-definedness is established.

The condition that q be constant on each fiber of F is the exact criterion being tested. It is not hidden inside the definition of g.

INDEPENDENT_JUSTIFICATION:

The proof follows directly from the definitions of image, function, fiber, and composition. No additional compatibility axiom is imported.

COUNTERMODEL_ATTEMPT_AND_RESULT:

If fiber constancy fails, there exist theta1,theta2 such that:

F(theta1)=F(theta2)
q(theta1) != q(theta2)

A single-valued function g would have to assign two different values to the same argument F(theta1). Therefore no such g can exist.

If fiber constancy holds, the proposed definition of g assigns the same value for every preimage of each point in F(Theta). It is therefore well-defined.

No countermodel satisfying the stated fiber-constancy condition while violating the factorization was found; the direct construction excludes such a countermodel.

PREMISE_REMOVAL_TEST:

- Removing fiber constancy invalidates well-definedness, as shown by the counterexample above.
- Removing the metric structure on Q does not invalidate D-1. D-1 is a set-theoretic result.
- Removing a metric on Y does not invalidate D-1.
- No metric on Theta is needed unless q=id_Theta is selected as a special case.

CLASSIFICATION:

NON-CIRCULAR DERIVATION.

RESULT:

D-1 is valid under its stated definitions. The induced map g exists exactly when q is constant on every fiber of F, and it is unique on F(Theta).

4. LOOP AUDIT: THEOREM 91.6D-2
==================================================

TARGET_C:

The inequality

d_Q(q(theta1),q(theta2))
<= K*d_Y(F(theta1),F(theta2))

for all theta1,theta2 in Theta and some finite K>=0

holds if and only if the induced map g:F(Theta)->Q exists and is Lipschitz.

PREMISES_AND_DEFINITIONS:

1. The metric spaces (Y,d_Y) and (Q,d_Q).
2. The maps F and q.
3. The exact-identifiability criterion from D-1.
4. The subspace metric on F(Theta), inherited from d_Y.
5. The ordinary definition of a globally Lipschitz map.

DEPENDENCY_CHAIN:

Direction A:

The inequality implies exact identifiability because d_Y(F(theta1),F(theta2))=0 forces d_Q(q(theta1),q(theta2))=0. Since d_Q is a metric, the target values are equal.

D-1 therefore supplies the unique induced map g.

For any y1,y2 in F(Theta), choose preimages theta1,theta2. Substitution into the original inequality gives:

d_Q(g(y1),g(y2)) <= K*d_Y(y1,y2)

Thus g is globally Lipschitz on F(Theta).

Direction B:

Assume the unique induced map g exists and is globally Lipschitz with constant K.

Using q=g o F:

d_Q(q(theta1),q(theta2))
= d_Q(g(F(theta1)),g(F(theta2)))
<= K*d_Y(F(theta1),F(theta2)).

This is exactly the required inequality.

POSSIBLE_EQUIVALENT_OR_EMBEDDED_TARGET:

The Lipschitz inequality in the definition is not assumed as a property of g in advance. In Direction A, it is transferred to the image F(Theta) and thereby proves that g is Lipschitz.

In Direction B, Lipschitz continuity of g is explicitly the premise. The result is a conditional equivalence, not a derivation that g must be Lipschitz from exact identifiability alone.

INDEPENDENT_JUSTIFICATION:

The proof uses D-1 only to establish the existence and uniqueness of g after exact identifiability has been obtained. The quantitative bound is independently carried through the metric inequality.

No unproved physical, statistical, or architectural compatibility condition is used.

COUNTERMODEL_ATTEMPT_AND_RESULT:

Take:

Theta=R
Y=R
Q=R
F(x)=x^3
q(x)=x

F is injective, so exact identifiability holds.

The induced map is:

g(y)=cube_root(y)

For y>0:

|g(y)-g(0)|/|y-0| = y^(-2/3)

This ratio is unbounded as y approaches zero from above. Therefore g is not globally Lipschitz, and no finite global K satisfies the stability inequality.

This is a valid counterexample to the stronger claim that exact identifiability alone implies Lipschitz-stable identifiability. It does not contradict D-2, because D-2 requires the Lipschitz property as the equivalent quantitative condition.

PREMISE_REMOVAL_TEST:

- Without metric structure on Y and Q, the Lipschitz statement is not defined.
- Without exact identifiability, a single-valued induced map g may not exist.
- Removing the global Lipschitz bound leaves only exact identifiability; it does not provide a finite global stability constant.
- No completeness or compactness assumption is needed for this equivalence.
- Replacing global Lipschitz continuity by local Lipschitz continuity would yield a different, local stability statement and must be formulated separately.

CLASSIFICATION:

NON-CIRCULAR DERIVATION.

RESULT:

D-2 is valid as a global metric-space equivalence under the stated assumptions.

==================================================
5. NON-IMPLICATION AUDIT
==================================================

CLAIM A:

Exact identifiability does not imply global Lipschitz-stable identifiability.

STATUS: VERIFIED.

The example F(x)=x^3 and q(x)=x establishes exact identifiability while the induced cube-root map is not globally Lipschitz.

CLAIM B:

Continuity of the induced map g does not imply global Lipschitz continuity.

STATUS: VERIFIED.

The same induced map g(y)=cube_root(y) is continuous on R but not globally Lipschitz.

PRECISION:

Continuity is understood on F(Theta) with the subspace topology inherited from Y. The counterexample uses F(Theta)=R, so the continuity statement is unambiguous.

CLAIM C:

Identifiability of q does not imply identifiability of the full parameter theta.

STATUS: VERIFIED BY THE GENERAL CRITERION.

The factor map g can exist when multiple parameters lie in the same fiber of F, provided q has the same value on those parameters. Therefore q may be identifiable even when F is not injective.

6. SPECIAL CASE q = id_Theta
==================================================

The special case is valid only when:

Q=Theta

and Theta is equipped with a metric d_Theta, with d_Q=d_Theta.

Then exact identifiability of q=id_Theta means:

F(theta1)=F(theta2) implies theta1=theta2.

This is exactly injectivity of F.

The metric qualification is necessary for the corresponding Lipschitz-stability statement. It is not needed for the bare set-theoretic statement that injectivity is equivalent to exact identification of the identity target.

STATUS: VERIFIED.

7. NON-IMPLICATIONS AND SCOPE
==================================================

D-1 and D-2 do not establish:

1. Physical realizability of F or q.
2. Statistical identifiability under a probabilistic observation model.
3. Measurability or experimental accessibility of F.
4. Global Lipschitz stability from exact identifiability alone.
5. Local stability unless a local condition is separately stated.
6. A result independent of the chosen metrics.
7. Universal applicability to every OMEGA-ISOP construction.
8. Shannon-information preservation or loss.
9. A causal, dynamical, or cosmological law.

The results are abstract mathematical criteria for maps between sets and metric spaces.
