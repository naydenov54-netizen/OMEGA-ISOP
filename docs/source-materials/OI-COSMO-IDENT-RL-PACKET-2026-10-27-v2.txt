TITLE: Future event-horizon non-identifiability under matched FLRW past histories
CLAIM: In the class of smooth expanding flat-FLRW histories for which the future event-horizon integral converges, two scale factors can agree on the entire history through a chosen time T and nevertheless have different future event-horizon radii; the construction can be realized by reconstructing an effective fluid with positive density and NEC for sufficiently small compactly supported deformation, under the stated Einstein-FLRW assumptions.

DEFINITIONS / DOMAIN / ASSUMPTIONS:
- Spatially flat FLRW geometry; cosmic time t; positive scale factor a(t).
- Event-horizon proper radius at t0: D(t0) = a(t0) integral from t0 to infinity of c dt/a(t), assuming convergence.
- Choose T > t0, T2 > T and f in C_c^infinity((T,T2)), f >= 0, f not identically zero.
- Define a2(t) = a1(t) exp(epsilon f(t)), epsilon > 0.
- For the effective-fluid realization, assume standard Einstein-FLRW equations with fixed Lambda and reconstruct rho_X and p_X from H2 and its derivative. The result is conditional on these external physical assumptions; they are not derived from the OMEGA-ISOP Frozen Core.

RESULT AND SUPPORTING DERIVATION OR METHOD:
Because f(t)=0 for t <= T, a2=a1 and all time derivatives agree throughout that past region. Thus local expansion observables determined by any finite set of time derivatives agree at t0.
Since a1(t0)=a2(t0),
D2(t0)-D1(t0) = c a1(t0) integral_T^T2 [(exp(-epsilon f(t))-1)/a1(t)] dt < 0.
Strictness follows from f >= 0 and f not identically zero, with f smooth and positive on a nonempty open subset.
For the effective fluid, with c=1:
rho_X = (3H^2-Lambda)/(8 pi G),
p_X = -(2 Hdot+3H^2-Lambda)/(8 pi G),
rho_X+p_X = -Hdot/(4 pi G).
For H2=H1+epsilon f_dot, Hdot2=Hdot1+epsilon f_ddot, the perturbation of rho+p is -epsilon f_ddot/(4 pi G). If the baseline has rho_X,1>0 and rho_X,1+p_X,1>0 on the compact support, boundedness of f_dot and f_ddot permits sufficiently small epsilon to preserve H2>0, rho_X,2>0 and NEC. The conservation equation follows from the reconstructed Friedmann quantities and the Bianchi identity/equivalent algebraic identity.
An explicit baseline used in the derivation is a1(t)=sinh^2(alpha t)/sinh^2(alpha t0), t>0, with Lambda=12 alpha^2 and rho_X,1=3 alpha^2 csch^2(alpha t)/(2 pi G), p_X,1=-2 rho_X,1/3, which has positive density and satisfies NEC.
