PURPOSE AND SCOPE
This artifact establishes a finite-set characterization connecting pairwise overlap among minimal transversals with Cartesian-product structure.

Let P be a finite, nonempty family of finite, nonempty sets.
Let Min(P) denote the inclusion-minimal members of P.
Let Tr(P) denote the family of inclusion-minimal transversals of P.

Define:
OVL_Tr(P) = TRUE
if and only if there exist distinct A,B in Tr(P) such that A ∩ B is nonempty.
Otherwise:
OVL_Tr(P) = FALSE.

For pairwise-disjoint, nonempty blocks V1,...,Vk, define:
CKP(V1,...,Vk)= { {v1,...,vk} : vi ∈ Vi for every i=1,...,k }.
Thus CKP contains every set obtained by choosing exactly one element from each block.

BASIC REDUCTION
Lemma 1.
Tr(P) = Tr(Min(P)).

Reason:
A set intersects every member of P if and only if it intersects every inclusion-minimal member of P. Minimality of a transversal is therefore preserved.

Consequently:
Tr(Tr(P)) = Min(P),
using finite blocker duality for clutters, as established in Lemma 2.

FINITE BLOCKER DUALITY
Lemma 2.
For every finite clutter C consisting of nonempty sets:
Tr(Tr(C)) = C.

Here a clutter is a family in which no distinct member contains another.

Proof.
Part A: C ⊆ Tr(Tr(C)).
Fix C0 ∈ C.
Every transversal T ∈ Tr(C) intersects C0, so C0 is a transversal of Tr(C).
To establish minimality, fix x ∈ C0.
For each C' ∈ C with C' ≠ C0, the clutter property implies C' is not a subset of C0. Choose y_C' ∈ C' \ C0.
Define:
Y = {x} ∪ {y_C' : C' ∈ C, C' ≠ C0}.
Y intersects every member of C and satisfies:
Y ∩ C0 = {x}.
Choose an inclusion-minimal transversal T of C contained in Y. Then T ∩ C0 = {x}. Hence C0 \ {x} fails to intersect T.
This holds for every x ∈ C0, so C0 is an inclusion-minimal transversal of Tr(C).

Part B: Tr(Tr(C)) ⊆ C.
Let X ∈ Tr(Tr(C)).
Suppose no C0 ∈ C satisfies C0 ⊆ X.
For every C0 ∈ C, choose c_C0 ∈ C0 \ X and define:
Y = {c_C0 : C0 ∈ C}.
Then Y is a transversal of C and Y ∩ X = ∅.
Choose an inclusion-minimal transversal T of C contained in Y. Since X is a transversal of Tr(C), X must intersect T. But T ⊆ Y and Y ∩ X = ∅, a contradiction.
Therefore some C0 ∈ C satisfies C0 ⊆ X.
Since X is a minimal transversal of Tr(C), and C0 itself is a transversal of Tr(C), minimality forces X = C0.
Thus:
Tr(Tr(C)) = C.

CARTESIAN-PRODUCT LEMMAS
Lemma 3.
For pairwise-disjoint, nonempty blocks V1,...,Vk:
Tr({V1,...,Vk}) = CKP(V1,...,Vk).

Proof:
A minimal transversal must intersect every block. Since the blocks are pairwise disjoint, inclusion-minimality requires exactly one selected element from each block.

Lemma 4.
For T contained in the union of V1,...,Vk:
T is a transversal of CKP(V1,...,Vk)
if and only if T contains at least one entire block Vi.

Proof:
If T contains an entire block Vi, every member of CKP intersects T through its selected element in Vi.
Conversely, suppose T contains no entire block. For every i, choose vi ∈ Vi \ T. Then {v1,...,vk} belongs to CKP and is disjoint from T. Hence T is not a transversal.

Lemma 5.
Tr(CKP(V1,...,Vk)) = {V1,...,Vk}.

Proof:
By Lemma 4, a transversal of CKP must contain an entire block. Each block is itself a transversal. Its proper subsets contain no entire block because the blocks are pairwise disjoint. Therefore each block is minimal, and every minimal transversal is one of these blocks.

MAIN THEOREM
Theorem 1.
For every finite, nonempty family P of finite, nonempty sets:
OVL_Tr(P) = FALSE
if and only if there exist pairwise-disjoint, nonempty blocks V1,...,Vk such that:
Min(P) = CKP(V1,...,Vk).

Proof.
Let M = Min(P).

Forward direction.
Assume OVL_Tr(P) = FALSE.
Then every two distinct members of Tr(P) are disjoint.
By finite blocker duality:
M = Tr(Tr(P)).
Set H = Tr(P). Its members are pairwise disjoint and nonempty. Consequently, H is a family of pairwise-disjoint, nonempty blocks.
By Lemma 3:
Tr(H) = CKP(H).
Since H = Tr(P):
M = Tr(Tr(P)) = CKP(H).

Reverse direction.
Assume:
M = CKP(V1,...,Vk)
for pairwise-disjoint, nonempty blocks V1,...,Vk.
By Lemma 1:
Tr(P) = Tr(M).
By Lemma 5:
Tr(M) = {V1,...,Vk}.
The blocks are pairwise disjoint, so no two distinct members of Tr(P) overlap.
Therefore OVL_Tr(P) = FALSE.
This proves the theorem.

STRUCTURAL RECOGNITION CRITERION
Let M = Min(P), and let V(M) be the union of all members of M.
Construct a simple graph G on V(M), where two distinct vertices are adjacent exactly when they occur together in at least one member of M.
Let Gᶜ be the complement graph.
Let V1,...,Vk be the vertex sets of the connected components of Gᶜ.

The following conditions characterize the Cartesian-product structure.

Condition A:
Every connected component of Gᶜ is a clique.

Condition B:
Every E ∈ M intersects each component Vi in exactly one element.

Condition C:
|M| = ∏ᵢ₌₁ᵏ |Vi|.

Recognition theorem.
M = CKP(V1,...,Vk)
if and only if Conditions A, B, and C all hold.

Reason.
If M is a Cartesian product over pairwise-disjoint blocks, two distinct vertices occur together in a member of M exactly when they lie in different blocks. Therefore Gᶜ has precisely the blocks as its connected components, and Conditions A and B hold. The product family has cardinality ∏ᵢ |Vi|, giving Condition C.
Conversely, Condition A identifies the connected components of Gᶜ as pairwise-disjoint cliques. Condition B places each member of M among the possible one-per-component choices. Condition C states that M has the same cardinality as the full Cartesian-product family. Since M is a subfamily of that finite product family and the cardinalities agree, M equals the full product family.

COMPUTATIONAL COMPLEXITY
For an explicitly represented finite family P, the recognition procedure is polynomial in the total input encoding length.

The procedure:
Computes Min(P) by pairwise inclusion comparisons.
Constructs G and Gᶜ.
Finds the connected components of Gᶜ.
Checks Conditions A, B, and C.

The number of pairwise set-inclusion comparisons in Step 1 is O(|P|²). This count alone is not a full runtime bound; the total runtime is polynomial in the total encoding length of the explicitly supplied family.
This result concerns recognition of the stated structure. It does not imply that generating all minimal transversals of an arbitrary hypergraph is polynomial-time solvable.

EXAMPLES
Example 1: Triangle.
Let M = {{a,b},{b,c},{a,c}}.
Its minimal transversals are:
Tr(M) = {{a,b},{b,c},{a,c}}.
Distinct minimal transversals overlap, so OVL_Tr(P) = TRUE.
The family is not a Cartesian product over pairwise-disjoint blocks.

Example 2: Star.
Let M = {{a,b},{a,c},{a,d}}.
Its minimal transversals are:
Tr(M) = {{a},{b,c,d}}.
These two transversals are disjoint, so OVL_Tr(P) = FALSE.
The original family has the Cartesian-product representation:
M = CKP({a},{b,c,d}).

Example 3: Same degree sequence, different overlap status.
For K3,3, the two bipartition classes are disjoint minimal vertex covers. Thus its minimal-transversal overlap status is OVL = FALSE.
For the triangular prism with vertex set {a,b,c,d,e,f}, take edge family:
{{a,b},{b,c},{c,a},{d,e},{e,f},{f,d},{a,d},{b,e},{c,f}}.
Two minimal vertex covers are:
T1 = {a,b,d,f}
T2 = {a,b,e,f}.
Both cover every edge and are inclusion-minimal. They are distinct, and:
T1 ∩ T2 = {a,b,f} ≠ ∅.
Therefore the triangular prism has OVL = TRUE.
Both K3,3 and the triangular prism are 3-regular graphs on six vertices, yet their minimal-transversal overlap status differs.
