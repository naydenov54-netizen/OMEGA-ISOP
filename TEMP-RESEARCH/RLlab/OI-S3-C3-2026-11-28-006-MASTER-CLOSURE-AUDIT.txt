3. QUOTIENT DESCENT — I_R
============================================================

For an n-ary relation R, define:

I_R:
(x1~y1 AND ... AND xn~yn)
->
(R(x1,...,xn) IFF R(y1,...,yn)).

Then:
I_R is necessary and sufficient for R to descend to a well-defined relation on S/~.

Proof:
If I_R holds, truth of R is independent of the chosen representatives, so R_bar([x1],...,[xn]) iff R(x1,...,xn) is well-defined.
Conversely, if R_bar is well-defined, changing representatives within the same quotient classes cannot change truth of R; hence I_R.

Status:
PROVED.

Important non-implication:
I_R is a quotient-descent condition for one relation. It is not a general compatibility theorem for an arbitrary constraint theory.

3.1 Counterexample: C_R + T_R does not imply I_R

Let:
U={a,b}
# = empty
~ = U x U
R={(a,a),(b,b)}

Then:
A1-A3 hold.
C_R holds, where C_R is R(x,y)->x~y.
T_R holds, where T_R is R(x,y) AND R(y,z)->R(x,z).

But I_R fails:
a~b and a~a,
R(a,a)=TRUE,
R(b,a)=FALSE.

Therefore:
C_R + T_R DOES NOT IMPLY I_R.

Status:
REFUTED BY COUNTEREXAMPLE.

This counterexample blocks any argument that silently uses C_R and T_R to obtain quotient descent.
