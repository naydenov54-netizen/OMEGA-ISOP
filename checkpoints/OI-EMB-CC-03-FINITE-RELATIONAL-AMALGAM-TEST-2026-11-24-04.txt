0. PURPOSE AND STATUS
======================================================================

This checkpoint tests the cocone witness candidate in a nontrivial
finite relational class and constructs incomparable minimal witnesses.

It is a model-specific mathematical result.
It is not a recovered original EMB_CC-03 source and is not a canonical
OMEGA-ISOP theorem.

The model uses:
- finite connected simple graphs;
- induced embeddings;
- an apartness relation # defined by inequality;
- graph adjacency E as an additional selected relational predicate.

The additional predicate E is a realization-level test structure.
It does not modify the Frozen Core or assert that every C_METACORE
realization must contain E.

======================================================================
1. TEST CATEGORY
======================================================================

Define C_ConnGraph as follows.

OBJECTS:
Finite, nonempty, connected, simple undirected graphs:

G=(V_G,E_G)

where:
- V_G is finite and nonempty;
- E_G is a symmetric, irreflexive binary relation;
- the graph is connected.

Equip each graph with the apartness relation:

x #_G y iff x != y.

This # relation satisfies:
- irreflexivity;
- symmetry;
- cotransitivity.

The graph relation E_G is an additional selected structural relation.

MORPHISMS:
An induced embedding f:G->H is an injective map such that, for all
x,y in V_G:

x E_G y iff f(x) E_H f(y).

Because f is injective, it also preserves and reflects apartness #.

Composition and identities are the ordinary composition and identity
maps. Thus C_ConnGraph is a category of finite admissible relational
configurations with embedding morphisms.

======================================================================
2. FINITE AMALGAM EXISTENCE FOR NONEMPTY SPANS
======================================================================

Let:

G1 <-i1- G0 -i2-> G2

be a span in C_ConnGraph.

Since G0 is an object, it is nonempty and connected.
Both i1 and i2 are induced embeddings.

Construct:

A = (V_G1 disjoint-union V_G2) / ~

where ~ identifies i1(x) with i2(x) for every x in V_G0 and makes no
other identifications.

Define E_A to be the union of the transported edge relations from G1
and G2. No cross-edge is added.

The canonical maps:

m1:G1->A
m2:G2->A

are injective.

They preserve and reflect E:
- edges within each side are retained;
- no new edge is introduced between two vertices from the same side;
- any pair of vertices in one side has exactly its original adjacency
  status.

They also preserve and reflect # because # is inequality.

They agree on G0:

m1 o i1 = m2 o i2.

A is connected:
- every vertex in G1 has a path to every selected vertex of G0 because
  G1 is connected;
- every vertex in G2 has a path to every selected vertex of G0 because
  G2 is connected;
- the two copies of G0 are identified and G0 is nonempty.

A is finite because G1 and G2 are finite.

Therefore:

THEOREM 1 — FINITE CONNECTED-GRAPH AMALGAM

Every span in C_ConnGraph has a finite admissible amalgam.

Proof:
The construction above supplies a finite connected graph A and
embedding legs m1,m2 satisfying the cocone equation.

SCOPE:
This is proved only for the stated category and span class. It does not
establish the amalgamation property for arbitrary relational classes,
arbitrary admissibility restrictions, or the canonical OMEGA-ISOP
C_METACORE.

======================================================================
3. TWO INCOMPARABLE MINIMAL AMALGAMS
======================================================================

Let G0 be the one-vertex graph:

V_G0={c}.

Let G1 be the one-edge graph on {c,a} with edge {c,a}.
Let G2 be the one-edge graph on {c,b} with edge {c,b}.

The diagram is the span:

G1 <- G0 -> G2

where both maps include the vertex c.

Construct two candidate amalgams.

W_P:
A_P is the path on three vertices:

c -- a
|
b

More precisely:
E_A_P={{c,a},{c,b}}
and there is no edge {a,b}.

The two span legs are the evident induced embeddings.

W_K:
A_K is the triangle on three vertices:

E_A_K={{c,a},{c,b},{a,b}}.

Again, the two span legs are induced embeddings.

Both are valid finite admissible amalgamating cocones.

----------------------------------------------------------------------
3.1 Minimality of W_P
----------------------------------------------------------------------

Suppose V=(D,(eta_0,eta_1,eta_2)) is any admissible witness and:

V <=_Delta W_P.

Then there is an induced embedding h:D->A_P commuting with all
diagram legs.

The image of c must be c.
The image of the non-base vertex from G1 must be a.
The image of the non-base vertex from G2 must be b.

These are three distinct vertices. Hence |V_D|>=3.

But A_P has exactly three vertices. Since h is injective, D has
exactly three vertices and h is bijective onto A_P. An induced
bijection is an isomorphism.

Therefore V is equivalent to W_P over the diagram.

No strictly smaller witness factors into W_P.
Thus W_P is minimal.

----------------------------------------------------------------------
3.2 Minimality of W_K
----------------------------------------------------------------------

The same argument applies to W_K.

Any witness factoring into W_K must contain the three distinct
distinguished images c,a,b. Therefore its apex has at least three
vertices.

Since A_K has exactly three vertices, any such embedding is an
isomorphism.

Thus W_K is minimal.

----------------------------------------------------------------------
3.3 Incomparability
----------------------------------------------------------------------

There is no witness factorization:

W_P <=_Delta W_K.

Such a factorization would be an induced embedding fixing the
distinguished vertices c,a,b. But A_P has no edge between a and b,
whereas A_K does. The embedding would fail to reflect adjacency.

There is also no factorization:

W_K <=_Delta W_P.

It would have to preserve the edge between a and b, which does not
exist in A_P.

Therefore W_P and W_K are incomparable minimal witnesses.

----------------------------------------------------------------------
3.4 No Least Witness
----------------------------------------------------------------------

Assume a least witness L existed.

Then:

L <=_Delta W_P
and
L <=_Delta W_K.

The factorization into W_P forces the two distinguished non-base
vertices of L to be nonadjacent, because their images a,b in A_P are
nonadjacent and the factorization is an induced embedding.

The factorization into W_K forces the same two distinguished vertices
to be adjacent, because their images a,b in A_K are adjacent and the
factorization is an induced embedding.

Contradiction.

Therefore no least witness exists for this span in C_ConnGraph.

THEOREM 2 — MINIMAL NON-UNIQUENESS

There exist finite diagrams in a finite relational embedding category
with two incomparable minimal admissible amalgamation witnesses and
with no least witness.

This theorem is established by the span above.

======================================================================
4. CONSEQUENCES FOR EMB_CC-03
======================================================================

The model establishes:

1. Finite amalgam existence can hold in a nontrivial constrained
   relational class.

2. Finite amalgam existence does not imply uniqueness of minimal
   witnesses.

3. Minimality does not imply leastness.

4. A least witness cannot be assumed merely because all individual
   witnesses are finite.

5. The witness preorder and its factorization maps are essential
   mathematical data. Cardinality alone does not capture the order.

6. Strong amalgamation is not needed for the two examples. The
   embeddings are induced, but the witness definition does not impose
   the strong-amalgamation intersection condition.

SCOPE:
These results concern C_ConnGraph. They are not automatically
transferable to every C_METACORE realization.
