DOCUMENT PURPOSE

This document consolidates the foundational mathematical development program, the physicalization-bridge audit, and the physical-research continuity checkpoint into one controlled research reference.

The objective is to strengthen OMEGA-ISOP as a meta-theory by developing and proving rigorous mathematical constructions describing the conditions under which structures arise, are organized, and persist in relational systems.

A separate research objective is to determine whether, and under what additional assumptions, these constructions admit interpretation and application in fundamental physical domains.

This document is a research program and continuity record. It does not amend the Frozen Core, replace primary mathematical sources, promote proof status, establish a physical law, or grant certification.

PART I — CORE RESEARCH PRINCIPLE AND METHOD

The governing sequence for mathematical-to-physical research is:

MATHEMATICAL CONSTRUCTION
→ MATHEMATICAL PROOF
→ STRUCTURAL CORRESPONDENCE
→ PHYSICAL INTERPRETATION
→ PHYSICAL MODEL
→ TESTABLE CONSEQUENCES
→ EMPIRICAL CHECK

Each transition is a separate research obligation. No transition is automatic.

Mathematical validity does not by itself establish physical reality. A mathematically correct construction may have no physical realization. A physical interpretation requires an explicit correspondence to physical states, observables, parameters, units, and operations. A physical model requires additional assumptions and dynamics. Empirical relevance requires consequences that can be measured and can, in principle, count against the model.

The theory must not be modified merely to fit a desired physical interpretation. If a physical application requires additional structure, that structure must be stated as an external or application-level assumption unless it is independently derived from the existing theory.

PART II — SIX-STAGE FOUNDATIONAL DEVELOPMENT

The foundational sequence is:

Difference → Relation → Constraint → Dynamics → Persistence → Organization

These arrows are research questions, not assumed theorems. A result at one stage does not automatically establish the next stage.

STAGE 2 — RELATION

Question:
Under what additional rule does a distinction support a relation between states?

Required work:
- Define a typed relation R ⊆ X × X or a richer relational structure.
- Explicitly test directionality, reflexivity, symmetry, transitivity, and composition conditions.
- Prove which relations follow from the difference data.
- Identify which relations require additional structure.

Exit criterion:
The derivable and additional relational information is distinguished by proof.

Stage 2 has not been opened as a completed foundational stage. It may be opened only as a separate research task. It must define the additional relational data or selection rule not determined by D, preserve the Core compatibility-diagram and colimit construction where applicable, and prove exactly which parts follow from D and which are additional inputs. No unconditional Difference → Relation theorem is assumed.

STAGE 3 — CONSTRAINT

Question:
Which configurations or relations are admissible?

Required work:
- Define a constraint predicate C, constraint set, or admissibility operator.
- Test satisfiability.
- Provide examples of allowed and forbidden configurations.
- Test consistency and independence.

Exit criterion:
Constraints are well-defined and non-vacuous.

STAGE 4 — DYNAMICS

Question:
What rule, if any, maps one admissible state to another?

Required work:
- Define the state space.
- Define a transition relation or operator and its domain/codomain.
- State existence and uniqueness conditions where relevant.
- State all assumptions about time or iteration.

Exit criterion:
The stated dynamical properties are proved. Dynamics must not be inferred merely from static relations or constraints.

STAGE 5 — PERSISTENCE

Question:
Under which dynamics and conditions does a state, property, relation, or invariant survive?

Required work:
- Define a precise persistence predicate or invariant.
- Prove a theorem linking it to specified dynamics.
- Provide counterexamples showing the limits of the theorem.

Exit criterion:
Persistence is proved relative to explicit update rules and assumptions, not asserted as a universal consequence.

STAGE 6 — ORGANIZATION

Question:
When do persistent structures form a higher-level, non-arbitrary organization?

Required work:
- Define a formal organization criterion.
- Define composition or coarse-graining rules.
- State stability or coherence conditions.
- Distinguish genuine organization from mere aggregation.
- Provide at least one worked model where the criterion is satisfied and one where it is not.

Exit criterion:
The criterion's consequences are proved and its limits are demonstrated.

CROSS-STAGE PROOF RULE

For every proposed implication A → B, provide:
1. Precise hypotheses.
2. A construction of B from A, or a theorem establishing the implication.
3. A proof.
4. A test case.
5. A counterexample or explicit boundary of applicability.
6. A statement of every external assumption.

If B requires information not contained in A, classify the transition as conditional and list the additional input. Do not describe it as a derivation from A alone.

EVIDENCE LABELS

PROVED:
Follows from stated definitions and premises by a checked argument.

TESTED:
Evaluated on stated examples. Testing alone is not proof.

CONDITIONAL:
Proved only under explicitly listed additional assumptions.

HYPOTHESIS:
Proposed interpretation awaiting proof or decisive tests.

REJECTED:
Failed a stated test or contradicted by a counterexample.

INTEGRATED:
Accepted into the working theory only after proof audit and explicit change-control. A mathematically correct result is not automatically integrated into Frozen Core.

PART III — PHYSICALIZATION BRIDGE

SOURCE-BASED ARCHITECTURAL FINDING

The protected source material separates:

CORE THEORY → REALIZATION → VALIDATION RESULT.

It defines a realization map Φ: Real(R) → M and, for a fixed realization, an admissible dynamics class A(R;Φ) ⊆ Evol(X).

The framework explicitly allows A(R;Φ) to be empty or to contain multiple dynamics. It does not derive a unique dynamics from R alone.

Volume III supplies a conditional operational layer:
- probes/interventions L_op;
- response maps Resp_M;
- tolerance-based operational comparison;
- persistence criteria;
- perturbation-relative robustness;
- explicit failure conditions.

These are bridge components, not a complete physical theory. A concrete physical realization, observables, dynamics, parameter interpretation, and discriminating empirical consequences must still be specified for a selected target.

MAIN PHYSICALIZATION GAP

The map from formal relational structure to a physical model remains underdetermined unless the following are fixed:

(a) The physical system and its state space X.
(b) The physical meaning and units of state variables and parameters.
(c) A concrete realization map Φ for the selected target.
(d) The admissible dynamics A(R;Φ), including whether they are uniquely selected or remain a family.
(e) The observable/probe set L_op and response map for a concrete experiment or astronomical data set.
(f) A target-specific result that distinguishes the model from relevant alternatives by a measurable outcome.

These are open research dependencies, not proof of inconsistency.

PHYSICALIZATION DEVELOPMENT ORDER

P1 — CHOOSE ONE PHYSICAL TARGET

State a concrete target, scale, system, and domain of validity. Do not attempt to physicalize every application simultaneously. Candidate targets must be selected explicitly, not inferred from the abstract Core.

P2 — SPECIFY A PHYSICAL REALIZATION

Give the state space X, state variables, parameter meanings and units, boundary/initial conditions, and a concrete Φ. Distinguish physical assumptions from mathematical consequences.

P3 — DERIVE RATHER THAN RENAME

From declared assumptions, derive the admissible dynamics A(R;Φ). If multiple dynamics remain, state the family and identify what additional principle or data would discriminate among them. Do not present an inserted law as if it had been derived.

P4 — DEFINE OBSERVABLES AND MEASUREMENTS

Specify operational probes L_op, response map Resp_M, measurement procedure, uncertainty/noise model, and relevant nuisance parameters. Use units and a clear correspondence between formal quantities and measured quantities.

P5 — PRODUCE A DISCRIMINATING PREDICTION

State at least one quantitative prediction or inequality that differs from a named baseline model within a specified regime. Give the parameter domain, expected magnitude, and uncertainty assumptions. If no discriminating prediction is available, classify the model as NOT YET EMPIRICALLY DISCRIMINATING.

P6 — TRY TO BREAK THE MODEL

Test limiting cases, dimensional consistency, conservation laws where applicable, stability, perturbations, and known empirical constraints. Use analytical derivations where possible and numerical experiments as supporting evidence only. Record failed cases rather than tuning them away without documented change control.

P7 — DECIDE WHETHER A CORE CHANGE IS WARRANTED

Propose a Frozen Core amendment only if the obstacle is genuinely in the Core rather than in an incomplete realization or application-specific model. Any proposal must state exact old/new wording, rationale, consequences, counterexamples considered, and validation plan. This program authorizes no Core edit.

DECISION RULE FOR CORE CORRECTIONS

A Core correction may be proposed if at least one of the following is demonstrated:
(i) An explicit inconsistency or contradiction in the Core.
(ii) A necessary physical bridge cannot be expressed without changing a Core commitment.
(iii) A Core claim is stronger than its derivation or validation supports.
(iv) A key term admits materially incompatible interpretations that block a well-defined realization.

If the gap can be resolved by choosing Φ, X, parameters, dynamics, or observables for a specific model, prefer an application-level extension rather than modifying the Core.

PHYSICALITY TESTS

Every candidate physical model must answer:
- What is the physical system?
- What are its states and observables?
- Which quantities have units, and which are dimensionless?
- What are the equations of motion or transition rules?
- What initial and boundary conditions apply?
- What is measured, and with what uncertainty?
- Which prediction could be wrong?
- What existing observation or experiment constrains it?
- What result would count against the model?

A formal colimit, admissibility condition, or structural relation is not by itself a physical mechanism or empirical prediction.

CATEGORY/COLIMIT DEPENDENCY

Category-level work can clarify whether a relational object R is constructible in a chosen category. Categorical existence alone does not make R physical. A category-level obstruction in a test model does not by itself invalidate a physical realization unless the project establishes that the test category is the intended one and that the obstructed construction is required.

Continue category audits as mathematical dependencies, but do not let them displace the physicalization path.
