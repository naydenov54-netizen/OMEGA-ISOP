1. QUESTION
Can a normalized probability measure derived from a metric volume measure determine the absolute global scale of a four-dimensional Lorentzian metric?

2. ASSUMPTIONS
Let M be a measurable four-dimensional spacetime with a Lorentzian metric g whose total metric volume is finite and positive:
0 < Vol_g(M) < infinity.
For measurable A subset M, define:
Vol_g(A) = integral over A of dvol(g).
Define the normalized metric-volume probability measure:
nu_g(A) = Vol_g(A) / Vol_g(M).

For a constant k > 0, let:
g_k = k^2 g.
This is a global constant conformal rescaling. The result below concerns this global scale degree of freedom and does not claim that normalized measures are invariant under arbitrary nonconstant conformal rescalings.

3. THEOREM N2 — NORMALIZATION DOES NOT FIX GLOBAL SCALE
In dimension n, the metric volume density transforms under g_k = k^2 g as:
dvol(g_k) = k^n dvol(g).
In four dimensions:
dvol(g_k) = k^4 dvol(g).

For each measurable A:
Vol_gk(A) = k^4 Vol_g(A),
and for the total space:
Vol_gk(M) = k^4 Vol_g(M).

Therefore:
nu_gk(A)
= Vol_gk(A) / Vol_gk(M)
= [k^4 Vol_g(A)] / [k^4 Vol_g(M)]
= nu_g(A).

Hence:
nu_gk = nu_g for every k > 0.

PROOF STATUS: PROVED under the assumptions in Section 2.

4. CONSEQUENCE
The normalized measure identifies relative volume proportions but is blind to the constant global scale k. Consequently, it cannot by itself select a unique metric from the family {k^2 g : k > 0}, nor determine an absolute four-volume or an absolute length scale.

If a target model maps the scale to a parameter such as the de Sitter Hubble parameter H, the normalized measure alone does not select H within a family whose metrics differ only by a global constant conformal factor and whose normalized volume measures therefore coincide.

5. DE SITTER APPLICATION
For the spatially flat de Sitter family:
g_H = a_H(eta)^2 [-c^2 d eta^2 + d chi^2 + chi^2 d Omega_2^2],
a_H(eta) = -1/(H eta), eta < 0.

For H_1,H_2 > 0:
g_H2 = (H_1/H_2)^2 g_H1.

Setting k = H_1/H_2 gives:
dvol(g_H2) = k^4 dvol(g_H1).
When normalized over the same finite-volume domain, with the same domain and identification:
nu_gH2 = nu_gH1.

Thus a normalized measure over that domain cannot distinguish H_1 from H_2. This conclusion is conditional on a finite, positive total volume over the domain used for normalization. If the full spacetime has infinite volume, a global normalized volume probability measure of this form is not defined; that failure does not supply an absolute scale.

The standard relation Lambda = 3 H^2/c^2 requires the additional Einstein-vacuum/de Sitter model premise. The present theorem neither derives that premise nor determines Lambda.

6. WHAT EXTRA INFORMATION COULD BREAK THE DEGENERACY?
At least one extra condition must fail to be invariant under g -> k^2 g. Examples of possible additional inputs include:
- a specified positive total physical four-volume;
- an independently calibrated dimensional density or fundamental length scale;
- a measured dimensionful observable with a justified mapping to the metric scale;
- a separately justified dynamical law that selects a scale.

These are candidates, not established OMEGA-ISOP derivations. Any candidate must be typed, constructed, and tested for independence from the target metric scale. A dimensional constant inserted by hand is an external scale unless its provenance is separately derived.

7. RELATION TO THE CAUSAL-ORDER NO-GO AUDIT
The causal-order no-go audit establishes that causal-order-only data cannot recover the metric volume density for every conformally related metric when those metrics share the same causal structure but have different volume densities.
The present result adds a distinct limitation: even after a volume measure is converted to a normalized probability measure, global scale information is erased by normalization.
These are separate arguments:
- causal-order-only input does not determine the metric volume density across a conformal class;
- normalized volume proportions do not determine the constant global scale.

Neither argument prohibits a successful construction from richer data. Both require that any additional scale-bearing input be stated explicitly rather than silently treated as a consequence of relational structure alone.

8. OMEGA-ISOP STATUS
The general OMEGA-ISOP framework allows conditional measures on specified measurable structures but does not universally guarantee an invariant measure or derive a unique physical metric scale.
This audit establishes a conditional mathematical degeneracy theorem. It does not establish a new universal physical law, does not alter the Frozen Core, and does not warrant automatic RL registration or CE-V promotion.
