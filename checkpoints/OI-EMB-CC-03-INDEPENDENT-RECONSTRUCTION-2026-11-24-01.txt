1. BASIC SETUP AND NOTATION
============================================================

RECONSTRUCTION R1.1 — AMBIENT CATEGORY

Let C be a category.
Ob(C) denotes its objects.
Mor(C) denotes its morphisms.
Composition is written g o f when f:X->Y and g:Y->Z.
Every subcategory is understood to contain the identities of its objects and to be closed under composition.

RECONSTRUCTION R1.2 — FINITE DIAGRAM

A finite diagram in C is a functor
Delta:J->C
where J is a finite category, meaning that Ob(J) and Mor(J) are finite sets.

The image data of Delta consists of:
- the objects Delta(j), for j in Ob(J);
- the morphisms Delta(u), for u in Mor(J).

The image data need not itself be a subcategory if one records only the literal image sets without closure. The subcategory generated by the image is denoted <Im(Delta)>_C.

RECONSTRUCTION R1.3 — DIAGRAM CONTAINMENT

A subcategory W of C contains Delta if Delta factors through the inclusion functor i:W->C.
Equivalently, every object and every morphism in the image of Delta belongs to W.

This is a strict containment/factorization condition. It does not by itself express any additional OMEGA-specific sufficiency property.

============================================================
2. WITNESS PREDICATE
============================================================

RECONSTRUCTION R2.1 — ADMISSIBLE WITNESS CLASS

For each finite diagram Delta:J->C, let Adm(Delta) be a specified class of subcategories W of C.

A member W of Adm(Delta) is called an admissible Delta-witness if:
(A) W contains Delta; and
(B) W satisfies the chosen admissibility predicate P_Delta(W).

P_Delta is a primitive parameter of the reconstruction. It must be explicitly supplied by a particular realization. It is not inferred from the word "sufficiency".

RECONSTRUCTION R2.2 — DIAGRAM-RELATIVE SUFFICIENCY

Define:
Suff(Delta,W) iff W is a subcategory of C, Delta factors through W, and P_Delta(W) holds.

Thus sufficiency is relative to:
- the ambient category C;
- the selected diagram Delta;
- the admissibility predicate P_Delta;
- the chosen class of witness subcategories.

No absolute or realization-independent sufficiency is asserted.

RECONSTRUCTION R2.3 — MINIMAL WITNESS

An admissible Delta-witness W is minimal if there is no proper subcategory V of W such that V is also an admissible Delta-witness.

Formally:
Minimal(Delta,W) iff Suff(Delta,W) and
for every V <= W,
(Suff(Delta,V) implies V=W).

The order is inclusion of subcategories, not cardinality and not a global leastness order over witnesses in different ambient categories.

RECONSTRUCTION R2.4 — LEAST WITNESS

A least Delta-witness W_* is an admissible witness such that W_* is contained in every admissible Delta-witness for Delta.

Leastness is stronger than minimality.
A witness class may have several incomparable minimal elements and no least element.
Therefore minimality does not imply global leastness.

============================================================
3. CLOSURE OPERATOR
============================================================

RECONSTRUCTION R3.1 — GENERATION

For a set S of objects and morphisms of C, define <S>_C as the smallest subcategory of C containing S, provided the object/morphism typing is valid.
It is obtained by adjoining required identities and all finite composable composites.

For a fixed diagram Delta, define the diagram-relative generated subcategory:
Cl_Delta(S) = <Im(Delta) union S>_C.

RECONSTRUCTION R3.2 — EXTRA CLOSURE OPERATIONS

A realization may require operations beyond ordinary categorical composition.
Represent these operations by a specified family Omega.
Then Cl_Delta,Omega(S) is the least subcategory containing Im(Delta) union S and closed under every operation in Omega, if such a least subcategory exists.

Omega must be specified operation-by-operation, including domains, codomains, and closure rules.
It must not be silently assumed that a closure operation is finitary or locally finite.

RECONSTRUCTION R3.3 — LOCAL FINITENESS

The closure operator is locally finite if, for every finite valid set S,
Cl_Delta,Omega(S) has finitely many objects and finitely many morphisms.

Local finiteness is a substantive hypothesis.
It does not follow merely because S and Delta are finite.

============================================================
4. EXISTENCE AND WELL-FOUNDEDNESS
============================================================

RECONSTRUCTION R4.1 — WITNESS PREORDER

Let W_Delta be the class of admissible Delta-witnesses, ordered by subcategory inclusion.

The witness order is well-founded if every nonempty subclass of W_Delta has an inclusion-minimal member.
A sufficient criterion is a rank function
rho:W_Delta->Ord
such that V properly contained in W implies rho(V)<rho(W).
For a natural-number-valued rank, there can be no infinite strictly descending chain.

RECONSTRUCTION R4.2 — MINIMAL-WITNESS EXISTENCE

If W_Delta is nonempty and its inclusion order is well-founded, then W_Delta contains a minimal witness.

Proof:
Apply well-foundedness to the nonempty class W_Delta itself. It has an inclusion-minimal element W. By definition, W is a minimal Delta-witness.

This establishes existence of a minimal witness, not its finiteness and not uniqueness.

RECONSTRUCTION R4.3 — FINITE-WITNESS EXISTENCE

Finite-witness existence is the stronger assertion:
there exists W in W_Delta such that W is finite.

It must be established separately, or derived from the finite-support and local-finiteness assumptions in Section 5.
Nonemptiness of W_Delta alone does not imply that any witness is finite.

============================================================
5. WHEN MINIMALITY IMPLIES FINITENESS
============================================================

RECONSTRUCTION R5.1 — FINITE-SUPPORT REDUCTION

The witness class has finite-support reduction if for every admissible witness W there exists a finite valid set S contained in the object/morphism data of W such that Cl_Delta,Omega(S) is itself an admissible Delta-witness and is contained in W.

This condition says that every witness contains a finitely supported subwitness.
It is not automatic for arbitrary categories or arbitrary admissibility predicates.

RECONSTRUCTION R5.2 — FINITE MINIMALITY THEOREM

Assume:
(FS) Finite-support reduction holds.
( LF) Cl_Delta,Omega is locally finite on finite valid sets.
(W) W is a minimal admissible Delta-witness.

Then W is finite.

Proof:
By (FS), choose finite S contained in W such that V=Cl_Delta,Omega(S) is an admissible Delta-witness and V is contained in W.
By minimality of W, V=W.
By (LF), V is finite.
Therefore W is finite.

The proof is valid only under the stated assumptions.
It does not establish (FS) or (LF) for the canonical OMEGA-ISOP realization.

RECONSTRUCTION R5.3 — FINITE DIAGRAM IS NOT ENOUGH

The finiteness of the indexing category J and of the diagram Delta does not by itself imply that <Im(Delta)>_C is finite.
A finite set of generating morphisms may generate infinitely many composites.
The infinite-dihedral example in Section 7 demonstrates this failure.

RECONSTRUCTION R5.4 — NO INFINITE MINIMAL WITNESS UNDER THE THEOREM'S ASSUMPTIONS

Under (FS) and (LF), every minimal admissible Delta-witness is finite.
Consequently, an infinite minimal witness is excluded in that realization.
Without these assumptions, the exclusion is not established.

============================================================
6. NON-UNIQUENESS AND GLOBAL LEASTNESS
============================================================

RECONSTRUCTION R6.1 — NON-UNIQUENESS

The witness class may contain two distinct minimal witnesses W_1 and W_2 such that neither is contained in the other.
This is compatible with existence of minimal witnesses and with finiteness of each minimal witness.

RECONSTRUCTION R6.2 — NO AUTOMATIC LEASTNESS

If W_1 and W_2 are incomparable minimal witnesses, there is no least witness equal to either one.
A least witness may still exist elsewhere in the class, but its existence requires a separate argument.

Thus:
minimal witness existence does not imply unique minimality;
unique minimality does not automatically imply leastness unless the order and class satisfy the relevant conditions;
finite minimal witnesses do not automatically yield a global least witness.

The reported status GLOBAL LEASTNESS: NOT GUARANTEED is consistent with this framework.

============================================================
7. FINITE-FAMILY EXTENSION AND D_INFINITY TEST
============================================================

RECONSTRUCTION R7.1 — COMMON WITNESS

For a finite family of finite diagrams Delta_1,...,Delta_n in C, a common witness W is a subcategory of C such that:
(1) W contains every Delta_i;
(2) W satisfies the jointly specified admissibility predicate P_(Delta_1,...,Delta_n)(W).

Individual admissibility P_Delta_i(W_i) does not imply joint admissibility of the union of the W_i.

RECONSTRUCTION R7.2 — INFINITE-DIHEDRAL COUNTERMODEL

Let
D_infinity = <a,b | a^2=e, b^2=e>
be the infinite dihedral group.

Let C be the one-object category with object X and
End_C(X)=D_infinity.
Composition is group multiplication.

Define two subcategories:
W_a: Ob(W_a)={X}, Hom_Wa(X,X)={e,a}.
W_b: Ob(W_b)={X}, Hom_Wb(X,X)={e,b}.

Both are finite subcategories:
a^2=e and b^2=e ensure closure under composition.

Let Delta_a and Delta_b be the corresponding finite one-object diagrams.
Each has an individual finite witness, namely W_a and W_b.

Any subcategory W of C containing both W_a and W_b must contain a and b and be closed under composition.
It therefore contains (ab)^n for every n>=0.
In D_infinity, ab has infinite order, so these morphisms are pairwise distinct.
Hence every common subcategory containing both W_a and W_b is infinite.

CONCLUSION:
Finite individual subcategory witnesses for a finite family do not, in general, imply a finite common subcategory witness.

SCOPE:
This is a counterexample to the unrestricted category/subcategory inference.
It is a CC-specific counterexample only if the selected CC realization defines witnesses by the containment and subcategory-closure conditions used here.
If the actual CC witness predicate differs, this model must be re-evaluated and may not refute the CC-specific statement.

============================================================
8. CONDITIONAL FINITE-FAMILY THEOREM
============================================================

RECONSTRUCTION R8.1 — FINITE COMMON WITNESS

Let Delta_1,...,Delta_n be a finite family.
Assume:
(C1) Each Delta_i has an individual finite witness W_i.
(C2) The family of witnesses is jointly compatible: their data can be embedded into a common admissible witness construction.
(C3) There is a finite valid support S containing the combined diagram data and the finite witness data required by the joint construction.
(C4) The joint closure Cl_(Delta_1,...,Delta_n),Omega(S) is finite.
(C5) The joint closure satisfies the common admissibility predicate.

Then
W = Cl_(Delta_1,...,Delta_n),Omega(S)
is a finite common witness.

Proof:
By construction, W contains the data of every Delta_i.
By (C2) and (C5), W is jointly admissible.
By (C4), W is finite.
Therefore W is a finite common witness.

The essential assumptions are joint compatibility and finite joint closure.
Neither follows from individual finite-witness existence alone.

RECONSTRUCTION R8.2 — FAMILY-LEVEL FINITE-SUPPORT CRITERION

A stronger route is available if the joint witness class satisfies finite-support reduction and local finiteness exactly as in Section 5.
If the joint witness class is nonempty and well-founded, it has a minimal member; finite-support reduction plus local finiteness then makes that minimal common witness finite.

This route requires the hypotheses to hold for the joint family, not merely for each diagram separately.

9. GLOBAL WAP AND STRONG AMALGAMATION
============================================================

The present reconstruction does not define a global WAP principle.
No global WAP conclusion is inferred from finite-witness existence, minimality, or the D_infinity model.

Strong amalgamation is not built into the reconstructed definition of a witness.
It may be imposed by a realization-specific admissibility predicate, but it is not a consequence of the general framework.

The reported status STRONG AMALGAMATION: NOT REQUIRED is preserved as a boundary statement, not upgraded into a theorem about every possible realization.

10. LOOP AUDIT
============================================================

TARGET T1:
A minimal admissible Delta-witness is finite.

DEPENDENCIES:
Finite-support reduction (FS).
Local finiteness (LF).
Minimality of W.

NO CIRCULARITY:
The conclusion "W is finite" is not included in the definition of minimality or in FS.
FS gives a finite generating support whose closure is a witness.
LF independently gives finiteness of that closure.
Minimality identifies W with that closure.

TARGET T2:
A finite family with finite individual witnesses has a finite common witness.

DEPENDENCIES:
Joint compatibility.
Finite joint support.
Finite joint closure.
Joint admissibility.

NO CIRCULARITY:
The finite common witness is concluded only after C2-C5 are stated independently.
No one of these assumptions is inferred from the desired conclusion.

KNOWN FAILURE:
D_infinity refutes T2 when only individual finite witnesses and categorical subcategory closure are assumed.
