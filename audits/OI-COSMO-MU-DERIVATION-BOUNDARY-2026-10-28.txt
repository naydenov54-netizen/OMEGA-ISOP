1. QUESTION
Does the available OMEGA-ISOP relational specification construct a unique spacetime volume measure mu from relational data R, without presupposing the target Lorentzian metric or inserting an external physical measure?

2. INPUTS AND REQUIRED TYPE
For the cosmological test, take R_cosmo to include a carrier E and a selected chronological relation ≪, as explicitly supplied in the prior conditional realization audit. This is already a test input; its derivation from the Frozen Core was not established there.

A spacetime measure suitable for the scale-fixing test requires at least:
(a) a measurable space (M, Sigma), or a specified measurable structure on E transported through a realization;
(b) a countably additive measure mu on Sigma;
(c) for a smooth four-dimensional metric interpretation, a specified relation between mu and the metric volume density, such as mu = dvol_g;
(d) a compatibility and regularity condition ensuring that the measure and causal/conformal structure can be represented together;
(e) if absolute physical scale is claimed, a normalization or dimensional calibration that is not merely an arbitrary convention.

The bare carrier and relation (E, ≪) do not, by their definitions alone, provide Sigma, countable additivity, a numerical assignment to measurable sets, a metric-volume matching condition, or an absolute physical unit.

3. UNDERDETERMINATION TEST
Suppose a measurable space (E, Sigma) and a nonzero measure mu have been added as extra data. For every constant c > 0, define:
mu_c(A) = c mu(A), for every A in Sigma.

Then mu_c is also a measure on the same measurable space. The underlying carrier and relational relation ≪ are unchanged. Unless the specification supplies a normalization, dimensional calibration, or independent rule that distinguishes c, the relational data do not choose between mu and mu_c.

If the total measure is finite and one imposes probability normalization mu(E) = 1, that fixes a dimensionless normalization by convention. It does not by itself supply a physical four-volume with units of length^4 or establish that the probability measure equals dvol_g for the target spacetime metric.

This scaling test shows that even after adding a measurable structure and a measure, absolute volume normalization is not supplied by the bare relational relation alone. It does not show that no richer relational structure or additional axiom could determine a measure.

4. MEASURE EXISTENCE IS A SEPARATE PROBLEM
The scaling test assumes a measure already exists. It does not prove existence of a suitable measure on every R_cosmo. The available specification does not provide a general construction:
R_cosmo -> (Sigma, mu)
with a proof of existence, uniqueness, regularity, covariance, and compatibility with a Lorentzian realization.

A rule that defines mu := dvol_g after selecting g is metric-induced. It is mathematically valid as a definition, but it does not independently select g or its scale. Using it as an independent scale selector after g has already been chosen would be circular.

Likewise, an invariant probability measure for a Markov kernel or dynamical map is not automatically a spacetime volume measure. It requires a measurable state space and dynamics, and its invariance property does not identify it with dvol_g.

5. CONDITIONAL BRIDGE RESULT
Under suitable spacetime assumptions, causal structure can constrain conformal geometry; adding compatible volume information can then fix the conformal factor. The previous OI-COSMO-VOLUME-MEASURE-SCALE-TEST record proves the elementary four-dimensional scaling statement conditionally:
g_2 = Omega^2 g_1 implies dvol(g_2) = Omega^4 dvol(g_1);
if both volume densities equal the same independently specified mu, then Omega = 1.

That result is downstream of the provision of mu. It does not construct mu from R_cosmo.

6. AUDIT FINDING
F1. Available OMEGA-ISOP relational definitions do not specify a cosmological measurable space and a spacetime volume measure as a derived output.
F2. No verified map R_cosmo -> mu, with existence and uniqueness conditions, is present in the inspected source material.
F3. Relational data plus an already supplied measure do not by themselves fix its absolute normalization: mu and c mu are distinct candidate measures for c > 0 unless an extra rule distinguishes them.
F4. A metric-induced measure mu = dvol_g is not an independent metric-scale selection principle.
F5. The existence of probability/invariant measures in the dynamics framework does not establish a spacetime volume measure or Einstein dynamics.
F6. Therefore, derivation of a unique physical spacetime measure from the available OMEGA-ISOP Frozen Core is NOT ESTABLISHED.
