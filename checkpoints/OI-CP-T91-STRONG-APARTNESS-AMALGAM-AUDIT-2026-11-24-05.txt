============================================================
1. QUESTION
============================================================

The previous checkpoint showed that the proposed object P is not a categorical pushout in the embedding-only category because compatible embeddings into a target may identify elements outside the common base.

The present test asks a different question:
Does there exist a common finite apartness structure into which both source configurations embed, with no identification of their non-base elements?

This is the strong amalgamation question, not the categorical pushout question.

============================================================
2. EXPLICIT CATEGORY
============================================================

Define APFin_emb as the class/category whose:
- objects are finite sets X with a binary relation # satisfying:
  (i) irreflexivity: not(x # x);
  (ii) symmetry: x # y iff y # x;
  (iii) cotransitivity: x # y implies (x # z) or (z # y), for every z in X;
- morphisms are embeddings f:X->Y satisfying:
  x #X y iff f(x) #Y f(y).

This is an explicitly defined auxiliary finite category/class. It is compatible with the standard apartness axioms stated in Volume II, but it is not asserted to be identical to the full canonical C_METACORE.

============================================================
3. SOURCE SPAN
============================================================

S0={a}, with empty apartness relation.
S1={a,b}, with apartness relation:
#1={(a,b),(b,a)}.
S2={a,c}, with apartness relation:
#2={(a,c),(c,a)}.

Embeddings:
e1:S0->S1, e1(a)=a.
e2:S0->S2, e2(a)=a.

Both are embeddings because the one-point source has no apart pairs and the maps are injective and relation-reflecting on their images.

============================================================
4. STRONG AMALGAM CANDIDATE
============================================================

Let:
P={a,b,c}
with:
#P={(a,b),(b,a),(a,c),(c,a)}.

Canonical maps:
j1:S1->P, j1(a)=a, j1(b)=b.
j2:S2->P, j2(a)=a, j2(c)=c.

Each map is injective and preserves and reflects the source relation:
the induced relation on {a,b} is exactly #1;
the induced relation on {a,c} is exactly #2.

The non-base images are disjoint:
j1(S1) \ j2(S2) = {b};
j2(S2) \ j1(S1) = {c};
j1(S1) intersect j2(S2) = {a}.

Thus P is a strong amalgam candidate for this span.

============================================================
5. APARTNESS AXIOM AUDIT FOR P
============================================================

IRREFLEXIVITY:
No pair (x,x) belongs to #P.

SYMMETRY:
Every listed pair has its reverse in #P.

COTRANSITIVITY:
It is enough to check the four apart pairs and all z in P.

For (a,b):
z=a: a#a is false, a#b is true.
z=b: a#b is true, b#b is false.
z=c: a#c is true, c#b is false.

For (b,a):
z=a: b#a is true, a#a is false.
z=b: b#b is false, b#a is true.
z=c: b#c is false, c#a is true.

For (a,c):
z=a: a#a is false, a#c is true.
z=b: a#b is true, b#c is false.
z=c: a#c is true, c#c is false.

For (c,a):
z=a: c#a is true, a#a is false.
z=b: c#b is false, b#a is true.
z=c: c#c is false, c#a is true.

Every required disjunction holds. Hence #P satisfies the stated apartness axioms.

6. STRONG AMALGAM CONCLUSION
============================================================

The maps j1 and j2 are embeddings in APFin_emb.
They agree on S0.
Their images intersect exactly in the image of S0.
P is itself an object of APFin_emb.

Therefore P is a strong amalgam of this specific span in the explicitly defined finite apartness embedding class.

This establishes a positive amalgamation result for this span. It does not by itself prove the strong amalgamation property for every span in APFin_emb.

============================================================
7. WHY THIS IS NOT A CATEGORICAL PUSHOUT
============================================================

A categorical pushout in the embedding category would require a unique embedding from P into every common embedding cocone.

Take Q=S1, with:
f1:S1->Q the identity;
f2:S2->Q defined by f2(a)=a and f2(c)=b.

Both f1 and f2 are embeddings and agree on S0.

Any set map h:P->Q satisfying the cocone equations must have:
h(a)=a;
h(b)=b;
h(c)=b.

Therefore h(b)=h(c), so h is not injective and cannot be an embedding.

Thus P is a strong amalgam for the span, but not a categorical pushout in APFin_emb.

There is no contradiction:
- amalgamation asks for at least one common embedding object, and strong amalgamation additionally keeps the non-base images disjoint;
- a categorical pushout asks for a unique morphism to every cocone.
These are different universal conditions.

============================================================
8. INVOLUTION AND STOCHASTIC PRESERVATION
============================================================

Define:
g(a)=a;
g(b)=c;
g(c)=b.

Then g is an involution:
g(g(x))=x.

It preserves and reflects #P:
x #P y iff g(x) #P g(y).

Therefore g is an automorphism of the strong amalgam P.

For p in [0,1], define:
kappa(x,.)=(1-p)delta_x+p delta_g(x).

For n-tuples:
Lambda_n(x1,...,xn;.)
=(1-p)delta_(x1,...,xn)
+p delta_(g(x1),...,g(xn)).

Each marginal equals kappa(x_i,.), and the joint support remains inside every coordinatewise #P constraint satisfied by the input tuple.

The family remains projectively consistent and permutation-equivariant.

This verifies stochastic relational preservation on this particular strong amalgam.
