PURPOSE:
To consolidate the development strategy of OMEGA-ISOP and establish a structured, evidence-based internal scheme for assessing the framework's mathematical integrity, proof traceability, architectural consistency, research governance, physical-interpretation discipline, and readiness for any later independent external assessment.

I. CORE IDENTITY AND SCOPE

OMEGA-ISOP is a meta-theory / conditional structural framework for investigating the conditions under which organization can arise and be maintained in relational structures.

Its identity, ontology, definitions, assumptions, limitations, and Frozen Core remain governed by their authoritative source records.

The physical research direction extends the investigated applicability of the framework. It does not, by itself, change the ontology or architecture of OMEGA-ISOP.

The internal assessment scheme evaluates the quality, traceability, consistency, and evidential status of the framework and its research records. It does not certify that OMEGA-ISOP is true, complete, universally applicable, or empirically confirmed.

II. MATHEMATICAL FOUNDATION AND SOURCE AUTHORITY

The mathematical basis consists of the existing formal constructions, definitions, theorems, proofs, validated extensions, and research records maintained in the OMEGA-ISOP source archive and GitHub repository.

Volume V receives particular attention, including its proof-bearing material and active research architecture.

Existing results must be reused with their original:
- definitions;
- assumptions;
- domains and types;
- notation;
- proof dependencies;
- limitations;
- evidential and validation status.

Every material claim must point to its exact authoritative source artifact and, where possible, exact section, theorem, proposition, lemma, proof, version, or commit.

Do not duplicate established proofs without a defined reason. Do not reconstruct missing source material from memory or inference.

A GitHub commit, file presence, internal review, or scheme assessment does not independently prove a mathematical theorem and does not establish canonical acceptance, RL registration, or CE-V promotion.

III. PHYSICAL RESEARCH DIRECTION

Investigate whether and under what explicit conditions existing OMEGA-ISOP mathematical structures can be interpreted or applied in physical research.

Potential domains:
- cosmology;
- quantum physics;
- gravitation;
- complex physical systems.

The research method is:

MATHEMATICAL RESULT
→ PHYSICAL INTERPRETATION
→ EXPLICIT REALIZATION / FORMAL MODEL
→ DERIVATION OF CONSEQUENCES
→ COMPARISON WITH ESTABLISHED THEORY
→ OBSERVATIONAL OR EXPERIMENTAL ASSESSMENT, WHERE POSSIBLE

Every transition must be justified.

A mathematical structure is not automatically a physical observable.
A formal correspondence is not automatically a physical mechanism.
A mathematical possibility is not automatically a physical realization.
A proved mathematical theorem does not automatically prove a physical interpretation.

OMEGA-ISOP is not presumed to replace General Relativity, quantum field theory, the Standard Model, or any established physical theory.

IV. EVIDENCE AND CLAIM CLASSIFICATION

Every assessed claim must be assigned an explicit evidence class.

M1 - ESTABLISHED MATHEMATICAL RESULT:
A theorem, lemma, proposition, or formal result proved in an identified source under stated assumptions.

M2 - FORMAL CONSEQUENCE:
A conclusion logically derived from identified mathematical results and explicitly stated additional premises.

P1 - PHYSICAL INTERPRETATION:
A proposed mapping between a mathematical structure and a physical concept. The mapping itself requires justification and must not inherit the proof status of its mathematical source.

P2 - PHYSICAL HYPOTHESIS:
A physical proposal not yet established, with assumptions and possible tests stated.

O1 - OPEN MATHEMATICAL PROBLEM:
A missing or incomplete formal link requiring further derivation or proof.

E1 - EMPIRICAL QUESTION:
A claim whose assessment requires observational, experimental, or independently validated physical data.

C1 - COMPUTATIONAL / NUMERICAL RESULT:
A result obtained computationally or numerically, with method, implementation, inputs, precision, and limitations recorded. It is not automatically an analytical proof.

Evidence class and workflow status must remain separate.

V. INTERNAL CONFORMITY-ASSESSMENT SCHEME

The scheme is named:

OMEGA-ISOP INTERNAL CONFORMITY ASSESSMENT (OICA)

OICA is an internal assessment procedure. It is not a legally recognized certification body, an accreditation body, or a substitute for independent peer review.

The scheme evaluates a defined assessment object. The object may be:
- the framework's identity and core documentation;
- a specific mathematical result or group of dependent results;
- a research volume or bounded research program;
- a physical-interpretation proposal;
- the project's research and document-control process.

No assessment may issue a blanket pass for all OMEGA-ISOP content merely because one object has passed.

VI. ASSESSMENT DOMAINS AND CRITERIA

DOMAIN D1 - IDENTITY, SCOPE, AND CLAIM BOUNDARIES

D1.1 The framework's identity is stated consistently across authoritative records.
D1.2 The distinction between meta-theory, mathematical result, physical interpretation, and empirical claim is explicit.
D1.3 Assumptions, scope, non-claims, and limitations are recorded.
D1.4 No unsupported claim of universality, physical explanation, or replacement of established theory is made.
D1.5 The assessment object is precisely identified.

Required evidence:
Identity statement, scope statement, source records, claim inventory, and explicit limitations.

DOMAIN D2 - MATHEMATICAL CORRECTNESS

D2.1 Definitions and mathematical objects are unambiguous within the stated formal system.
D2.2 The theorem or result is stated with all required assumptions.
D2.3 Domains, types, quantifiers, dependencies, and boundary conditions are explicit where relevant.
D2.4 Proof steps are logically valid and support the exact claim made.
D2.5 Counterexamples, exceptional cases, and known limitations are recorded.
D2.6 Reused results preserve the scope and assumptions of their original proofs.
D2.7 New mathematical claims are proved independently and are not treated as established before proof review.

Required evidence:
Exact theorem/proposition source, complete proof or derivation, dependency references, and review record.

DOMAIN D3 - PROOF AND SOURCE TRACEABILITY

D3.1 Every material mathematical claim has an exact source reference.
D3.2 The source version, file path, and relevant section or identifier are recorded.
D3.3 Dependencies between results are traceable.
D3.4 Any transformation, restatement, or reuse preserves the original meaning.
D3.5 Conflicting source records are identified and not silently reconciled.
D3.6 Volume V proof-bearing material is referenced at source level rather than replaced by summaries.
D3.7 Repository presence is not used as a substitute for proof verification.

Required evidence:
Source map, proof dependency map, version references, and claim-to-evidence matrix.

DOMAIN D4 - FROZEN CORE AND ARCHITECTURAL INTEGRITY

D4.1 The authoritative Frozen Core source is identified.
D4.2 Research extensions are distinguished from Core content.
D4.3 No undocumented or unauthorized Core modification is present.
D4.4 New claims are checked for compatibility with the Core's definitions and restrictions.
D4.5 Any proposed Core change has a documented change request and mathematical justification.
D4.6 Impact on dependent theorems, proofs, volumes, and records is assessed.
D4.7 Prior versions and their provenance are preserved.

Required evidence:
Core reference, architecture map, change log, compatibility analysis, and version history.

DOMAIN D5 - REPRODUCIBILITY AND TECHNICAL VERIFICATION

D5.1 Computational or numerical claims identify the method and relevant inputs.
D5.2 Code, notebooks, parameters, and execution environment are traceable where applicable.
D5.3 Reproduction status is recorded accurately.
D5.4 Numerical precision, tolerances, and error sources are stated where relevant.
D5.5 A computational check is not presented as an analytical proof.
D5.6 Manual review is not described as automated testing or independent verification.
D5.7 Checks are proportional to the claim and its risk.

Required evidence:
Reproducibility record, code/notebook source, execution output, test description, and limitations.

DOMAIN D6 - PHYSICAL-INTERPRETATION DISCIPLINE

D6.1 The mathematical source result is identified before the physical interpretation is evaluated.
D6.2 The mapping from mathematical objects to physical concepts is explicit.
D6.3 Physical assumptions and realization conditions are stated.
D6.4 Observable quantities and measurable consequences are defined where claimed.
D6.5 The proposal is compared with relevant established physical theories.
D6.6 Possible falsifying observations, experiments, or discriminating predictions are identified where feasible.
D6.7 No physical claim inherits theorem status from a mathematical source alone.
D6.8 Absence of empirical evidence is stated without converting it into confirmation or refutation.

Required evidence:
Mathematics-to-physics bridge record, formal model, assumptions, derived consequences, and empirical assessment plan.

DOMAIN D7 - DOCUMENT CONTROL AND RESEARCH GOVERNANCE

D7.1 Each controlled document has a unique title or identifier, version, date, and status.
D7.2 Draft, working candidate, reviewed, accepted, superseded, and archived states are distinguished.
D7.3 The authoritative source location is identified.
D7.4 GitHub changes are checked for conflicts and duplicates before writing.
D7.5 Every material write is followed by readback verification.
D7.6 Commit history and prior versions remain traceable.
D7.7 A GitHub commit is not treated as scientific validation, certification, RL registration, or CE-V promotion.
D7.8 Open issues and unresolved decisions are visible.

Required evidence:
Document register, status register, change records, commit references, and readback results.

DOMAIN D8 - INDEPENDENCE, REVIEW, AND DECISION INTEGRITY

D8.1 The assessor's role and competence are recorded.
D8.2 Conflicts of interest are disclosed.
D8.3 Author self-review is distinguished from independent review.
D8.4 Material mathematical claims receive review proportionate to their significance.
D8.5 Findings cite objective evidence and exact requirements.
D8.6 Decisions are reproducible from the assessment record.
D8.7 A reviewer does not claim authority or accreditation that they do not possess.

Required evidence:
Assessor declaration, review record, findings, and decision rationale.

VII. ASSESSMENT PROCESS

STAGE 0 - REGISTRATION OF ASSESSMENT OBJECT

Identify:
- assessment object;
- responsible author or research owner;
- source version and repository location;
- intended assessment scope;
- claims to be evaluated;
- exclusions and known limitations.

Output:
OICA assessment record with unique identifier.

STAGE 1 - SOURCE AND SCOPE REVIEW

Confirm the authoritative sources, relevant versions, claim boundaries, and evidence classes.

Output:
Source and claim inventory.

STAGE 2 - TECHNICAL ASSESSMENT

Apply only the criteria relevant to the assessment object.

For mathematical results, prioritize D2 and D3.
For Core or architecture changes, prioritize D1, D3, and D4.
For computational results, apply D2 where applicable and D5.
For physical interpretations, apply D1, D2, D3, and D6.
For document or process assessment, apply D1, D4, D7, and D8.

Output:
Criterion-by-criterion findings with evidence references.

STAGE 3 - FINDINGS CLASSIFICATION

Each criterion receives one of the following statuses:

PASS:
Objective evidence demonstrates that the applicable criterion is met.

PASS WITH LIMITATIONS:
The criterion is met within an explicitly restricted scope. Limitations are recorded.

MINOR NONCONFORMITY:
A bounded documentation or process defect that does not invalidate the central assessed claim.

MAJOR NONCONFORMITY:
A material defect affecting the validity, traceability, architectural consistency, or reliability of the assessed object.

NOT ASSESSED:
The criterion was not examined.

NOT APPLICABLE:
The criterion does not apply to the defined assessment object. A reason is mandatory.

OPEN:
Evidence is insufficient or the issue remains unresolved.

A criterion must not be marked PASS solely because no contradiction has yet been found.

STAGE 4 - CORRECTIVE ACTION

For every nonconformity:
- identify the exact criterion;
- identify the objective evidence;
- describe the defect;
- determine its scope and impact;
- identify the minimum adequate corrective action;
- assign responsibility;
- define the evidence needed to close it.

A documentation defect must not automatically trigger a Core change.

A Core change must not be used to conceal a proof defect or an unsupported physical claim.

STAGE 5 - REASSESSMENT

Reassess only the affected criteria and any dependent criteria whose validity may have changed.

Verify corrective evidence and update the findings register.

STAGE 6 - INTERNAL DECISION

The final internal decision is one of:

OICA-CONFORMANT:
All applicable critical criteria pass, and no unresolved major nonconformity remains.

OICA-CONFORMANT-WITH-LIMITATIONS:
The assessed object meets its defined criteria within explicitly recorded restrictions.

OICA-REQUIRES-CORRECTIVE-ACTION:
One or more material nonconformities remain, but the object may be reassessed after correction.

OICA-NOT-CONFORMANT:
A critical requirement is not met or a material defect invalidates the assessed claim within the stated scope.

OICA-INCOMPLETE:
The evidence is insufficient to reach a defensible decision.

The decision applies only to the named assessment object, source version, scope, and assessment date.

VIII. CRITICAL NONCONFORMITIES

The following are critical when they affect the assessed claim:

CNC-01: A theorem is claimed without a valid proof or without necessary assumptions.
CNC-02: A proof is materially inconsistent with its definitions or formal dependencies.
CNC-03: A result is materially misrepresented beyond its proved scope.
CNC-04: An unapproved change alters the Frozen Core.
CNC-05: A physical hypothesis is presented as an established physical result without adequate evidence.
CNC-06: A source or proof reference is fabricated, materially incorrect, or insufficient to trace the claim.
CNC-07: A material conflict between authoritative sources is concealed.
CNC-08: A certification, accreditation, independent review, or empirical validation status is falsely claimed.

Critical status requires documented evidence and impact analysis. It is not assigned merely because a topic is unresolved or exploratory.

IX. CONTROLLED CHANGE OF FROZEN CORE

The Frozen Core is protected by default, but it is not declared immune to correction if a genuine mathematical defect is demonstrated.

A proposed Core change may proceed only through the following controlled sequence:

1. Identify the exact Core statement or dependency in question.
2. State the defect or necessity precisely.
3. Distinguish a mathematical defect from a documentation, interpretation, or external conformity requirement.
4. Provide a formal argument or proof demonstrating why a change is necessary.
5. Identify all dependent definitions, theorems, proofs, volumes, and research records.
6. Evaluate backward compatibility and consequences for prior results.
7. Preserve the previous Core version unchanged.
8. Prepare a versioned change proposal with rationale and impact analysis.
9. Obtain explicit authorization before modifying the authoritative Core.
10. Verify the resulting architecture and all affected dependencies.

An external certification criterion does not automatically override mathematical correctness or authorize a Core change.

If an external scheme requires a property that is not appropriate to a mathematical meta-theory, record the mismatch and seek clarification or a more suitable assessment route.

X. EXTERNAL STANDARDS AND LIMITS OF CLAIM

The scheme uses the following standards as methodological references, subject to checking the full applicable editions before any formal external conformity claim:

A. ISO/IEC 17000:2020
Conformity assessment - Vocabulary and general principles.
Used as a terminology and general-principles reference for conformity assessment.

B. ISO 19011:2026
Guidelines for auditing management systems.
Used as guidance for audit planning, conduct, evidence, assessor competence, and reporting where relevant. It is not itself a product or theory certification standard.

C. ISO/IEC 17025:2017
General requirements for the competence of testing and calibration laboratories.
Not applicable to OMEGA-ISOP as a theory merely because it contains mathematical or physical research. It may become relevant only if a separate laboratory performs testing or calibration and seeks laboratory accreditation.

No claim of compliance with an entire ISO standard may be made on the basis of this document alone.

ISO does not itself perform certification. Any external certification or accreditation claim requires an appropriate external body, a defined certification object, applicable criteria, and an authorized assessment process.

The present OICA scheme is an internal project assessment framework. It does not issue an internationally recognized certificate.

XI. THREE PARALLEL DEVELOPMENT DIRECTIONS

A. MATHEMATICAL DEVELOPMENT

Use the existing mathematical base, especially Volume V.
Verify source claims and proof dependencies.
Develop only those new mathematical results that are required and can be independently proved.

B. PHYSICAL APPLICABILITY

Investigate physical interpretations and models.
Derive consequences only from explicit premises.
Keep mathematical proof, physical interpretation, and empirical evidence separate.

C. CERTIFICATION AND CONFORMITY READINESS

Maintain the OICA criteria and assessment records.
Identify gaps in documentation, proof traceability, architecture, and governance.
Determine whether a relevant external assessment route exists before making an external certification claim.

The three directions are parallel but methodologically independent. Progress in one direction does not automatically establish success in another.

XIII. INITIAL IMPLEMENTATION PLAN

PHASE 1 - BASELINE INVENTORY
Identify authoritative identity, Core, Volume II, Volume III, Volume IV, Volume V, CE-V archive, and operational sources. Record exact paths, versions, and status.

PHASE 2 - CLAIM AND PROOF MAPPING
Select a bounded set of important results, beginning with proof-bearing Volume V material. Map each claim to its exact source and dependencies.

PHASE 3 - PILOT ASSESSMENT
Run OICA on one bounded mathematical result or result set. Do not claim whole-project conformity from the pilot.

PHASE 4 - PHYSICAL BRIDGE PILOT
Select one clearly scoped physical interpretation. Assess the mathematical source, mapping assumptions, formal model, and possible empirical consequences separately.

PHASE 5 - DOCUMENT AND GOVERNANCE REVIEW
Assess source provenance, document status, version control, GitHub readback, and change management.

PHASE 6 - INDEPENDENT REVIEW
Where feasible, obtain review by a qualified person who did not author the assessed result. Record the reviewer's scope and competence. Do not call an internal self-review independent.

PHASE 7 - EXTERNAL ROUTE DECISION
Only after the object and intended outcome are clear, determine whether an external standard, peer-review process, laboratory accreditation, institutional evaluation, or another route is appropriate. Do not select a certification body or claim eligibility without evidence that it assesses this type of object.
