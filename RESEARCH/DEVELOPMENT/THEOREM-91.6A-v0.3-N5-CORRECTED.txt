1. PURPOSE AND SCOPE

This version incorporates the N5 correction concerning the bounded-noise recovery-uncertainty estimate in Section 6. It makes the positive-gain premise explicit and proves sharpness of the resulting universal diameter bound.

The mathematical scope remains finite-dimensional linear observations with Euclidean norms.

The theorem establishes:
1. Exact identifiability through a common-kernel and rank criterion.
2. Equivalence between injectivity and positive minimum observation gain in finite dimensions.
3. A Lipschitz bound for recovery on the observation image.
4. Separate statements for bounded-noise separation and recovery uncertainty.
5. The sharp universal diameter bound for states compatible with one observation under bounded deterministic noise.

This remains a new research construction. No claim is made that it reproduces a lost historical THEOREM 91.6A.

==================================================

2. FRAMEWORK

Let X = R^m with m>=1 and its Euclidean norm.
Let r >= 1 be finite.
For each i=1,...,r, let A_i:R^m->R^(p_i) be linear.

Define the stacked observation map:

A(x) = (A_1*x,...,A_r*x)

The observation space is the product Euclidean space
Y = R^(p_1+...+p_r).

Let A_stacked be the matrix formed by vertically stacking the matrices A_i.

Define the common kernel:

K = intersection(i=1 to r) ker(A_i).

Define:

gamma_A = inf(||z||=1) ||A_stacked*z||

M_A = ||A_stacked||.

==================================================

3. EXACT IDENTIFIABILITY

THEOREM 91.6A-1:

The following are equivalent:

(1) A is injective.
(2) K = {0}.
(3) rank(A_stacked) = m.

PROOF:

If A is injective and z belongs to K, then A(z)=A(0), so z=0.

If K={0} and A(x1)=A(x2), linearity gives
A_i*(x1-x2)=0 for every i. Thus x1-x2 belongs to K, so x1=x2.

The kernel of A_stacked is exactly K. By rank-nullity,
rank(A_stacked)+dim(K)=m. Therefore K={0} iff rank(A_stacked)=m.

Q.E.D.

==================================================

4. POSITIVE MINIMUM GAIN

THEOREM 91.6A-2:

For the stated finite-dimensional Euclidean setting:

A is injective iff gamma_A>0.

PROOF:

If gamma_A>0, then
||A_stacked*z|| >= gamma_A*||z||
for every z. Equality of observations therefore forces the state difference to be zero.

Conversely, if A is injective, its kernel is {0}. The continuous function
z -> ||A_stacked*z||
is strictly positive on the Euclidean unit sphere. The sphere is compact, so the function attains a strictly positive minimum.

Q.E.D.

The finite-dimensional compactness step is essential to this proof as written. The infinite-dimensional counterexample in Section 8 shows why injectivity alone does not generally imply a positive lower gain.

==================================================

5. LIPSCHITZ RECOVERY

For all x1,x2 in R^m:

gamma_A*||x1-x2||
<= ||A(x1)-A(x2)||
<= M_A*||x1-x2||.

This follows directly from the definition of gamma_A and the operator norm.

If A is injective, the inverse on the image,

A^(-1):A(R^m)->R^m,

is well-defined and satisfies:

Lip(A^(-1)) <= 1/gamma_A.

Thus, in this finite-dimensional linear setting, exact identifiability and global Lipschitz-stable recovery are equivalent. This equivalence is not asserted for arbitrary nonlinear maps or arbitrary infinite-dimensional spaces.

==================================================

6. BOUNDED NOISE: SEPARATION, RECOVERY UNCERTAINTY AND SHARPNESS

Let epsilon>=0 and consider the deterministic bounded-noise model:

y=A(x)+eta
||eta||<=epsilon.

For a fixed candidate x, the compatible observation set is the closed epsilon-ball centered at A(x).

SEPARATION CLAIM:

For two fixed states x1,x2, their closed noise balls are disjoint iff:

||A(x1)-A(x2)|| > 2*epsilon.

If the center distance is <=2*epsilon, the balls intersect or touch. Thus the two candidates cannot be guaranteed to be distinguished for every observation permitted by this bounded-noise model.

SUFFICIENT STATE-SEPARATION CONDITION:

By the lower-gain inequality,

||A(x1)-A(x2)|| >= gamma_A*||x1-x2||.

Assume A is injective. By THEOREM 91.6A-2, this is equivalent in the present finite-dimensional setting to:

gamma_A > 0.

Therefore the condition

||x1-x2|| > 2*epsilon/gamma_A

is sufficient for disjoint noise balls. It is not necessary for every individual pair, because the actual observation distance may be larger than its lower-gain estimate.

RECOVERY-UNCERTAINTY BOUND:

Fix one observation y. Suppose that y is compatible with both x1 and x2. Then there exist eta1 and eta2 such that:

y=A(x1)+eta1
y=A(x2)+eta2

with:

||eta1||<=epsilon
||eta2||<=epsilon.

Subtracting the two observation equations gives:

A(x1-x2)=eta2-eta1.

Hence, by the triangle inequality:

||A(x1-x2)||<=2*epsilon.

Since gamma_A>0:

gamma_A*||x1-x2||<=||A(x1-x2)||.

Consequently:

||x1-x2||<=2*epsilon/gamma_A.

Thus, for every fixed observation y, the set

C(y)={x in R^m : ||y-A(x)||<=epsilon}

of states compatible with y has diameter at most:

diam(C(y))<=2*epsilon/gamma_A.

This is a worst-case uncertainty bound. It does not guarantee unique recovery when epsilon>0.

SHARPNESS OF THE DIAMETER BOUND:

For epsilon>0, the bound is attained.

Because the Euclidean unit sphere in R^m is compact and
z -> ||A_stacked*z||
is continuous, there exists a unit vector v such that:

||v||=1
||A(v)||=gamma_A.

Choose:

x1=0
x2=(2*epsilon/gamma_A)*v
y=A(x2)/2.

Define the two noise vectors by:

eta1=y-A(x1)=A(x2)/2
eta2=y-A(x2)=-A(x2)/2.

Their norms are:

||eta1||=||eta2||
=||A(x2)||/2
=(1/2)*(2*epsilon/gamma_A)*gamma_A
=epsilon.

Therefore both x1 and x2 belong to C(y), while:

||x2-x1||=2*epsilon/gamma_A.

The universal diameter bound is therefore attained exactly. No smaller universal constant can replace 2*epsilon/gamma_A under the stated assumptions.

For epsilon=0 and gamma_A>0, C(y) is a singleton for every y in the image, and the same diameter formula gives zero.

If A is not injective, then gamma_A=0 in this finite-dimensional setting. A nonzero kernel vector produces distinct states with exactly the same observation, even when epsilon=0. Therefore no finite uniform recovery-uncertainty bound follows in the non-injective case.

==================================================

7. EXAMPLE

Let X=R² and define:

A_1(u,v)=(u,0)
A_2(u,v)=(0,v).

Neither component map is injective on R². The stacked map is:

A(u,v)=(u,0,0,v).

Its common kernel is {0}, and its two nonzero singular values are 1. Hence:

gamma_A=1
M_A=1.

The combined map preserves Euclidean distances exactly.

==================================================

8. INFINITE-DIMENSIONAL COUNTEREXAMPLE

On l² define:

T(x1,x2,x3,...)=(x1,x2/2,x3/3,...).

T is bounded and injective. For the standard unit vector e_n:

||e_n||=1
||T(e_n)||=1/n -> 0.

Therefore no gamma>0 satisfies
||T(x)||>=gamma*||x|| for all x.

The inverse on the image is not bounded. Thus the finite-dimensional equivalence between injectivity and positive minimum gain does not extend automatically to infinite dimensions.

10. NON-IMPLICATIONS AND LIMITATIONS

This theorem does not establish:
- results for arbitrary nonlinear observations;
- positive minimum gain in arbitrary infinite-dimensional spaces;
- unique recovery under nonzero bounded noise;
- statistical identifiability under a probability model;
- Shannon information preservation or loss;
- physical realizability, empirical validity, or a physical interpretation;
- universal applicability to OMEGA-ISOP models.

The proven content concerns mathematical distinguishability and worst-case metric uncertainty under the stated observation map, Euclidean norms, and deterministic bounded-noise model. The word "information" must not be used as a synonym for Shannon information in this theorem.

11. LOOP AUDIT RECORD

TARGET:
Injectivity of the finite stacked linear map iff trivial common kernel iff full column rank; and, in finite dimensions, iff positive minimum gain. Under bounded deterministic noise and positive minimum gain, the compatible-state set for any one observation has diameter at most 2*epsilon/gamma_A, and this universal bound is sharp.

PREMISES:
Finite-dimensional real vector space R^m with m>=1; finite family of linear maps; stacked product Euclidean norm; rank-nullity; compactness of the unit sphere; deterministic noise bounded in norm by epsilon.

DEPENDENCY:
The common-kernel equivalence follows from linearity and definitions. The rank equivalence follows from rank-nullity. Positive gain follows from compactness and continuity. The recovery-uncertainty bound follows from the triangle inequality and positive minimum gain. Sharpness follows by selecting a minimizing unit vector and constructing two states and one observation whose noise norms both equal epsilon.

COUNTERMODEL:
The l² diagonal operator in Section 8 refutes an unrestricted infinite-dimensional extension.

CLASSIFICATION:
NON-CIRCULAR DERIVATION WITHIN THE STATED FINITE-DIMENSIONAL LINEAR SCOPE. THE BOUNDED-NOISE DIAMETER CONSTANT IS SHARP.

NOISE AUDIT:
Separation of two closed noise balls, the sufficient state-separation condition, and the compatible-state diameter bound are distinct statements. None claims unique recovery under nonzero bounded noise.
