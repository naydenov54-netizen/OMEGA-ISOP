============================================================
1. OBJECTIVE
============================================================

S3.19 proved a strong-amalgamation theorem for quotient-invariant
pure-relational expansions of Boolean-apartness structures.

The construction descends each relation to the quotient Q, forms
a relational amalgam on Q, and pulls the result back to the P2.3
carrier.

S3.20 audits what happens when additional axioms are imposed on
the quotient-level relations.

The key question is not merely whether a relation descends.
It is whether the chosen class of quotient relational structures
is closed under the selected quotient-level amalgam.

We test:
  (i) symmetry;
  (ii) irreflexivity;
  (iii) transitivity;
  (iv) functionality;
  (v) acyclicity.

We distinguish:
  A. preservation by the free quotient union;
  B. existence of some strong amalgam;
  C. existence of any amalgam, possibly non-strong;
  D. preservation of the property after pullback to S_P.


============================================================
2. COMMON SETUP
============================================================

Let C -> A and C -> B be embeddings of relational structures.
Use disjoint copies over C, so:

    A intersect B = C.

For a binary relation R, the free relational amalgam on the
pushout set is:

    R_free = R_A union R_B.

A pair whose two coordinates are not both supplied by one side
is false unless it already belongs to a side relation.

For the OMEGA-ISOP application, assume these are quotient
structures Q_C, Q_A, Q_B obtained from the Boolean-apartness
quotients in S3.19. The same examples can be realized as
quotient structures by taking singleton fibers, or lifted to
nontrivial fibers by replacing quotient points with
indistinguishability classes.

After constructing a quotient-level amalgam (Q,R_Q), the
carrier-level relation is:

    R_P(x,y) IFF R_Q(pi(x),pi(y)).

This pullback is quotient-invariant by construction.


============================================================
3. PROPERTIES PRESERVED BY FREE QUOTIENT UNION
============================================================

3.1 Symmetry

Assume R_A and R_B are symmetric.

If (x,y) belongs to R_free, it belongs to R_A or R_B.
Symmetry in that same side gives (y,x) in the same relation.
Therefore (y,x) belongs to R_free.

Conclusion:
The free quotient union preserves symmetry.

3.2 Irreflexivity

Assume neither R_A nor R_B contains a loop.

Every loop (x,x) in R_free would have to belong to R_A or R_B,
because R_free is their union. This is impossible.

Conclusion:
The free quotient union preserves irreflexivity.

3.3 Reflexivity

Assume each side relation is reflexive on its own carrier.

Every element of the pushout belongs to A or B. Its loop is
therefore present in R_A or R_B, and hence in R_free.

Conclusion:
The free quotient union preserves reflexivity.

These proofs do not depend on finiteness.


============================================================
4. TRANSITIVITY: FREE UNION CAN FAIL, BUT COMPLETION MAY WORK
============================================================

Let:

    C = {c}
    A = {a,c}, with R_A = {(a,c)}
    B = {c,b}, with R_B = {(c,b)}.

Both R_A and R_B are transitive: neither contains a composable
pair of edges whose required composite is missing.

The free union contains:

    a R_free c
    c R_free b

but does not contain:

    a R_free b.

Therefore R_free is not transitive.

However, this does not prove that no transitive amalgam exists.

Define instead:

    R_D = {(a,c),(c,b),(a,b)}.

Then R_D is transitive, and its restrictions to A and B are
exactly R_A and R_B. The embeddings remain induced.

Thus this span has a strong transitive amalgam, but not the
free relational union.

Conclusion:
Transitivity is not preserved by free union in general.
For this span, a non-free strong amalgam exists.

No general amalgamation theorem for all transitive relations
is claimed here.


============================================================
5. FUNCTIONALITY: STRONG AMALGAM CAN FAIL
============================================================

Interpret R(x,y) as a partial-function graph, with functionality:

    For all x,y,z:
      (R(x,y) AND R(x,z)) IMPLIES y=z.

Take:

    C = {c}
    A = {c,a}, R_A = {(c,a)}
    B = {c,b}, R_B = {(c,b)}

where a and b are distinct and outside C.

Each input relation is functional.
The restrictions to C agree: both have no pair in C x C.

The free union contains:

    R_free(c,a)
    R_free(c,b)

with a != b. Hence it is not functional.

More strongly, no strong amalgam in the class of functional
relations can preserve both embeddings.

Proof:
In any amalgam D, the images of a and b must remain distinct
under a strong amalgam. But the preserved input relations require
both R_D(c,a) and R_D(c,b). Functionality then forces a=b,
contradicting strongness.

A non-strong amalgam can identify a and b. Therefore this example
shows failure of strong amalgamation, not failure of every
possible amalgam.

Conclusion:
Functionality is not preserved by free union, and the class of
functional binary relations does not have strong amalgamation
for this span.


============================================================
6. ACYCLICITY: A SPAN WITH NO AMALGAM
============================================================

Interpret R as a directed edge relation. A directed cycle is
a finite sequence:

    x_0 R x_1 R ... R x_(n-1) R x_0.

Let:

    C = {c1,c2}, with no R-edges.

Let A add a new point a and edges:

    c1 R_A a
    a R_A c2

Let B add a distinct new point b and edges:

    c2 R_B b
    b R_B c1.

A and B are individually acyclic.
Their induced restrictions to C agree and are empty.

Any amalgam preserving both embeddings contains the cycle:

    c1 -> a -> c2 -> b -> c1.

Adding more edges cannot remove this cycle. Identifying a and b
does not remove it either.

Therefore this span has no amalgam in the class of acyclic
directed relations, strong or otherwise.

Conclusion:
Acyclicity is not preserved by free union, and for this specific
span no alternative amalgam can exist.

============================================================
8. EFFECT OF PULLBACK TO THE P2.3 CARRIER
============================================================

Let pi:S_P -> Q be the P2.3 quotient projection and define:

    R_P(x,y) IFF R_Q(pi(x),pi(y)).

Then:

8.1 Properties preserved by inverse image under pi

If R_Q is symmetric, R_P is symmetric.

If R_Q is irreflexive, R_P is irreflexive:
R_P(x,x) would imply R_Q(pi(x),pi(x)).

If R_Q is reflexive, R_P is reflexive.

If R_Q is transitive, R_P is transitive:
R_P(x,y) and R_P(y,z) imply
R_Q(pi(x),pi(y)) and R_Q(pi(y),pi(z));
transitivity gives R_Q(pi(x),pi(z)), hence R_P(x,z).

If R_Q is acyclic, R_P is acyclic:
a directed cycle in S_P would project to a closed directed walk
in Q, which contains a directed cycle.

8.2 Functionality is not generally preserved

Suppose R_Q(q,r), and the fiber pi^(-1)(r) contains two
distinct elements y1 and y2.

For any x in pi^(-1)(q), pullback gives:

    R_P(x,y1)
    R_P(x,y2).

Thus R_P is not functional.

Even if R_Q is functional, pullback need not be functional
unless every relevant target fiber has size at most one.

A sufficient condition for functionality of the pullback is:

    For every q with an R_Q-successor r,
    |pi^(-1)(r)| <= 1.

This is a restrictive fiber condition, not a consequence of
S3.19.

Conclusion:
A quotient-level axiom must be checked twice:
first under quotient amalgamation, then under pullback to S_P.
The quotient-level property alone does not guarantee its
carrier-level preservation.


============================================================
9. CONSEQUENCES FOR THE S3.19 THEOREM
============================================================

S3.19 remains valid as stated for the class whose only extra
condition is coordinatewise quotient invariance.

S3.20 identifies the additional closure obligations.

For a proposed quotient-level constraint K:

K1. Determine whether each input quotient structure satisfies K.

K2. Determine whether the free quotient union preserves K.

K3. If not, test whether another quotient-level strong amalgam
    can preserve K.

K4. If no strong amalgam exists, test separately whether a
    non-strong amalgam exists.

K5. After selecting a quotient amalgam, test whether pullback
    along pi preserves K on the P2.3 carrier.

The examples show three genuinely different outcomes:
  - symmetry/irreflexivity: free union succeeds;
  - transitivity: free union fails but a strong completion
    succeeds in the displayed span;
  - functionality: strong amalgamation fails in the displayed
    span, although a non-strong amalgam exists;
  - acyclicity: a displayed span has no amalgam at all.

Therefore no single inference from "free union fails" to
"amalgamation fails" is valid.


============================================================
10. LIMITATIONS AND NEXT RESEARCH QUESTION
============================================================

This audit treats one binary relation at a time and the listed
properties only.

It does not classify:
  - arbitrary first-order theories;
  - combinations of constraints;
  - constraints coupling multiple relation symbols;
  - operations or function symbols in the OMEGA-ISOP language;
  - dynamics, Markov kernels, realization maps, or response maps;
  - the complete OMEGA-ISOP operational architecture.

The next rigorous step is to formulate a constraint-preservation
criterion in terms of quotient-level amalgamation, then test
whether useful syntactic fragments (for example, selected
universal Horn constraints) satisfy it.

Do not claim that all universal Horn constraints are preserved.
The functionality example already shows that such a blanket
claim would be false.

11. FINAL STATUS
============================================================

S3.20 RESULT:
Explicit positive and negative constraint-preservation results
have been established.

POSITIVE:
Free quotient union preserves symmetry, irreflexivity, and
reflexivity.

NEGATIVE:
Free quotient union need not preserve transitivity,
functionality, or acyclicity.

STRONGER NEGATIVE:
A specific functional-relation span has no strong amalgam.
A specific acyclic-relation span has no amalgam at all.

PULLBACK AUDIT:
Symmetry, irreflexivity, reflexivity, transitivity, and
acyclicity are preserved by inverse image along pi.
Functionality is not generally preserved.
