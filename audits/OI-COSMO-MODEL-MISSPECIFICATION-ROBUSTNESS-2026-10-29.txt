ROBUSTNESS OF COSMOLOGICAL SCALE IDENTIFIABILITY UNDER MODEL MISSPECIFICATION
AUDIT ID: OI-COSMO-MODEL-MISSPECIFICATION-ROBUSTNESS-2026-10-29
STATUS: CONDITIONAL MATHEMATICAL AUDIT
DATE: 2026-10-29

1. PURPOSE

This audit examines how conclusions about H change when the exact observation model, calibration model, redshift values, or noise covariance are imperfectly specified. It distinguishes:
(A) random measurement uncertainty under a correct model;
(B) systematic calibration or design-variable error;
(C) cosmological model misspecification;
(D) structural non-identifiability.

These are different failure modes. Increasing sample size can reduce some random errors but does not, by itself, remove systematic bias, correct a wrong model, or break an exact parameter symmetry.

No universal robustness guarantee is asserted. Every result is conditional on the explicit model and perturbation assumptions below.

2. BASELINE MODEL AND EXACT IDENTIFIABILITY

The idealized baseline is

s_i = z_i H/q + epsilon_i,

with H>0, q>0, known z_i>0. If the complete data distribution depends on H and q only through H/q, then for any a>0 the transformation

(H,q) -> (aH, aq)

preserves the distribution while changing H. Hence H is not statistically identifiable from these observations alone.

Adding more observations of the same form, including additional redshifts, does not break that symmetry if all z_i are fixed known values and the noise law depends on parameters only through H/q. This is a structural statement, not a claim that the ratio H/q cannot be estimated precisely.

A genuine independent observation of q can break this symmetry within the stated model. But that conclusion depends on the calibration observable really being linked to q in a known convention, and on the joint observation model being adequately specified.

3. RANDOM ERROR VERSUS SYSTEMATIC CALIBRATION ERROR

Suppose the true exact-de-Sitter relation at a fixed z>0 is

s = zH/q,

but the reported calibration is u=q+eta and the estimator uses Hhat=s_hat u/z.

If the measurement errors are random, zero-mean, and their joint distribution is correctly modeled, uncertainty can be quantified through the likelihood or a justified approximation. If instead the calibration reading has an unknown systematic offset b,

u = q + b + eta,

and b is unrestricted, then u does not uniquely determine q. The transformation

(H,q,b) -> (aH, aq, q+b-aq)

preserves both H/q and q+b, provided the transformed nuisance parameter remains in its admissible domain. Thus, where that domain permits such transformations, H remains non-identifiable from (s,u) alone.

If calibration instead has an unknown multiplicative factor r>0,

u = q/r + eta,

then (H,q,r)->(aH,aq,ar) preserves H/q and q/r. Again, if the full noise distribution shares the symmetry, H is not statistically identifiable.

The general lesson is not that all calibration errors destroy identifiability. It is that an unrestricted calibration nuisance parameter may restore a target-changing equivalence. Bounds, prior information, or an independent measurement can change the result, but their information content must be explicitly modeled.

4. FIRST-ORDER EFFECT OF REDSHIFT UNCERTAINTY

For exact de Sitter with a genuinely calibrated observer proper time, the ideal relation is

s = zH,

where s denotes the measured redshift drift per unit proper time. If the redshift used in the estimator is uncertain, write zhat=z+delta_z and shat=s+delta_s. The plug-in estimator is

Hhat = shat/zhat.

For z>0 and |delta_z|<z, the exact error identity is

Hhat-H = (delta_s - H delta_z)/(z+delta_z).

To first order around delta_s=delta_z=0,

delta_H = Hhat-H approximately equals delta_s/z - H delta_z/z.

Thus redshift uncertainty contributes directly to the uncertainty in H and may correlate with drift uncertainty. If delta_s and delta_z are small, zero-mean, and have variances sigma_s^2 and sigma_z^2 with covariance sigma_sz, the first-order variance approximation is

Var(Hhat) approximately equals
sigma_s^2/z^2 + H^2 sigma_z^2/z^2 - 2H sigma_sz/z^2.

This is a local approximation, not an exact finite-error result. The exact identity also shows why denominators near zero are ill-conditioned: if z is small relative to its uncertainty, linearization and stable recovery can fail. The analysis does not treat z=0 as an identifiable case.

5. COSMOLOGICAL MODEL MISSPECIFICATION: EXACT DE SITTER VERSUS GENERAL FLRW

The exact-de-Sitter redshift-drift relation is s(z)=zH for a constant H in the specified idealized model. In a general FLRW model, the standard relation is

s(z) = (1+z) H0 - H(z),

with the appropriate observer proper-time convention and model assumptions.

If data are generated by a general FLRW function H(z) but analyzed using the exact-de-Sitter form s=zH_fit, then each redshift implies

H_fit(z) = [(1+z)H0 - H(z)]/z, for z>0.

This apparent H_fit need not be constant across redshifts. A single-redshift fit can absorb model discrepancy into a redshift-specific apparent value; multiple redshifts can test consistency with the exact-de-Sitter restriction. Such a test requires an error model and adequate redshift coverage.

For an illustrative perturbation H(z)=H0+delta_H(z), the general FLRW relation becomes

s(z)=zH0-delta_H(z).

If the data are then fit with s=zH_fit, the apparent parameter is

H_fit(z)=H0-delta_H(z)/z.

Therefore the bias relative to H0 is -delta_H(z)/z under this particular misspecified fit. This formula is conditional on the stated perturbation and fixed H0; it is not a universal bias formula for arbitrary estimators or cosmological models.

More observations reduce random error only to the extent permitted by their covariance. They do not force a misspecified model to become correct. Under misspecification, a fitted parameter may converge to a model-dependent pseudo-true value rather than the physical target.

6. COVARIANCE MISSPECIFICATION

Let the observation vector have mean mu(theta), true covariance Sigma_true, and fitted covariance Sigma_fit. If the mean model is correct and the estimator is generalized least squares using the wrong fixed covariance, the estimator may remain unbiased in a linear model under suitable exogeneity assumptions, but its efficiency and reported uncertainty can be wrong. In nonlinear models, finite-sample bias and asymptotic target behavior require separate analysis.

For a linear mean model y=X beta+epsilon with E[epsilon]=0 and full-column-rank X, the generalized least-squares estimator using any specified positive-definite weight W is

beta_hat=(X^T W X)^(-1) X^T W y.

Its expectation is beta, while its true covariance is

Var(beta_hat)=(X^T W X)^(-1) X^T W Sigma_true W X (X^T W X)^(-1).

The conventional covariance formula based on the assumed covariance is valid only when the weighting and covariance assumptions justify it. Thus using the wrong covariance can misstate uncertainty even where unbiasedness survives in this linear setting.

In a nonlinear model, parameter-dependent covariance model, or model with data-dependent weights, no blanket unbiasedness conclusion follows. The complete likelihood or estimating equations must be analyzed.

If the covariance is parameter-dependent, it may carry statistical information beyond the mean. A Fisher-information calculation that includes only J^T Sigma^{-1}J is then generally incomplete. Conversely, an assumed covariance pattern must not be treated as an independently observed calibration signal unless it is justified and identifiable in the full data model.

7. ROBUSTNESS CHECKS AND SENSITIVITY

For a proposed H estimate, a minimal robustness report should include:

(a) Structural identifiability: Are there parameter transformations preserving the complete data distribution while changing H?
(b) Calibration sensitivity: Does the calibration map identify q, or only q combined with an offset, scale, or nuisance parameter?
(c) Model sensitivity: How does the estimate change under admissible departures from exact de Sitter, including a nonconstant H(z)?
(d) Design-variable sensitivity: Are redshift values treated as fixed known quantities, or are their errors and correlations modeled?
(e) Covariance sensitivity: Does the conclusion survive plausible alternative covariance structures? Are uncertainties computed from the true or assumed covariance?
(f) Statistical uncertainty: What sampling distribution, interval method, or asymptotic approximation is being used?
(g) Domain and conditioning: Are z and calibration observables bounded away from values where the inverse becomes unstable?
(h) Scope: Which conclusions are global, which are local, and which depend on perturbative approximations?

A useful stress test is to fit the baseline model, then vary one assumption at a time and jointly: calibration offset or scale, nonconstant H(z), redshift error, and covariance misspecification. Sensitivity analysis is diagnostic, not a substitute for proving identifiability under the revised model.

8. WHAT CAN AND CANNOT BE CONCLUDED

Conditional conclusions:
- Exact symmetry under (H,q)->(aH,aq) prevents separate identification of H when the full observation law depends only on H/q.
- Unrestricted additive or multiplicative calibration nuisance parameters can preserve or restore target-changing symmetries.
- Redshift uncertainty propagates into H estimation; the displayed variance formula is first-order only.
- General FLRW redshift drift need not follow the exact-de-Sitter relation, and forcing that relation can yield redshift-dependent apparent H values.
- Covariance misspecification can misstate uncertainty; in a linear correctly specified mean model with zero-mean errors, wrong fixed GLS weights can preserve unbiasedness while changing true variance.
- More data do not automatically cure systematic model discrepancy or structural non-identifiability.

Not established:
- A universal numerical robustness bound for arbitrary cosmological models.
- A universal estimator that is unbiased under arbitrary misspecification.
- That an OMEGA-ISOP formal structure independently derives physical calibration or selects a cosmological model.
- That redshift drift alone identifies H0 in a general FLRW model with unknown H(z) and other nuisance parameters.
- Any Frozen Core modification, CE-V promotion, or RL registration.
