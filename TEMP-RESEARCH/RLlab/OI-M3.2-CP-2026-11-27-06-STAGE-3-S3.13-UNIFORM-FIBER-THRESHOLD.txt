============================================================
1. PURPOSE
============================================================

S3.12 established an exact criterion for a fixed surjection pi:P->Q:
a finite forbidden relational structure F occurs as an induced substructure of the pullback P^pi exactly when there is an induced relational map h:F->Q whose point-preimages fit inside the corresponding fibers of pi.

S3.13 studies the uniform case in which every fiber has the same finite size m. The objective is to determine the first fiber size at which a forbidden structure appears, and to characterize preservation for all finite uniform amplifications.

This checkpoint does not replace S3.12. It extracts a numerical threshold from its exact fiber-capacity criterion.

============================================================
2. FORMAL SETUP
============================================================

Let Sigma be a purely relational signature whose relation symbols have finite arities.

Let Q be a Sigma-structure.

Let F be a nonempty finite Sigma-structure.

An induced relational map h:F->Q is a function such that, for every relation symbol R of arity n and every tuple (v_1,...,v_n) in F (including tuples with repeated coordinates),

R_F(v_1,...,v_n) iff R_Q(h(v_1),...,h(v_n)).

The map h is not required to be injective.

For each induced relational map h:F->Q define its collision number:

c(h) = max { |h^(-1)({q})| : q in h(F) }.

If no induced relational map F->Q exists, the set of collision numbers is empty.

Define the induced-map fiber threshold:

kappa_F(Q) = min { c(h) : h:F->Q is an induced relational map }.

Convention:

kappa_F(Q) = infinity if there is no induced relational map F->Q.

Because F is finite and nonempty, every c(h) is a positive integer. The minimum exists whenever the set is nonempty.

For a positive integer m, define the uniform m-fold blow-up of Q:

Q^(m) = Q x {1,...,m}

with projection pi_m(q,i)=q.

All relations are pulled back along pi_m. Thus, for every R of arity n,

R_Q^(m)((q_1,i_1),...,(q_n,i_n))
iff
R_Q(q_1,...,q_n).

Every fiber of pi_m has exactly m elements.

Let K=Forb(Fset), where Fset is a family of nonempty finite Sigma-structures and membership means that no member of Fset embeds as an induced substructure.

============================================================
3. MAIN THRESHOLD THEOREM
============================================================

THEOREM S3.13-A:

For every nonempty finite relational structure F and every positive integer m,

F embeds as an induced substructure of Q^(m)
iff
kappa_F(Q) <= m.

PROOF:

(=>)

Assume e:F->Q^(m) is an induced embedding.

Define h=pi_m composed with e.

Since Q^(m) is the pullback of Q along pi_m, the induced-relational equivalence for e implies that h is an induced relational map F->Q.

For each q in h(F), the restriction of e to h^(-1)({q}) is an injective map into the fiber pi_m^(-1)({q}), which has exactly m elements.

Therefore,

|h^(-1)({q})| <= m

for every q in h(F).

Hence c(h)<=m, and by the definition of kappa_F(Q),

kappa_F(Q)<=m.

(<=)

Assume kappa_F(Q)<=m.

Then there exists an induced relational map h:F->Q with c(h)<=m.

For every q in h(F), choose an injection

s_q:h^(-1)({q})->{1,...,m}.

Define

e(v)=(h(v),s_(h(v))(v)).

If v and w are distinct and h(v) differs from h(w), then e(v) and e(w) differ in their first coordinates.

If h(v)=h(w), injectivity of s_(h(v)) makes their second coordinates different.

Thus e is injective.

Also pi_m composed with e equals h. Since all relations in Q^(m) are pulled back from Q, the induced-relational equivalence for h transfers directly to e.

Therefore e is an induced embedding of F into Q^(m).

This proves the theorem.

============================================================
4. CLASS-LEVEL THRESHOLD
============================================================

For K=Forb(Fset), define

kappa_K(Q) = min { kappa_F(Q) : F in Fset }.

If every kappa_F(Q) is infinity, set kappa_K(Q)=infinity.

Then:

THEOREM S3.13-B:

For every positive integer m,

Q^(m) belongs to K
iff
m < kappa_K(Q).

PROOF:

Q^(m) fails to belong to K exactly when at least one F in Fset embeds as an induced substructure of Q^(m).

By S3.13-A, this is equivalent to the existence of F in Fset with kappa_F(Q)<=m.

That is equivalent to kappa_K(Q)<=m.

Negating both sides gives:

Q^(m) belongs to K iff m<kappa_K(Q).

The result follows.

============================================================
5. CONSEQUENCES WHEN THE QUOTIENT ALREADY BELONGS TO K
============================================================

Assume Q belongs to K.

Then no forbidden F in Fset has an injective induced relational map into Q, because such a map would be an induced embedding and would contradict Q in K.

Consequently, for every forbidden F:

Either no induced relational map F->Q exists and kappa_F(Q)=infinity,

or every induced relational map is non-injective and kappa_F(Q)>=2.

Therefore:

1. A forbidden structure cannot first appear at m=1 when Q is already in K.

2. If kappa_K(Q)=r is finite, then all uniform blow-ups with m<r remain in K, while every uniform blow-up with m>=r fails to belong to K.

3. If kappa_K(Q)=infinity, then every finite uniform blow-up Q^(m) belongs to K.

4. Preservation under every finite uniform amplification is equivalent to the absence of every induced relational map from every forbidden F into Q, including non-injective maps.

The fourth statement is stronger than Q in K. Membership of Q in K rules out injective induced maps, but does not rule out non-injective induced maps.

============================================================
6. RECOVERY OF S3.10 AND S3.11
============================================================

S3.10:

F has three elements and the universal binary relation.

Q has one element q and the loop R_Q(q,q).

The constant map h:F->Q is induced because every ordered pair in F is related and the only ordered pair in Q is related.

Its only nonempty point-preimage has size 3.

Thus:

kappa_F(Q)=3.

Therefore:

Q^(1) belongs to Forb(F),
Q^(2) belongs to Forb(F),
Q^(3) does not belong to Forb(F),
and Q^(m) does not belong to Forb(F) for every m>=3.

This recovers the three-point obstruction in S3.10 and identifies its exact onset.

S3.11:

F has two elements u,v and exactly one directed relation R_F(u,v).

Q is a singleton with a loop.

The only possible map h:F->Q is constant.

It is not induced, because R_F(v,u) is false while R_Q(q,q) is true.

Thus no induced relational map F->Q exists, and

kappa_F(Q)=infinity.

Consequently Q^(m) avoids F for every positive integer m.

This recovers the preservation result in S3.11 for this forbidden structure.

============================================================
7. UNIFORM AND NONUNIFORM FIBERS
============================================================

S3.13 gives a single integer threshold only for uniform fibers.

For a general surjection pi:P->Q, fiber sizes may vary.

S3.12 remains the exact criterion:

F embeds into P^pi iff there is an induced relational map h:F->Q such that

|h^(-1)({q})| <= |pi^(-1)({q})|

for every q in h(F).

Thus the full nonuniform obstruction is a capacity vector indexed by h(F), not merely one maximum integer.

The number kappa_F(Q) is the smallest uniform capacity sufficient for at least one induced map to lift.

It does not replace the pointwise capacity inequalities for nonuniform fibers.

============================================================
8. SCOPE AND LIMITATIONS
============================================================

The results rely on:

- a purely relational signature;
- finite arity for each relation symbol;
- nonempty finite forbidden structures;
- induced embeddings defined by preservation and reflection of every relation;
- the pullback interpretation of all relations.

The proof does not automatically extend to signatures with function symbols or constants, because arbitrary choices of representatives in fibers need not preserve those operations.

The proof also does not establish a corresponding theorem for infinitary relation arities or infinite forbidden structures.

For an infinite forbidden structure, the finite integer collision threshold may not exist in this form. A separate cardinal-valued formulation would be required.

No irreducibility assumption on forbidden structures is used.

============================================================
9. LOGICAL DISTINCTIONS PRESERVED
============================================================

S3.9:
Structural equality between free relational amalgam and quotient pullback is characterized by fiber saturation.

S3.12:
For a fixed surjection, class membership is characterized by pointwise fiber capacities for induced maps from forbidden structures.

S3.13:
For uniform finite fibers, the first failure size is the minimum collision number among all induced maps from forbidden structures into Q.

These are three different levels:

(A) equality of relational structures;
(B) membership in a forbidden class;
(C) existence of some other amalgam in the class.

S3.13 does not identify (A), (B), and (C).

It does not prove that every failure of this particular pullback construction rules out every possible amalgam.

It does not alter the independent P2.3 strong-amalgamation result.
