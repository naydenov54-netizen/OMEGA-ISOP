0. SCOPE

This audit tests the reconstructed A1–A3 framework in infinite-dimensional normed spaces, Banach spaces, Hilbert spaces, and nonlinear operator settings.

It does not recover the original Stage 3 specification. All A1–A3 formulations used here remain reconstructed working definitions. No result in this document is canonical unless separately approved through the project's required process.

Status labels:
PROVEN: follows from the explicitly stated definitions and assumptions.
CONDITIONAL: holds only under listed additional assumptions.
COUNTERMODEL: an explicit construction disproves a proposed implication.
RECONSTRUCTED: working formulation used for the audit.
UNVERIFIED: not established.

1. FRAMEWORK

Let X and Y be Banach spaces, with norms ||.||_X and ||.||_Y.
Let I be a nonempty parameter set.
For every i in I, let:
F_i : X -> Y
T_i : X -> X

Let A be a nonempty target subset of X and B a nonempty initial subset of X.

The norm uniformity on X is generated by entourages:
E_epsilon = {(x,x') in X×X : ||x-x'||_X < epsilon}, epsilon>0.

The norm topology and norm uniformity are related, but they are not interchangeable with the weak topology and its associated locally convex uniformity. The choice of topology or uniformity changes the meaning of convergence and equicontinuity.

Infinite-dimensionality alone does not invalidate continuity, covariance, or contraction arguments. It invalidates unqualified use of finite-dimensional compactness principles.

2. A1 — COVARIANCE IN INFINITE DIMENSION AND FOR NONLINEAR ACTIONS

Let G be a group acting on X and Y. Write the actions as:
alpha_g : X -> X
beta_g : Y -> Y

For each i in I, let F_i:X->Y.

Reconstructed A1:
For all i in I, g in G, and x in X:
F_i(alpha_g(x)) = beta_g(F_i(x)).

No linearity of F_i, alpha_g, or beta_g is required for this identity. No finite-dimensionality is required. The same algebraic statement applies to nonlinear operators and infinite-dimensional spaces.

If X and Y are topological or uniform spaces, continuity or uniform continuity of the actions and operators must be imposed separately. A1 covariance does not imply those regularity properties.

RESULTS:
PROVEN: the algebraic covariance identity extends unchanged to infinite-dimensional spaces.
PROVEN: linearity and finite dimensionality are not required for the identity.
CONDITIONAL: continuity or uniform continuity of the action maps requires explicit assumptions.
UNVERIFIED: whether original A1 imposed any regularity, boundedness, or domain-invariance conditions.

3. A2 — UNIFORM ATTRACTION IN A BANACH OR HILBERT SPACE

Use the reconstructed norm-neighborhood definition:
For every epsilon>0, there exists N such that for all i in I and all n>=N:
T_i^n(B) subset {x in X : dist(x,A)<epsilon}.

Here:
dist(x,A) = inf{||x-a||_X : a in A}.

This formulation is equivalent to the norm-uniformity neighborhood condition using open epsilon-neighborhoods of A. It is not automatically equivalent to every possible convention for attraction to a set in the unrecovered original specification.

3.1 Infinite-dimensional countermodel: individual attraction does not imply uniform attraction

Let X=ell2, the infinite-dimensional Hilbert space of square-summable sequences.
Let e_1=(1,0,0,...), A={0}, B={e_1}, and I={1,2,3,...}.

For each i define the bounded linear operator:
T_i(x) = q_i*x
where:
q_i = 1 - 1/(i+1).

For every fixed i:
0<q_i<1,
T_i^n(e_1)=q_i^n*e_1,
and therefore ||T_i^n(e_1)||=q_i^n -> 0 as n->infinity.

Thus each fixed parameter attracts B to A in norm.

But uniform attraction fails. Choose epsilon=1/2.
For any proposed common N, choose i sufficiently large that:
q_i^N >= 1/2.
Then:
||T_i^N(e_1)|| >= 1/2.
So T_i^N(B) is not contained in the open norm ball of radius 1/2 around A.

RESULT:
COUNTERMODEL: even in an infinite-dimensional Hilbert space, individual attraction for every parameter does not imply uniform attraction over an infinite parameter family.

This construction is a direct infinite-dimensional realization of the earlier scalar obstruction. It does not depend on any finite-dimensional compactness issue.

3.2 Nonlinear example: uniform attraction without a strict contraction

Let X be any nonzero Banach space and define:
T(x) = x/(1+||x||).

This is a nonlinear self-map of X.

For n>=0, direct iteration gives:
T^n(x) = x/(1+n*||x||).

Let B be any bounded subset of X, with ||x||<=R for every x in B, for some finite R.

Then:
sup over x in B of ||T^n(x)|| <= R/(1+n*R).

The right-hand side tends to zero as n tends to infinity. Therefore T^n(B) converges uniformly to A={0} in norm.

However, T is not a strict contraction on X. Indeed:
||T(x)-T(0)||/||x-0|| = 1/(1+||x||) for x!=0,
and this ratio approaches 1 as ||x|| approaches zero.
Consequently no global Lipschitz constant q<1 can satisfy:
||T(x)-T(y)|| <= q*||x-y|| for all x,y in X,
because setting y=0 and taking nonzero x arbitrarily close to zero contradicts such a q.

RESULT:
PROVEN: uniform attraction of every bounded initial set can occur for a nonlinear map that is not a strict contraction.
Therefore strict contraction is sufficient in many settings, but is not necessary for uniform attraction.

The boundedness of B is essential in this example. The result does not claim uniform attraction of arbitrary unbounded initial sets.

3.3 Infinite-dimensional compactness boundary

In an infinite-dimensional normed space, the closed unit ball is not compact in the norm topology.

For example, in ell2 the sequence of standard unit vectors:
e_1, e_2, e_3, ...
satisfies:
||e_n-e_m|| = sqrt(2) whenever n!=m.
It has no norm-convergent subsequence.

Therefore one cannot invoke the finite-dimensional theorem that closed and bounded subsets are compact.

Any proof using compactness of B, compactness of an invariant region, or extraction of norm-convergent subsequences must state an additional compactness hypothesis or use a different argument.

RESULT: PROVEN.

4. A3 — EQUICONTINUITY OF OPERATOR FAMILIES

For a family F_i:X->Y between normed spaces, reconstructed A3 in norm form is:

For every epsilon>0, there exists delta>0 such that for all i in I and all x,x' in X:
if ||x-x'||_X<delta, then ||F_i(x)-F_i(x')||_Y<epsilon.

The same delta must work for every i.

This is family equicontinuity in the norm uniformities. It is not merely pointwise continuity of each F_i.

4.1 Countermodel in an infinite-dimensional Hilbert space

Let X=Y=ell2 and I={1,2,3,...}.
Define:
F_i(x)=i*x.

Every F_i is bounded linear and continuous. In fact, each is globally Lipschitz with its own constant i.

The family is not equicontinuous at zero. Choose epsilon=1.
For any proposed delta>0, choose x=(delta/2)*e_1 and x'=0. Choose i>2/delta. Then:
||x-x'||=delta/2<delta,
but:
||F_i(x)-F_i(0)||=i*delta/2>1.

This contradicts the common-delta requirement.

RESULT:
COUNTERMODEL: individual continuity and individual uniform continuity do not imply family equicontinuity, even for bounded linear operators on a Hilbert space.

4.2 Compact operators: individual compactness is insufficient

Let P:ell2->ell2 be the rank-one projection:
P(x_1,x_2,...)=(x_1,0,0,...).

For every positive integer i define:
F_i=i*P.

Each F_i has finite-dimensional range and is therefore a compact linear operator.

Nevertheless, the family is not equicontinuous at zero. Taking x=(delta/2)*e_1 and x'=0 gives:
||F_i(x)-F_i(0)||=i*delta/2,
which can exceed 1 for sufficiently large i.

Thus:
Each operator compact
does not imply
The family is equicontinuous.

Individual compactness must not be confused with collective compactness of the family. In this example the union of the images of the unit ball is unbounded, so it cannot be relatively compact.

RESULT:
COUNTERMODEL: individual compactness of every operator does not imply family equicontinuity or collective compactness.

4.3 Sufficient condition: common Lipschitz bound

Suppose there is a finite constant L>=0 such that for every i in I and all x,x' in X:
||F_i(x)-F_i(x')||_Y <= L*||x-x'||_X.

Then the family is equicontinuous.

Proof:
Fix epsilon>0.
If L>0, choose delta=epsilon/L. Then ||x-x'||<delta implies:
||F_i(x)-F_i(x')|| <= L*||x-x'|| < epsilon
for every i.
If L=0, every F_i is constant on X and any delta>0 works.
Therefore the family is equicontinuous.

RESULT: PROVEN.
A common Lipschitz bound is sufficient, not necessary.

5. WEAK TOPOLOGY VERSUS NORM TOPOLOGY

Let X=ell2 and consider its standard unit vectors e_n.

The sequence e_n converges weakly to zero:
For every fixed y in ell2, the inner product <e_n,y> equals the n-th coordinate of y, which tends to zero.

But:
||e_n-0||=1 for every n.

Thus e_n does not converge to zero in norm.

Consequently, attraction to A={0} under the weak topology is weaker than attraction under the norm topology. The weak topology on a Banach space is generated by continuous linear functionals and has its own compatible locally convex uniformity.

A statement that says only "converges" or "approaches A" is incomplete in this setting unless the topology or uniformity is specified.

RESULT: PROVEN.

6. NECESSITY VERSUS SUFFICIENCY

A1:
The covariance identity is algebraic and requires neither linearity nor finite dimensionality.
Regularity of actions and operators is separate.
No necessity claim about a particular factorization is made.

A2:
The common-parameter convergence condition is the exact criterion under the reconstructed A2 definition.
A common strict contraction estimate is sufficient under appropriate invariant-set and domain assumptions.
The nonlinear radial example proves that strict contraction is not necessary.
Boundedness of the initial set is needed for the stated nonlinear example.

A3:
The common-delta condition is the exact criterion under reconstructed norm-equicontinuity.
A common Lipschitz bound is sufficient.
It is not necessary.
Individual continuity, individual uniform continuity, and individual compactness are each insufficient for an infinite family, as shown by explicit countermodels.

7. ROBUSTNESS SUMMARY

PROVEN:
1. Algebraic A1 extends to nonlinear maps and infinite-dimensional spaces.
2. Individual attraction does not imply uniform attraction in ell2.
3. A nonlinear map can uniformly attract every bounded set without being a strict contraction.
4. The closed unit ball in an infinite-dimensional normed space need not be compact.
5. Individual continuity and individual uniform continuity do not imply family equicontinuity.
6. Individual compactness of every operator does not imply family equicontinuity.
7. A common Lipschitz bound implies family equicontinuity.
8. Weak convergence and norm convergence can differ substantially in infinite-dimensional Hilbert spaces.

CONDITIONAL:
1. Contraction-based attraction requires explicit common contraction and invariant-domain assumptions.
2. Compactness-based arguments require actual compactness in the topology being used.
3. A3 depends on the chosen norm or uniform structure.

COUNTERMODEL:
1. T_i=q_i*I on ell2 disproves individual attraction => uniform attraction.
2. F_i=i*I on ell2 disproves individual uniform continuity => family equicontinuity.
3. F_i=i*P disproves individual compactness => family equicontinuity.

RECONSTRUCTED:
1. Norm-based A2 and A3 are working formulations for this audit.
2. The nonlinear radial map is a test model, not a proposed OMEGA-ISOP axiom.

UNVERIFIED:
1. Whether the original A2 used norm convergence, weak convergence, another topology, or a different attraction notion.
2. Whether the original A3 was defined on all of X, on a restricted domain, or only near an anchor.
3. Whether original A1–A3 imposed boundedness, compactness, differentiability, or invariance assumptions.
4. Whether the A1–A3 pairwise independence constructions from NIT-01 remain independent under the exact original Stage 3 domains and quantifiers.
5. Any uniform-space or infinite-dimensional interpretation of A4.
