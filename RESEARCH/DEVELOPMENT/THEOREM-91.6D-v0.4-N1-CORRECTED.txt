GENERAL IDENTIFIABILITY AND STABILITY CRITERION

==================================================
1. PURPOSE
==================================================

This theorem distinguishes identification of a full parameter from identification of a target quantity derived from that parameter. It also distinguishes exact identifiability, global Lipschitz stability, and local Lipschitz stability.

==================================================
2. FRAMEWORK
==================================================

Let (Theta,tau) be a nonempty topological space.

If a metric d_Theta is specified on Theta, tau may be taken as the topology induced by d_Theta.

Let (Y,d_Y) and (Q,d_Q) be metric spaces.

Let F:Theta->Y be an observation map.
Let q:Theta->Q be a target map.

The topology tau specifies the meaning of neighborhoods in the local stability definition.

No continuity of F or q is assumed unless explicitly stated.

==================================================
3. EXACT IDENTIFIABILITY AND FACTORIZATION
==================================================

The target q is exactly identifiable from F iff:

F(theta1)=F(theta2) => q(theta1)=q(theta2)

for all theta1,theta2 in Theta.

THEOREM 91.6D-1:

q is exactly identifiable from F iff there exists a unique map
g:F(Theta)->Q such that:

q=g o F.

Proof:
If q is constant on each fiber of F, define g(y)=q(theta) for any theta with F(theta)=y. Fiber constancy makes this well-defined. Every y in F(Theta) has a preimage, so the definition is unique. Conversely, if q=g o F, equality of F values implies equality of q values.

Q.E.D.

When q=id_Theta, exact identifiability is equivalent to injectivity of F. This special case requires Q=Theta and a specified metric on Theta.

==================================================
4. GLOBAL LIPSCHITZ STABILITY
==================================================

The target q is globally Lipschitz-stably identifiable from F iff there exists K>=0 such that:

d_Q(q(theta1),q(theta2))
<=K*d_Y(F(theta1),F(theta2))

for all theta1,theta2 in Theta.

THEOREM 91.6D-2:

Assuming exact identifiability, let g be the unique induced map on F(Theta). The global stability inequality holds iff g is globally Lipschitz on F(Theta), equipped with the subspace metric inherited from Y.

Proof:
Substitute q(theta)=g(F(theta)). The displayed inequality is exactly the Lipschitz inequality for g on pairs of points in F(Theta). Conversely, the Lipschitz inequality for g gives the stability inequality for q.

Q.E.D.

The global stability inequality itself implies exact identifiability because zero observation distance forces zero target distance.

==================================================
5. EXACT IDENTIFIABILITY DOES NOT IMPLY GLOBAL STABILITY
==================================================

Let Theta=R, Y=R, Q=R, with usual metrics:

F(x)=x^3
q(x)=x.

F is injective, so q is exactly identifiable. The induced map is:

g(y)=cube_root(y).

It is continuous on R but is not globally Lipschitz. For y>0:

|g(y)-g(0)|/|y-0|=y^(-2/3),

which is unbounded as y approaches zero.

In fact, this example is not locally Lipschitz at zero either. Do not use it to illustrate a function that is locally Lipschitz everywhere.

==================================================
6. LOCAL LIPSCHITZ STABILITY
==================================================

DEFINITION 91.6D-3:

The target q is locally Lipschitz-stably identifiable at theta_0 if there exist:

1. A neighborhood U of theta_0 in the specified topological space (Theta,tau).
2. A finite constant K_U>=0.

Such that:

d_Q(q(theta1),q(theta2))
<=K_U*d_Y(F(theta1),F(theta2))

for all theta1,theta2 in U.

This is a pairwise local condition, not merely continuity at theta_0.

The topology tau determines which sets qualify as neighborhoods. If no topology on Theta is specified, this local definition is not available.

The inequality on U itself implies that q is constant on every fiber of F restricted to U. Consequently, there exists a unique induced map g_U:F(U)->Q, and the local inequality is equivalent to g_U being Lipschitz on F(U) with the subspace metric inherited from Y.

LOCAL-BUT-NOT-GLOBAL LIPSCHITZ EXAMPLE:

Let Theta=R with its usual topology, Y=(0,infinity), Q=R:

F(x)=e^x
q(x)=x.

F is injective and the induced map is:

g(y)=ln(y).

For every x_0 in R, ln(y) is Lipschitz on a sufficiently small closed interval around e^(x_0) that stays inside (0,infinity), because its derivative 1/y is bounded there.

However, ln(y) is not globally Lipschitz on (0,infinity), since 1/y is unbounded as y approaches zero.

Thus local Lipschitz stability at every parameter value need not imply global Lipschitz stability.

LOCAL STABILITY DOES NOT IMPLY GLOBAL EXACT IDENTIFIABILITY:

Let Theta=R with its usual topology, Y=R, Q=R, all with their usual metrics.

Define:

F(x)=x^2
q(x)=x.

At theta_0=1 choose U=(1/2,3/2).

For all x_1,x_2 in U:

|q(x_1)-q(x_2)|
=|x_1-x_2|
=|F(x_1)-F(x_2)|/|x_1+x_2|
<=|F(x_1)-F(x_2)|,

because x_1+x_2>1.

Therefore q is locally Lipschitz-stably identifiable at theta_0=1 with K_U=1.

Nevertheless:

F(1)=F(-1)=1
q(1)=1
q(-1)=-1.

Hence q is not globally exactly identifiable from F.

At theta_0=0, local Lipschitz stability fails: for every neighborhood U of zero and every finite K_U, points x and 0 can be chosen in U with x nonzero and arbitrarily close to zero, while |x|<=K_U*x^2 fails for sufficiently small |x|.

This example establishes that local Lipschitz stability at one parameter value does not imply global exact identifiability. It does not establish local stability at every parameter value.

==================================================
7. IDENTIFICATION OF A TARGET VERSUS THE FULL PARAMETER
==================================================

The condition for q to be identifiable is only that q be constant on each fiber of F. F itself need not be injective.

If q=id_Theta, the condition becomes injectivity of F.

This distinction is essential: an observation can identify a target quantity without identifying every component of the underlying parameter.

==================================================
8. LIMITATIONS AND NON-IMPLICATIONS
==================================================

This theorem does not establish:
- statistical identifiability under a probability model;
- stability for arbitrary metrics not specified in the theorem;
- local stability without a specified domain topology and neighborhood definition;
- global exact identifiability from local stability at one parameter value;
- physical realizability or empirical validity;
- Shannon information preservation or loss;
- universal applicability to OMEGA-ISOP models;
- any formal connection to THEOREM 91 or to A, B and C beyond the stated abstract mathematical comparison.

The theorem's stability statements are metric statements about the induced factor map. The local statement additionally depends on the specified topology of Theta.

==================================================
9. LOOP AUDIT
==================================================

TARGET:
Exact identifiability iff unique factorization through F(Theta); global Lipschitz stability iff the induced factor is globally Lipschitz; local stability under the explicit topological and metric definition.

DEPENDENCIES:
Definitions of fibers, image F(Theta), metric subspace, Lipschitz maps, and the specified topology tau on Theta.

INDEPENDENCE:
The factor map is constructed only after fiber constancy is established. Its well-definedness is proved, not assumed.

COUNTEREXAMPLES:
F(x)=x^3 with q(x)=x refutes exact-identifiability => global Lipschitz stability and also refutes local Lipschitz stability at zero.
F(x)=e^x with q(x)=x shows local Lipschitz stability at each point without global Lipschitz stability.
F(x)=x^2 with q(x)=x shows local Lipschitz stability at theta_0=1 without global exact identifiability.

CLASSIFICATION:
NON-CIRCULAR DERIVATION WITHIN THE STATED SET-THEORETIC, TOPOLOGICAL AND METRIC FRAMEWORK.
